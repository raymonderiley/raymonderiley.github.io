Beyond the Hype – Why Resilience Professionals Must Treat AI as Both a Threat and a Tool


Artificial intelligence has officially crossed the threshold from experimental labs into the core operating engine of the modern enterprise. According to recent research highlighted by the Disaster Recovery Journal (DRJ), task-specific AI agents are projected to embed into 40% of enterprise applications by the end of 2026, while 15% of day-to-day business decisions will be made autonomously by AI agents by 2028.

For executives, boards, and technologists, this represents a massive leap in efficiency. But for the professionals tasked with keeping organizations running through disruption—business continuity, disaster recovery, crisis management, and operational risk teams—it presents a sobering reality: our traditional playbooks were written for human-speed environments.

How do you build a continuity plan for a system that can make thousands of erroneous, automated decisions before your team even returns from lunch? How do you verify an executive's identity when a deepfake can flawlessly clone their face and voice on a live video call?

Enter The AI in Resilience Framework (Version 1.0)—a landmark reference framework recently published by DRJ. Rather than acting as a rigid governance mandate or a machine learning engineering manual, it provides resilience teams with a shared vocabulary, a maturity model, and a practical structure to navigate the AI era.


The Dual Nature of AI: Risk vs. Capability
The core premise of the framework is simple yet profound - AI has a dual nature.
	1.	AI for Resilience: How artificial intelligence can supercharge our own toolkits—accelerating business impact analyses, automating anomaly detection, and drafting dynamic exercise scenarios.

    2.	Resilience for AI: How organizations defend against the novel risks, quiet model drift, toxic data poisoning, and autonomous failure cascades introduced by AI systems.
These two concepts cannot be treated as a single undifferentiated conversation, nor can they operate in silos. An AI tool adopted to streamline your continuity planning today can easily become your biggest operational vulnerability tomorrow if it goes down or hallucinates critical data.


Navigating the Three Pillars
The framework organizes this discipline into three distinct pillars and ten domains:

	⚬	Govern (The Spine): Corporate GRC owns enterprise AI policy, but resilience teams must operationalize it—ensuring clear accountability, robust audit trails, and strict compliance with standards like ISO/IEC 42001 and the EU AI Act.

    ⚬	Defend (Operational Risk): This pillar confronts the dark side of AI. It tackles deepfake-fueled social engineering, quiet model degradation, third-party model supply chain dependencies, and the terrifying velocity of autonomous agent failures.

    ⚬	Apply (Business Continuity): Here, AI becomes an operational ally. It streamlines BIAs, turbocharges incident detection and response dashboards, aids in real-time crisis communication filtering, and evaluates vendor tooling—always keeping human review firmly "on the switch".


Where Does Your Program Stand?
Most organizations today sit somewhere between Level 1 (Ad Hoc) and Level 2 (Aware) on the Al Resilience Maturity Model—meaning autonomous tools are creeping into operations without formal oversight, and risk handling is purely reactive.

Moving toward Defined (Level 3), Integrated (Level 4), and ultimately Adaptive (Level 5) doesn't require tearing up your existing program. The framework is explicitly built to fit seamlessly on top of ISO 22301, the BCI Good Practice Guidelines, and the DRI Professional Practices.


Change Starts on Monday: The 90-Day Playbook
Theory is only useful if it changes how you operate next week. You don't need a multi-year initiative to begin:
	⚬	Governance Track: Inventory where AI agents are acting in your workflows and assign explicit program accountability.
	⚬	Defend Track: Have your operational risk team map out your material AI dependencies and draft initial failure scenarios for deepfake threats.
	⚬	Apply Track: Pick a low-risk, high-toil task—like transcribing BIA interviews or drafting tabletop exercise scenarios—and pilot an AI-assisted approach with human verification.


The Road Ahead
Artificial intelligence is moving faster than any technological wave that preceded it. By embracing a shared vocabulary and structured framework, resilience professionals can stop playing catch-up and start building organizations that are truly prepared for whatever disruption comes next.
