AI-Powered Tabletop Exercises: Generating Disaster Recovery Scenarios (And Why Humans Must Review The Tabltops Prior to Use!)
As organizations strive to keep pace with an evolving threat landscape, disaster recovery (DR) and incident response teams face a constant bottleneck: scenario fatigue. Designing realistic, engaging, and challenging tabletop exercises (TTXs) requires significant time, creativity, and deep technical knowledge. Crafting timelines, secondary injects, and regulatory complications from scratch can take weeks.

Enter artificial intelligence (Super Intelligence?). By leveraging large language models and automation engines, security and resilience leaders can now generate comprehensive disaster recovery scenarios, inject timelines, and evaluation templates in a matter of minutes.

However, while AI is an exceptional engine for rapid ideation and drafting, putting an AI-generated tabletop straight into production without human oversight is a recipe for disaster (and embarrassment!). Human eyes—and critical human judgment—are non-negotiable.



The New Engine: How AI Accelerates Tabletop Generation
AI excels at data crunching, pattern recognition, and rapid text generation. When applied to tabletop creation, AI transforms how teams build training simulations:

Rapid Scenario Ideation:
Instead of brainstorming in a silo, teams can prompt an AI to generate 10 unique scenarios tailored to specific industries (e.g., healthcare, fintech, supply chain) instantly.
Vast Threat Vector Integration:
AI can seamlessly weave complex, multi-layered threat vectors together—such as combining a ransomware encryption event with a simultaneous physical HVAC failure and a public relations leak.
Dynamic Injects and Twists:
Generating realistic time-stamped injects (e.g., a sudden text message from a board member or an aggressive media inquiry) is tedious for humans; AI can generate dozens of cascading twists in seconds.
Personalization to Organizational Profiles:
By feeding an AI high-level descriptions of your tech stack, cloud providers, and regulatory environment (without exposing sensitive secrets), the tool can draft a scenario specifically mapped to your environment.




The Vital Filter: Why Human Review is Mandatory
Despite its speed and versatility, AI is not a standalone crisis manager. It operates on probability, pattern matching, and generalized training data. Running a tabletop exercise based purely on raw AI output introduces severe risks that make human subject matter expert (SME) review essential:

1. Spotting Hallucinations and Technical Impossibilities
AI models frequently "hallucinate"—inventing technical terms, impossible system architectures, or non-existent error codes that sound authoritative but make zero technical sense.
The Risk:
If a technical tabletop script features a recovery mechanism that violates basic cloud architecture rules or database replication physics, the technical team will spend the exercise arguing with the scenario rather than practicing their response. Human engineers must review the mechanics to ensure absolute technical realism.

2. Contextual Relevance and Organizational Nuance
An AI does not truly "know" your company culture, unwritten political structures, legacy technical debt, or internal vendor agreements.
The Risk:
An AI might generate a scenario assuming you have automated cloud failover when your organization actually relies on a manual, cold-site tape restoration process. Humans must calibrate the scenario to reflect the company's actual current maturity level, not an idealized version.

3. Validating Complexity Levels and Pacing
AI can easily over-engineer a scenario, packing so many catastrophes into a 2-hour window that participants experience total cognitive overload and shut down. Conversely, it might create an exercise that is far too simplistic.
The Risk:
Human facilitators must review and tune the pacing, ensuring that the "injects" arrive at a realistic cadence that tests decision-making without turning the exercise into an unmanageable farce.

4. Strategic Alignment with Business Goals
A tabletop exercise is only as good as the objectives it measures. AI might build an exciting, high-stakes cyber thriller that fails to test your actual compliance requirements, board reporting structures, or specific recovery time objectives (RTOs).
The Risk:
Human leaders must guide the AI prompt and review the final output to guarantee that the exercise directly aligns with audit mandates, executive priorities, and risk management frameworks.




The Synergy: Combining AI Speed with Human Wisdom

The most effective resilience programs don't choose between AI and human creation -— they combine them!


What AI Does Best - Heavy lifting, initial drafting, scenario variety, expanding timeline injects, and structuring documentation.


What Humans Do Best - Final strategy, validation of technical logic, emotional intelligence, tailoring to company culture, and ensuring regulatory accuracy.

By treating AI as an advanced drafting assistant rather than an autonomous decision-maker, organizations can slash the time it takes to build tabletop exercises while maintaining the rigor, realism, and precision required to protect the business when a real crisis strikes.
