Azure Purview Data Catalog Metadata Corruption and Ingestion Halt

Azure Cloud Resiliency

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines an Azure Purview (Microsoft Purview Data Map) incident in which a faulty scan rule set and a bulk API update corrupt classification and lineage metadata, ingestion jobs halt, sensitivity labels are removed from assets, and downstream access policies and data loss prevention controls that depend on the catalog stop working correctly.

Participants are working-level: data governance engineers, data platform engineers, data owners, the service desk, security operations and monitoring staff, the Microsoft support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of scans, classifications, collections, lineage, glossary terms, policy dependency on metadata and recovery of catalog state.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Teal Financial Group uses Microsoft Purview as the catalog for 4,000 data assets across Azure SQL, ADLS and Synapse. Sensitivity labels and classifications such as 'Customer PII' and 'Payment Card' drive data access policies, masking rules in downstream tools and DLP alerts. A nightly governance job syncs glossary terms and labels to business intelligence tools.

At 22:00 on Sunday, a data governance engineer applies a new scan rule set and runs a bulk update script against the Data Map API intended to re-map collections. A bug in the script overwrites classification and label attributes with null for about 1,900 assets and breaks lineage edges for pipelines. Scan jobs fail with schema validation errors and the ingestion of new metadata halts. At 06:00 Monday, BI tools stop masking sensitive columns because labels are missing, and DLP rules stop firing for some stores.

What the team does not yet know: Purview does not keep a full point-in-time backup of the Data Map, some assets were exposed in dashboards without masking for several hours, an export of the catalog from 3 weeks ago exists but is out of date, and another team's data product launch depends on the catalog tomorrow.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize metadata corruption. Participants read scan history, audit logs and asset samples to determine the extent of overwritten classifications and labels.
  • Contain data exposure. The team identifies where missing labels removed protections and restores controls manually where necessary.
  • Restore the catalog. Engineers rebuild classifications and lineage from scans, exports and source systems, in priority order.
  • Stop further corruption. Participants disable the faulty scan rule set and script and protect the API with change control.
  • Assess privacy and compliance impact. The team determines whether sensitive data was exposed and who must be told.
  • Improve data governance resilience. Participants review catalog export schedules, defense in depth, policy independence and script testing.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Treat any asset without a label as unprotected until proven otherwise.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Failed scans and missing masking lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Faulty jobs are stopped and exposure is contained.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Scope of corrupted metadata is determined.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A restore and compensating control plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Classifications and policies are restored in priority order.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Catalog and controls stabilize.

T+06:00 — Validation. Exposure assessment and validation are completed.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Injects

Inject #1: Labels vanish and masking stops

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 07:00. BI users notice previously masked columns showing full values. Purview scans have failed since 22:00 and 1,900 assets show no classifications.

The governance engineer discovers a bulk update script ran last night against the Data Map API and wrote null values to classification and label attributes. A new scan rule set was applied at the same time.

DLP and masking policies depend on the labels. No point-in-time backup exists; a three-week-old export is available. A data product launch is tomorrow.

Facilitator provides: scan history, audit log excerpts, the script, asset counts by tier and the old export.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Authorize temporary restrictions on sensitive stores.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Data Governance Engineering (Purview)
  • Stop jobs and scope affected assets.
  • Plan restoration by tier.
  • Plan lineage rebuild.
Data Platform Engineering
  • Apply compensating source controls.
  • Check BI dependencies.
  • Validate after restoration.
Data Owners & Service Desk
  • Confirm sensitivity of key assets.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Plan validation.
Security Operations & Monitoring
  • Review access logs.
  • Check DLP coverage.
  • Preserve logs.
Microsoft Support & TAM Liaison
  • Open a case.
  • Ask about restore.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform leadership.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Plan launch decision.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about privacy obligations.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. What is the first thing you do to protect sensitive data now?
  2. How do you determine which assets lost labels?
  3. What can you restore from the old export and what needs rescanning?
  4. How do you reduce reliance on catalog labels for protection?
  5. Who accessed unmasked data and how will you know?
  6. Do you proceed with the launch?
  7. Why did a bulk script run against production without a dry run?
  8. What does leadership need to hear?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal who accessed unmasked data until OPS reviews logs.
  • If players rescan everything blindly, ask about priority and time.
  • Look for containment first.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes restoring protections on the most sensitive data and sets the exposure assessment rule.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes temporary access restrictions.
  • Decides on notifications.

Data Governance Engineering (Purview)

  • Stops the faulty scan and script and reviews audit logs.
  • Identifies affected assets and priority tiers.
  • Restores labels and classifications from exports and rescans.
  • Rebuilds lineage.

Data Platform Engineering

  • Applies compensating controls at the source such as database masking and access restrictions.
  • Checks downstream BI and policy dependencies.
  • Validates restored metadata against sources.
  • Fixes scripts.

Data Owners & Service Desk

  • Data owners confirm sensitivity of key assets and approve temporary restrictions.
  • The service desk handles user questions about blocked data.
  • Owners plan priority validation.
  • Prepare checks.

Security Operations & Monitoring

  • Reviews access logs to see who accessed unmasked data during the gap.
  • Checks DLP and alert coverage.
  • Preserves audit logs.
  • Adds detections for metadata changes.

Microsoft Support & TAM Liaison

  • Opens a Sev A case on Data Map restore options.
  • Asks about audit and version history.
  • Requests guidance on bulk API behavior.
  • Records guidance.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for analytics and data product launches and informs leadership.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates the launch decision.
  • Tracks impact.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.28, 5.12 classification, 5.13 labelling; SOC 2 confidentiality).
  • Assesses privacy obligations.
  • Captures evidence of exposure analysis.
  • Logs findings on governance controls.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Do not let one metadata store be the only guard: enforce masking and access at the source with defense in depth, so missing labels do not equal missing protection.

Protect the catalog: scheduled exports, API permission scoping, dry-run modes and peer review for bulk updates.

Fail closed: when labels are absent, downstream policies should treat data as sensitive by default.

Alert on large-scale metadata changes and scan failures.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (5.12, 5.13) and SOC 2 expect classification controls and evidence of protection continuity.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on fail-closed protection and exposure assessment.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

  5. How did you handle false positives?
  6. Who accessed unmasked data?
  7. How did you decide on the launch?
  8. How was lineage rebuilt?
  9. How accurate were updates to the CDO and CISO?
  10. What did Microsoft support add?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?

  13. What access or permissions were missing?
  14. What alerts are missing?
