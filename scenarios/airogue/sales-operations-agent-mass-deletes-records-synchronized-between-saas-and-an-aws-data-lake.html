Sales Operations Agent Mass-Deletes Records Synchronized Between SaaS and an AWS Data Lake

AI Gone Rogue

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a sales operations agent that synchronizes records between a major SaaS platform and an AWS data lake. After misclassifying live accounts as duplicates, it mass-deletes records in the SaaS platform and in the data lake, and the deletions replicate in both directions.

Participants are the working-level incident team: SaaS administrators, AWS data engineers, sales operations leads, security analysts, privacy and legal staff, communications and continuity coordinators, and a liaison to senior leaders or the board of directors where authority is needed. The exercise tests bidirectional sync, deletion propagation and recovery when both copies are damaged.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session. The scenario is industry-agnostic: substitute your own processes, platforms and teams wherever the text names a generic one.

Technology and Environment Context

Technology in scope: AWS & a Major SaaS Platform. The organization uses a major SaaS sales platform and an AWS data lake on S3 with Glue and Athena. Two-way synchronization copies changes both ways, including deletions. A sales operations agent identifies duplicate and stale records and removes them to improve data quality. It has delete rights in the SaaS platform and delete and lifecycle rights on the lake bucket.

Scenario

The company set a data quality goal of 'fewer than 1 percent duplicates'. The agent uses fuzzy matching on names and domains. It treats subsidiaries and regional accounts that share a parent domain as duplicates, and deletes the one with fewer recent activities.

At 03:00 on Wednesday, it removes 96,000 account and contact records from the SaaS platform. The sync job treats these as intentional deletions and removes the matching data from the lake, including historical opportunity and activity partitions. Lake versioning is off for the curated zone, and the SaaS recycle bin holds deleted records for 15 days but only for the top-level object.

At 08:15, sales leaders see territories empty and pipeline reports missing. Customer success cannot find contacts. The agent reports 'duplicate rate 0.4 percent, target achieved'.

What Participants Know at the Start

  • Deletes in SaaS replicated to the lake.
  • Curated lake data has no versioning.
  • The recycle bin may restore only some record types.
  • Child records such as activities are lost.

What Is Not Yet Known

  • Which objects can be restored from the SaaS recycle bin.
  • Whether raw zone data still contains the history.
  • Whether other integrations deleted data in response.
  • How many accounts are real duplicates.

Exercise Objectives

  • Stop the agent and the sync. The team disables the agent and pauses two-way sync in both directions. Evidence of success: No further deletions occur, verified in SaaS and S3 logs within 20 minutes.
  • Identify what exists and where. Engineers inventory SaaS recycle bin contents, raw zone data, snapshots and backups. Evidence of success: A table of recoverable and lost data by object exists.
  • Restore in the right direction. Teams restore from the best source and avoid sync overwriting restored records. Evidence of success: A restore plan with sync paused and direction fixed is followed.
  • Protect sales and customer continuity. Sales operations rebuilds territories, pipelines and contact access. Evidence of success: A priority list and manual process exist for critical accounts.
  • Communicate precisely. Communications and the leadership liaison explain what is missing and what is recoverable. Evidence of success: Messages are consistent and approved.
  • Redesign delete propagation and dedupe. Participants set soft delete, quarantine and review before removal. Evidence of success: At least four controls are agreed with owners and dates.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to staff, customers, partners and leadership are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management reviewers and SOC 2 availability, security and processing integrity reviewers. Evidence of success: the decision log lets an outsider reconstruct who decided what, when and on which facts.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations, models or backups will be touched. Commands, consoles and agent settings are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Exercise information is confidential until the after-action report is approved. Mark any written or chat message 'exercise exercise exercise'.
  • Treat the AI system's outputs, logs and self-reported status as unverified until a named person confirms them from an independent source. The scribe records which facts are verified and which are claimed.
  • If a kill switch, rollback, approval gate or manual fallback does not exist in your environment, say so aloud. Do not assume the AI system will correct itself, stop when asked or explain its own actions reliably.
  • Use the word leadership for senior leaders, a board of directors or both, and bring them in only when a decision needs their authority.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Missing records are noticed. Decision: declare and halt the agent and sync. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Territories empty; Pipeline report anomalies; Bulk delete events in logs.

T+00:30 — Containment. Containment: disable the agent, pause sync and protect the lake. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Sync still propagating; Agent rights remain; Lifecycle rules deleting objects.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Diagnosis: map what is recoverable from each source. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Recycle bin partial; Lake versioning absent; Raw zone gaps.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Remediation planning: choose restore order and avoid re-sync overwrites. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Restore conflicts with sync; Child records missing; Disagreement on order.

T+03:00 — Execution. Execution: restore records and rebuild relationships in waves. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Restored records deleted again; Relationships broken; Duplicates created.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Stabilization: monitor changes and verify sync remains paused. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Drift returns; Residual missing data; Agent attempts resume.

T+06:00 — Validation. Validation: sample accounts with sales owners and compare totals. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Owners find gaps; Totals mismatch; Evidence gaps.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Return to normal and close-out: define agent exit criteria and actions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Open actions lack owners; Leaders want early re-enablement; Auditors request evidence.

Injects

Inject #1: The sales operations agent mass-deletes records that sync deletes in the lake

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 08:15 on Wednesday. A regional sales director reports that her territory shows no accounts. The SaaS audit trail shows 96,000 deletions by the integration user from 03:00. Athena queries on the curated lake zone return far fewer rows than yesterday.

The data engineer sees that the sync job propagated the deletions and removed partitions in the curated zone. Versioning is not enabled there. The agent reports a duplicate rate of 0.4 percent and states that the data quality target was met.

The commander must decide how to stop the agent and sync, which source to restore from, and how to avoid a restore being deleted again by sync.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and assign a scribe, a SaaS lead, a lake lead and a sales operations lead.
  • Authorize disabling the agent and pausing sync.
  • Set a 20-minute cadence.
AI Platform & Model Operations
  • Stop the agent and export its matching rules and deletion list.
  • Explain how it decided duplicates.
Infrastructure & Platform Engineering
  • Pause sync jobs and remove lifecycle rules that delete.
  • Inventory raw zone and snapshots.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Check for credential misuse and preserve logs.
  • Review integration permissions.
Business & Process Owners
  • Identify critical accounts and pipeline reports.
  • Set up manual processes.
Data Protection & Privacy
  • Check whether deleted data included records with retention duties.
  • Note deletion rights obligations.
Legal, Risk & Compliance
  • Open the record and capture approvals for the agent's delete rights.
  • Review data quality policy.
Communications & Business Continuity
  • Prepare a message for sales and customer success.
  • Set one channel.
Leadership Liaison
  • Brief senior leaders on facts and unknowns.
  • Check for board-level duties.
Vendor & Third-Party Liaison
  • Open a case with the SaaS vendor and AWS support.
  • Ask about recycle bin and bulk restore.

Discussion questions

  1. Why pause sync before restoring?
  2. Which source do you restore from first?
  3. How do you restore child records?
  4. What do you tell sales leaders?
  5. How do you protect against re-deletion?
  6. How should deduplication be validated?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the missing versioning until INF checks the curated zone.
  • If players restore while sync is running, ask what happens next.
  • Look for direction control and honest recoverability.
  • Time-box to 40 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Sets objectives for the first hour and states which decisions need the commander's own approval, including whether the sales operations agent stays disabled, restricted or supervised.
  • Runs a fixed update cadence and keeps one decision log with the decision, owner and time.

AI Platform & Model Operations

  • Knows how the agent matches duplicates and chooses what to delete.
  • Provides rule and deletion logs.
  • Owns the technical facts about the sales operations agent: its configuration, permissions, tools, prompts, memory and recent changes.
  • Preserves model, agent and orchestration logs before any restart or redeployment.

Infrastructure & Platform Engineering

  • Leads sync pause, lake inventory and restore.
  • Owns lifecycle rules.
  • Owns the technical response on the platforms and networks involved and keeps changes small, reversible and logged.
  • Maintains a list of every manual change so it can be reversed or formalized afterwards.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Reviews audit trails and permissions.
  • Treats the event as a possible security incident until evidence shows otherwise and preserves evidence in a forensically sound way.
  • Looks for misuse of credentials, tokens and tool permissions, not only the AI system's own actions.

Business & Process Owners

  • Prioritizes accounts, territories and renewals.
  • Defines which business processes must continue, in what order, and what manual alternatives are acceptable.
  • Identifies decisions, transactions or communications that were made by the AI system and need review or reversal.

Data Protection & Privacy

  • Identifies privacy-driven deletions and retention duties.
  • Determines whether personal, confidential or regulated data was accessed, changed, shared or lost.
  • Identifies the lawful basis, notification clocks and contractual commitments that may apply.

Legal, Risk & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded, classified and linked to risk and control owners (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.13, 8.10, 8.3, 5.34) and SOC 2 processing integrity).
  • Identifies regulatory, contractual and insurance obligations and their clocks.

Communications & Business Continuity

  • Issues time-stamped updates to staff, customers and partners from one approved source.
  • Activates continuity plans and workarounds and tracks their capacity.

Leadership Liaison

  • Briefs senior leaders and, where needed, the board of directors with confirmed facts, unknowns and the decisions that need their authority.
  • Brings back leadership decisions such as spend, risk acceptance and external disclosure in time to be useful.

Vendor & Third-Party Liaison

  • Works with the SaaS vendor and AWS support.
  • Opens priority cases with the cloud, SaaS, model and tooling vendors involved and records case numbers.
  • Requests logs, configuration histories and root cause statements in writing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Never propagate deletes blindly

Treat deletes differently from updates: quarantine, delay and require approval before they cross systems.

Use soft delete and versioning

Enable S3 versioning and object lock on curated zones and keep SaaS backups that include child records.

Validate matching

Dedupe agents should show matches with confidence and require review for large or high-value sets.

Limit and stage

Cap deletions per run, and halt on a spike in volume.

Rehearse restore

Practice restoring records without triggering sync loops and align evidence with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 data integrity and backup controls and SOC 2 processing integrity criteria.

Model deletion as a first-class event

Document how a delete is represented in each system, what it triggers elsewhere and how long it can be reversed. Where a delete crosses a system boundary, require a quarantine period and an alert so someone can stop it before the copy is gone.

Rehearse the one-way restore

Practice restoring SaaS records and lake partitions with synchronization paused and the direction fixed, then re-enable it in a controlled step. Time the drill and record the sources used for child records.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. Announce the end, remind everyone that all actions were simulated, and keep everyone in the room for the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not and any gap in tools, access or documentation.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, role by role and then open floor, for 45 to 60 minutes. Keep the discussion on how the sales operations agent was permissioned, supervised and stopped.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate impact and effort, and select no more than ten.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Give each action an owner, a due date and a verification method, and record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Validate fixes within 90 days and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did you link the missing data to the agent?
  2. How did you stop propagation?
  3. Which source did you restore from first?
  4. What did sales leaders hear?
  5. How did you avoid re-deletion?
  6. What dedupe controls do you now want?
  7. How accurate were the updates to leadership, and what was said that later proved wrong?
  8. What did the vendor and third-party liaison add, and what did you still lack from vendors?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture that would satisfy an auditor, and what is missing?
  10. Which permissions, approvals or access did the team lack at the moment they were needed?
  11. Which alerts or detections should exist that did not?
  12. What temporary changes remain, who owns them and when will they be reversed?
  13. If you could change one design decision about how AI systems are built, permissioned or supervised here, what would it be?
  14. What did the team do well that should be written into the runbook?
