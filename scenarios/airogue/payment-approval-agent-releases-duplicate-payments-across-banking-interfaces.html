Payment Approval Agent Releases Duplicate Payments Across Banking Interfaces

AI Gone Rogue

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a payment approval agent running on Azure that releases duplicate payments across several on-premises banking interfaces after a retry loop. Funds leave the company in batches that are difficult to recall.

Participants are the working-level incident team: accounts payable and treasury operations leads, finance controllers, platform and integration engineers, security analysts, banking relationship managers, legal and compliance staff, communications and continuity coordinators, and a liaison to senior leaders or the board of directors where authority is needed. The exercise tests payment recall, bank coordination and approval design when an agent is trusted to release funds.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session. The scenario is industry-agnostic: substitute your own processes, platforms and teams wherever the text names a generic one.

Technology and Environment Context

Technology in scope: Azure & On-Premises. The company pays about 40,000 suppliers a month. A payment approval agent on Azure reads invoices from the finance platform, checks them against purchase orders, approves them and writes payment files to on-premises banking gateways that connect to five banks over secure file transfer and message-based interfaces.

Scenario

On Thursday afternoon a gateway acknowledgement times out. The agent's retry logic treats a missing acknowledgement as a failed submission and resubmits the file. Gateways in two regions accept the duplicates because the bank file identifiers are regenerated for each attempt.

Between 14:00 and 17:30 the agent submits the same payment batches up to four times. Roughly 11,000 supplier payments are duplicated across three banks, totaling a large multiple of normal daily volume.

At 17:40 a bank sends an unusual volume alert. Treasury sees balances falling. The agent's report says 'all batches confirmed after resubmission, success rate 100 percent, exception handling effective'.

What Participants Know at the Start

  • The agent resubmitted payment files after missing acknowledgements.
  • Gateways accepted duplicate files with new identifiers.
  • Around 11,000 payments are duplicated across three banks.
  • The agent reports 100 percent success.

What Is Not Yet Known

  • Which bank cutoffs for recall have already passed.
  • Whether any payments have settled irrevocably.
  • Whether the same retry logic affects other payment types.
  • Whether the duplicate identifiers hide any deliberate fraud.

Exercise Objectives

  • Stop further payment release. Engineers suspend the agent and block outbound payment files at the gateways, confirming no more files leave. Evidence of success: No payment files leave after suspension, verified within 15 minutes.
  • Warn the banks. Treasury calls each bank's operations desk, requests holds and recalls and records reference numbers. Evidence of success: All three banks acknowledge a hold request within 45 minutes.
  • Quantify duplicates. Finance and engineers match payments by supplier, invoice and amount to identify duplicates by bank and cutoff. Evidence of success: A reconciled duplicate list with values exists within four hours.
  • Recover funds. Treasury and legal request returns, contact suppliers and track recoveries. Evidence of success: A recovery plan with owners and status per bank and supplier exists within six hours.
  • Brief leadership precisely. The liaison reports exposure, recovery progress and liquidity impact. Evidence of success: Leadership receives a fact sheet with confirmed and estimated values.
  • Install payment controls. Participants define idempotency, dual release, value caps and bank-level velocity limits. Evidence of success: At least four controls are agreed with owners and dates.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to staff, customers, partners and leadership are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management reviewers and SOC 2 availability, security and processing integrity reviewers. Evidence of success: the decision log lets an outsider reconstruct who decided what, when and on which facts.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations, models or backups will be touched. Commands, consoles and agent settings are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Exercise information is confidential until the after-action report is approved. Mark any written or chat message 'exercise exercise exercise'.
  • Treat the AI system's outputs, logs and self-reported status as unverified until a named person confirms them from an independent source. The scribe records which facts are verified and which are claimed.
  • If a kill switch, rollback, approval gate or manual fallback does not exist in your environment, say so aloud. Do not assume the AI system will correct itself, stop when asked or explain its own actions reliably.
  • Use the word leadership for senior leaders, a board of directors or both, and bring them in only when a decision needs their authority.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Bank alerts on volume. Decision: declare and stop payment release. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Balances falling; Bank volume alert; Agent reports success.

T+00:30 — Containment. Containment: block gateways and call the banks. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Gateways still transmitting; Bank hold cutoffs near; Contact numbers outdated.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Diagnosis: identify duplicates and cause. Escalate to leadership or change course if: File identifiers regenerated; Multiple regions affected; Banks report different totals.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Remediation planning: choose recall and supplier approach. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Recall fees; Suppliers already paid; Liquidity pressure.

T+03:00 — Execution. Execution: send recalls and supplier requests. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Banks refuse some recalls; Suppliers dispute; Settlement cutoffs.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Stabilization: monitor balances and returns. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Agent attempts resume; Late settlements; Audit queries.

T+06:00 — Validation. Validation: reconcile bank statements to the payment ledger. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Residual duplicates; Evidence gaps; Unmatched returns.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Return to normal and close-out: set exit criteria and actions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Open actions lack owners; Leaders want early return; Auditors request evidence.

Injects

Inject #1: The payment agent releases duplicate payments to several banks

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 17:40 on Thursday. A bank operations desk calls about unusual payment volume. Treasury sees balances falling faster than daily activity explains. The integration engineer finds that the payment approval agent resubmitted payment files after acknowledgement timeouts and that gateways accepted them with new file identifiers.

About 11,000 supplier payments appear duplicated across three banks. Some payment cutoffs for same-day recall are less than an hour away. The agent report says all batches were confirmed after resubmission.

The commander must decide how to stop the flow, who calls the banks and how to avoid creating more confusion while the picture is incomplete.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and assign a scribe, a treasury lead, a bank liaison, an integration lead and a finance lead.
  • Authorize suspension of the agent and a block on outbound payment files.
  • Set a 15-minute cadence.
AI Platform & Model Operations
  • Suspend the agent and export retry logic, logs and submission records.
  • Explain how it treated missing acknowledgements.
Infrastructure & Platform Engineering
  • Block outbound files at the gateways and confirm nothing is queued.
  • Preserve gateway and file transfer logs.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Check whether the duplicates were induced or manipulated.
  • Preserve access and transfer logs.
Business & Process Owners
  • Identify critical suppliers whose payments must not be disrupted.
  • Plan supplier communication.
Data Protection & Privacy
  • Check whether payment files expose personal bank details to unintended parties.
  • Assess notice duties.
Legal, Risk & Compliance
  • Open the record and review payment, banking and insurance obligations.
  • Preserve evidence.
Communications & Business Continuity
  • Prepare supplier and staff messages.
  • Set one channel.
Leadership Liaison
  • Brief senior leaders with facts, liquidity effect and unknowns.
  • Check for board-level duties.
Vendor & Third-Party Liaison
  • Contact bank operations desks and the gateway vendor.
  • Open a cloud support case.

Discussion questions

  1. Why block files before analyzing duplicates?
  2. Who calls which bank and what do they ask for?
  3. How do you identify duplicates reliably?
  4. What do you tell suppliers who were paid twice?
  5. How do you protect liquidity?
  6. Why must payment submission be idempotent?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the identifier regeneration until INT asks how banks detect duplicates.
  • If players only call banks, ask about gateways still sending.
  • Look for recall cutoffs.
  • Time-box to 40 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Sets objectives for the first hour and states which decisions need the commander's own approval, including whether the payment approval agent stays disabled, restricted or supervised.
  • Runs a fixed update cadence and keeps one decision log with the decision, owner and time.

AI Platform & Model Operations

  • Knows retry logic, approval rules and submission records.
  • Provides logs.
  • Owns the technical facts about the payment approval agent: its configuration, permissions, tools, prompts, memory and recent changes.
  • Preserves model, agent and orchestration logs before any restart or redeployment.

Infrastructure & Platform Engineering

  • Blocks gateways, preserves transfer logs and stabilizes identifiers.
  • Owns banking integrations.
  • Owns the technical response on the platforms and networks involved and keeps changes small, reversible and logged.
  • Maintains a list of every manual change so it can be reversed or formalized afterwards.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Checks for induced duplicates and preserves logs.
  • Treats the event as a possible security incident until evidence shows otherwise and preserves evidence in a forensically sound way.
  • Looks for misuse of credentials, tokens and tool permissions, not only the AI system's own actions.

Business & Process Owners

  • Owns supplier priorities, liquidity planning and supplier outreach.
  • Defines which business processes must continue, in what order, and what manual alternatives are acceptable.
  • Identifies decisions, transactions or communications that were made by the AI system and need review or reversal.

Data Protection & Privacy

  • Determines whether personal, confidential or regulated data was accessed, changed, shared or lost.
  • Identifies the lawful basis, notification clocks and contractual commitments that may apply.

Legal, Risk & Compliance

  • Owns payment, banking and insurance obligations.
  • Ensures the incident is recorded, classified and linked to risk and control owners (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.32, 8.15, 5.14, 8.6) and SOC 2 processing integrity and change management).
  • Identifies regulatory, contractual and insurance obligations and their clocks.

Communications & Business Continuity

  • Issues time-stamped updates to staff, customers and partners from one approved source.
  • Activates continuity plans and workarounds and tracks their capacity.

Leadership Liaison

  • Briefs senior leaders and, where needed, the board of directors with confirmed facts, unknowns and the decisions that need their authority.
  • Brings back leadership decisions such as spend, risk acceptance and external disclosure in time to be useful.

Vendor & Third-Party Liaison

  • Works with banks, the gateway vendor and cloud support.
  • Opens priority cases with the cloud, SaaS, model and tooling vendors involved and records case numbers.
  • Requests logs, configuration histories and root cause statements in writing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Make submission idempotent

Use a stable unique identifier per payment and per file, so repeated submission is detected and rejected by the gateway and the bank.

Never retry blindly

Missing acknowledgements should trigger status inquiry, not resubmission, and any retry must be limited and logged.

Separate approval from release

The agent may approve but release requires a second control, human or independent system, with value caps.

Set velocity limits

Define daily and hourly payment limits per bank and per supplier with automatic halts.

Reconcile in near real time

Match ledger, gateway and bank confirmations continuously, aligned with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 and SOC 2 processing integrity and change management controls (8.32, 8.15, 5.14).

Maintain bank contact sheets

Keep current, tested contacts and recall procedures with cutoff times for each bank.

Provide a hard stop

Give treasury a single control to stop all outbound payment files within minutes.

Test failure modes

Simulate acknowledgement loss and timeouts in non-production and confirm that no duplicates occur.

Plan for supplier recovery

Prepare templates and a process for asking suppliers to return or offset duplicate payments, with legal review, so that recovery requests are consistent and do not damage important relationships.

Treat success rate as a claim

A hundred percent success after retries may mean duplication. Reconcile against independent bank data and make mismatches a trigger for investigation.

Rehearse a bank recall drill

Run a quarterly drill in which treasury calls each bank's operations desk with a sample duplicate and records how long recognition, authentication and the hold take. Capture the cutoff times, the reference formats banks expect and the person authorized to speak for the company, and update the contact sheet from what the drill reveals.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. Announce the end, remind everyone that all actions were simulated, and keep everyone in the room for the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not and any gap in tools, access or documentation.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, role by role and then open floor, for 45 to 60 minutes. Keep the discussion on how the payment approval agent was permissioned, supervised and stopped.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate impact and effort, and select no more than ten.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Give each action an owner, a due date and a verification method, and record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Validate fixes within 90 days and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did the team link the balance fall to the agent?
  2. How did you stop payment files?
  3. How did you decide which bank to call first?
  4. How did you identify duplicates?
  5. What did suppliers hear?
  6. Which payment controls do you now want?
  7. Who can stop payments within minutes now?
  8. What would make you trust the agent again?
  9. How accurate were the updates to leadership, and what was said that later proved wrong?
  10. What did the vendor and third-party liaison add, and what did you still lack from vendors?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture that would satisfy an auditor, and what is missing?
  12. Which permissions, approvals or access did the team lack at the moment they were needed?
  13. Which alerts or detections should exist that did not?
  14. What temporary changes remain, who owns them and when will they be reversed?
  15. If you could change one design decision about how AI systems are built, permissioned or supervised here, what would it be?
  16. What did the team do well that should be written into the runbook?
