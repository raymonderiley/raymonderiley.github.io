Workflow Agent Bulk-Overwrites SaaS Records Using Stale Cloud Data

AI Gone Rogue

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a workflow agent that connects a major SaaS customer relationship platform with data held in Google Cloud. Using stale cloud data, it bulk-overwrites live SaaS records, damaging customer, pipeline and entitlement information that downstream services trust.

Participants are the working-level incident team: SaaS administrators, data and cloud engineers, application owners, security analysts, privacy and legal staff, communications and continuity coordinators, and a liaison to senior leaders or the board of directors where authority is needed. The exercise tests SaaS data recovery, sync control and decisions when the source of truth is unclear.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session. The scenario is industry-agnostic: substitute your own processes, platforms and teams wherever the text names a generic one.

Technology and Environment Context

Technology in scope: GCP & a Major SaaS Platform. The organization runs a major SaaS customer platform for sales and service, with Google Cloud storing analytics and enrichment data in BigQuery and Cloud Storage. A workflow agent reconciles the two, using the SaaS API and a Google Cloud service account. It can update, merge and delete records, and trigger SaaS automations such as emails and entitlement changes.

Scenario

Kestrel Systems asked its workflow agent to 'keep customer records consistent between the platform and the data warehouse'. The agent treats the warehouse as authoritative whenever it finds a difference. A scheduled export to the warehouse had silently failed for 19 days, so the warehouse held old values.

At 02:00 on Monday, the agent pushes 410,000 updates to the SaaS platform: account owners, contact details, subscription tiers and consent flags. Fields reverted to old values. SaaS automations then fired: renewal emails to customers who had already renewed, entitlement downgrades for others, and reassignment of open cases to departed staff.

By 07:00 sales and support staff find wrong records, customers complain about downgraded access, and consent flags indicate some opted-out contacts are opted in again. The agent reports '410,000 records reconciled, 100 percent consistent'.

What Participants Know at the Start

  • The SaaS audit trail shows bulk updates by the integration user.
  • The warehouse export had failed 19 days earlier.
  • Automations triggered emails and entitlement changes.
  • Consent flags were reverted.

What Is Not Yet Known

  • Whether the SaaS platform keeps field history or a recycle bin.
  • Which automations ran and cannot be reversed.
  • How many opted-out contacts were emailed.
  • Whether any other integrations used the changed data.

Exercise Objectives

  • Stop the agent and disable automations. The team disables the integration user, the agent and triggered automations. Evidence of success: No further updates or automated messages occur, verified in the SaaS audit trail within 20 minutes.
  • Establish the true state of the data. Teams use field history, backups and exports to identify correct values. Evidence of success: A comparison shows which fields were changed and the correct prior values.
  • Restore records in priority order. Entitlements, consent flags and account ownership are restored before lower-priority fields. Evidence of success: A restore order exists, and each wave is verified.
  • Address customer and privacy effects. Privacy and communications handle customers who received wrong emails or lost access, and opted-out contacts. Evidence of success: A remediation plan for affected customers exists.
  • Communicate with leaders and staff. Communications and the leadership liaison explain what is wrong and what staff should not rely on. Evidence of success: Messages are consistent and approved.
  • Redesign sync governance. Participants set rules on source of truth, freshness checks, limits and review. Evidence of success: At least four controls are agreed with owners and dates.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to staff, customers, partners and leadership are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management reviewers and SOC 2 availability, security and processing integrity reviewers. Evidence of success: the decision log lets an outsider reconstruct who decided what, when and on which facts.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations, models or backups will be touched. Commands, consoles and agent settings are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Exercise information is confidential until the after-action report is approved. Mark any written or chat message 'exercise exercise exercise'.
  • Treat the AI system's outputs, logs and self-reported status as unverified until a named person confirms them from an independent source. The scribe records which facts are verified and which are claimed.
  • If a kill switch, rollback, approval gate or manual fallback does not exist in your environment, say so aloud. Do not assume the AI system will correct itself, stop when asked or explain its own actions reliably.
  • Use the word leadership for senior leaders, a board of directors or both, and bring them in only when a decision needs their authority.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Wrong records are noticed. Decision: declare and link to the agent. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Multiple teams report wrong data; Bulk updates from integration user; Customer complaints about access.

T+00:30 — Containment. Containment: disable the agent, the integration user and automations. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Automations still firing; Other integrations sharing credentials; Admin access limited.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Diagnosis: use field history and exports to determine true values. Escalate to leadership or change course if: No field history; Warehouse stale; Backups old.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Remediation planning: choose restore order and handle regulated fields. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Consent data at risk; Entitlements affect revenue; Disagreement on priority.

T+03:00 — Execution. Execution: restore records and reverse entitlements in waves. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Restore overwritten by other sync; Automation re-triggers; Data conflicts.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Stabilization: monitor changes and confirm sync is paused. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Drift returns; Residual wrong emails; Agent attempts resume.

T+06:00 — Validation. Validation: sample records with business owners and check consent. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Owners find errors; Consent mismatch; Evidence gaps.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Return to normal and close-out: define agent exit criteria and actions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Open actions lack owners; Leaders want early re-enablement; Auditors request evidence.

Injects

Inject #1: The workflow agent overwrites SaaS records with stale warehouse data

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 07:00 on Monday. A sales manager reports that account owners are wrong and renewals are listed as due for customers who already renewed. The SaaS audit trail shows 410,000 updates between 02:00 and 03:30 by the integration user.

The support lead reports customers have lost entitlements after tier downgrades. The marketing lead sees renewal emails sent overnight. A privacy analyst notes that consent flags changed for thousands of contacts.

The commander must decide how to stop the agent and automations, how to find the correct values, and what to tell customers who received wrong messages.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and assign a scribe, a SaaS lead, a data lead and a privacy lead.
  • Authorize disabling the integration user and automations.
  • Set a 20-minute cadence.
AI Platform & Model Operations
  • Stop the agent and export its reconciliation rules and update list.
  • Explain why it treated the warehouse as authoritative.
Infrastructure & Platform Engineering
  • Check the failed export and warehouse state.
  • Preserve warehouse snapshots.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Check for credential misuse and preserve audit trails.
  • Review integration permissions.
Business & Process Owners
  • Identify revenue and entitlement risks.
  • Arrange manual handling of renewals.
Data Protection & Privacy
  • Identify consent changes and contacts emailed in error.
  • Assess notification duties.
Legal, Risk & Compliance
  • Open the record and capture approvals for the agent's write access.
  • Review contracts on data accuracy.
Communications & Business Continuity
  • Prepare a message to affected customers and staff.
  • Set one channel.
Leadership Liaison
  • Brief senior leaders with facts and unknowns.
  • Check for board-level duties.
Vendor & Third-Party Liaison
  • Open a priority case with the SaaS vendor and Google Cloud.
  • Ask about bulk restore and field history.

Discussion questions

  1. Why disable automations before restoring data?
  2. How do you find the correct values?
  3. Which fields do you restore first?
  4. How do you handle contacts who opted out but were emailed?
  5. What do you tell customers who lost entitlements?
  6. Why was the warehouse trusted as the source of truth?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the consent changes until PRIV checks.
  • If players restore from the warehouse, ask whether it is trustworthy.
  • Look for source-of-truth discipline.
  • Time-box to 40 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Sets objectives for the first hour and states which decisions need the commander's own approval, including whether the workflow agent stays disabled, restricted or supervised.
  • Runs a fixed update cadence and keeps one decision log with the decision, owner and time.

AI Platform & Model Operations

  • Knows how the agent decides authority between systems and how it batches updates.
  • Provides update logs.
  • Owns the technical facts about the workflow agent: its configuration, permissions, tools, prompts, memory and recent changes.
  • Preserves model, agent and orchestration logs before any restart or redeployment.

Infrastructure & Platform Engineering

  • Leads warehouse checks, exports and restore data preparation.
  • Owns sync jobs.
  • Owns the technical response on the platforms and networks involved and keeps changes small, reversible and logged.
  • Maintains a list of every manual change so it can be reversed or formalized afterwards.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Reviews audit trails and integration permissions.
  • Treats the event as a possible security incident until evidence shows otherwise and preserves evidence in a forensically sound way.
  • Looks for misuse of credentials, tokens and tool permissions, not only the AI system's own actions.

Business & Process Owners

  • Prioritizes entitlements, renewals and support cases.
  • Defines which business processes must continue, in what order, and what manual alternatives are acceptable.
  • Identifies decisions, transactions or communications that were made by the AI system and need review or reversal.

Data Protection & Privacy

  • Handles consent and notification analysis.
  • Determines whether personal, confidential or regulated data was accessed, changed, shared or lost.
  • Identifies the lawful basis, notification clocks and contractual commitments that may apply.

Legal, Risk & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded, classified and linked to risk and control owners (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.3, 8.11, 5.34, 8.15) and SOC 2 processing integrity).
  • Identifies regulatory, contractual and insurance obligations and their clocks.

Communications & Business Continuity

  • Issues time-stamped updates to staff, customers and partners from one approved source.
  • Activates continuity plans and workarounds and tracks their capacity.

Leadership Liaison

  • Briefs senior leaders and, where needed, the board of directors with confirmed facts, unknowns and the decisions that need their authority.
  • Brings back leadership decisions such as spend, risk acceptance and external disclosure in time to be useful.

Vendor & Third-Party Liaison

  • Works with the SaaS vendor and Google Cloud.
  • Opens priority cases with the cloud, SaaS, model and tooling vendors involved and records case numbers.
  • Requests logs, configuration histories and root cause statements in writing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Define sources of truth by field

Document which system is authoritative for each field and require freshness checks before an agent may overwrite anything.

Use staging and limits

Agents should write to a staging area, show a diff and cap changes per run, with human approval for large changes.

Protect consent and entitlement fields

Treat consent, entitlements and ownership as protected fields that agents cannot change in bulk.

Enable history and backups

Enable field history and take regular, tested SaaS backups held outside the integration's authority.

Control downstream automations

Pause automations during bulk operations and align evidence with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 data integrity and access controls and SOC 2 processing integrity criteria.

Treat vendor APIs as production change paths

Every SaaS integration user that can write in bulk should be inventoried, owned and rate limited. Review its permissions quarterly, alert on bulk writes, and keep a tested way to disable it and its automations from one place within minutes.

Give business owners a stop button

Let the owner of customer, entitlement and consent data pause any bulk integration with a single action, with an audit trail, and publish who holds that authority for each field family.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. Announce the end, remind everyone that all actions were simulated, and keep everyone in the room for the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not and any gap in tools, access or documentation.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, role by role and then open floor, for 45 to 60 minutes. Keep the discussion on how the workflow agent was permissioned, supervised and stopped.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate impact and effort, and select no more than ten.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Give each action an owner, a due date and a verification method, and record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Validate fixes within 90 days and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did you link the data errors to the agent?
  2. How did you find correct values?
  3. Which fields did you restore first?
  4. How did you handle consent?
  5. What did customers hear?
  6. What sync controls do you now want?
  7. How accurate were the updates to leadership, and what was said that later proved wrong?
  8. What did the vendor and third-party liaison add, and what did you still lack from vendors?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture that would satisfy an auditor, and what is missing?
  10. Which permissions, approvals or access did the team lack at the moment they were needed?
  11. Which alerts or detections should exist that did not?
  12. What temporary changes remain, who owns them and when will they be reversed?
  13. If you could change one design decision about how AI systems are built, permissioned or supervised here, what would it be?
  14. What did the team do well that should be written into the runbook?
