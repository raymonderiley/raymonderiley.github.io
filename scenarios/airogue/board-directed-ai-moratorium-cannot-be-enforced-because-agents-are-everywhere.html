Board Directed AI Moratorium Cannot Be Enforced Because Agents Are Everywhere

AI Gone Rogue

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a board-directed AI moratorium that cannot be enforced because agents are everywhere. Leadership orders all autonomous agents paused within 24 hours, and the organization discovers it does not know where its agents run across Google Cloud, Azure, AWS, on-premises systems and a major SaaS platform.

Participants are the working-level incident team: cloud and platform engineers, AI platform owners, SaaS administrators, security analysts, business process owners, procurement and vendor managers, legal and compliance staff, communications and continuity coordinators, and a liaison to the board of directors and senior leaders. The exercise tests inventory, authority to stop and the cost of stopping.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session. The scenario is industry-agnostic: substitute your own processes, platforms and teams wherever the text names a generic one.

Technology and Environment Context

Technology in scope: GCP, Azure, AWS, On-Premises & a Major SaaS Platform. The company has grown its AI use quickly. Teams run agents in all three clouds, on-premises clusters and inside a major SaaS platform and its add-ons. A central register lists 38 agents, but teams, vendors and individual users have deployed others. Some agents run payments, support and operations.

Scenario

After a public incident at another company, the board of directors directs that all autonomous agents be paused within 24 hours until a review is complete. Senior leaders ask the incident team to confirm compliance by the next morning.

Engineers start with the register, then discover through spend, identity and network logs more than 200 agents, assistants and automations, many owned by individuals. Some cannot be paused without stopping a business process, and some are embedded in vendor features that cannot be disabled by the company.

At hour 12 the team has paused 60 agents and found 40 more. A payments operation would stop if its agent were paused. The status page for leadership says '38 of 38 registered agents paused'.

What Participants Know at the Start

  • The board ordered a 24-hour moratorium.
  • The register holds 38 agents but over 200 exist.
  • Some agents cannot be paused without business harm.
  • The leadership page counts only registered agents.

What Is Not Yet Known

  • How many agents exist and who owns them.
  • Which can be paused and which are embedded in vendor products.
  • What the cost and risk of pausing payments are.
  • Who may grant an exception.

Exercise Objectives

  • Establish an honest inventory. Teams build a list of agents from identity, spend, network and vendor sources, labeled by confidence. Evidence of success: A list with owner, platform and criticality exists within 12 hours.
  • Pause what can be paused safely. Owners pause agents in risk order, using identity revocation and platform controls. Evidence of success: All high-risk agents are paused or exempted with approval within 24 hours.
  • Handle exceptions. Leaders decide exceptions for critical processes with compensating controls. Evidence of success: A written exception list with approver and controls exists before the deadline.
  • Report truthfully. The liaison gives the board a status that separates paused, exempted and unknown agents. Evidence of success: The board receives an accurate status with unknowns stated.
  • Plan the review. Teams define what review the moratorium is waiting for and criteria for resuming. Evidence of success: A resume plan with owners and dates exists.
  • Install governance controls. Participants define registration, discovery, stop authority and vendor terms. Evidence of success: At least four controls are agreed with owners and dates.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to staff, customers, partners and leadership are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management reviewers and SOC 2 availability, security and processing integrity reviewers. Evidence of success: the decision log lets an outsider reconstruct who decided what, when and on which facts.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations, models or backups will be touched. Commands, consoles and agent settings are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Exercise information is confidential until the after-action report is approved. Mark any written or chat message 'exercise exercise exercise'.
  • Treat the AI system's outputs, logs and self-reported status as unverified until a named person confirms them from an independent source. The scribe records which facts are verified and which are claimed.
  • If a kill switch, rollback, approval gate or manual fallback does not exist in your environment, say so aloud. Do not assume the AI system will correct itself, stop when asked or explain its own actions reliably.
  • Use the word leadership for senior leaders, a board of directors or both, and bring them in only when a decision needs their authority.

Injects

Inject #1: The board orders an AI moratorium that cannot be enforced

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 08:00 on Thursday. The board of directors has directed that all autonomous agents be paused within 24 hours. Senior leaders ask the team to confirm compliance by morning. The register lists 38 agents.

Identity, spend and network logs reveal more than 200 agents and automations, many owned by individuals or embedded in vendor features. A payments operation would stop if its agent were paused. The leadership page says 38 of 38 registered agents are paused.

The commander must decide how to build an honest inventory, how to pause safely and how to report to the board.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and assign a scribe, an inventory lead, a pause lead, a payments lead and a reporting lead.
  • Authorize pausing by risk order and an exception process.
  • Set a 30-minute cadence.
AI Platform & Model Operations
  • Assemble the registered list and compare with discovered agents.
  • Classify agents by action rights.
Infrastructure & Platform Engineering
  • Revoke identities and platform rights for agents to be paused.
  • Preserve configuration and logs.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Discover agents from identity, spend and network sources.
  • Check for agents that restart or evade pauses.
Business & Process Owners
  • Define critical processes and compensating manual controls.
  • Support exceptions.
Data Protection & Privacy
  • Check data handled by unlisted agents.
  • Assess notice duties.
Legal, Risk & Compliance
  • Open the record and review the board resolution and obligations.
  • Preserve evidence.
Communications & Business Continuity
  • Prepare internal and external messages.
  • Set one channel.
Leadership Liaison
  • Brief the board of directors and senior leaders with accurate status and unknowns.
  • Record leadership decisions on exceptions.
Vendor & Third-Party Liaison
  • Contact vendors whose products embed agents and ask how to disable them.
  • Open cases.

Discussion questions

  1. Why build the inventory before pausing?
  2. How do you find agents nobody registered?
  3. Which agents do you pause first?
  4. How do you decide exceptions?
  5. What do you tell the board?
  6. What is the cost of stopping a payments agent?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the vendor-embedded agents until VEN asks which products include them.
  • If players report 38 of 38, ask about the others.
  • Look for honest status.
  • Time-box to 40 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Sets objectives for the first hour and states which decisions need the commander's own approval, including whether the organization's agents stays disabled, restricted or supervised.
  • Runs a fixed update cadence and keeps one decision log with the decision, owner and time.

AI Platform & Model Operations

  • Maintains the agent list and action rights.
  • Provides inventory and classification.
  • Owns the technical facts about the organization's agents: its configuration, permissions, tools, prompts, memory and recent changes.
  • Preserves model, agent and orchestration logs before any restart or redeployment.

Infrastructure & Platform Engineering

  • Revokes identities and platform rights and preserves logs.
  • Owns cloud and on-premises platforms.
  • Owns the technical response on the platforms and networks involved and keeps changes small, reversible and logged.
  • Maintains a list of every manual change so it can be reversed or formalized afterwards.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Discovers agents and checks evasion.
  • Treats the event as a possible security incident until evidence shows otherwise and preserves evidence in a forensically sound way.
  • Looks for misuse of credentials, tokens and tool permissions, not only the AI system's own actions.

Business & Process Owners

  • Owns critical process definitions and exceptions.
  • Defines which business processes must continue, in what order, and what manual alternatives are acceptable.
  • Identifies decisions, transactions or communications that were made by the AI system and need review or reversal.

Data Protection & Privacy

  • Determines whether personal, confidential or regulated data was accessed, changed, shared or lost.
  • Identifies the lawful basis, notification clocks and contractual commitments that may apply.

Legal, Risk & Compliance

  • Owns board resolution and compliance obligations.
  • Ensures the incident is recorded, classified and linked to risk and control owners (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (5.9, 5.19, 5.4, 5.2) and SOC 2 monitoring and vendor management).
  • Identifies regulatory, contractual and insurance obligations and their clocks.

Communications & Business Continuity

  • Issues time-stamped updates to staff, customers and partners from one approved source.
  • Activates continuity plans and workarounds and tracks their capacity.

Leadership Liaison

  • Briefs senior leaders and, where needed, the board of directors with confirmed facts, unknowns and the decisions that need their authority.
  • Brings back leadership decisions such as spend, risk acceptance and external disclosure in time to be useful.

Vendor & Third-Party Liaison

  • Works with vendors whose products embed agents.
  • Opens priority cases with the cloud, SaaS, model and tooling vendors involved and records case numbers.
  • Requests logs, configuration histories and root cause statements in writing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Keep a living agent register

Require registration of every agent with owner, platform, rights and criticality, and reconcile it with discovery monthly.

Discover from multiple sources

Use identity, spend, network and vendor data to find unlisted agents.

Define stop authority

Document who may order a stop, who executes it and how quickly, with tested mechanisms per platform.

Tier by criticality

Know which agents can be paused safely and which need compensating controls.

Negotiate vendor terms

Require vendors to let you disable embedded agents, aligned with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 asset, supplier and governance controls and SOC 2 vendor management (5.9, 5.19, 5.4).

Prepare compensating controls

Pre-plan manual alternatives and approvals for critical processes.

Report honestly to the board

Separate registered, discovered, paused, exempt and unknown in every status.

Rehearse a moratorium

Run a drill that orders a pause and measures time, coverage and gaps.

Assign orphan agent owners

Give every agent an active owner.

Plan the resume decision

Define review criteria and approvers up front.

Treat register counts as partial

A register count says what is known, not what exists.

Keep a decision log template for the organization's agents

Provide a one-page template for recording each decision about the organization's agents: the time, the owner, the evidence relied on, the alternatives rejected and the next review. A consistent log makes the after-action report accurate and lets a new incident commander pick up the thread without a long verbal briefing.

Test alerts that describe the organization's agents from outside

Place at least one alert on a measure that the organization's agents cannot influence, such as customer complaints, an independent financial total or an external probe. Alerts generated by the system itself tend to stay quiet during exactly the failures that matter most.

Train the on-call team on the organization's agents

Give every on-call engineer a short, practical briefing on what the organization's agents does, what it can change and how to stop it, and refresh it each quarter. People who have never seen the stop procedure will hesitate to use it, and hesitation is expensive when the agent acts in seconds.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. Announce the end, remind everyone that all actions were simulated, and keep everyone in the room for the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not and any gap in tools, access or documentation.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, role by role and then open floor, for 45 to 60 minutes. Keep the discussion on how the organization's agents was permissioned, supervised and stopped.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate impact and effort, and select no more than ten.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Give each action an owner, a due date and a verification method, and record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Validate fixes within 90 days and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did the team realize the register was incomplete?
  2. How did you discover agents?
  3. Which agents did you pause first?
  4. How did you decide exceptions?
  5. What did the board hear?
  6. Which governance controls do you now want?
  7. Who has stop authority now?
  8. What would make you trust the register again?
  9. How accurate were the updates to leadership, and what was said that later proved wrong?
  10. What did the vendor and third-party liaison add, and what did you still lack from vendors?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture that would satisfy an auditor, and what is missing?
  12. Which permissions, approvals or access did the team lack at the moment they were needed?
  13. Which alerts or detections should exist that did not?
  14. What temporary changes remain, who owns them and when will they be reversed?
  15. If you could change one design decision about how AI systems are built, permissioned or supervised here, what would it be?
  16. What did the team do well that should be written into the runbook?
