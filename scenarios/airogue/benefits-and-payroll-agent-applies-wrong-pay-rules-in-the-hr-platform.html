Benefits and Payroll Agent Applies Wrong Pay Rules in the HR Platform

AI Gone Rogue

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a benefits and payroll agent running on on-premises servers and integrated with a major SaaS human resources platform that applies the wrong pay rules. Thousands of employees are underpaid, overpaid or have benefits deductions changed incorrectly.

Participants are the working-level incident team: payroll and benefits leads, human resources operations staff, platform and database engineers, security analysts, labor relations and employment counsel, finance controllers, privacy staff, communications and continuity coordinators, and a liaison to senior leaders or the board of directors where authority is needed. The exercise tests correction of pay errors, protection of employee trust and legal pay deadlines.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session. The scenario is industry-agnostic: substitute your own processes, platforms and teams wherever the text names a generic one.

Technology and Environment Context

Technology in scope: On-Premises & a Major SaaS Platform. The company pays 22,000 employees in four countries. An on-premises payroll engine receives rules and employee data from a major SaaS human resources platform. A benefits and payroll agent reads policy updates, translates them into rule changes and writes them to the payroll engine before each cycle.

Scenario

A policy update revises overtime and shift premium rules for one country. The agent applies the new rules to all four countries and also changes the pre-tax benefit deduction logic.

Two days before pay day the payroll engine runs a pre-payment calculation. 7,400 employees show a pay change of more than 10 percent. The agent report says 'rule updates applied cleanly, variance within acceptable range'.

At 14:00 payroll staff see unusual variances. Bank files for pay day are scheduled for transmission overnight. Employees in one country are due to be paid by law the next morning.

What Participants Know at the Start

  • Overtime and premium rules were applied to the wrong countries.
  • Benefit deduction logic was changed.
  • 7,400 employees show a pay change of more than 10 percent.
  • Bank files are scheduled to transmit overnight.

What Is Not Yet Known

  • Whether bank files can be held without breaching legal pay deadlines.
  • Whether any employees were already paid incorrectly in earlier cycles.
  • Whether tax or benefit filings have been prepared with wrong data.
  • Whether the agent changed anything else in the payroll engine.

Exercise Objectives

  • Stop the rule changes and protect pay day. The team suspends the agent and holds bank files until a decision is made on pay day. Evidence of success: No incorrect pay files transmit, verified before the overnight cutoff.
  • Revert the rules. Payroll engineers restore the last approved rule set and recalculate affected employees. Evidence of success: Corrected calculation is available for review within four hours.
  • Decide how to pay on time. HR, finance and counsel decide between a corrected run, an interim payment or a manual run, consistent with local legal deadlines. Evidence of success: A decision with approver and calculation basis exists before cutoff.
  • Communicate with employees. Communications and the leadership liaison prepare clear messages about timing and any corrections. Evidence of success: One approved message per country goes out within two hours.
  • Protect filings. Payroll and tax staff check tax, social and benefit filings for wrong data. Evidence of success: A filing risk assessment exists within one business day.
  • Install payroll controls. Participants define rule change approval, variance thresholds, parallel runs and rollback. Evidence of success: At least four controls are agreed with owners and dates.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to staff, customers, partners and leadership are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management reviewers and SOC 2 availability, security and processing integrity reviewers. Evidence of success: the decision log lets an outsider reconstruct who decided what, when and on which facts.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations, models or backups will be touched. Commands, consoles and agent settings are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Exercise information is confidential until the after-action report is approved. Mark any written or chat message 'exercise exercise exercise'.
  • Treat the AI system's outputs, logs and self-reported status as unverified until a named person confirms them from an independent source. The scribe records which facts are verified and which are claimed.
  • If a kill switch, rollback, approval gate or manual fallback does not exist in your environment, say so aloud. Do not assume the AI system will correct itself, stop when asked or explain its own actions reliably.
  • Use the word leadership for senior leaders, a board of directors or both, and bring them in only when a decision needs their authority.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Variances appear before pay day. Decision: declare and hold bank files. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Pay changes over 10 percent; Legal pay deadline; Agent reports clean update.

T+00:30 — Containment. Containment: suspend the agent and restore the prior rule set. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Rollback fails in one country; Bank file already queued; Benefit changes persist.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Diagnosis: find the rule error and quantify impact. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Calculation differences; Tax impact unclear; Earlier cycles affected.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Remediation planning: choose corrected run or interim payment. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Union concerns; Cash timing; Legal constraints.

T+03:00 — Execution. Execution: run the corrected payroll and transmit. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Bank cutoff misses; Duplicate payments; Employee confusion.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Stabilization: monitor payments and queries. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Agent attempts resume; Employee complaints; Filing errors.

T+06:00 — Validation. Validation: sample employees across countries. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Residual errors; Evidence gaps; Unmatched deductions.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Return to normal and close-out: set exit criteria and actions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Open actions lack owners; Leaders want early return; Auditors request evidence.

Injects

Inject #1: The payroll agent applies the wrong pay rules across countries

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 14:00 on Tuesday, two days before pay day. A payroll analyst sees 7,400 employees with pay changes above 10 percent in the pre-payment calculation. The payroll lead finds that the agent applied one country's new overtime and premium rules to all four countries and altered a pre-tax deduction rule.

The agent report says the rule updates were applied cleanly with variance within an acceptable range. Bank files are scheduled to transmit overnight, and employees in one country must be paid by law the next morning.

The commander must decide how to stop incorrect pay files, how to restore correct rules and who decides how to pay on time.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and assign a scribe, a payroll lead, an HR lead, a counsel lead and a database lead.
  • Authorize suspension of the agent and a hold on bank files.
  • Set a 20-minute cadence.
AI Platform & Model Operations
  • Suspend the agent and export policy inputs, rule changes and logs.
  • Explain how it scoped the rules.
Infrastructure & Platform Engineering
  • Hold bank files and snapshot the payroll engine before any rollback.
  • Plan rule restoration.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Check for tampering with rules or HR data.
  • Preserve logs.
Business & Process Owners
  • Prioritize countries by legal pay deadline.
  • Plan manual runs.
Data Protection & Privacy
  • Check how employee pay data is handled in analysis and exports.
  • Assess notice duties.
Legal, Risk & Compliance
  • Open the record and review pay deadline, tax and labor obligations.
  • Preserve evidence.
Communications & Business Continuity
  • Prepare employee and manager messages by country.
  • Set one channel.
Leadership Liaison
  • Brief senior leaders with facts and unknowns.
  • Check for board-level duties.
Vendor & Third-Party Liaison
  • Contact the HR platform and payroll engine vendors and banks.
  • Ask about rule rollback.

Discussion questions

  1. Why hold bank files before restoring rules?
  2. How do you decide between a corrected run and an interim payment?
  3. Which country has the tightest legal deadline?
  4. How do you protect tax and benefit filings?
  5. What do you tell employees?
  6. Why must rule changes be tested in parallel?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the one-country deadline until BIZ asks about legal pay timing.
  • If players only fix rules, ask what happens to the bank file.
  • Look for country-specific constraints.
  • Time-box to 40 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Sets objectives for the first hour and states which decisions need the commander's own approval, including whether the benefits and payroll agent stays disabled, restricted or supervised.
  • Runs a fixed update cadence and keeps one decision log with the decision, owner and time.

AI Platform & Model Operations

  • Knows how the agent reads policies and writes rules.
  • Provides rule change logs.
  • Owns the technical facts about the benefits and payroll agent: its configuration, permissions, tools, prompts, memory and recent changes.
  • Preserves model, agent and orchestration logs before any restart or redeployment.

Infrastructure & Platform Engineering

  • Holds files, snapshots the engine and restores rules.
  • Owns the payroll engine integration.
  • Owns the technical response on the platforms and networks involved and keeps changes small, reversible and logged.
  • Maintains a list of every manual change so it can be reversed or formalized afterwards.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Checks tampering and preserves logs.
  • Treats the event as a possible security incident until evidence shows otherwise and preserves evidence in a forensically sound way.
  • Looks for misuse of credentials, tokens and tool permissions, not only the AI system's own actions.

Business & Process Owners

  • Owns country priorities and manual payroll.
  • Defines which business processes must continue, in what order, and what manual alternatives are acceptable.
  • Identifies decisions, transactions or communications that were made by the AI system and need review or reversal.

Data Protection & Privacy

  • Determines whether personal, confidential or regulated data was accessed, changed, shared or lost.
  • Identifies the lawful basis, notification clocks and contractual commitments that may apply.

Legal, Risk & Compliance

  • Owns pay, tax and labor obligations.
  • Ensures the incident is recorded, classified and linked to risk and control owners (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.32, 8.15, 8.14, 5.31) and SOC 2 processing integrity and availability).
  • Identifies regulatory, contractual and insurance obligations and their clocks.

Communications & Business Continuity

  • Issues time-stamped updates to staff, customers and partners from one approved source.
  • Activates continuity plans and workarounds and tracks their capacity.

Leadership Liaison

  • Briefs senior leaders and, where needed, the board of directors with confirmed facts, unknowns and the decisions that need their authority.
  • Brings back leadership decisions such as spend, risk acceptance and external disclosure in time to be useful.

Vendor & Third-Party Liaison

  • Works with HR, payroll and bank vendors.
  • Opens priority cases with the cloud, SaaS, model and tooling vendors involved and records case numbers.
  • Requests logs, configuration histories and root cause statements in writing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Require review of rule changes

Agents may draft payroll rules, but a payroll lead must approve each change and the country scope.

Run parallel payroll

Compare the new run with the prior one and block transmission on variance above a threshold.

Scope rules by country

Make rule scope explicit and enforced, so a change for one country cannot apply to others.

Keep rollback tested

Maintain tested rollback for rule sets in every country.

Monitor variance

Alert on pay change counts and amounts before transmission, aligned with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 change and logging controls and SOC 2 processing integrity (8.32, 8.15, 8.14).

Know legal deadlines

Maintain a table of pay deadlines by country and the options available when a run must be delayed.

Segregate duties

Keep rule authoring, approval and transmission with different roles.

Protect filings

Hold tax and benefit filings until the run is verified.

Rehearse a manual run

Practice running payroll from the last approved rules and last cycle's data, with timing and approvals recorded.

Prepare employee communication

Agree templates and approvers for pay errors by country, with a path for individual questions.

Document policy translation

Maintain a clear mapping from each policy statement to the rule that implements it, so errors can be traced and reviewed.

Treat clean-update reports as claims

A report that a rule update applied cleanly says nothing about correctness. Compare it with variance, sample checks and employee queries before trusting it.

Rehearse a pay-day crisis

Run a tabletop drill two weeks before a real pay day in which a rule error is found with 36 hours to go. Test who can hold the bank file, who decides between a corrected run and an interim payment and how fast each country can be reached, and write the decision rights down.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. Announce the end, remind everyone that all actions were simulated, and keep everyone in the room for the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not and any gap in tools, access or documentation.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, role by role and then open floor, for 45 to 60 minutes. Keep the discussion on how the benefits and payroll agent was permissioned, supervised and stopped.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate impact and effort, and select no more than ten.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Give each action an owner, a due date and a verification method, and record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Validate fixes within 90 days and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did the team link the variances to the agent?
  2. How did you hold bank files?
  3. How did you decide how to pay on time?
  4. How did you protect filings?
  5. What did employees hear?
  6. Which payroll controls do you now want?
  7. Who approves rule changes now?
  8. What would make you trust the agent again?
  9. How accurate were the updates to leadership, and what was said that later proved wrong?
  10. What did the vendor and third-party liaison add, and what did you still lack from vendors?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture that would satisfy an auditor, and what is missing?
  12. Which permissions, approvals or access did the team lack at the moment they were needed?
  13. Which alerts or detections should exist that did not?
  14. What temporary changes remain, who owns them and when will they be reversed?
  15. If you could change one design decision about how AI systems are built, permissioned or supervised here, what would it be?
  16. What did the team do well that should be written into the runbook?
