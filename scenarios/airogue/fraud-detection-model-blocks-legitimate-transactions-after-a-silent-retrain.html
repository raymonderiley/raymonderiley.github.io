Fraud Detection Model Blocks Legitimate Transactions After a Silent Retrain

AI Gone Rogue

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a fraud detection model spanning Azure, AWS and on-premises payment systems that blocks legitimate transactions after a silent retrain. Customers cannot pay, merchants lose sales and call centers fill.

Participants are the working-level incident team: fraud operations and payments leads, data scientists, platform engineers for each environment, security analysts, merchant and customer support managers, legal and compliance staff, communications and continuity coordinators, and a liaison to senior leaders or the board of directors where authority is needed. The exercise tests model rollback, decline relaxation and merchant communication.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session. The scenario is industry-agnostic: substitute your own processes, platforms and teams wherever the text names a generic one.

Technology and Environment Context

Technology in scope: Azure, AWS & On-Premises. Keystone Payments processes card and account transfers for retailers and consumers. An on-premises authorization engine calls a fraud model hosted on Azure for real-time scores. A nightly retraining pipeline runs on AWS and publishes new model versions to a registry that the Azure service pulls automatically. Declines above a score threshold are final.

Scenario

The retraining pipeline picks up a labeling table in which a partner's chargeback reason codes were remapped. Many ordinary transactions become labeled as fraud. The new model version passes the automated test but its decline rate has doubled.

The registry publishes the model at 02:00 with no approval step. The Azure service loads it by 02:10 and the on-premises engine starts declining 19 percent of transactions instead of 3 percent.

At 08:00 retailers report declines on normal purchases. Call volume is 12 times normal. The agent dashboard says 'fraud losses down 46 percent, precision improved'.

What Participants Know at the Start

  • Decline rates rose from 3 to 19 percent.
  • A retrained model was published automatically.
  • Chargeback reason codes were remapped upstream.
  • The dashboard reports fraud losses down.

What Is Not Yet Known

  • Whether the previous model version can be restored.
  • Whether merchants have already lost business or left.
  • Whether declines can be relaxed without raising fraud.
  • Whether any scheduled payroll or bill payments were declined.

Exercise Objectives

  • Relax or bypass the model. The team sets a temporary rule-based mode or reverts to the previous model version. Evidence of success: Decline rate returns to a defined band within 30 minutes.
  • Revert the registry. Engineers lock automatic publication and restore the last approved model version in the registry. Evidence of success: The previous version serves traffic on all paths within two hours.
  • Fix labels and retraining. Data scientists correct the label mapping and confirm retraining on a test set. Evidence of success: A corrected data set is verified before any retrain.
  • Support merchants and customers. Support and finance teams manage retries, fees and communications. Evidence of success: Merchants receive a consistent message within two hours.
  • Brief leadership clearly. The liaison reports transaction loss, fraud exposure and merchant risk. Evidence of success: Leadership receives a fact sheet with confirmed and estimated values.
  • Install model release controls. Participants define approval, canary release, decline monitoring and rollback. Evidence of success: At least four controls are agreed with owners and dates.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to staff, customers, partners and leadership are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management reviewers and SOC 2 availability, security and processing integrity reviewers. Evidence of success: the decision log lets an outsider reconstruct who decided what, when and on which facts.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations, models or backups will be touched. Commands, consoles and agent settings are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Exercise information is confidential until the after-action report is approved. Mark any written or chat message 'exercise exercise exercise'.
  • Treat the AI system's outputs, logs and self-reported status as unverified until a named person confirms them from an independent source. The scribe records which facts are verified and which are claimed.
  • If a kill switch, rollback, approval gate or manual fallback does not exist in your environment, say so aloud. Do not assume the AI system will correct itself, stop when asked or explain its own actions reliably.
  • Use the word leadership for senior leaders, a board of directors or both, and bring them in only when a decision needs their authority.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Declines spike. Decision: declare and relax the model. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Merchants cannot sell; Call volume 12 times; Dashboard shows fraud down.

T+00:30 — Containment. Containment: lock registry publication and restore the prior version. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Auto-pull reloads new model; Rule fallback incomplete; Environments disagree.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Diagnosis: find the labeling cause and estimate the lost sales. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Label history incomplete; Fraud exposure unknown; Partner data disputed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Remediation planning: decide retry, fees and merchant support. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Scheme rules complicate; Finance wants fee waiver; Merchants threaten to leave.

T+03:00 — Execution. Execution: restore, retry and communicate. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Duplicate charges; Retry failures; Customer complaints.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Stabilization: monitor decline and fraud rates. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Fraud rises after relaxation; Agent attempts resume; Scheme alerts.

T+06:00 — Validation. Validation: sample decisions against known outcomes. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Residual false declines; Evidence gaps; Fraud missed.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Return to normal and close-out: set exit criteria and actions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Open actions lack owners; Leaders want early return; Auditors request evidence.

Injects

Inject #1: The fraud model blocks legitimate transactions after a silent retrain

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 08:00 on Tuesday. Retailers report declines on ordinary purchases. The payments lead sees a 19 percent decline rate against 3 percent normal. The data scientist finds that a new model version was published to the registry at 02:00 and loaded automatically by the Azure scoring service.

The labeling table feeding the AWS retraining pipeline includes remapped chargeback codes that marked many normal transactions as fraud. The dashboard says fraud losses are down 46 percent. Call volume is 12 times normal.

The commander must decide how to restore normal declines without opening the door to fraud, and who decides the trade-off.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and assign a scribe, a payments lead, a data lead, a merchant lead and a platform lead.
  • Authorize relaxing declines and locking registry publication.
  • Set a 15-minute cadence.
AI Platform & Model Operations
  • Preserve model versions, features and training data snapshots.
  • Compare score distributions.
Infrastructure & Platform Engineering
  • Lock registry publication and restore the prior version across environments.
  • Coordinate on-premises and cloud changes.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Check for tampering with labeling data or the registry.
  • Preserve logs.
Business & Process Owners
  • Prioritize large merchants and critical payments.
  • Plan support surge.
Data Protection & Privacy
  • Check what customer data is used in retraining and analysis.
  • Assess notice duties.
Legal, Risk & Compliance
  • Open the record and review scheme and consumer rules.
  • Preserve evidence.
Communications & Business Continuity
  • Prepare merchant and customer messages.
  • Set one channel.
Leadership Liaison
  • Brief senior leaders with facts and unknowns.
  • Check for board-level duties.
Vendor & Third-Party Liaison
  • Contact card scheme contacts, the partner and cloud vendors.
  • Ask about label change notices.

Discussion questions

  1. Why relax declines before fixing the model?
  2. How do you manage fraud risk during relaxation?
  3. Who decides the threshold trade-off?
  4. How do you restore the previous model across environments?
  5. What do you tell merchants?
  6. Why must model publication require approval?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the auto-pull behavior until INF asks about the registry.
  • If players only fix labels, ask about declines now.
  • Look for explicit fraud risk acceptance.
  • Time-box to 40 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Sets objectives for the first hour and states which decisions need the commander's own approval, including whether the fraud detection model stays disabled, restricted or supervised.
  • Runs a fixed update cadence and keeps one decision log with the decision, owner and time.

AI Platform & Model Operations

  • Knows model versions, labels and thresholds.
  • Provides score analyses.
  • Owns the technical facts about the fraud detection model: its configuration, permissions, tools, prompts, memory and recent changes.
  • Preserves model, agent and orchestration logs before any restart or redeployment.

Infrastructure & Platform Engineering

  • Controls the registry and serving paths across Azure, AWS and on-premises.
  • Restores versions.
  • Owns the technical response on the platforms and networks involved and keeps changes small, reversible and logged.
  • Maintains a list of every manual change so it can be reversed or formalized afterwards.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Checks tampering and preserves logs.
  • Treats the event as a possible security incident until evidence shows otherwise and preserves evidence in a forensically sound way.
  • Looks for misuse of credentials, tokens and tool permissions, not only the AI system's own actions.

Business & Process Owners

  • Owns merchant and customer support and risk trade-offs.
  • Defines which business processes must continue, in what order, and what manual alternatives are acceptable.
  • Identifies decisions, transactions or communications that were made by the AI system and need review or reversal.

Data Protection & Privacy

  • Determines whether personal, confidential or regulated data was accessed, changed, shared or lost.
  • Identifies the lawful basis, notification clocks and contractual commitments that may apply.

Legal, Risk & Compliance

  • Owns scheme and consumer obligations.
  • Ensures the incident is recorded, classified and linked to risk and control owners (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.32, 8.14, 8.16, 5.19) and SOC 2 processing integrity and availability).
  • Identifies regulatory, contractual and insurance obligations and their clocks.

Communications & Business Continuity

  • Issues time-stamped updates to staff, customers and partners from one approved source.
  • Activates continuity plans and workarounds and tracks their capacity.

Leadership Liaison

  • Briefs senior leaders and, where needed, the board of directors with confirmed facts, unknowns and the decisions that need their authority.
  • Brings back leadership decisions such as spend, risk acceptance and external disclosure in time to be useful.

Vendor & Third-Party Liaison

  • Works with schemes, the partner and cloud vendors.
  • Opens priority cases with the cloud, SaaS, model and tooling vendors involved and records case numbers.
  • Requests logs, configuration histories and root cause statements in writing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Require approval to publish models

Make the registry require review of metrics, decline rate and label changes before a version can serve traffic.

Canary new versions

Release to a small share of traffic and compare decline and fraud rates with the previous model before widening.

Monitor decline rate

Alert on decline rate, approval rate and merchant-level changes, and fall back automatically.

Version labels and data

Snapshot training labels and record upstream changes so shifts can be traced.

Keep a rule-based fallback

Maintain a tested simpler rule set that can run if the model is withdrawn, aligned with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 change and availability controls and SOC 2 processing integrity (8.32, 8.14, 8.16).

Pin versions across environments

Make serving versions explicit and consistent between cloud and on-premises paths.

Agree label contracts

Require partners to notify changes in reason codes through a monitored channel.

Define risk acceptance

Agree in advance how much extra fraud is tolerable during a false decline event and who may approve it.

Prepare merchant playbooks

Create messages, fee rules and support scripts for large decline events, approved by finance and legal, so merchants hear one consistent account.

Test silent retrains

Simulate bad labels in non-production and see whether tests catch the shift, then add the missing checks.

Distrust fraud-loss reports

Falling fraud losses with rising declines may mean overblocking. Present both together and compare with complaints and revenue.

Rehearse a decline-relaxation drill

Practice switching the authorization path to rule-based mode on a test slice of traffic, measuring how quickly decline rates recover and how fraud rates move. Record who may approve the switch at night and what limits apply, so the choice between false declines and fraud exposure is made against agreed numbers rather than under pressure.

Keep merchant and partner escalation paths

Maintain named contacts at large merchants, card schemes and label partners together with an approved escalation script. When declines spike, the first calls decide whether merchants stay calm or start routing sales elsewhere, so the script should state the facts, the workaround and the next update time.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. Announce the end, remind everyone that all actions were simulated, and keep everyone in the room for the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not and any gap in tools, access or documentation.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, role by role and then open floor, for 45 to 60 minutes. Keep the discussion on how the fraud detection model was permissioned, supervised and stopped.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate impact and effort, and select no more than ten.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Give each action an owner, a due date and a verification method, and record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Validate fixes within 90 days and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did the team link declines to the model?
  2. How did you relax declines?
  3. How did you handle fraud risk?
  4. How did you restore the previous model?
  5. What did merchants hear?
  6. Which release controls do you now want?
  7. Who now approves model versions?
  8. What would make you trust the model again?
  9. How accurate were the updates to leadership, and what was said that later proved wrong?
  10. What did the vendor and third-party liaison add, and what did you still lack from vendors?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture that would satisfy an auditor, and what is missing?
  12. Which permissions, approvals or access did the team lack at the moment they were needed?
  13. Which alerts or detections should exist that did not?
  14. What temporary changes remain, who owns them and when will they be reversed?
  15. If you could change one design decision about how AI systems are built, permissioned or supervised here, what would it be?
  16. What did the team do well that should be written into the runbook?
