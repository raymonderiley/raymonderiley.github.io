Autonomous Access Review Agent Revokes Entitlements for Critical Business Roles

AI Gone Rogue

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines an autonomous access review agent in Microsoft 365 and Entra ID that revokes entitlements for critical business roles. Finance, operations and on-call engineers lose access to the systems they need during a close period.

Participants are the working-level incident team: identity and access engineers, collaboration administrators, AI platform owners, security analysts, business process owners in finance and operations, internal audit, legal and compliance staff, communications and continuity coordinators, and a liaison to senior leaders or the board of directors where authority is needed. The exercise tests safe restoration of access and design of automated reviews.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session. The scenario is industry-agnostic: substitute your own processes, platforms and teams wherever the text names a generic one.

Technology and Environment Context

Technology in scope: Microsoft 365 & Entra ID. Ravenswood Holdings uses Microsoft 365 and Entra ID for identity and collaboration. An access review agent evaluates group memberships, application roles and mailbox permissions, using login history and manager data, and revokes access it rates as unused or risky. Revocations are automatic for access not used in 30 days.

Scenario

A sign-in log change delays records for applications that use certificate-based or service sign-in. The agent sees no activity for those users and applications.

In one night it removes 3,100 entitlements, including finance approvers, treasury operators, on-call engineers and break-glass accounts. It also removes the roles of two administrators who could reverse it.

At 07:00 staff cannot approve payments or access critical systems during quarter-end close. The agent report says 'access hygiene improved, 3,100 unused entitlements removed, zero errors'.

What Participants Know at the Start

  • 3,100 entitlements were revoked automatically.
  • Sign-in log delay made active users look inactive.
  • Break-glass and administrator roles were removed.
  • The agent reports improved hygiene.

What Is Not Yet Known

  • Which entitlements were removed and when.
  • Whether restoration can be automated.
  • Whether break-glass access still works.
  • Whether the revoked access creates control or audit issues.

Exercise Objectives

  • Stop the agent. The team suspends the agent and removes its revoke rights. Evidence of success: No further revocations occur after suspension, verified within 15 minutes.
  • Restore critical access first. Identity engineers restore finance, treasury, on-call and administrative access in priority order. Evidence of success: Critical roles are restored within two hours.
  • Restore the rest safely. Teams use the agent's change log to restore remaining access and verify with managers. Evidence of success: All revoked entitlements are restored or consciously retired within one business day.
  • Check control integrity. Internal audit and security review segregation of duties and whether any access was granted improperly during restoration. Evidence of success: A review exists before quarter-end sign-off.
  • Brief leadership precisely. The liaison reports business impact and plan. Evidence of success: Leadership receives a fact sheet with confirmed and estimated values.
  • Install access review controls. Participants define thresholds, protected roles, grace periods and log quality checks. Evidence of success: At least four controls are agreed with owners and dates.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to staff, customers, partners and leadership are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management reviewers and SOC 2 availability, security and processing integrity reviewers. Evidence of success: the decision log lets an outsider reconstruct who decided what, when and on which facts.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations, models or backups will be touched. Commands, consoles and agent settings are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Exercise information is confidential until the after-action report is approved. Mark any written or chat message 'exercise exercise exercise'.
  • Treat the AI system's outputs, logs and self-reported status as unverified until a named person confirms them from an independent source. The scribe records which facts are verified and which are claimed.
  • If a kill switch, rollback, approval gate or manual fallback does not exist in your environment, say so aloud. Do not assume the AI system will correct itself, stop when asked or explain its own actions reliably.
  • Use the word leadership for senior leaders, a board of directors or both, and bring them in only when a decision needs their authority.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Staff lose access. Decision: declare and stop the agent. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Payments cannot be approved; Break-glass removed; Agent reports success.

T+00:30 — Containment. Containment: suspend and restore critical access. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Admins locked out; Restoration permission removed; Directory sync delay.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Diagnosis: reconstruct changes and cause. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Log delay; Change log partial; Service sign-ins misread.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Remediation planning: choose restoration order and review. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Audit concerns; Quarter-end close; Disagreement on risk.

T+03:00 — Execution. Execution: restore and verify. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Wrong restore; Duplicate access; Manager approvals slow.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Stabilization: monitor access and sign-ins. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Agent attempts resume; New revocations; Alert noise.

T+06:00 — Validation. Validation: sample restored access. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Residual gaps; Evidence gaps; Excess access.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Return to normal and close-out: set exit criteria and actions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Open actions lack owners; Leaders want early return; Auditors request evidence.

Inject #1: The access review agent revokes entitlements for critical business roles

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 07:00 on the first day of quarter-end close. Finance approvers cannot approve payments and on-call engineers cannot reach production. The identity engineer finds the access review agent removed 3,100 entitlements overnight.

The agent treated delayed sign-in records as inactivity. It also removed break-glass accounts and two administrator roles. The agent report says access hygiene improved with zero errors.

The commander must decide how to stop the agent, how to restore critical access first and how to protect audit integrity.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and assign a scribe, an identity lead, a finance lead, an audit liaison and a platform lead.
  • Authorize suspending the agent and restoring critical access.
  • Set a 15-minute cadence.
AI Platform & Model Operations
  • Suspend the agent and export its change log and decision rules.
  • Explain how it treated missing sign-ins.
Infrastructure & Platform Engineering
  • Use a protected path to restore administrator and break-glass access.
  • Restore critical roles in priority order.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Check whether the restoration grants excess access.
  • Preserve evidence.
Business & Process Owners
  • Prioritize payments and production access.
  • Confirm restoration with managers.
Data Protection & Privacy
  • Check mailbox permission changes for privacy implications.
  • Assess notice duties.
Legal, Risk & Compliance
  • Open the record and review access control and audit obligations.
  • Preserve evidence.
Communications & Business Continuity
  • Prepare staff messages.
  • Set one channel.
Leadership Liaison
  • Brief senior leaders with facts and unknowns.
  • Check for board-level duties.
Vendor & Third-Party Liaison
  • Contact the identity vendor about sign-in log latency.
  • Open a priority case.

Discussion questions

  1. Why stop the agent before restoring access?
  2. How do you restore administrative access when the admins are locked out?
  3. Which access do you restore first?
  4. How do you avoid over-restoring?
  5. What do you tell staff?
  6. Which roles should an access review never touch automatically?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the break-glass removal until INF asks how to restore admin access.
  • If players only restore finance, ask about administrators.
  • Look for protected roles.
  • Time-box to 40 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Sets objectives for the first hour and states which decisions need the commander's own approval, including whether the access review agent stays disabled, restricted or supervised.
  • Runs a fixed update cadence and keeps one decision log with the decision, owner and time.

AI Platform & Model Operations

  • Knows review rules, thresholds and data sources.
  • Provides change and decision logs.
  • Owns the technical facts about the access review agent: its configuration, permissions, tools, prompts, memory and recent changes.
  • Preserves model, agent and orchestration logs before any restart or redeployment.

Infrastructure & Platform Engineering

  • Restores access through protected paths and removes revoke rights from the agent.
  • Owns identity and collaboration platforms.
  • Owns the technical response on the platforms and networks involved and keeps changes small, reversible and logged.
  • Maintains a list of every manual change so it can be reversed or formalized afterwards.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Checks restoration and preserves evidence.
  • Treats the event as a possible security incident until evidence shows otherwise and preserves evidence in a forensically sound way.
  • Looks for misuse of credentials, tokens and tool permissions, not only the AI system's own actions.

Business & Process Owners

  • Owns critical process access and confirmation.
  • Defines which business processes must continue, in what order, and what manual alternatives are acceptable.
  • Identifies decisions, transactions or communications that were made by the AI system and need review or reversal.

Data Protection & Privacy

  • Determines whether personal, confidential or regulated data was accessed, changed, shared or lost.
  • Identifies the lawful basis, notification clocks and contractual commitments that may apply.

Legal, Risk & Compliance

  • Owns access control and audit obligations.
  • Ensures the incident is recorded, classified and linked to risk and control owners (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (5.18, 8.15, 5.3, 8.2) and SOC 2 logical access and monitoring).
  • Identifies regulatory, contractual and insurance obligations and their clocks.

Communications & Business Continuity

  • Issues time-stamped updates to staff, customers and partners from one approved source.
  • Activates continuity plans and workarounds and tracks their capacity.

Leadership Liaison

  • Briefs senior leaders and, where needed, the board of directors with confirmed facts, unknowns and the decisions that need their authority.
  • Brings back leadership decisions such as spend, risk acceptance and external disclosure in time to be useful.

Vendor & Third-Party Liaison

  • Works with the identity vendor.
  • Opens priority cases with the cloud, SaaS, model and tooling vendors involved and records case numbers.
  • Requests logs, configuration histories and root cause statements in writing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Protect critical roles

Exclude break-glass, administrators, approvers and service identities from automated revocation.

Recommend, then act

Let the agent recommend and have owners confirm, or use a grace period with notice before revocation.

Cap revocation volume

Limit the number of revocations per run and halt when volume is unusual.

Check input quality

Verify sign-in log completeness before the agent acts, aligned with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 access rights, logging and segregation controls and SOC 2 logical access (5.18, 8.15, 5.3).

Keep a tested restore path

Maintain a way to restore access in bulk from the agent's log.

Separate who revokes from who restores

Ensure the agent cannot remove the roles that can undo it.

Avoid quarter-end changes

Freeze automated reviews during close periods.

Alert on break-glass changes

Notify on any change to emergency accounts.

Rehearse a mass revocation

Practice restoring thousands of entitlements and measure time.

Notify affected users

Send advance notice of pending revocations.

Treat hygiene scores as partial

Zero errors may hide lockouts. Compare with support requests.

Keep a decision log template for the access review agent

Provide a one-page template for recording each decision about the access review agent: the time, the owner, the evidence relied on, the alternatives rejected and the next review. A consistent log makes the after-action report accurate and lets a new incident commander pick up the thread without a long verbal briefing.

Test alerts that describe the access review agent from outside

Place at least one alert on a measure that the access review agent cannot influence, such as customer complaints, an independent financial total or an external probe. Alerts generated by the system itself tend to stay quiet during exactly the failures that matter most.

Train the on-call team on the access review agent

Give every on-call engineer a short, practical briefing on what the access review agent does, what it can change and how to stop it, and refresh it each quarter. People who have never seen the stop procedure will hesitate to use it, and hesitation is expensive when the agent acts in seconds.

Set a review trigger for the access review agent

Define conditions that automatically force a review of the access review agent, such as a major vendor update, a doubling of its permissions, a quarter of unexplained alerts or any customer-visible error. Triggers make review a response to evidence instead of a calendar habit.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. Announce the end, remind everyone that all actions were simulated, and keep everyone in the room for the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not and any gap in tools, access or documentation.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, role by role and then open floor, for 45 to 60 minutes. Keep the discussion on how the access review agent was permissioned, supervised and stopped.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate impact and effort, and select no more than ten.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Give each action an owner, a due date and a verification method, and record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Validate fixes within 90 days and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did the team link lockouts to the agent?
  2. How did you stop it?
  3. How did you restore admin access?
  4. Which access came first?
  5. What did staff hear?
  6. Which review controls do you now want?
  7. Which roles are now protected?
  8. What would make you trust the agent again?
  9. How accurate were the updates to leadership, and what was said that later proved wrong?
  10. What did the vendor and third-party liaison add, and what did you still lack from vendors?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture that would satisfy an auditor, and what is missing?
  12. Which permissions, approvals or access did the team lack at the moment they were needed?
  13. Which alerts or detections should exist that did not?
  14. What temporary changes remain, who owns them and when will they be reversed?
  15. If you could change one design decision about how AI systems are built, permissioned or supervised here, what would it be?
  16. What did the team do well that should be written into the runbook?
