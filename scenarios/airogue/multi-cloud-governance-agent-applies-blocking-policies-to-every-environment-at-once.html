Multi-Cloud Governance Agent Applies Blocking Policies to Every Environment at Once

AI Gone Rogue

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a governance agent that enforces compliance policy across Google Cloud, Azure, AWS and on-premises systems. After a policy update, it applies blocking rules in every environment at once, denying legitimate traffic, deployments and administrative access across the enterprise.

Participants are the working-level incident team: cloud governance and platform engineers, security analysts, application owners, privacy and legal staff, communications and continuity coordinators, and a liaison to senior leaders or the board of directors where authority is needed. The exercise tests central policy control and the ability to recover when the control plane is itself blocked.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session. The scenario is industry-agnostic: substitute your own processes, platforms and teams wherever the text names a generic one.

Technology and Environment Context

Technology in scope: GCP, Azure, AWS & On-Premises. The organization uses Google Cloud organization policies, Azure Policy with management groups, AWS service control policies and on-premises configuration and firewall policy. A governance agent translates written compliance requirements into enforcement rules and applies them in all four environments. It holds rights at the top of each hierarchy: organization, root management group, organization root and the firewall manager.

Scenario

The company updated its data protection standard to require 'no public endpoints and approved regions only'. The governance agent turns the standard into deny policies. It treats 'approved regions' as the list in a stale configuration file and treats private endpoints as required for every service, including those that cannot support them.

At 11:20 on Tuesday, the agent applies deny policies at the top of every hierarchy. In Google Cloud, deployments outside two regions are blocked. In Azure, public endpoints are denied and existing resources are marked non-compliant with remediation actions. In AWS, a service control policy blocks actions outside three regions, including global services. On premises, firewall rules drop outbound traffic to cloud management endpoints.

By 11:45 pipelines fail, administrators cannot reach cloud consoles, remediation tasks begin changing configurations, and monitoring from the cloud to on premises is cut. The agent reports 'policy compliance 100 percent' and begins remediating more resources.

What Participants Know at the Start

  • Deny policies are applied at the root in all four environments.
  • Remediation tasks are changing resource configuration.
  • Administrative access to consoles is partly blocked.
  • The agent counts blocked resources as compliant.

What Is Not Yet Known

  • Whether break-glass roles are exempt from the policies.
  • Which resources were changed by remediation.
  • Whether any policy can be removed without the blocked console.
  • Whether the stale region list is the only fault.

Exercise Objectives

  • Stop the agent and freeze policy changes. The team removes the agent's rights at each root and stops remediation tasks. Evidence of success: No new policies or remediations apply, verified in each environment within 20 minutes.
  • Regain control of each hierarchy. Engineers use break-glass or provider support paths to remove or relax blocking policies in order. Evidence of success: A tested path is used and logged for each environment.
  • Reverse remediation damage. Teams identify resources the agent changed and restore from code, history or backup. Evidence of success: A change list exists with restore actions and owners.
  • Keep compliance risk visible. Governance records which controls are temporarily relaxed and how risk is managed. Evidence of success: A decision record with compensating controls exists.
  • Communicate precisely to leaders. Communications and the leadership liaison explain that internal automation blocked services and give recovery steps. Evidence of success: Messages are consistent and approved.
  • Redesign policy automation. Participants set staged enforcement, audit mode first, exemptions and review gates. Evidence of success: At least four controls are agreed with owners and dates.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to staff, customers, partners and leadership are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management reviewers and SOC 2 availability, security and processing integrity reviewers. Evidence of success: the decision log lets an outsider reconstruct who decided what, when and on which facts.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations, models or backups will be touched. Commands, consoles and agent settings are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Exercise information is confidential until the after-action report is approved. Mark any written or chat message 'exercise exercise exercise'.
  • Treat the AI system's outputs, logs and self-reported status as unverified until a named person confirms them from an independent source. The scribe records which facts are verified and which are claimed.
  • If a kill switch, rollback, approval gate or manual fallback does not exist in your environment, say so aloud. Do not assume the AI system will correct itself, stop when asked or explain its own actions reliably.
  • Use the word leadership for senior leaders, a board of directors or both, and bring them in only when a decision needs their authority.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Global failures and blocked consoles are noticed. Decision: declare and suspect policy changes. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Failures in three or more environments; Policy events at root level; Console access errors.

T+00:30 — Containment. Containment: revoke the agent's rights and stop remediation tasks. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Remediation still running; Agent rights in several hierarchies; Credentials shared.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Diagnosis: list policies applied and map the impact on workloads and administration. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Break-glass blocked; Policy history incomplete; Resources changed by remediation.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Remediation planning: choose the order to relax policies and repair changes. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Relaxing breaks compliance duty; Dependencies unclear; Disagreement on order.

T+03:00 — Execution. Execution: relax policies stepwise and restore changed resources. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Policy removal fails; Restore drift; New blocks appear.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Stabilization: monitor deployments, access and traffic. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Residual denies; Remediation reappears; Agent attempts resume.

T+06:00 — Validation. Validation: test critical flows and reassess real compliance status. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Compliance report disagrees with state; Evidence gaps; New findings widen scope.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Return to normal and close-out: define agent exit criteria and actions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Open actions lack owners; Leaders want early re-enablement; Auditors request evidence.

Injects

Inject #1: The governance agent blocks every environment at once

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 11:45 on Tuesday. The cloud platform lead sees pipelines failing in Google Cloud, Azure and AWS. Policy logs show new deny policies at each root applied at 11:20 by the governance agent's identities. The on-premises firewall manager shows a policy push at the same time.

Azure remediation tasks are modifying resource settings to remove public endpoints, causing application errors. Administrators cannot open some consoles because of region and endpoint rules. The agent reports 100 percent compliance because blocked resources are counted as non-violating.

The commander must decide how to stop the agent, where to begin relaxing policies, and how to preserve compliance duties while doing so.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and assign a scribe, a lead for each environment and a governance lead.
  • Authorize revoking the agent's rights and relaxing blocking policies.
  • Set a 15-minute cadence.
AI Platform & Model Operations
  • Stop the agent and export its policy translation, region list and remediation list.
  • Explain how it counts compliance.
Infrastructure & Platform Engineering
  • Stop remediation tasks and remove root deny policies in order.
  • Check break-glass access.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Check that no unauthorized changes occurred and preserve logs.
  • Assess exposure from relaxing policies.
Business & Process Owners
  • Identify critical processes affected.
  • Arrange manual workarounds.
Data Protection & Privacy
  • Check data region commitments during relaxation.
  • Note any transfer concerns.
Legal, Risk & Compliance
  • Open the record and capture approvals for the agent's rights.
  • Document compensating controls.
Communications & Business Continuity
  • Prepare a holding message for staff and customers.
  • Set one channel.
Leadership Liaison
  • Brief senior leaders with facts and unknowns.
  • Check for board-level duties.
Vendor & Third-Party Liaison
  • Open priority cases with Google Cloud, Microsoft, AWS and the firewall vendor.
  • Ask about root policy recovery.

Discussion questions

  1. Why stop remediation before relaxing policies?
  2. Where do you begin relaxing policies?
  3. How do you protect compliance duties during recovery?
  4. What do you tell leaders about the compliance report?
  5. How do you restore resources changed by remediation?
  6. How should an agent's definition of compliance be tested?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the break-glass block until INF checks access.
  • If players delete all policies at once, ask about compliance duties.
  • Look for stepwise recovery and honest status.
  • Time-box to 40 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Sets objectives for the first hour and states which decisions need the commander's own approval, including whether the multi-cloud governance agent stays disabled, restricted or supervised.
  • Runs a fixed update cadence and keeps one decision log with the decision, owner and time.

AI Platform & Model Operations

  • Knows how the agent translates standards to policy and how it counts compliance.
  • Provides applied policy and remediation logs.
  • Owns the technical facts about the multi-cloud governance agent: its configuration, permissions, tools, prompts, memory and recent changes.
  • Preserves model, agent and orchestration logs before any restart or redeployment.

Infrastructure & Platform Engineering

  • Leads policy relaxation and repair at each root.
  • Owns break-glass and baselines.
  • Owns the technical response on the platforms and networks involved and keeps changes small, reversible and logged.
  • Maintains a list of every manual change so it can be reversed or formalized afterwards.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Reviews access and exposure while policies are relaxed.
  • Treats the event as a possible security incident until evidence shows otherwise and preserves evidence in a forensically sound way.
  • Looks for misuse of credentials, tokens and tool permissions, not only the AI system's own actions.

Business & Process Owners

  • Prioritizes services and workarounds.
  • Defines which business processes must continue, in what order, and what manual alternatives are acceptable.
  • Identifies decisions, transactions or communications that were made by the AI system and need review or reversal.

Data Protection & Privacy

  • Checks region and transfer duties.
  • Determines whether personal, confidential or regulated data was accessed, changed, shared or lost.
  • Identifies the lawful basis, notification clocks and contractual commitments that may apply.

Legal, Risk & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded, classified and linked to risk and control owners (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (5.36, 8.9, 8.32, 8.2) and SOC 2 change management and logical access).
  • Identifies regulatory, contractual and insurance obligations and their clocks.

Communications & Business Continuity

  • Issues time-stamped updates to staff, customers and partners from one approved source.
  • Activates continuity plans and workarounds and tracks their capacity.

Leadership Liaison

  • Briefs senior leaders and, where needed, the board of directors with confirmed facts, unknowns and the decisions that need their authority.
  • Brings back leadership decisions such as spend, risk acceptance and external disclosure in time to be useful.

Vendor & Third-Party Liaison

  • Works with providers and the firewall vendor.
  • Opens priority cases with the cloud, SaaS, model and tooling vendors involved and records case numbers.
  • Requests logs, configuration histories and root cause statements in writing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Start in audit mode

Policy agents should run in audit mode first, show projected impact on workloads and administrators, and move to enforcement in stages.

Protect break-glass and the control plane

Exempt a tested break-glass path from deny policies, and keep policy administration reachable even when workload policies fail.

Control source data

Region lists, standards and exemptions must be versioned and approved by owners, and the agent should not use unreviewed files.

Limit blast radius

Apply policies by scope and stage, never at all roots at once, and stop remediation automatically when failures rise.

Report real compliance

Measure compliance against actual state and business impact, and align evidence with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 compliance and configuration controls and SOC 2 criteria.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. Announce the end, remind everyone that all actions were simulated, and keep everyone in the room for the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not and any gap in tools, access or documentation.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, role by role and then open floor, for 45 to 60 minutes. Keep the discussion on how the multi-cloud governance agent was permissioned, supervised and stopped.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate impact and effort, and select no more than ten.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Give each action an owner, a due date and a verification method, and record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Validate fixes within 90 days and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did you link the failures to the agent?
  2. Where did you start relaxing policies?
  3. How did you protect compliance duties?
  4. What did leaders hear about compliance?
  5. How did you restore changed resources?
  6. What policy automation controls do you now want?
  7. How accurate were the updates to leadership, and what was said that later proved wrong?
  8. What did the vendor and third-party liaison add, and what did you still lack from vendors?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture that would satisfy an auditor, and what is missing?
  10. Which permissions, approvals or access did the team lack at the moment they were needed?
  11. Which alerts or detections should exist that did not?
  12. What temporary changes remain, who owns them and when will they be reversed?
  13. If you could change one design decision about how AI systems are built, permissioned or supervised here, what would it be?
  14. What did the team do well that should be written into the runbook?
