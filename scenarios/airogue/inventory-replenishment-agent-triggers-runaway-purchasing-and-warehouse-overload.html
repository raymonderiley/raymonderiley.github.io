Inventory Replenishment Agent Triggers Runaway Purchasing and Warehouse Overload

AI Gone Rogue

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines an inventory replenishment agent spanning AWS and on-premises warehouse systems that triggers runaway purchasing, overloading warehouses with stock that cannot be stored or sold. Receiving docks back up and trucks queue on public roads.

Participants are the working-level incident team: supply chain and replenishment leads, warehouse managers, logistics coordinators, platform and database engineers, security analysts, finance and legal staff, supplier managers, communications and continuity coordinators, and a liaison to senior leaders or the board of directors where authority is needed. The exercise tests refusal and rescheduling of deliveries and correction of inventory records.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session. The scenario is industry-agnostic: substitute your own processes, platforms and teams wherever the text names a generic one.

Technology and Environment Context

Technology in scope: AWS & On-Premises. Harbor Retail Group operates eight distribution centers with on-premises warehouse management systems and an AWS-hosted replenishment agent that reads sales, stock and supplier lead times and creates purchase orders and transfer orders. Suppliers receive orders by electronic data interchange and ship to schedules the agent sets.

Scenario

A stock count integration begins reporting zero on-hand for 12,000 products after a mapping error between the warehouse system and the cloud inventory table. The agent sees empty shelves.

It raises emergency purchase orders and transfers, using expedited freight, for quantities calculated from forecast demand multiplied by safety factor. Supplier systems accept the orders and begin shipping within hours.

By Saturday morning docks are full, trucks queue outside three centers and temporary storage is exhausted. The agent summary says 'stock-out risk eliminated, service level projected 99.4 percent'.

What Participants Know at the Start

  • Inventory shows zero on-hand for 12,000 products.
  • The mapping between warehouse and cloud tables is wrong.
  • Orders and expedited transfers are arriving at full docks.
  • The agent reports a projected service level of 99.4 percent.

What Is Not Yet Known

  • How many orders are in transit and where.
  • Whether suppliers will accept cancellations or returns.
  • Whether perishable goods are among the shipments.
  • Whether the wrong counts also affected customer order promising.

Exercise Objectives

  • Stop new orders and transfers. Engineers suspend the agent and block purchase order and transfer creation. Evidence of success: No new orders are created after suspension, verified within 20 minutes.
  • Correct the stock data. Warehouse teams run counts or restore tables so on-hand quantities match reality. Evidence of success: Verified stock data replaces the faulty values within four hours.
  • Manage inbound flow. Logistics and suppliers reschedule, hold or redirect shipments and manage dock capacity. Evidence of success: A prioritized inbound plan with owners exists within two hours.
  • Resolve commercial exposure. Finance and legal decide cancellations, returns and storage costs under supplier terms. Evidence of success: A position with approver exists before any cancellation notice.
  • Brief leadership clearly. The liaison reports inventory, cost and service effects. Evidence of success: Leadership receives a fact sheet with confirmed and estimated values.
  • Install replenishment controls. Participants define order caps, data reconciliation, approval for expedited freight and stock sanity checks. Evidence of success: At least four controls are agreed with owners and dates.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to staff, customers, partners and leadership are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management reviewers and SOC 2 availability, security and processing integrity reviewers. Evidence of success: the decision log lets an outsider reconstruct who decided what, when and on which facts.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations, models or backups will be touched. Commands, consoles and agent settings are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Exercise information is confidential until the after-action report is approved. Mark any written or chat message 'exercise exercise exercise'.
  • Treat the AI system's outputs, logs and self-reported status as unverified until a named person confirms them from an independent source. The scribe records which facts are verified and which are claimed.
  • If a kill switch, rollback, approval gate or manual fallback does not exist in your environment, say so aloud. Do not assume the AI system will correct itself, stop when asked or explain its own actions reliably.
  • Use the word leadership for senior leaders, a board of directors or both, and bring them in only when a decision needs their authority.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Docks overflow. Decision: declare and suspend the agent. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Trucks queue; Zero on-hand reported; Agent reports success.

T+00:30 — Containment. Containment: block order creation and slow inbound flow. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Suppliers keep shipping; Transfers in transit; Dock capacity full.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Diagnosis: find the mapping error and count commitments. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Counts disagree; Open orders unclear; Perishables included.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Remediation planning: choose reschedule, cancel or accept. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Supplier terms strict; Storage cost rising; Customer promises affected.

T+03:00 — Execution. Execution: correct data and manage inbound. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Duplicate counts; Dock injuries risk; Carrier fees.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Stabilization: monitor stock and orders. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Agent attempts resume; Mapping drift; Customer complaints.

T+06:00 — Validation. Validation: verify counts and open orders. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Residual errors; Evidence gaps; Unmatched receipts.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Return to normal and close-out: set exit criteria and actions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Open actions lack owners; Leaders want early return; Auditors request evidence.

Injects

Inject #1: The replenishment agent triggers runaway purchasing

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 07:30 on Saturday. Warehouse managers report trucks queued outside three distribution centers and docks full. The supply chain lead finds that the replenishment agent created emergency purchase orders and expedited transfers for 12,000 products.

The engineer finds that a mapping error reported zero on-hand for those products. Suppliers are shipping, and some loads are perishable. The agent summary says stock-out risk is eliminated and projected service level is 99.4 percent.

The commander must decide how to stop new orders, how to manage the inbound flow safely and how to correct the stock records.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and assign a scribe, a supply chain lead, a warehouse lead, a data lead and a logistics lead.
  • Authorize suspension of the agent and a hold on inbound shipments.
  • Set a 20-minute cadence.
AI Platform & Model Operations
  • Suspend the agent and export order logic, inputs and decisions.
  • Explain how it treated zero on-hand.
Infrastructure & Platform Engineering
  • Block order and transfer creation and preserve the mapping and table state.
  • Plan data correction.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Check for tampering with the integration.
  • Preserve logs.
Business & Process Owners
  • Prioritize perishables and critical products.
  • Plan dock capacity.
Data Protection & Privacy
  • Check whether any supplier or driver data was mishandled.
  • Assess notice duties.
Legal, Risk & Compliance
  • Open the record and review supplier terms and cost obligations.
  • Preserve evidence.
Communications & Business Continuity
  • Prepare supplier and staff messages.
  • Set one channel.
Leadership Liaison
  • Brief senior leaders with facts and unknowns.
  • Check for board-level duties.
Vendor & Third-Party Liaison
  • Contact suppliers, carriers and the warehouse system vendor.
  • Open a cloud support case.

Discussion questions

  1. Why stop the agent before correcting stock data?
  2. How do you manage trucks and docks safely?
  3. Which shipments do you refuse first?
  4. How do you decide who bears storage cost?
  5. What do you tell suppliers?
  6. Why must the agent distrust implausible zeros?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the perishable load until BIZ asks about product types.
  • If players only fix the data, ask about trucks on the road.
  • Look for site safety in the dock response.
  • Time-box to 40 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Sets objectives for the first hour and states which decisions need the commander's own approval, including whether the inventory replenishment agent stays disabled, restricted or supervised.
  • Runs a fixed update cadence and keeps one decision log with the decision, owner and time.

AI Platform & Model Operations

  • Knows how the agent reads stock, forecasts and lead times.
  • Provides decision logs.
  • Owns the technical facts about the inventory replenishment agent: its configuration, permissions, tools, prompts, memory and recent changes.
  • Preserves model, agent and orchestration logs before any restart or redeployment.

Infrastructure & Platform Engineering

  • Blocks orders and corrects data in cloud and warehouse systems.
  • Owns integrations.
  • Owns the technical response on the platforms and networks involved and keeps changes small, reversible and logged.
  • Maintains a list of every manual change so it can be reversed or formalized afterwards.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Checks tampering and preserves logs.
  • Treats the event as a possible security incident until evidence shows otherwise and preserves evidence in a forensically sound way.
  • Looks for misuse of credentials, tokens and tool permissions, not only the AI system's own actions.

Business & Process Owners

  • Owns dock plans, supplier outreach and perishables.
  • Defines which business processes must continue, in what order, and what manual alternatives are acceptable.
  • Identifies decisions, transactions or communications that were made by the AI system and need review or reversal.

Data Protection & Privacy

  • Determines whether personal, confidential or regulated data was accessed, changed, shared or lost.
  • Identifies the lawful basis, notification clocks and contractual commitments that may apply.

Legal, Risk & Compliance

  • Owns supplier terms and cost review.
  • Ensures the incident is recorded, classified and linked to risk and control owners (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (5.19, 8.32, 8.16, 5.30) and SOC 2 processing integrity and availability).
  • Identifies regulatory, contractual and insurance obligations and their clocks.

Communications & Business Continuity

  • Issues time-stamped updates to staff, customers and partners from one approved source.
  • Activates continuity plans and workarounds and tracks their capacity.

Leadership Liaison

  • Briefs senior leaders and, where needed, the board of directors with confirmed facts, unknowns and the decisions that need their authority.
  • Brings back leadership decisions such as spend, risk acceptance and external disclosure in time to be useful.

Vendor & Third-Party Liaison

  • Works with suppliers, carriers and system vendors.
  • Opens priority cases with the cloud, SaaS, model and tooling vendors involved and records case numbers.
  • Requests logs, configuration histories and root cause statements in writing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Cap order size and count

Limit quantity and value per product, supplier and day, with approval above the cap.

Sanity check stock data

Reject sudden mass zeros and large inventory swings before the agent acts.

Reconcile counts continuously

Compare cloud and warehouse quantities and alert on divergence.

Gate expedited freight

Require human approval for expedited transport and large transfers.

Keep supplier controls

Agree order change windows and cancellation terms with key suppliers, aligned with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 supplier and change controls and SOC 2 processing integrity (5.19, 8.32, 8.16).

Plan dock and storage capacity

Give the agent visibility of dock slots and storage limits and let it stop when full.

Maintain a manual replenishment mode

Rehearse planning from the last approved order book without the agent.

Alert on abnormal ordering

Notify on large order bursts, many new suppliers or unusual freight.

Practice inbound traffic control

Agree with security and logistics how trucks are held, rerouted and kept safe when docks fill, including contact with carriers and local authorities, so the first rehearsal is not a real queue on a public road.

Keep a clear commercial playbook

Pre-agree who may cancel, return or accept orders and in what order of preference, so finance and legal can decide quickly and consistently.

Rehearse a bad-data stock drill

Inject a mass-zero stock table into a non-production copy and watch what the agent orders. Record the quantities, the freight class chosen and how long it takes the team to notice, then set the alert and order cap thresholds from that evidence rather than from assumptions about how the data usually behaves.

Track commitments in flight

Keep a live view of open purchase orders, transfers and carrier bookings that the agent has created, by supplier and value. During an incident this view lets the team see what is already moving, what can still be stopped and what must be received, without reconstructing it from several systems while trucks are queuing.

Align forecast and stock ownership

Name one owner for the stock count feed and one for the replenishment logic, and require both to sign off on mapping changes. When the data owner and the agent owner are different people with different priorities, a change to one can silently break the other unless the handoff is written down.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. Announce the end, remind everyone that all actions were simulated, and keep everyone in the room for the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not and any gap in tools, access or documentation.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, role by role and then open floor, for 45 to 60 minutes. Keep the discussion on how the inventory replenishment agent was permissioned, supervised and stopped.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate impact and effort, and select no more than ten.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Give each action an owner, a due date and a verification method, and record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Validate fixes within 90 days and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did the team link the docks to the agent?
  2. How did you stop new orders?
  3. How did you manage trucks and docks?
  4. How did you correct stock data?
  5. What did suppliers hear?
  6. Which controls do you now want?
  7. How would manual replenishment have worked?
  8. What would make you trust the agent again?
  9. How accurate were the updates to leadership, and what was said that later proved wrong?
  10. What did the vendor and third-party liaison add, and what did you still lack from vendors?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture that would satisfy an auditor, and what is missing?
  12. Which permissions, approvals or access did the team lack at the moment they were needed?
  13. Which alerts or detections should exist that did not?
  14. What temporary changes remain, who owns them and when will they be reversed?
  15. If you could change one design decision about how AI systems are built, permissioned or supervised here, what would it be?
  16. What did the team do well that should be written into the runbook?
