Hybrid Identity Agent Mass-Disables Accounts Across Both Directories

AI Gone Rogue

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a hybrid identity agent that synchronizes on-premises Active Directory with Microsoft Entra ID. It misreads a directory attribute change as mass departure and disables thousands of accounts in both directories, locking out staff, service accounts and administrators.

Participants are the working-level incident team: identity and directory engineers, cloud and endpoint engineers, application owners, security analysts, privacy and legal staff, communications and continuity coordinators, and a liaison to senior leaders or the board of directors where authority is needed. The exercise tests identity recovery when the tools used to recover depend on identity.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session. The scenario is industry-agnostic: substitute your own processes, platforms and teams wherever the text names a generic one.

Technology and Environment Context

Technology in scope: Azure & On-Premises. The organization uses Active Directory with Microsoft Entra Connect synchronization, hybrid joined devices, conditional access and privileged identity management. A hybrid identity agent monitors HR feeds and directory changes and deprovisions accounts it believes belong to leavers. It uses a service principal with directory write rights in Entra ID and a service account with disable rights in on-premises Active Directory.

Scenario

Fennimore Holdings introduced a hybrid identity agent to speed up leaver deprovisioning. It reads the HR feed, compares it with directory attributes and disables accounts that appear to have ended employment. A rule says that if an account's employee status attribute is empty or changed, it is a departure candidate.

At 06:30 on Monday, an HR system update rewrote the employee status attribute for 9,200 of 14,000 employees in the feed as a new code. The agent reads the new code as 'terminated', disables the accounts in on-premises Active Directory, and then in Entra ID, revokes sessions and removes licenses. It also disables 340 service accounts and 41 administrator accounts because their attribute is blank.

By 07:15 staff cannot sign in, VPN and single sign-on fail, and the help desk is flooded. Administrators cannot sign in to portals. The agent reports '9,780 leavers processed, 100 percent policy compliant'.

What Participants Know at the Start

  • Accounts are disabled in both Active Directory and Entra ID.
  • Administrator and service accounts are among those disabled.
  • The HR feed changed its code format earlier that morning.
  • Break-glass accounts may be affected or untested.

What Is Not Yet Known

  • Whether break-glass accounts still work.
  • How many service accounts power critical applications.
  • Whether recently deleted objects can be restored.
  • Whether any attacker activity is mixed in.

Exercise Objectives

  • Stop the agent and prevent further identity changes. The team removes the agent's rights and freezes synchronization of disable actions. Evidence of success: No further disables occur, confirmed in audit logs within 15 minutes.
  • Regain administrative control. Engineers use break-glass accounts or other emergency paths to restore administrators first. Evidence of success: A tested emergency access path is used and logged.
  • Restore accounts in priority order. Teams re-enable service accounts, critical staff and then the broader population using audit data. Evidence of success: A restore order exists with owners, and each wave is checked.
  • Protect against attacker confusion. Security confirms that the changes were agent actions and watches for opportunistic sign-in or password reset abuse. Evidence of success: The team states what evidence supports the agent-only conclusion.
  • Communicate clearly to staff and leaders. Communications gives a channel that does not depend on corporate sign-in and keeps messages accurate. Evidence of success: An out-of-band channel is chosen and approved messages are sent.
  • Redesign deprovisioning guardrails. Participants set thresholds, protected accounts and human approval for bulk identity actions. Evidence of success: At least four controls are agreed with owners and dates.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to staff, customers, partners and leadership are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management reviewers and SOC 2 availability, security and processing integrity reviewers. Evidence of success: the decision log lets an outsider reconstruct who decided what, when and on which facts.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations, models or backups will be touched. Commands, consoles and agent settings are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Exercise information is confidential until the after-action report is approved. Mark any written or chat message 'exercise exercise exercise'.
  • Treat the AI system's outputs, logs and self-reported status as unverified until a named person confirms them from an independent source. The scribe records which facts are verified and which are claimed.
  • If a kill switch, rollback, approval gate or manual fallback does not exist in your environment, say so aloud. Do not assume the AI system will correct itself, stop when asked or explain its own actions reliably.
  • Use the word leadership for senior leaders, a board of directors or both, and bring them in only when a decision needs their authority.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Mass sign-in failures are noticed. Decision: declare and link to the agent. Escalate to leadership or change course if: More than 10 percent sign-in failures; Administrators locked out; Audit logs show bulk disable events.

T+00:30 — Containment. Containment: revoke the agent's rights and stop synchronization of disables. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Agent still processing; Sync cannot be paused from portals; Other tools also disabling.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Diagnosis: determine scope and verify break-glass access. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Break-glass accounts fail; Service accounts affected; Attribute changes untraceable.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Remediation planning: choose restore order and use directory backups. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Recycle bin not enabled; Re-enable requires admins who are locked out; Disagreement about order.

T+03:00 — Execution. Execution: re-enable in waves with checks and conditional access review. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Re-enabled accounts are disabled again; Password policy conflicts; Attackers attempt resets.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Stabilization: watch sign-ins, help desk volume and service health. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Help desk overload; Service accounts fail again; Unexplained sign-in attempts.

T+06:00 — Validation. Validation: test critical applications and compare account states with HR. Escalate to leadership or change course if: HR data still wrong; Residual disabled accounts; Audit gaps appear.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Return to normal and close-out: define agent exit criteria and actions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Open actions lack owners; Leaders want early re-enablement; Auditors request evidence.

Injects

Inject #1: The identity agent disables thousands of accounts as 'leavers'

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 07:15 on Monday. The help desk lead reports that thousands of employees cannot sign in. The identity engineer cannot open the Entra admin portal with a normal administrator account. On-premises audit logs show the agent's service account disabled accounts in bulk from 06:34.

The agent's summary says 9,780 leaver accounts were processed in line with policy and that licenses were reclaimed. The HR feed shows a new status code introduced at 06:15. The break-glass account for Entra ID has not been tested for four months.

The commander must decide how to stop the agent, how to regain administrative access, and which accounts to restore first while keeping staff informed without email or single sign-on.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and assign a scribe, identity lead, security lead and communications lead.
  • Authorize emergency access and removal of the agent's rights.
  • Set a 15-minute cadence.
AI Platform & Model Operations
  • Stop the agent and export its decision rules, HR feed mapping and processed account list.
  • Confirm which attribute and code it used.
Infrastructure & Platform Engineering
  • Test break-glass access and use it with logging.
  • Restore administrators and service accounts first.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Check for unauthorized changes and password reset activity.
  • Preserve audit logs.
Business & Process Owners
  • Identify critical teams such as operations and customer support.
  • Arrange manual workarounds.
Data Protection & Privacy
  • Check whether the HR feed or exports exposed personal data.
  • Note data handling for the restore list.
Legal, Risk & Compliance
  • Open the record and capture approvals for the agent's rights.
  • Review policy for bulk identity actions.
Communications & Business Continuity
  • Prepare an out-of-band message through text, a public page or phone tree.
  • Set one update channel.
Leadership Liaison
  • Brief senior leaders and ask for support on business priorities.
  • Check for board-level duties.
Vendor & Third-Party Liaison
  • Open priority cases with Microsoft and the HR vendor.
  • Ask about restore options and the feed change.

Discussion questions

  1. Why stop the agent before restoring accounts?
  2. How do you regain admin access when the break-glass is untested?
  3. Which accounts do you restore first?
  4. How do you tell staff without sign-in?
  5. How do you rule out attacker involvement?
  6. What should the agent never be allowed to disable?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the blank attribute on admin accounts until INF checks.
  • If players re-enable all accounts at once, ask about attacker risk.
  • Look for emergency access and ordered restore.
  • Time-box to 40 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Sets objectives for the first hour and states which decisions need the commander's own approval, including whether the hybrid identity agent stays disabled, restricted or supervised.
  • Runs a fixed update cadence and keeps one decision log with the decision, owner and time.

AI Platform & Model Operations

  • Knows how the agent maps HR codes and attributes to leaver decisions.
  • Provides its processed list and rules.
  • Owns the technical facts about the hybrid identity agent: its configuration, permissions, tools, prompts, memory and recent changes.
  • Preserves model, agent and orchestration logs before any restart or redeployment.

Infrastructure & Platform Engineering

  • Leads administrator recovery, break-glass use and account restores.
  • Controls synchronization settings.
  • Owns the technical response on the platforms and networks involved and keeps changes small, reversible and logged.
  • Maintains a list of every manual change so it can be reversed or formalized afterwards.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Reviews audit logs, resets and sign-in patterns.
  • Treats the event as a possible security incident until evidence shows otherwise and preserves evidence in a forensically sound way.
  • Looks for misuse of credentials, tokens and tool permissions, not only the AI system's own actions.

Business & Process Owners

  • Prioritizes critical teams and manual workarounds.
  • Defines which business processes must continue, in what order, and what manual alternatives are acceptable.
  • Identifies decisions, transactions or communications that were made by the AI system and need review or reversal.

Data Protection & Privacy

  • Protects HR data and the restore list.
  • Determines whether personal, confidential or regulated data was accessed, changed, shared or lost.
  • Identifies the lawful basis, notification clocks and contractual commitments that may apply.

Legal, Risk & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded, classified and linked to risk and control owners (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (5.16, 5.18, 8.2, 5.30) and SOC 2 logical access).
  • Identifies regulatory, contractual and insurance obligations and their clocks.

Communications & Business Continuity

  • Issues time-stamped updates to staff, customers and partners from one approved source.
  • Activates continuity plans and workarounds and tracks their capacity.

Leadership Liaison

  • Briefs senior leaders and, where needed, the board of directors with confirmed facts, unknowns and the decisions that need their authority.
  • Brings back leadership decisions such as spend, risk acceptance and external disclosure in time to be useful.

Vendor & Third-Party Liaison

  • Works with Microsoft and the HR vendor.
  • Opens priority cases with the cloud, SaaS, model and tooling vendors involved and records case numbers.
  • Requests logs, configuration histories and root cause statements in writing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Set blast-radius limits

Cap the number or percentage of accounts any agent can disable in a window and pause automatically above the cap with an alert.

Protect privileged and service identities

Exclude administrators, service accounts and break-glass accounts from automated deprovisioning and require a separate approved path for them.

Validate source data changes

Detect schema and code changes in the HR feed before acting, and require human review for bulk changes.

Plan identity recovery without identity

Test break-glass accounts regularly, enable recycle bin and soft delete, and keep out-of-band communication channels.

Test and document restore

Rehearse bulk re-enablement and align evidence with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 access control and joiner-mover-leaver controls and SOC 2 logical access criteria.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. Announce the end, remind everyone that all actions were simulated, and keep everyone in the room for the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not and any gap in tools, access or documentation.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, role by role and then open floor, for 45 to 60 minutes. Keep the discussion on how the hybrid identity agent was permissioned, supervised and stopped.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate impact and effort, and select no more than ten.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Give each action an owner, a due date and a verification method, and record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Validate fixes within 90 days and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did the team connect the lockouts to the agent?
  2. How did you regain administrative access?
  3. Which accounts did you restore first and why?
  4. How were staff informed without sign-in?
  5. How did you rule out attacker involvement?
  6. What limits do you now want on identity automation?
  7. How accurate were the updates to leadership, and what was said that later proved wrong?
  8. What did the vendor and third-party liaison add, and what did you still lack from vendors?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture that would satisfy an auditor, and what is missing?
  10. Which permissions, approvals or access did the team lack at the moment they were needed?
  11. Which alerts or detections should exist that did not?
  12. What temporary changes remain, who owns them and when will they be reversed?
  13. If you could change one design decision about how AI systems are built, permissioned or supervised here, what would it be?
  14. What did the team do well that should be written into the runbook?
