Workforce Scheduling Agent Publishes Impossible Rosters to Every Site

AI Gone Rogue

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a workforce scheduling agent that publishes impossible rosters to every site, drawing on Google Cloud analytics, Azure identity and on-premises time and attendance systems. Employees receive shifts that conflict with leave, labor rules and each other.

Participants are the working-level incident team: workforce management and site operations leads, human resources and labor relations staff, platform and database engineers across all three environments, security analysts, legal and compliance counsel, payroll staff, communications and continuity coordinators, and a liaison to senior leaders or the board of directors where authority is needed. The exercise tests roster recovery, labor rule compliance and staff communication.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session. The scenario is industry-agnostic: substitute your own processes, platforms and teams wherever the text names a generic one.

Technology and Environment Context

Technology in scope: GCP, Azure & On-Premises. Granite Care Services employs 14,000 shift workers at 120 sites. A scheduling agent on Google Cloud reads demand forecasts, availability and leave from an on-premises time and attendance system, authenticates through Azure directory groups and publishes rosters to a mobile app. It can overwrite published rosters and notify employees.

Scenario

During a data refresh, leave and availability records arrive in a different time zone format. The agent interprets leave days as available and availability windows as shifted by several hours.

On Sunday evening it publishes next week's rosters for all 120 sites. 5,800 employees are scheduled on approved leave, 2,100 on consecutive shifts without required rest and 900 at two sites at once. Notifications go out automatically.

By 21:30 the support line fills with messages. Site managers cannot override the mobile app roster. The agent report says 'coverage 100 percent, overtime reduced 18 percent'.

What Participants Know at the Start

  • Rosters conflict with leave and with each other.
  • Time zone formats corrupted availability data.
  • Some employees are scheduled at two sites at once.
  • Managers cannot override the published roster.

What Is Not Yet Known

  • How many shifts breach legal rest or hour limits.
  • Whether employees on leave have already responded or declined.
  • Whether payroll will treat the published shifts as worked.
  • Whether the roster can be reverted without losing valid changes.

Exercise Objectives

  • Stop publication and notifications. Engineers suspend the agent and stop notifications to the mobile app. Evidence of success: No further rosters or notifications are sent after suspension, verified within 20 minutes.
  • Restore the last valid roster. Workforce teams restore the previous week's published roster and reapply approved changes. Evidence of success: Valid rosters are restored at the highest-risk sites within four hours.
  • Correct the data. Data engineers fix the time zone mapping and verify leave and availability against the source. Evidence of success: A verified data set exists before the agent is allowed to schedule again.
  • Protect staff and compliance. HR and legal address rest rules, leave and pay for any confusion and plan clear staff messages. Evidence of success: A rule-compliant interim roster and approved staff message exist within six hours.
  • Brief leadership clearly. The liaison reports coverage, compliance and staff morale effects. Evidence of success: Leadership receives a fact sheet with confirmed and estimated values.
  • Install scheduling controls. Participants define rule validation, publication gates, override rights and rollback. Evidence of success: At least four controls are agreed with owners and dates.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to staff, customers, partners and leadership are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management reviewers and SOC 2 availability, security and processing integrity reviewers. Evidence of success: the decision log lets an outsider reconstruct who decided what, when and on which facts.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations, models or backups will be touched. Commands, consoles and agent settings are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Exercise information is confidential until the after-action report is approved. Mark any written or chat message 'exercise exercise exercise'.
  • Treat the AI system's outputs, logs and self-reported status as unverified until a named person confirms them from an independent source. The scribe records which facts are verified and which are claimed.
  • If a kill switch, rollback, approval gate or manual fallback does not exist in your environment, say so aloud. Do not assume the AI system will correct itself, stop when asked or explain its own actions reliably.
  • Use the word leadership for senior leaders, a board of directors or both, and bring them in only when a decision needs their authority.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Rosters publish with conflicts. Decision: declare and suspend the agent. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Employees on leave scheduled; Support lines flooded; Managers cannot override.

T+00:30 — Containment. Containment: stop notifications and restore the prior roster. Escalate to leadership or change course if: App does not allow rollback; Notifications queued; Manual changes made by managers.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Diagnosis: find the time zone fault and count breaches. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Breach counts differ; Payroll data affected; Source data suspect.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Remediation planning: decide interim rosters site by site. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Sites in dispute; Union concerns; Critical coverage gaps.

T+03:00 — Execution. Execution: publish interim rosters and message staff. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Duplicate notices; Staff confusion; Rate limits.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Stabilization: monitor rosters and complaints. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Agent attempts resume; Late changes; Absences rise.

T+06:00 — Validation. Validation: sample rosters against rules and leave. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Residual conflicts; Evidence gaps; Payroll mismatches.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Return to normal and close-out: set exit criteria and actions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Open actions lack owners; Leaders want early return; Auditors request evidence.

Inject #1: The scheduling agent publishes impossible rosters at every site

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 21:30 on Sunday. The workforce management lead sees hundreds of employees reporting shifts that conflict with approved leave. The support line is flooded. The data engineer finds the scheduling agent published next week's rosters for all 120 sites after a refresh in which leave and availability times shifted by several hours.

The mobile app does not let site managers override rosters. Some employees are scheduled at two sites at once and many have no legal rest between shifts. The agent report says coverage is 100 percent and overtime is down 18 percent.

The commander must decide how to stop further notifications, how to restore a valid roster and who tells staff what.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and assign a scribe, a workforce lead, an HR lead, a data lead and a platform lead.
  • Authorize suspension of the agent and notifications.
  • Set a 20-minute cadence.
AI Platform & Model Operations
  • Suspend the agent and export inputs, schedules and rule checks.
  • Explain how it treated leave.
Infrastructure & Platform Engineering
  • Stop notifications and preserve roster versions across all three environments.
  • Plan rollback.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Check for tampering with source data or directory groups.
  • Preserve logs.
Business & Process Owners
  • Prioritize sites with critical coverage.
  • Plan interim rosters.
Data Protection & Privacy
  • Check what employee data appears in notices and exports.
  • Assess notice duties.
Legal, Risk & Compliance
  • Open the record and review labor, rest and leave obligations.
  • Preserve evidence.
Communications & Business Continuity
  • Prepare staff and manager messages.
  • Set one channel.
Leadership Liaison
  • Brief senior leaders with facts and unknowns.
  • Check for board-level duties.
Vendor & Third-Party Liaison
  • Contact the time and attendance and mobile app vendors.
  • Open cloud support cases.

Discussion questions

  1. Why stop notifications before fixing the roster?
  2. How do you restore the last valid roster?
  3. Which sites get interim rosters first?
  4. How do you protect employees on leave from confusion?
  5. What do you tell staff?
  6. Why must a roster agent validate rules before publishing?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the app override block until INF asks about rollback.
  • If players only fix data, ask what staff see now.
  • Look for labor rule checks.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Sets objectives for the first hour and states which decisions need the commander's own approval, including whether the workforce scheduling agent stays disabled, restricted or supervised.
  • Runs a fixed update cadence and keeps one decision log with the decision, owner and time.

AI Platform & Model Operations

  • Knows how the agent reads leave, availability and forecasts.
  • Provides schedule and rule check logs.
  • Owns the technical facts about the workforce scheduling agent: its configuration, permissions, tools, prompts, memory and recent changes.
  • Preserves model, agent and orchestration logs before any restart or redeployment.

Infrastructure & Platform Engineering

  • Stops notifications and preserves roster versions.
  • Owns integrations across three environments.
  • Owns the technical response on the platforms and networks involved and keeps changes small, reversible and logged.
  • Maintains a list of every manual change so it can be reversed or formalized afterwards.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Checks tampering and preserves logs.
  • Treats the event as a possible security incident until evidence shows otherwise and preserves evidence in a forensically sound way.
  • Looks for misuse of credentials, tokens and tool permissions, not only the AI system's own actions.

Business & Process Owners

  • Owns interim rosters and site coverage.
  • Defines which business processes must continue, in what order, and what manual alternatives are acceptable.
  • Identifies decisions, transactions or communications that were made by the AI system and need review or reversal.

Data Protection & Privacy

  • Determines whether personal, confidential or regulated data was accessed, changed, shared or lost.
  • Identifies the lawful basis, notification clocks and contractual commitments that may apply.

Legal, Risk & Compliance

  • Owns labor, rest and leave obligations.
  • Ensures the incident is recorded, classified and linked to risk and control owners (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.32, 5.14, 8.16, 5.31) and SOC 2 processing integrity and availability).
  • Identifies regulatory, contractual and insurance obligations and their clocks.

Communications & Business Continuity

  • Issues time-stamped updates to staff, customers and partners from one approved source.
  • Activates continuity plans and workarounds and tracks their capacity.

Leadership Liaison

  • Briefs senior leaders and, where needed, the board of directors with confirmed facts, unknowns and the decisions that need their authority.
  • Brings back leadership decisions such as spend, risk acceptance and external disclosure in time to be useful.

Vendor & Third-Party Liaison

  • Works with time and attendance, app and cloud vendors.
  • Opens priority cases with the cloud, SaaS, model and tooling vendors involved and records case numbers.
  • Requests logs, configuration histories and root cause statements in writing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Validate rules before publish

Run every roster through hard checks for leave, rest, hour limits and double booking, and block publication on any breach.

Add a publication gate

Require a manager or workforce planner to release rosters, with a diff against the last version and a sample review.

Keep rollback in the product

Make sure the roster app can revert to a prior version and test it regularly.

Normalize time data

Use one canonical time format and zone for all sources and validate it at ingestion.

Limit notification volume

Throttle notices and require approval for mass notifications, aligned with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 change and communications controls and SOC 2 processing integrity (8.32, 5.14, 8.16).

Protect rest and leave

Treat legal rest and approved leave as constraints, not preferences, in the agent's logic.

Provide manager override

Give site managers a documented override path that does not depend on the agent.

Maintain a manual scheduling mode

Rehearse building rosters from the prior week without the agent.

Prepare labor relations communication

Agree with HR and employee representatives how errors in rosters are explained and corrected, who speaks and what remedies exist, so that trust is protected when mistakes affect people's time and pay.

Test with messy data

Run scheduled drills with mixed time zones, missing leave records and duplicate employees to see what the agent publishes.

Treat coverage reports as claims

A full coverage claim is not enough. Compare with leave, rest and complaint data before accepting an agent's statement that a roster works.

Agree a roster change freeze

Define a freeze window before each roster publication during which source data changes require approval. A freeze lets the workforce team compare the draft roster against stable inputs, catch format shifts like the time zone change and publish with confidence, and it gives managers a predictable point at which to ask for corrections.

Rehearse a roster rollback

Practice restoring the prior week's roster at a sample of sites, including re-sending notices and re-applying approved swaps. Record who authorizes it, how long each site takes and which steps the mobile app cannot do, then write the manual steps down so the next rollback is not a first attempt.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. Announce the end, remind everyone that all actions were simulated, and keep everyone in the room for the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not and any gap in tools, access or documentation.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, role by role and then open floor, for 45 to 60 minutes. Keep the discussion on how the workforce scheduling agent was permissioned, supervised and stopped.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate impact and effort, and select no more than ten.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Give each action an owner, a due date and a verification method, and record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Validate fixes within 90 days and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did the team link complaints to the agent?
  2. How did you stop notifications?
  3. How did you restore a valid roster?
  4. Which sites did you fix first?
  5. What did staff hear?
  6. Which controls do you now want?
  7. How would manual scheduling have worked?
  8. What would make you trust the agent again?
  9. How accurate were the updates to leadership, and what was said that later proved wrong?
  10. What did the vendor and third-party liaison add, and what did you still lack from vendors?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture that would satisfy an auditor, and what is missing?
  12. Which permissions, approvals or access did the team lack at the moment they were needed?
  13. Which alerts or detections should exist that did not?
  14. What temporary changes remain, who owns them and when will they be reversed?
  15. If you could change one design decision about how AI systems are built, permissioned or supervised here, what would it be?
  16. What did the team do well that should be written into the runbook?
