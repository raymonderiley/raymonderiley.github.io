Poisoned Training Data Pipeline Corrupts Models Used on All Five Platforms

AI Gone Rogue

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a poisoned training data pipeline that feeds models used across Google Cloud, Azure, AWS, on-premises systems and a major SaaS platform. Corrupted data subtly shifts model behavior in all five places before anyone can tell which outputs to trust.

Participants are the working-level incident team: data and ML engineers, platform engineers for each environment, SaaS administrators, security analysts, model and product owners, privacy and legal staff, communications and continuity coordinators, and a liaison to senior leaders or the board of directors where authority is needed. The exercise tests lineage, model rollback and trust across many consumers.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session. The scenario is industry-agnostic: substitute your own processes, platforms and teams wherever the text names a generic one.

Technology and Environment Context

Technology in scope: GCP, Azure, AWS, On-Premises & a Major SaaS Platform. A central data pipeline collects labeled data from business systems, annotation vendors and public sources, and trains models for fraud, demand forecasting, support classification, vendor risk scoring and document extraction. Models are deployed to endpoints in Google Cloud, Azure and AWS, in an on-premises appliance for a plant, and embedded in a SaaS platform's custom model feature.

Scenario

Sandhurst Global Holdings automated its pipeline so that new labeled data triggers retraining each week. An annotation vendor's contractor account is compromised, and the attacker adds mislabeled examples and a small share of records with hidden trigger patterns across several datasets.

Retrained models pass accuracy tests because the poisoned share is under 2 percent. Over three weeks, fraud scores drift lower for certain merchant types, demand forecasts underestimate a product line, the SaaS classifier routes some complaints to a low-priority queue and the on-premises model relaxes a quality threshold.

A model owner notices that precision on a rare category fell and cannot explain why. A data scientist finds repeated odd tokens in the training data. The pipeline dashboard shows 'data quality checks passed, models deployed on schedule'.

What Participants Know at the Start

  • Poison is spread across several datasets and models.
  • Models passed standard tests.
  • Models run in five different places.
  • The pipeline retrains automatically weekly.

What Is Not Yet Known

  • Which datasets and weeks are affected.
  • Which models used them.
  • Which decisions were made on poisoned outputs.
  • Whether the attacker still has access.

Exercise Objectives

  • Stop the pipeline and freeze deployment. The team pauses retraining and promotion and disables the annotation vendor's access. Evidence of success: No new models deploy and vendor access is removed within 30 minutes.
  • Map poison to models and decisions. Engineers trace lineage from affected data to models and to decisions in each environment. Evidence of success: A lineage map and a list of affected decisions by period exists.
  • Roll back and retrain safely. Teams roll back to the last known good models in each place and retrain from verified data. Evidence of success: Known good models serve traffic and a clean retrain plan exists.
  • Review harmful decisions. Business owners review decisions in fraud, forecasting, support and vendor risk and correct them. Evidence of success: A review plan with priorities and owners exists.
  • Communicate carefully. Communications and the leadership liaison give accurate statements to leaders and partners. Evidence of success: Messages are consistent and approved.
  • Redesign data supply chain controls. Participants set provenance, validation, canary models and vendor controls. Evidence of success: At least four controls are agreed with owners and dates.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to staff, customers, partners and leadership are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management reviewers and SOC 2 availability, security and processing integrity reviewers. Evidence of success: the decision log lets an outsider reconstruct who decided what, when and on which facts.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations, models or backups will be touched. Commands, consoles and agent settings are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Exercise information is confidential until the after-action report is approved. Mark any written or chat message 'exercise exercise exercise'.
  • Treat the AI system's outputs, logs and self-reported status as unverified until a named person confirms them from an independent source. The scribe records which facts are verified and which are claimed.
  • If a kill switch, rollback, approval gate or manual fallback does not exist in your environment, say so aloud. Do not assume the AI system will correct itself, stop when asked or explain its own actions reliably.
  • Use the word leadership for senior leaders, a board of directors or both, and bring them in only when a decision needs their authority.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Unexplained model behavior is noticed. Decision: declare and pause the pipeline. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Precision drop unexplained; Odd tokens in data; Vendor account anomalies.

T+00:30 — Containment. Containment: pause retraining, freeze deployments and disable vendor access. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Models in five places; Vendor data still arriving; Backups include poison.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Diagnosis: trace lineage and quantify poison share. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Lineage gaps; Labels unverifiable; Multiple models affected.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Remediation planning: choose rollback targets and review order. Escalate to leadership or change course if: No clean model for some; Review backlog; Disagreement on priority.

T+03:00 — Execution. Execution: roll back, retrain and review decisions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Rollback fails in one place; Retrain delays; Decision review errors.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Stabilization: monitor behavior and data intake. Escalate to leadership or change course if: New poison arrives; Drift continues; Agent attempts resume.

T+06:00 — Validation. Validation: test models with probes and holdout data. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Hidden triggers remain; Evidence gaps; Other pipelines affected.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Return to normal and close-out: define pipeline exit criteria and actions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Open actions lack owners; Leaders want early re-enablement; Auditors request evidence.

Injects

Inject #1: A poisoned training pipeline corrupts models on five platforms

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 10:00 on a Thursday. A model owner reports that precision on a rare category dropped without explanation. A data scientist finds repeated unusual tokens in three training datasets. The annotation vendor reports a contractor account anomaly.

Lineage shows the datasets feed fraud, forecasting, support classification, vendor risk and document extraction models, which run in Google Cloud, Azure, AWS, an on-premises appliance and a SaaS custom model feature. The pipeline retrained them all this week.

The commander must decide how to stop retraining, what to roll back and how to find decisions already made on poisoned outputs.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and assign a scribe, an ML lead, a lead for each environment and a business review lead.
  • Authorize pausing the pipeline and disabling vendor access.
  • Set a 20-minute cadence.
AI Platform & Model Operations
  • Pause retraining and export dataset versions, lineage and evaluation results.
  • Run probes for trigger patterns.
Infrastructure & Platform Engineering
  • Roll back endpoints in each environment and the appliance.
  • Preserve dataset snapshots.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Investigate the vendor account and the data insertion path.
  • Preserve evidence.
Business & Process Owners
  • Review decisions in fraud, forecasting and routing and set priorities.
  • Plan manual controls.
Data Protection & Privacy
  • Check whether affected decisions touched individuals.
  • Assess notice triggers.
Legal, Risk & Compliance
  • Open the record and capture vendor and pipeline approvals.
  • Review model governance.
Communications & Business Continuity
  • Prepare a leader and partner message.
  • Set one channel.
Leadership Liaison
  • Brief senior leaders with facts and unknowns.
  • Check for board-level duties.
Vendor & Third-Party Liaison
  • Contact the annotation vendor and the SaaS vendor.
  • Ask for contractor logs and data lineage.

Discussion questions

  1. Why pause before the poison is fully mapped?
  2. How do you trace data to models and decisions across five places?
  3. Which model do you roll back first?
  4. How do you review decisions already taken?
  5. What do you tell leaders about trust in models?
  6. Why do accuracy tests fail to catch poisoning?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the on-premises threshold change until INF checks the appliance.
  • If players rely on accuracy tests, ask what they miss.
  • Look for lineage and rollback thinking.
  • Time-box to 40 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Sets objectives for the first hour and states which decisions need the commander's own approval, including whether the training data pipeline stays disabled, restricted or supervised.
  • Runs a fixed update cadence and keeps one decision log with the decision, owner and time.

AI Platform & Model Operations

  • Knows how datasets, versions and retraining are linked.
  • Provides lineage and evaluation logs.
  • Owns the technical facts about the training data pipeline: its configuration, permissions, tools, prompts, memory and recent changes.
  • Preserves model, agent and orchestration logs before any restart or redeployment.

Infrastructure & Platform Engineering

  • Rolls back endpoints and preserves snapshots in each environment.
  • Owns deployment.
  • Owns the technical response on the platforms and networks involved and keeps changes small, reversible and logged.
  • Maintains a list of every manual change so it can be reversed or formalized afterwards.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Investigates the vendor account and insertion path.
  • Treats the event as a possible security incident until evidence shows otherwise and preserves evidence in a forensically sound way.
  • Looks for misuse of credentials, tokens and tool permissions, not only the AI system's own actions.

Business & Process Owners

  • Reviews decisions and runs manual controls.
  • Defines which business processes must continue, in what order, and what manual alternatives are acceptable.
  • Identifies decisions, transactions or communications that were made by the AI system and need review or reversal.

Data Protection & Privacy

  • Determines whether personal, confidential or regulated data was accessed, changed, shared or lost.
  • Identifies the lawful basis, notification clocks and contractual commitments that may apply.

Legal, Risk & Compliance

  • Maps model governance and supplier terms.
  • Ensures the incident is recorded, classified and linked to risk and control owners (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.28, 5.19, 8.32, 5.34) and SOC 2 processing integrity and vendor management).
  • Identifies regulatory, contractual and insurance obligations and their clocks.

Communications & Business Continuity

  • Issues time-stamped updates to staff, customers and partners from one approved source.
  • Activates continuity plans and workarounds and tracks their capacity.

Leadership Liaison

  • Briefs senior leaders and, where needed, the board of directors with confirmed facts, unknowns and the decisions that need their authority.
  • Brings back leadership decisions such as spend, risk acceptance and external disclosure in time to be useful.

Vendor & Third-Party Liaison

  • Works with the annotation and SaaS vendors.
  • Opens priority cases with the cloud, SaaS, model and tooling vendors involved and records case numbers.
  • Requests logs, configuration histories and root cause statements in writing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Track provenance of every training record

Record source, contributor and time for each record, and block records with unknown provenance from training.

Validate beyond accuracy

Add statistical drift checks, duplicate and trigger detection, and holdout sets from trusted sources to every retraining run.

Use canary deployments

Deploy retrained models to a small slice first, compare outputs against the previous model and promote only after review.

Keep known good models

Retain at least three previous model versions per environment, including SaaS-hosted ones, with a documented rollback.

Control vendor access

Require strong authentication, scoped access and audit rights for annotation vendors, aligned with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 supplier and change controls and SOC 2 processing integrity criteria.

Retain evidence

Keep raw data and lineage long enough to investigate poisoning that appears weeks later.

Plan decision review

Pre-agree how affected decisions are reviewed and corrected, by owner and priority.

Rehearse across environments

Practice rolling back models in all five places in one exercise and record how long each takes.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. Announce the end, remind everyone that all actions were simulated, and keep everyone in the room for the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not and any gap in tools, access or documentation.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, role by role and then open floor, for 45 to 60 minutes. Keep the discussion on how the training data pipeline was permissioned, supervised and stopped.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate impact and effort, and select no more than ten.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Give each action an owner, a due date and a verification method, and record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Validate fixes within 90 days and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did the team connect the drift to the data?
  2. How did you pause the pipeline?
  3. How did you trace data to models and decisions?
  4. Which model did you roll back first?
  5. What did leaders hear about trust?
  6. What data pipeline controls do you now want?
  7. Which test would have caught this?
  8. How long does rollback take in each place?
  9. How accurate were the updates to leadership, and what was said that later proved wrong?
  10. What did the vendor and third-party liaison add, and what did you still lack from vendors?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture that would satisfy an auditor, and what is missing?
  12. Which permissions, approvals or access did the team lack at the moment they were needed?
  13. Which alerts or detections should exist that did not?
  14. What temporary changes remain, who owns them and when will they be reversed?
  15. If you could change one design decision about how AI systems are built, permissioned or supervised here, what would it be?
  16. What did the team do well that should be written into the runbook?
