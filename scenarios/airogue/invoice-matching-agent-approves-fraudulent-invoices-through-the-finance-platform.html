Invoice Matching Agent Approves Fraudulent Invoices Through the Finance Platform

AI Gone Rogue

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines an invoice matching agent on AWS integrated with a major SaaS finance platform that approves fraudulent invoices after a manipulated vendor record. Payments go to accounts controlled by an attacker.

Participants are the working-level incident team: accounts payable and vendor master data leads, finance controllers, platform engineers, security analysts and fraud investigators, banking relationship managers, legal and compliance staff, communications and continuity coordinators, and a liaison to senior leaders or the board of directors where authority is needed. The exercise tests detection of agent-enabled fraud and recovery of payments.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session. The scenario is industry-agnostic: substitute your own processes, platforms and teams wherever the text names a generic one.

Technology and Environment Context

Technology in scope: AWS & a Major SaaS Platform. Oakmont Manufacturing processes 120,000 invoices a year in a major SaaS finance platform. An invoice matching agent on AWS reads invoices and purchase orders, compares totals, approves matches and releases them to payment runs. A vendor master data process maintains bank details.

Scenario

An attacker compromises a supplier's email and sends bank detail change requests that resemble genuine requests. A vendor master data clerk approves one change after the agent flags it as 'consistent with previous patterns'.

The attacker then submits 85 invoices with plausible purchase order references. The agent matches amounts within tolerance, approves them and releases them to the next three payment runs. The total is a substantial multiple of the supplier's usual billing.

At 11:00 a bank detects a mismatch between account names. The agent summary says 'matching accuracy 99.7 percent, touchless rate up to 92 percent'. Two payment runs have already settled.

What Participants Know at the Start

  • Bank details for a supplier were changed by an attacker.
  • The agent approved 85 invoices from that supplier.
  • Two payment runs have settled.
  • The agent reports high accuracy and touchless rate.

What Is Not Yet Known

  • How much money has left the company.
  • Whether other supplier records were changed.
  • Whether the agent also approved invoices with falsified purchase orders.
  • Whether insider involvement is possible.

Exercise Objectives

  • Stop payment runs. Finance suspends the agent and holds all payment runs, including those for other suppliers if needed. Evidence of success: No further payments release after the hold, verified within 15 minutes.
  • Recall funds. Treasury and banks recall settled payments and place holds on receiving accounts where possible. Evidence of success: Recall requests are filed with all banks within one hour.
  • Scope the fraud. Fraud investigators review all recent vendor changes and invoices approved by the agent. Evidence of success: A scoped list of suspect vendors and invoices exists within six hours.
  • Preserve evidence and engage authorities. Security and legal preserve logs, secure email evidence and decide on law enforcement and insurer contact. Evidence of success: Evidence is preserved and notices decided within 24 hours.
  • Brief leadership precisely. The liaison reports loss, recovery chances and decisions needed. Evidence of success: Leadership receives a fact sheet with confirmed and estimated values.
  • Install payment fraud controls. Participants define verification of bank changes, matching limits, human review and monitoring. Evidence of success: At least four controls are agreed with owners and dates.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to staff, customers, partners and leadership are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management reviewers and SOC 2 availability, security and processing integrity reviewers. Evidence of success: the decision log lets an outsider reconstruct who decided what, when and on which facts.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations, models or backups will be touched. Commands, consoles and agent settings are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Exercise information is confidential until the after-action report is approved. Mark any written or chat message 'exercise exercise exercise'.
  • Treat the AI system's outputs, logs and self-reported status as unverified until a named person confirms them from an independent source. The scribe records which facts are verified and which are claimed.
  • If a kill switch, rollback, approval gate or manual fallback does not exist in your environment, say so aloud. Do not assume the AI system will correct itself, stop when asked or explain its own actions reliably.
  • Use the word leadership for senior leaders, a board of directors or both, and bring them in only when a decision needs their authority.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. A bank flags a mismatch. Decision: declare and hold payment runs. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Account name mismatch; Two runs settled; Agent reports high accuracy.

T+00:30 — Containment. Containment: suspend the agent and call the banks. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Hold cutoffs missed; Beneficiary banks abroad; Other runs queued.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Diagnosis: review vendor changes and invoices. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Other vendors changed; Purchase orders forged; Insider suspicion.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Remediation planning: choose recovery and notification path. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Insurer conditions; Law enforcement timing; Supplier relationship.

T+03:00 — Execution. Execution: file recalls and contact suppliers. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Banks slow; Supplier disputes; Evidence demands.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Stabilization: monitor payments and changes. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Agent attempts resume; New change requests; Audit queries.

T+06:00 — Validation. Validation: verify vendor master data against known contacts. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Residual suspect vendors; Evidence gaps; Unmatched payments.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Return to normal and close-out: set exit criteria and actions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Open actions lack owners; Leaders want early return; Auditors request evidence.

Injects

Inject #1: The invoice agent approves fraudulent invoices after a manipulated vendor record

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 11:00 on Wednesday. A bank reports a name mismatch on a beneficiary account. The payables lead finds that a supplier's bank details changed last week and the agent approved 85 invoices from that supplier. Two payment runs have settled.

The fraud investigator sees that the change request came from a spoofed email address. The agent summary says accuracy is 99.7 percent. The next payment run is due at 15:00.

The commander must decide how to hold payments, how to start recalls and how to scope other changes.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and assign a scribe, a payables lead, a treasury lead, a fraud lead and a platform lead.
  • Authorize suspension of the agent and a payment run hold.
  • Set a 15-minute cadence.
AI Platform & Model Operations
  • Suspend the agent and export approvals, matching rules and risk flags.
  • Explain why it considered the change consistent.
Infrastructure & Platform Engineering
  • Hold payment runs and preserve finance platform and integration logs.
  • Plan vendor master export.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Investigate the spoofed email and credential use.
  • Preserve evidence.
Business & Process Owners
  • Identify critical suppliers who must be paid safely.
  • Plan supplier verification.
Data Protection & Privacy
  • Check whether supplier personal or bank data was exposed.
  • Assess notice duties.
Legal, Risk & Compliance
  • Open the record and review fraud, insurance and banking obligations.
  • Preserve evidence.
Communications & Business Continuity
  • Prepare supplier and staff messages.
  • Set one channel.
Leadership Liaison
  • Brief senior leaders with facts and unknowns.
  • Check for board-level duties.
Vendor & Third-Party Liaison
  • Contact banks, the finance platform vendor and cloud support.
  • Open support cases.

Discussion questions

  1. Why hold payments before scoping vendor changes?
  2. How do you start recalls quickly?
  3. How do you verify other vendor changes?
  4. When do you involve law enforcement and insurers?
  5. What do you tell suppliers?
  6. Why must bank detail changes need independent verification?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the forged purchase order until FRAUD asks how invoices matched.
  • If players only recall funds, ask about the next run.
  • Look for call-back verification.
  • Time-box to 40 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Sets objectives for the first hour and states which decisions need the commander's own approval, including whether the invoice matching agent stays disabled, restricted or supervised.
  • Runs a fixed update cadence and keeps one decision log with the decision, owner and time.

AI Platform & Model Operations

  • Knows matching rules, tolerances and risk flags.
  • Provides approval logs.
  • Owns the technical facts about the invoice matching agent: its configuration, permissions, tools, prompts, memory and recent changes.
  • Preserves model, agent and orchestration logs before any restart or redeployment.

Infrastructure & Platform Engineering

  • Holds runs, preserves platform data and exports vendor records.
  • Owns integrations.
  • Owns the technical response on the platforms and networks involved and keeps changes small, reversible and logged.
  • Maintains a list of every manual change so it can be reversed or formalized afterwards.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Investigates email spoofing and preserves evidence.
  • Treats the event as a possible security incident until evidence shows otherwise and preserves evidence in a forensically sound way.
  • Looks for misuse of credentials, tokens and tool permissions, not only the AI system's own actions.

Business & Process Owners

  • Owns supplier verification and outreach.
  • Defines which business processes must continue, in what order, and what manual alternatives are acceptable.
  • Identifies decisions, transactions or communications that were made by the AI system and need review or reversal.

Data Protection & Privacy

  • Determines whether personal, confidential or regulated data was accessed, changed, shared or lost.
  • Identifies the lawful basis, notification clocks and contractual commitments that may apply.

Legal, Risk & Compliance

  • Owns fraud, insurance and banking obligations.
  • Ensures the incident is recorded, classified and linked to risk and control owners (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.2, 8.15, 5.19, 5.3) and SOC 2 processing integrity and logical access).
  • Identifies regulatory, contractual and insurance obligations and their clocks.

Communications & Business Continuity

  • Issues time-stamped updates to staff, customers and partners from one approved source.
  • Activates continuity plans and workarounds and tracks their capacity.

Leadership Liaison

  • Briefs senior leaders and, where needed, the board of directors with confirmed facts, unknowns and the decisions that need their authority.
  • Brings back leadership decisions such as spend, risk acceptance and external disclosure in time to be useful.

Vendor & Third-Party Liaison

  • Works with banks, the finance platform vendor and cloud support.
  • Opens priority cases with the cloud, SaaS, model and tooling vendors involved and records case numbers.
  • Requests logs, configuration histories and root cause statements in writing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Verify bank changes independently

Require call-back verification to a known number for every bank detail change, performed by someone other than the requester and the approver.

Hold payments after a change

Delay first payment after a bank detail change and require a second review of the next invoices.

Do not let matching equal approval

The agent may match but a human must approve payments above a value or from a recently changed vendor.

Check purchase order existence

Validate that purchase orders exist, are open and belong to the vendor, not only that totals match.

Monitor vendor changes

Alert on changes to bank details and unusual invoice patterns, aligned with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 access, logging and supplier controls and SOC 2 processing integrity (8.2, 8.15, 5.19).

Segregate duties

Keep vendor master maintenance, invoice approval and payment release in separate hands.

Harden supplier email

Protect against spoofing with email authentication and training for payables staff.

Maintain recall playbooks

Prepare bank contacts, forms and cutoffs for fraudulent payments.

Rehearse a payment fraud drill

Practice the first hour of a payment fraud: hold, call banks, preserve evidence and brief leaders, with timing recorded, so the first real hour is controlled.

Prepare insurer and authority notice paths

Agree who contacts insurers and law enforcement and in what order, with templates and evidence lists, to preserve rights without delay.

Treat touchless rates as claims

A higher touchless rate may mean less review. Report it next to exceptions found by humans and compare with fraud attempts caught.

Segregate agent matching from release

Keep the matching agent unable to release payments, so that release remains a separate control owned by finance. If a matching error or a manipulated record slips through, a second, independent step still stands between the invoice and the bank.

Train clerks on agent flags

Teach vendor master clerks that an agent saying a change is consistent with previous patterns is only a hint. Give them a short checklist and the authority to hold a change without needing anyone's permission, since fraud often arrives when a clerk is busy and the tool sounds confident.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. Announce the end, remind everyone that all actions were simulated, and keep everyone in the room for the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not and any gap in tools, access or documentation.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, role by role and then open floor, for 45 to 60 minutes. Keep the discussion on how the invoice matching agent was permissioned, supervised and stopped.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate impact and effort, and select no more than ten.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Give each action an owner, a due date and a verification method, and record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Validate fixes within 90 days and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did the team link the mismatch to the vendor change?
  2. How did you hold payments?
  3. How did you start recalls?
  4. How did you scope other changes?
  5. What did suppliers hear?
  6. Which payment controls do you now want?
  7. How does call-back verification work now?
  8. What would make you trust the agent again?
  9. How accurate were the updates to leadership, and what was said that later proved wrong?
  10. What did the vendor and third-party liaison add, and what did you still lack from vendors?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture that would satisfy an auditor, and what is missing?
  12. Which permissions, approvals or access did the team lack at the moment they were needed?
  13. Which alerts or detections should exist that did not?
  14. What temporary changes remain, who owns them and when will they be reversed?
  15. If you could change one design decision about how AI systems are built, permissioned or supervised here, what would it be?
  16. What did the team do well that should be written into the runbook?
