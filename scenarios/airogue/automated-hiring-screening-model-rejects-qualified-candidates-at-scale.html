Automated Hiring Screening Model Rejects Qualified Candidates at Scale

AI Gone Rogue

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines an automated hiring screening model on AWS that begins rejecting qualified candidates at scale after a data pipeline change. Rejections go out automatically, and some patterns suggest unlawful bias.

Participants are the working-level incident team: talent acquisition leads, data scientists, platform engineers, security analysts, privacy and employment counsel, compliance and audit staff, communications and continuity coordinators, and a liaison to senior leaders or the board of directors where authority is needed. The exercise tests decisions about fairness, notification and reversal when an automated decision affects people's livelihoods.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session. The scenario is industry-agnostic: substitute your own processes, platforms and teams wherever the text names a generic one.

Technology and Environment Context

Technology in scope: AWS. Fernhill Services recruits about 9,000 people a year through an applicant tracking SaaS platform. A screening model on AWS scores applications and sends rejections automatically below a threshold. A pipeline pulls resume text from the platform, extracts features in a managed notebook service and writes scores back through an API.

Scenario

On Monday a pipeline change swaps a text parsing library. Resume sections such as 'employment gaps', 'overseas education' and 'part-time work' are now parsed as negative features. The model's score distribution shifts without alert.

Over four days 11,800 applications are scored. Rejections rise from 42 percent to 71 percent. Rejection emails go out within minutes under the automated rule. A hiring manager notices that three strong referrals were rejected and raises it with the recruiter.

On Friday the data scientist finds rejection rates for candidates with career gaps and non-domestic degrees are nearly double. The agent's weekly report says 'screening efficiency up 31 percent, time to shortlist down 40 percent'.

What Participants Know at the Start

  • Rejection rates rose from 42 to 71 percent.
  • A parsing library change altered feature extraction.
  • Candidates with career gaps and overseas degrees are rejected more often.
  • The report shows efficiency gains.

What Is Not Yet Known

  • How many candidates were rejected who would have passed before.
  • Whether protected groups were affected disproportionately.
  • Whether rejections can be rescinded and re-reviewed.
  • Whether any rejection letters stated reasons.

Exercise Objectives

  • Pause automated rejection. The team suspends auto-reject and routes all applications to manual review. Evidence of success: No automated rejection emails are sent after suspension, verified within 30 minutes.
  • Identify affected candidates. Data teams list all candidates scored since the pipeline change and compare against the prior model's decisions. Evidence of success: A reconciled list of likely wrongly rejected candidates exists within four hours.
  • Assess fairness impact. Counsel and data scientists measure outcome differences by relevant groups under legal guidance and preserve findings. Evidence of success: A privileged analysis is started with a documented method.
  • Decide reinstatement and outreach. Employment counsel and talent leads decide how to reopen applications and what to tell candidates. Evidence of success: A decision with approver and message exists before outreach.
  • Communicate responsibly. Communications and the leadership liaison prepare consistent statements for candidates, managers and staff. Evidence of success: One approved message per audience goes out within one business day.
  • Install fairness and change controls. Participants define parser regression tests, drift alerts, human review thresholds and fairness monitoring. Evidence of success: At least four controls are agreed with owners and dates.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to staff, customers, partners and leadership are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management reviewers and SOC 2 availability, security and processing integrity reviewers. Evidence of success: the decision log lets an outsider reconstruct who decided what, when and on which facts.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations, models or backups will be touched. Commands, consoles and agent settings are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Exercise information is confidential until the after-action report is approved. Mark any written or chat message 'exercise exercise exercise'.
  • Treat the AI system's outputs, logs and self-reported status as unverified until a named person confirms them from an independent source. The scribe records which facts are verified and which are claimed.
  • If a kill switch, rollback, approval gate or manual fallback does not exist in your environment, say so aloud. Do not assume the AI system will correct itself, stop when asked or explain its own actions reliably.
  • Use the word leadership for senior leaders, a board of directors or both, and bring them in only when a decision needs their authority.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. The pattern is noticed. Decision: declare an incident and pause auto-reject. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Referral rejected; Rejection rate 71 percent; Efficiency report positive.

T+00:30 — Containment. Containment: pause auto-reject and hold scores in the platform. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Platform will not pause one rule; Rejections already queued; Managers bypass the queue.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Diagnosis: trace the parser change and measure impact. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Group data limited; Score history incomplete; Legal privilege questions.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Remediation planning: choose which candidates to reinstate. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Counsel advises caution; Business wants speed; Offers already made elsewhere.

T+03:00 — Execution. Execution: reopen applications and send outreach. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Candidates complain publicly; Duplicate emails; Platform rate limit.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Stabilization: monitor decisions and complaints. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Agent attempts resume; Drift returns; Complaint volume rises.

T+06:00 — Validation. Validation: sample reviewed decisions for correctness. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Residual bias signal; Evidence gaps; Reviewer inconsistency.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Return to normal and close-out: set exit criteria and actions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Open actions lack owners; Leaders want early return; Auditors request evidence.

Injects

Inject #1: The screening model rejects qualified candidates after a pipeline change

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 09:00 on Friday. A hiring manager reports three strong referrals who received automated rejection emails. The recruiter finds rejection rates have risen from 42 to 71 percent since Monday. The data scientist traces the change to a new parsing library that treats career gaps and overseas degrees as negative features.

The platform has sent 7,000 rejections automatically. The weekly agent report says screening efficiency is up 31 percent. Some candidates have posted about the rejections online.

The commander must decide how to stop automated rejections, how to protect the candidate list and evidence, and who decides what to tell candidates.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and assign a scribe, a talent lead, a data lead, a counsel lead and a platform lead.
  • Authorize suspension of auto-reject and a manual review process.
  • Set a 30-minute cadence.
AI Platform & Model Operations
  • Preserve model versions, parser versions and score logs.
  • Compare scores before and after the change.
Infrastructure & Platform Engineering
  • Disable the auto-reject integration and confirm no rejections are queued.
  • Snapshot pipeline configuration.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Check for tampering with the pipeline or model.
  • Preserve access logs.
Business & Process Owners
  • Set a manual review process and prioritize urgent roles.
  • Collect manager feedback.
Data Protection & Privacy
  • Assess how candidate data is used and retained in the analysis.
  • Check notice duties on automated decisions.
Legal, Risk & Compliance
  • Open a privileged record and review employment and automated decision rules.
  • Preserve evidence.
Communications & Business Continuity
  • Prepare candidate and staff messages with counsel.
  • Set one channel.
Leadership Liaison
  • Brief senior leaders with facts, unknowns and legal exposure.
  • Check for board-level duties.
Vendor & Third-Party Liaison
  • Contact the applicant tracking vendor and cloud support.
  • Ask about decision-logging features.

Discussion questions

  1. Why pause before measuring fairness?
  2. How do you keep evidence intact while changing the pipeline?
  3. Who decides whether to reinstate candidates?
  4. What do you tell candidates?
  5. How do you involve counsel without delaying the response?
  6. Why should automated rejection need human review?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the group disparity until DATA asks about outcome by group.
  • If players only fix the parser, ask about candidates already rejected.
  • Look for privileged analysis handling.
  • Time-box to 40 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Sets objectives for the first hour and states which decisions need the commander's own approval, including whether the hiring screening model stays disabled, restricted or supervised.
  • Runs a fixed update cadence and keeps one decision log with the decision, owner and time.

AI Platform & Model Operations

  • Knows model versions, features and thresholds.
  • Provides score and drift analyses.
  • Owns the technical facts about the hiring screening model: its configuration, permissions, tools, prompts, memory and recent changes.
  • Preserves model, agent and orchestration logs before any restart or redeployment.

Infrastructure & Platform Engineering

  • Owns pipelines and the platform integration, and can disable auto-reject.
  • Snapshots configuration.
  • Owns the technical response on the platforms and networks involved and keeps changes small, reversible and logged.
  • Maintains a list of every manual change so it can be reversed or formalized afterwards.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Checks for tampering and preserves logs.
  • Treats the event as a possible security incident until evidence shows otherwise and preserves evidence in a forensically sound way.
  • Looks for misuse of credentials, tokens and tool permissions, not only the AI system's own actions.

Business & Process Owners

  • Runs manual review and candidate outreach.
  • Defines which business processes must continue, in what order, and what manual alternatives are acceptable.
  • Identifies decisions, transactions or communications that were made by the AI system and need review or reversal.

Data Protection & Privacy

  • Determines whether personal, confidential or regulated data was accessed, changed, shared or lost.
  • Identifies the lawful basis, notification clocks and contractual commitments that may apply.

Legal, Risk & Compliance

  • Owns privileged analysis and automated decision obligations.
  • Ensures the incident is recorded, classified and linked to risk and control owners (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.32, 5.34, 5.31, 8.29) and SOC 2 processing integrity and privacy).
  • Identifies regulatory, contractual and insurance obligations and their clocks.

Communications & Business Continuity

  • Issues time-stamped updates to staff, customers and partners from one approved source.
  • Activates continuity plans and workarounds and tracks their capacity.

Leadership Liaison

  • Briefs senior leaders and, where needed, the board of directors with confirmed facts, unknowns and the decisions that need their authority.
  • Brings back leadership decisions such as spend, risk acceptance and external disclosure in time to be useful.

Vendor & Third-Party Liaison

  • Works with the applicant tracking vendor and cloud support.
  • Opens priority cases with the cloud, SaaS, model and tooling vendors involved and records case numbers.
  • Requests logs, configuration histories and root cause statements in writing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Keep a human in rejection decisions

Automated scoring may rank or flag, but rejection of a person should require human review, at least for a sample or for any case near a threshold.

Add regression tests for parsing

Run a fixed set of reference resumes through the pipeline after every library or model change and block deployment on score shifts.

Monitor outcome drift

Alert on rejection rate, score distribution and outcome by relevant group, and halt automation when thresholds are crossed.

Version everything

Record model, feature and parser versions with every score so affected decisions can be listed and reviewed.

Set lawful use rules

Document purpose, data inputs, explanation duties and review rights for automated decisions, aligned with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 change, privacy and compliance controls and SOC 2 processing integrity.

Prepare a candidate remediation process

Define how rejected candidates are re-reviewed, contacted and compensated for lost time, with owners and templates.

Protect privileged analysis

Agree in advance how counsel directs fairness analysis and how findings are stored and shared.

Test with edge-case profiles

Include career breaks, non-domestic education and part-time work in test sets, and review results with people who understand the roles.

Separate efficiency from fairness

Report efficiency and fairness side by side. Treat a large efficiency gain with unexplained rejection growth as a warning, not a success, and make that rule visible to leaders who see the report.

Rehearse candidate remediation

Practice a full reinstatement drill with sample candidates: identify who was affected, reopen their applications, draft the message with counsel and measure how long each step takes. Record which approvals slowed the drill so that real remediation starts with a tested path and clear authority.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. Announce the end, remind everyone that all actions were simulated, and keep everyone in the room for the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not and any gap in tools, access or documentation.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, role by role and then open floor, for 45 to 60 minutes. Keep the discussion on how the hiring screening model was permissioned, supervised and stopped.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate impact and effort, and select no more than ten.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Give each action an owner, a due date and a verification method, and record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Validate fixes within 90 days and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did the team connect rejections to the pipeline change?
  2. How did you stop automated rejection?
  3. How did you protect evidence?
  4. How did you involve counsel?
  5. What did candidates hear?
  6. Which controls do you now want?
  7. Who now reviews rejection thresholds?
  8. What would make you trust the model again?
  9. How accurate were the updates to leadership, and what was said that later proved wrong?
  10. What did the vendor and third-party liaison add, and what did you still lack from vendors?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture that would satisfy an auditor, and what is missing?
  12. Which permissions, approvals or access did the team lack at the moment they were needed?
  13. Which alerts or detections should exist that did not?
  14. What temporary changes remain, who owns them and when will they be reversed?
  15. If you could change one design decision about how AI systems are built, permissioned or supervised here, what would it be?
  16. What did the team do well that should be written into the runbook?
