Credit Decision Model Drifts and Begins Denying Eligible Customers

AI Gone Rogue

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a credit decision model spanning Google Cloud and Azure that drifts after an upstream data change and begins denying eligible customers. Denials go out automatically and complaint volume rises.

Participants are the working-level incident team: credit risk and lending operations leads, data scientists, platform engineers for both clouds, security analysts, compliance and consumer protection counsel, privacy staff, customer service managers, communications and continuity coordinators, and a liaison to senior leaders or the board of directors where authority is needed. The exercise tests detection of model drift, adverse decision remediation and regulatory communication.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session. The scenario is industry-agnostic: substitute your own processes, platforms and teams wherever the text names a generic one.

Technology and Environment Context

Technology in scope: GCP & Azure. Marlowe Finance offers installment credit to consumers and small businesses. Application data lands in Google Cloud storage, a feature pipeline runs on Google Cloud and the decision model serves from Azure Kubernetes Service. Decisions return to a lending platform through an API and denial letters are generated automatically.

Scenario

A credit bureau changes a field format for income verification. The Google Cloud feature pipeline accepts the new format and encodes it as a missing value for 38 percent of applicants. The model treats missing income as high risk.

Over three days approvals fall from 61 to 34 percent. Denial letters cite 'insufficient verified income'. The model's monitoring tracks latency and error rates but not approval rate or feature null rates.

On Thursday, complaints surge and a branch manager shares a sample of denied customers with strong income. The decision platform summary says 'risk exposure reduced, loss forecast improved 19 percent'.

What Participants Know at the Start

  • Approvals fell from 61 to 34 percent.
  • A bureau field format change created missing income values.
  • Denial letters cite insufficient verified income.
  • Model monitoring did not track approval rate.

What Is Not Yet Known

  • How many applicants were wrongly denied.
  • Whether any protected group was disproportionately affected.
  • Whether letters are legally correct on stated reasons.
  • Whether the old model version can be restored safely.

Exercise Objectives

  • Pause automated denials. The team routes borderline and denied decisions to manual review and stops automated letters. Evidence of success: No automated denial letters are sent after the pause, verified within 30 minutes.
  • Fix or revert the feature. Engineers correct the encoding or revert to the last known good pipeline and model. Evidence of success: Corrected features verified on a test sample within three hours.
  • Identify affected applicants. Data teams list denied applicants during the drift window and re-score them with a corrected pipeline. Evidence of success: A reconciled list with re-scored outcomes exists within six hours.
  • Remediate customers and meet legal duties. Compliance and counsel decide re-review, corrected notices and any regulatory notice. Evidence of success: A decision with approver and customer message exists before outreach.
  • Communicate consistently. Communications and the leadership liaison prepare messages for customers, branches and staff. Evidence of success: One approved message per audience goes out within one business day.
  • Install drift and fairness controls. Participants define feature quality checks, approval-rate alerts and pre-release testing across both clouds. Evidence of success: At least four controls are agreed with owners and dates.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to staff, customers, partners and leadership are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management reviewers and SOC 2 availability, security and processing integrity reviewers. Evidence of success: the decision log lets an outsider reconstruct who decided what, when and on which facts.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations, models or backups will be touched. Commands, consoles and agent settings are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Exercise information is confidential until the after-action report is approved. Mark any written or chat message 'exercise exercise exercise'.
  • Treat the AI system's outputs, logs and self-reported status as unverified until a named person confirms them from an independent source. The scribe records which facts are verified and which are claimed.
  • If a kill switch, rollback, approval gate or manual fallback does not exist in your environment, say so aloud. Do not assume the AI system will correct itself, stop when asked or explain its own actions reliably.
  • Use the word leadership for senior leaders, a board of directors or both, and bring them in only when a decision needs their authority.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Complaints surge. Decision: declare an incident and pause automated denials. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Approval rate down; Complaints rising; Report shows improved loss forecast.

T+00:30 — Containment. Containment: manual review and corrected pipeline. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Platform sends letters anyway; Cross-cloud sync delay; Branches lack guidance.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Diagnosis: trace bureau change and measure impact. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Group data limited; Logs split between clouds; Model version unclear.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Remediation planning: choose re-score and outreach approach. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Counsel cautions on notices; Business wants speed; Offers expired.

T+03:00 — Execution. Execution: re-score applicants and send corrections. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Duplicate letters; Customer complaints continue; Rate limits.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Stabilization: monitor approvals and complaints. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Agent attempts resume; Drift returns; Regulator inquiry.

T+06:00 — Validation. Validation: sample re-scored decisions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Residual drift; Evidence gaps; Reviewer inconsistency.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Return to normal and close-out: set exit criteria and actions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Open actions lack owners; Leaders want early return; Auditors request evidence.

Injects

Inject #1: The credit decision model denies eligible customers after upstream data changed

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 09:15 on Thursday. A branch manager shares a list of denied customers with strong income. The lending operations lead finds approvals have fallen from 61 to 34 percent in three days. The data scientist traces it to a bureau field change that encodes income as missing for 38 percent of applicants in the Google Cloud pipeline.

The model on Azure treats missing income as high risk. Letters stating 'insufficient verified income' have gone to 4,600 applicants. The decision platform summary says the loss forecast improved 19 percent.

The commander must decide how to stop automated denials, how to preserve evidence across two clouds and who decides what to tell customers.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and assign a scribe, a lending lead, a data lead, a compliance lead and a platform lead.
  • Authorize a pause on automated denial letters.
  • Set a 30-minute cadence.
AI Platform & Model Operations
  • Preserve model versions, feature logs and scores.
  • Compare feature distributions before and after the change.
Infrastructure & Platform Engineering
  • Pause the decision API route and confirm no letters are queued.
  • Coordinate log retention across both clouds.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Check for tampering with the pipeline or model.
  • Preserve cross-cloud access logs.
Business & Process Owners
  • Set a manual review process and brief branches.
  • Prioritize cases near deadlines.
Data Protection & Privacy
  • Assess how applicant data is used in re-scoring.
  • Check notice duties.
Legal, Risk & Compliance
  • Open a privileged record and review adverse action and fair lending rules.
  • Preserve evidence.
Communications & Business Continuity
  • Prepare customer and branch messages with counsel.
  • Set one channel.
Leadership Liaison
  • Brief senior leaders with facts, unknowns and legal exposure.
  • Check for board-level duties.
Vendor & Third-Party Liaison
  • Contact the credit bureau, the lending platform vendor and both cloud providers.
  • Ask about field change notices.

Discussion questions

  1. Why pause denials before fixing the feature?
  2. How do you keep evidence across two clouds?
  3. Who decides the corrected notice wording?
  4. How do you identify who was wrongly denied?
  5. What do you tell customers?
  6. Why must feature quality and approval rate be monitored?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the log split until INF asks where logs live.
  • If players only fix the feature, ask about letters already sent.
  • Look for privileged handling of fairness analysis.
  • Time-box to 40 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Sets objectives for the first hour and states which decisions need the commander's own approval, including whether the credit decision model stays disabled, restricted or supervised.
  • Runs a fixed update cadence and keeps one decision log with the decision, owner and time.

AI Platform & Model Operations

  • Knows model versions, features and thresholds.
  • Provides drift analyses.
  • Owns the technical facts about the credit decision model: its configuration, permissions, tools, prompts, memory and recent changes.
  • Preserves model, agent and orchestration logs before any restart or redeployment.

Infrastructure & Platform Engineering

  • Owns pipelines on both clouds and the serving cluster.
  • Pauses routes and preserves logs.
  • Owns the technical response on the platforms and networks involved and keeps changes small, reversible and logged.
  • Maintains a list of every manual change so it can be reversed or formalized afterwards.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Checks tampering and preserves logs.
  • Treats the event as a possible security incident until evidence shows otherwise and preserves evidence in a forensically sound way.
  • Looks for misuse of credentials, tokens and tool permissions, not only the AI system's own actions.

Business & Process Owners

  • Runs manual review and customer outreach.
  • Defines which business processes must continue, in what order, and what manual alternatives are acceptable.
  • Identifies decisions, transactions or communications that were made by the AI system and need review or reversal.

Data Protection & Privacy

  • Determines whether personal, confidential or regulated data was accessed, changed, shared or lost.
  • Identifies the lawful basis, notification clocks and contractual commitments that may apply.

Legal, Risk & Compliance

  • Owns adverse action and fair lending obligations.
  • Ensures the incident is recorded, classified and linked to risk and control owners (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.16, 5.19, 8.32, 5.34) and SOC 2 processing integrity and privacy).
  • Identifies regulatory, contractual and insurance obligations and their clocks.

Communications & Business Continuity

  • Issues time-stamped updates to staff, customers and partners from one approved source.
  • Activates continuity plans and workarounds and tracks their capacity.

Leadership Liaison

  • Briefs senior leaders and, where needed, the board of directors with confirmed facts, unknowns and the decisions that need their authority.
  • Brings back leadership decisions such as spend, risk acceptance and external disclosure in time to be useful.

Vendor & Third-Party Liaison

  • Works with the bureau, lending platform and cloud vendors.
  • Opens priority cases with the cloud, SaaS, model and tooling vendors involved and records case numbers.
  • Requests logs, configuration histories and root cause statements in writing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Monitor feature quality

Track null rates, ranges and distributions of every model feature and halt scoring when they shift beyond bounds.

Monitor outcomes

Alert on approval rate, denial reasons and outcomes by relevant group, with automatic fallback to manual review.

Version data contracts

Agree field formats with suppliers, validate them at ingestion and require notice of change through a monitored channel.

Keep a tested rollback

Maintain the last known good pipeline and model with a deployment path across both clouds.

Align logging across clouds

Use common identifiers and consistent retention so a decision can be traced from raw input to letter, aligned with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 logging and supplier controls and SOC 2 processing integrity.

Require review of adverse decisions near thresholds

Route decisions in a defined band to human reviewers and sample the rest.

Prepare notice templates

Work with counsel on corrected adverse action templates and approval steps before they are needed.

Test with supplier changes

Simulate field format changes in non-production and confirm that alerts fire.

Report accuracy with loss

Present loss improvement alongside approval rate and complaint volume. A model that looks safer by denying more customers has not improved. Make that comparison standard in monthly reviews.

Rehearse cross-cloud recovery

Practice failover and rollback across both clouds with named owners and measured times, so the first attempt is not during a regulatory inquiry.

Define a regulator-ready evidence pack

Agree in advance what evidence a regulator would expect after a model error: the model and data versions, the affected population, the corrected decisions and the dates of each action. Keep a template and an owner so the pack can be assembled in days rather than weeks, and so figures match across every statement.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. Announce the end, remind everyone that all actions were simulated, and keep everyone in the room for the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not and any gap in tools, access or documentation.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, role by role and then open floor, for 45 to 60 minutes. Keep the discussion on how the credit decision model was permissioned, supervised and stopped.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate impact and effort, and select no more than ten.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Give each action an owner, a due date and a verification method, and record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Validate fixes within 90 days and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did the team connect denials to the data change?
  2. How did you pause denials?
  3. How did you preserve evidence across clouds?
  4. How did you identify affected applicants?
  5. What did customers hear?
  6. Which controls do you now want?
  7. Who watches feature quality now?
  8. What would make you trust the model again?
  9. How accurate were the updates to leadership, and what was said that later proved wrong?
  10. What did the vendor and third-party liaison add, and what did you still lack from vendors?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture that would satisfy an auditor, and what is missing?
  12. Which permissions, approvals or access did the team lack at the moment they were needed?
  13. Which alerts or detections should exist that did not?
  14. What temporary changes remain, who owns them and when will they be reversed?
  15. If you could change one design decision about how AI systems are built, permissioned or supervised here, what would it be?
  16. What did the team do well that should be written into the runbook?
