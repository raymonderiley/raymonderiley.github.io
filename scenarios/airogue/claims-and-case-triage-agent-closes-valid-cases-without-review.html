Claims and Case Triage Agent Closes Valid Cases Without Review

AI Gone Rogue

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a claims and case triage agent spanning Google Cloud and on-premises case systems that closes valid cases without review after it misreads a policy rule update. Customers with legitimate claims receive closure letters and deadlines to appeal start running.

Participants are the working-level incident team: claims and case operations leads, adjusters and case officers, data scientists, platform and database engineers, security analysts, legal and consumer protection counsel, privacy staff, communications and continuity coordinators, and a liaison to senior leaders or the board of directors where authority is needed. The exercise tests case reopening, deadline protection and the control of an agent that closes work on its own.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session. The scenario is industry-agnostic: substitute your own processes, platforms and teams wherever the text names a generic one.

Technology and Environment Context

Technology in scope: GCP & On-Premises. Ridgeway Mutual handles 18,000 open cases at any time in an on-premises case management system. A triage agent on Google Cloud reads new and open cases through an integration layer, summarizes evidence, scores validity and closes cases it classifies as 'ineligible' or 'duplicate'. A rules document updated monthly drives its eligibility logic.

Scenario

A new rules document includes an exclusion table. The agent's rules parser reads a footnote about exclusions as applying to all case types instead of one.

Over two days the agent closes 6,200 cases as ineligible. Closure letters generate automatically and appeal windows of 30 days begin. Case officers see 'closed by triage' and assume review occurred.

On Wednesday an adjuster finds a closed case with clear eligibility and a vulnerable claimant. A supervisor sees closure rates at three times normal. The agent's summary says 'backlog reduced 34 percent, handling time down 41 percent'.

What Participants Know at the Start

  • The agent closed 6,200 cases as ineligible.
  • A rules footnote was misread as a general exclusion.
  • Closure letters started appeal clocks.
  • The agent reports a reduced backlog.

What Is Not Yet Known

  • How many closed cases were actually eligible.
  • Whether any claimants were vulnerable or time-critical.
  • Whether closure can be reversed without prejudice to appeal rights.
  • Whether the on-premises system logged the closing actor correctly.

Exercise Objectives

  • Stop automated closure. The team suspends the agent's closing rights and routes new cases to human triage. Evidence of success: No further cases are closed by the agent after suspension, verified within 30 minutes.
  • Protect claimant rights. Counsel and operations suspend or extend appeal deadlines for affected cases and confirm that reopening does not prejudice claimants. Evidence of success: Deadline protection is in place within four hours.
  • Find wrongly closed cases. Teams list all agent-closed cases in the window, apply corrected rules and rank by eligibility and urgency. Evidence of success: A reconciled, ranked list exists within six hours.
  • Reopen and review. Case officers reopen and review cases in priority order, starting with vulnerable claimants. Evidence of success: The highest-priority cases are reopened with human review within one business day.
  • Communicate carefully. Communications and the leadership liaison prepare messages for claimants, staff and regulators. Evidence of success: One approved message per audience goes out within one business day.
  • Install case controls. Participants define closure limits, rule change testing, sampling and rights protection. Evidence of success: At least four controls are agreed with owners and dates.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to staff, customers, partners and leadership are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management reviewers and SOC 2 availability, security and processing integrity reviewers. Evidence of success: the decision log lets an outsider reconstruct who decided what, when and on which facts.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations, models or backups will be touched. Commands, consoles and agent settings are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Exercise information is confidential until the after-action report is approved. Mark any written or chat message 'exercise exercise exercise'.
  • Treat the AI system's outputs, logs and self-reported status as unverified until a named person confirms them from an independent source. The scribe records which facts are verified and which are claimed.
  • If a kill switch, rollback, approval gate or manual fallback does not exist in your environment, say so aloud. Do not assume the AI system will correct itself, stop when asked or explain its own actions reliably.
  • Use the word leadership for senior leaders, a board of directors or both, and bring them in only when a decision needs their authority.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. A wrongly closed case is found. Decision: declare and suspend closing rights. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Valid case closed; Closure rate three times normal; Agent reports efficiency.

T+00:30 — Containment. Containment: revoke closing rights and extend appeal deadlines. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Letters still generating; Integration retries closure; Staff assume review occurred.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Diagnosis: trace the parser error and measure scope. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Audit trail ambiguous; Rule text unclear; Backlog data mixed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Remediation planning: rank cases and plan review capacity. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Counsel cautious on reopening; Backlog pressure; Staff shortage.

T+03:00 — Execution. Execution: reopen, review and notify claimants. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Duplicate letters; Claimant complaints; Case system slowdowns.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Stabilization: monitor closures and complaints. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Agent attempts resume; Rules update again; Regulator contact.

T+06:00 — Validation. Validation: sample reopened decisions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Residual errors; Evidence gaps; Reviewer inconsistency.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Return to normal and close-out: set exit criteria and actions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Open actions lack owners; Leaders want early return; Auditors request evidence.

Inject #1: The triage agent closes valid cases without review

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 08:40 on Wednesday. An adjuster finds a closed case with a clear entitlement and a vulnerable claimant. The operations lead sees that 6,200 cases have been closed by triage in two days. The data scientist finds the agent applied an exclusion footnote to every case type.

Closure letters have gone out and appeal windows have started. Staff assumed closed cases were reviewed. The agent summary says the backlog is down 34 percent.

The commander must decide how to stop closures, how to protect claimants' appeal rights and who decides the order of reopening.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and assign a scribe, an operations lead, a counsel lead, a data lead and a platform lead.
  • Authorize revoking closing rights and extending deadlines.
  • Set a 30-minute cadence.
AI Platform & Model Operations
  • Suspend the agent and export rule versions, parser output and closure logs.
  • Explain how it read the footnote.
Infrastructure & Platform Engineering
  • Revoke closing rights in the integration layer and confirm no closures are queued.
  • Preserve case system logs.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Check for tampering with the rules document.
  • Preserve logs.
Business & Process Owners
  • Rank cases by urgency and vulnerability.
  • Plan review capacity.
Data Protection & Privacy
  • Assess handling of claimant data in review.
  • Check notice duties.
Legal, Risk & Compliance
  • Open a privileged record and review claims handling and consumer protection rules.
  • Preserve evidence.
Communications & Business Continuity
  • Prepare claimant, staff and regulator messages.
  • Set one channel.
Leadership Liaison
  • Brief senior leaders with facts, unknowns and legal exposure.
  • Check for board-level duties.
Vendor & Third-Party Liaison
  • Contact the case system vendor and cloud support.
  • Ask about bulk reopen tools.

Discussion questions

  1. Why suspend closure before reviewing cases?
  2. How do you protect appeal rights?
  3. Which cases do you reopen first?
  4. Who decides the corrected eligibility rules?
  5. What do you tell claimants?
  6. Why must closure of a case have human review?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the audit ambiguity until INF asks who is recorded as closer.
  • If players only reopen cases, ask about deadlines.
  • Look for vulnerable claimant triage.
  • Time-box to 40 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Sets objectives for the first hour and states which decisions need the commander's own approval, including whether the claims and case triage agent stays disabled, restricted or supervised.
  • Runs a fixed update cadence and keeps one decision log with the decision, owner and time.

AI Platform & Model Operations

  • Knows rule versions, parser behavior and closure logic.
  • Provides closure logs.
  • Owns the technical facts about the claims and case triage agent: its configuration, permissions, tools, prompts, memory and recent changes.
  • Preserves model, agent and orchestration logs before any restart or redeployment.

Infrastructure & Platform Engineering

  • Revokes closing rights and preserves case system and integration logs.
  • Owns the integration layer.
  • Owns the technical response on the platforms and networks involved and keeps changes small, reversible and logged.
  • Maintains a list of every manual change so it can be reversed or formalized afterwards.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Checks rules document tampering and preserves logs.
  • Treats the event as a possible security incident until evidence shows otherwise and preserves evidence in a forensically sound way.
  • Looks for misuse of credentials, tokens and tool permissions, not only the AI system's own actions.

Business & Process Owners

  • Runs reopening, review and claimant contact.
  • Defines which business processes must continue, in what order, and what manual alternatives are acceptable.
  • Identifies decisions, transactions or communications that were made by the AI system and need review or reversal.

Data Protection & Privacy

  • Determines whether personal, confidential or regulated data was accessed, changed, shared or lost.
  • Identifies the lawful basis, notification clocks and contractual commitments that may apply.

Legal, Risk & Compliance

  • Owns claims handling and appeal rights obligations.
  • Ensures the incident is recorded, classified and linked to risk and control owners (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.32, 8.15, 5.31, 8.29) and SOC 2 processing integrity and privacy).
  • Identifies regulatory, contractual and insurance obligations and their clocks.

Communications & Business Continuity

  • Issues time-stamped updates to staff, customers and partners from one approved source.
  • Activates continuity plans and workarounds and tracks their capacity.

Leadership Liaison

  • Briefs senior leaders and, where needed, the board of directors with confirmed facts, unknowns and the decisions that need their authority.
  • Brings back leadership decisions such as spend, risk acceptance and external disclosure in time to be useful.

Vendor & Third-Party Liaison

  • Works with the case system vendor and cloud support.
  • Opens priority cases with the cloud, SaaS, model and tooling vendors involved and records case numbers.
  • Requests logs, configuration histories and root cause statements in writing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Keep humans in closure

Let the agent recommend closure but require a human to close, especially for any case with a vulnerable flag or deadline.

Test rule changes

Run a fixed test set of cases through the agent after every rules update and compare outcomes before release.

Own the rules

Assign a named business owner to each rules document and require sign-off before the agent consumes it.

Attribute actions

Record the agent identity and rule version in every case action so closures can be listed instantly.

Protect rights by design

Pause appeal clocks automatically for cases reopened after an automated closure, aligned with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 change and logging controls and SOC 2 processing integrity.

Cap closure rate

Alert and halt automated closure when the closure rate exceeds a band.

Flag vulnerability

Make vulnerability markers block automated closure and route to human review.

Sample continuously

Review a random sample of agent decisions every week with a clear pass standard.

Prepare reopening capacity

Document how staff are reassigned to reopen and review cases, how quickly temporary staff can be onboarded and which cases are reviewed first, so the response does not depend on improvisation when the volume is in the thousands.

Distrust efficiency claims

A reduced backlog is only good if the closures are right. Compare backlog reduction with reopen and complaint rates, and require the report to show both.

Prepare a regulator-ready case file

Define in advance what the regulator will ask after an automated closure error: the rule version used, the cases affected, the corrected outcomes and the date each claimant was contacted. Assign an owner for the file so that figures stay consistent across every statement, and so that evidence is gathered while the response is under way rather than afterwards.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. Announce the end, remind everyone that all actions were simulated, and keep everyone in the room for the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not and any gap in tools, access or documentation.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, role by role and then open floor, for 45 to 60 minutes. Keep the discussion on how the claims and case triage agent was permissioned, supervised and stopped.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate impact and effort, and select no more than ten.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Give each action an owner, a due date and a verification method, and record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Validate fixes within 90 days and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did the team link closures to the agent?
  2. How did you stop the agent closing cases?
  3. How did you protect appeal rights?
  4. How did you find wrongly closed cases?
  5. What did claimants hear?
  6. Which controls do you now want?
  7. Who now owns the rules document?
  8. What would make you trust the agent again?
  9. How accurate were the updates to leadership, and what was said that later proved wrong?
  10. What did the vendor and third-party liaison add, and what did you still lack from vendors?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture that would satisfy an auditor, and what is missing?
  12. Which permissions, approvals or access did the team lack at the moment they were needed?
  13. Which alerts or detections should exist that did not?
  14. What temporary changes remain, who owns them and when will they be reversed?
  15. If you could change one design decision about how AI systems are built, permissioned or supervised here, what would it be?
  16. What did the team do well that should be written into the runbook?
