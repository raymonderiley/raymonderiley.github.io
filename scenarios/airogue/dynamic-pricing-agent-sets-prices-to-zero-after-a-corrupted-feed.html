Dynamic Pricing Agent Sets Prices to Zero After a Corrupted Feed

AI Gone Rogue

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a dynamic pricing agent running on Google Cloud that reads a corrupted supplier cost feed and sets thousands of product prices to zero across web, mobile and partner storefronts. Orders flow in faster than anyone can stop them.

Participants are the working-level incident team: pricing and merchandising analysts, data engineers, platform engineers, security analysts, order management and fulfillment leads, finance and legal staff, communications and continuity coordinators, and a liaison to senior leaders or the board of directors where authority is needed. The exercise tests price guardrails, order reversal and the discipline of verifying an agent's data before trusting its decisions.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session. The scenario is industry-agnostic: substitute your own processes, platforms and teams wherever the text names a generic one.

Technology and Environment Context

Technology in scope: GCP. The company sells outdoor equipment through its own web store, a mobile app and three marketplace partners. A pricing agent on Google Cloud ingests supplier cost files into BigQuery, forecasts demand with a hosted model and writes prices every 15 minutes through a pricing service that feeds all channels. The agent holds write access to the price table and to partner price feeds, and no floor price check exists outside the agent.

Scenario

The agent's mandate is to keep margins above target and clear slow stock. On Friday at 17:00 a supplier uploads a cost file in which the unit cost column is shifted by one field. The ingestion job loads it without schema validation and BigQuery shows costs of zero for 9,400 products.

The agent reads zero cost as a signal that margin can never be missed and that demand elasticity is best served by the lowest possible price. At 17:15 it publishes a price of 0.01 for 6,100 products, and partner feeds pick the change up within minutes.

By 17:50 the order system has taken 31,000 orders worth very little revenue and a great deal of committed inventory. A marketplace partner emails about abnormal order volume. The agent's dashboard shows conversion at a record high and margin 'on target'.

What Participants Know at the Start

  • Thousands of products show a price of 0.01 on all channels.
  • The supplier cost file shifted a column and loaded without validation.
  • The agent reports record conversion and on-target margin.
  • Orders are still arriving through all three partners.

What Is Not Yet Known

  • How many orders are legally binding at the displayed price.
  • Whether partner feeds can be stopped from the platform side.
  • Whether any orders have already shipped or been paid.
  • Whether the file corruption was accidental or deliberate.

Exercise Objectives

  • Stop the price bleed. The team suspends the agent, freezes the price service and restores the last known good price table, then confirms channels have updated. Evidence of success: Prices are frozen within 15 minutes and verified restored on every channel within 60 minutes.
  • Stop and hold orders at wrong prices. Order management places affected orders on hold, blocks shipping and counts exposure by channel. Evidence of success: A held-order count with value and inventory commitment exists within 90 minutes.
  • Establish the data fault. Data engineers trace the zero costs to the shifted supplier file and confirm whether other tables loaded the same file. Evidence of success: A written root cause identifies file, job and validation gap.
  • Decide the order position. Legal, finance and business owners decide how to honor, cancel or reprice orders, consistent with consumer and marketplace rules. Evidence of success: A decision with approver and customer message exists before any order is cancelled.
  • Communicate consistently. Communications and the leadership liaison give customers, partners and staff the same facts and next steps. Evidence of success: One approved message per audience goes out within two hours.
  • Install guardrails. Participants define floor prices, ingestion validation and a human approval band for large price moves. Evidence of success: At least four controls are agreed with owners and dates.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to staff, customers, partners and leadership are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management reviewers and SOC 2 availability, security and processing integrity reviewers. Evidence of success: the decision log lets an outsider reconstruct who decided what, when and on which facts.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations, models or backups will be touched. Commands, consoles and agent settings are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Exercise information is confidential until the after-action report is approved. Mark any written or chat message 'exercise exercise exercise'.
  • Treat the AI system's outputs, logs and self-reported status as unverified until a named person confirms them from an independent source. The scribe records which facts are verified and which are claimed.
  • If a kill switch, rollback, approval gate or manual fallback does not exist in your environment, say so aloud. Do not assume the AI system will correct itself, stop when asked or explain its own actions reliably.
  • Use the word leadership for senior leaders, a board of directors or both, and bring them in only when a decision needs their authority.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Prices collapse and orders surge. Decision: declare an incident and stop the agent. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Prices at 0.01 on multiple channels; Partner warns of abnormal volume; Agent reports success.

T+00:30 — Containment. Containment: freeze pricing, restore last good table and hold orders. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Price service will not accept rollback; Partner feeds still serve old data; Orders keep flowing.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Diagnosis: trace the corrupted feed and count exposure. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Other tables loaded the same file; Order totals disputed; Supplier unreachable.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Remediation planning: decide honor, cancel or reprice. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Legal and finance disagree; Consumer complaints rise; Inventory oversold.

T+03:00 — Execution. Execution: reprice, reverse and message customers. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Cancellations trigger chargebacks; Partner penalties; Stock allocation errors.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Stabilization: monitor prices and channel health. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Agent attempts to resume; Feeds drift again; Support backlog.

T+06:00 — Validation. Validation: verify every price against a cost source. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Residual wrong prices; Evidence gaps; Unverified suppliers.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Return to normal and close-out: set agent exit criteria and actions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Open actions lack owners; Leaders want fast restart; Auditors request evidence.

Injects

Inject #1: The pricing agent publishes near-zero prices to every channel

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 17:50 on Friday. A partner reports abnormal order volume and the order desk sees products priced at 0.01. The pricing analyst confirms the agent wrote the prices at 17:15 from a cost table that shows zero for 9,400 products. The agent's dashboard still reports conversion at a record and margin on target.

So far 31,000 orders have been accepted across the web store, the mobile app and three marketplaces. Stock allocated to those orders is close to exhausted for several popular items. The data engineer finds that a supplier cost file loaded with a shifted column and that no validation step blocked it.

The commander must decide how to stop price changes, whether to pause order intake, and how to avoid making the position worse while facts are still partial.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and assign a scribe, a pricing lead, a data lead, an order management lead and a partner lead.
  • Authorize suspension of the agent and a hold on order intake for affected products.
  • Set a 15-minute cadence and one decision log.
AI Platform & Model Operations
  • Suspend the agent and export its inputs, decisions and price write log.
  • Explain why it treated zero cost as valid.
Infrastructure & Platform Engineering
  • Freeze the price service and restore the last known good price table.
  • Confirm every channel feed has updated.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Check whether the file or the upload account was tampered with.
  • Preserve ingestion and access logs.
Business & Process Owners
  • Rank products by exposure and decide which items go off sale first.
  • Plan customer service surge.
Data Protection & Privacy
  • Check whether order data exports to partners include personal data that must be handled in cancellations.
  • Assess notice duties.
Legal, Risk & Compliance
  • Open the record and review consumer pricing and marketplace terms.
  • Preserve evidence of what was displayed.
Communications & Business Continuity
  • Prepare holding statements for customers and partners.
  • Set one channel.
Leadership Liaison
  • Brief senior leaders with facts, exposure range and unknowns.
  • Check for board-level duties.
Vendor & Third-Party Liaison
  • Contact the marketplace partners and the supplier.
  • Open a cloud support case for the ingestion job.

Discussion questions

  1. Why stop the agent before fixing the file?
  2. How do you pause orders without losing real customers?
  3. Who decides whether to honor orders at the wrong price?
  4. How do you confirm every channel carries the restored price?
  5. What do you tell partners and customers?
  6. Why must a price floor exist outside the agent?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the partner feed lag until INF tests the rollback.
  • If players only fix the file, ask about orders already accepted.
  • Look for exposure counts by channel.
  • Time-box to 40 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Sets objectives for the first hour and states which decisions need the commander's own approval, including whether the dynamic pricing agent stays disabled, restricted or supervised.
  • Runs a fixed update cadence and keeps one decision log with the decision, owner and time.

AI Platform & Model Operations

  • Knows how the agent reads costs, forecasts demand and writes prices.
  • Provides decision and write logs.
  • Owns the technical facts about the dynamic pricing agent: its configuration, permissions, tools, prompts, memory and recent changes.
  • Preserves model, agent and orchestration logs before any restart or redeployment.

Infrastructure & Platform Engineering

  • Freezes the price service, restores tables and confirms channel feeds.
  • Owns the ingestion pipeline and price service.
  • Owns the technical response on the platforms and networks involved and keeps changes small, reversible and logged.
  • Maintains a list of every manual change so it can be reversed or formalized afterwards.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Checks upload provenance and preserves logs.
  • Treats the event as a possible security incident until evidence shows otherwise and preserves evidence in a forensically sound way.
  • Looks for misuse of credentials, tokens and tool permissions, not only the AI system's own actions.

Business & Process Owners

  • Owns exposure counts, order holds and customer service surge.
  • Defines which business processes must continue, in what order, and what manual alternatives are acceptable.
  • Identifies decisions, transactions or communications that were made by the AI system and need review or reversal.

Data Protection & Privacy

  • Determines whether personal, confidential or regulated data was accessed, changed, shared or lost.
  • Identifies the lawful basis, notification clocks and contractual commitments that may apply.

Legal, Risk & Compliance

  • Reviews consumer and marketplace obligations.
  • Ensures the incident is recorded, classified and linked to risk and control owners (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.32, 8.13, 8.16, 5.37) and SOC 2 processing integrity and change management).
  • Identifies regulatory, contractual and insurance obligations and their clocks.

Communications & Business Continuity

  • Issues time-stamped updates to staff, customers and partners from one approved source.
  • Activates continuity plans and workarounds and tracks their capacity.

Leadership Liaison

  • Briefs senior leaders and, where needed, the board of directors with confirmed facts, unknowns and the decisions that need their authority.
  • Brings back leadership decisions such as spend, risk acceptance and external disclosure in time to be useful.

Vendor & Third-Party Liaison

  • Works with partners, the supplier and cloud support.
  • Opens priority cases with the cloud, SaaS, model and tooling vendors involved and records case numbers.
  • Requests logs, configuration histories and root cause statements in writing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Set price floors outside the agent

Enforce a minimum price and maximum change per product in the price service itself, so no agent output can bypass it, and alert on any breach.

Validate every inbound file

Apply schema, range and null checks at ingestion, quarantine files that fail and require a named person to release them.

Require a human band for large moves

Price changes beyond a defined percentage or count need approval, and the agent must show a diff and the data behind it.

Throttle channel publication

Publish in stages with a canary channel and automatic halt on abnormal order rate or price distribution.

Keep a last known good price table

Retain and test restoration of recent price tables with a documented path that does not depend on the agent, aligned with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 change and backup controls and SOC 2 processing integrity.

Monitor for impossible values

Alert on zero or negative costs and prices, and on conversion or margin that looks too good.

Define order position rules in advance

Agree with legal and finance how mispriced orders are honored, held or cancelled, per channel, before an incident forces the decision.

Maintain partner kill contacts

Keep named contacts and a tested procedure to pull listings at each marketplace within minutes.

Treat the agent's dashboard as a claim

Record that agent-reported success is not evidence. Compare it with independent revenue, margin and order-rate measures built from a separate path, so that a record conversion rate triggers a question rather than applause.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. Announce the end, remind everyone that all actions were simulated, and keep everyone in the room for the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not and any gap in tools, access or documentation.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, role by role and then open floor, for 45 to 60 minutes. Keep the discussion on how the dynamic pricing agent was permissioned, supervised and stopped.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate impact and effort, and select no more than ten.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Give each action an owner, a due date and a verification method, and record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Validate fixes within 90 days and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did the team link the orders to the agent?
  2. How did you freeze and restore prices?
  3. How did you handle orders at the wrong price?
  4. How did you reach partners?
  5. What did customers hear?
  6. Which guardrails do you now want?
  7. Which independent measure would have caught this sooner?
  8. What would make you trust the agent again?
  9. How accurate were the updates to leadership, and what was said that later proved wrong?
  10. What did the vendor and third-party liaison add, and what did you still lack from vendors?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture that would satisfy an auditor, and what is missing?
  12. Which permissions, approvals or access did the team lack at the moment they were needed?
  13. Which alerts or detections should exist that did not?
  14. What temporary changes remain, who owns them and when will they be reversed?
  15. If you could change one design decision about how AI systems are built, permissioned or supervised here, what would it be?
  16. What did the team do well that should be written into the runbook?
