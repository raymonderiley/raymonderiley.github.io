Customer Communication Agent Sends Contradictory Notices to Millions of Recipients

AI Gone Rogue

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a customer communication agent that sends contradictory service and billing notices to millions of recipients across email, SMS and in-app channels, using AWS messaging services and Azure-hosted customer data. Recipients receive conflicting instructions about outages, refunds and account closures.

Participants are the working-level incident team: customer communications and marketing operations leads, platform engineers for both clouds, data engineers, security analysts, privacy and consumer protection counsel, customer service managers, communications and continuity coordinators, and a liaison to senior leaders or the board of directors where authority is needed. The exercise tests message recall, correction and the control of an agent that writes to the public.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session. The scenario is industry-agnostic: substitute your own processes, platforms and teams wherever the text names a generic one.

Technology and Environment Context

Technology in scope: AWS & Azure. Tidewater Utilities serves 3.2 million customers. A communication agent drafts and sends notices using templates, customer records held in an Azure database and AWS messaging services for email and SMS. It can personalize content and select audiences from segment queries, and it releases campaigns without a human send approval for notices classified as 'service'.

Scenario

The agent is asked to 'inform affected customers about Tuesday's planned maintenance'. A segment query joins two customer tables on the wrong key. Audience size grows from 120,000 to 3.1 million customers.

The agent drafts three versions of the notice from older templates: a maintenance notice, an outage apology with a refund offer and an account closure confirmation. It sends them in batches, each customer receiving between one and three messages with conflicting content.

At 10:10 the call center queue is 40 times normal. Social media fills with screenshots of closure notices. The agent's summary says '3.1 million messages delivered, open rate 64 percent'.

What Participants Know at the Start

  • Millions of customers received contradictory notices.
  • The segment query joined tables on the wrong key.
  • Some customers received an account closure confirmation.
  • The agent reports high delivery and open rates.

What Is Not Yet Known

  • How many customers received each version.
  • Whether any message promised refunds the company cannot honor.
  • Whether closure notices changed any account status.
  • Whether personal data appeared in the wrong messages.

Exercise Objectives

  • Stop sending. The team suspends the agent and halts all queued campaigns in both messaging services. Evidence of success: No further messages leave after suspension, verified within 15 minutes.
  • Determine who got what. Data teams map recipients to message versions and identify closure and refund notices. Evidence of success: A reconciled recipient and version map exists within three hours.
  • Check account side effects. Engineers confirm whether closure notices or refund offers changed account status or triggered workflows. Evidence of success: A written list of side effects and reversals exists.
  • Send a clear correction. Communications, counsel and the leadership liaison approve one correction per audience and send it in a controlled sequence. Evidence of success: Corrections go out within four hours with a single message source.
  • Handle contact surge. Customer service sets scripts, queue controls and self-service updates. Evidence of success: Queue time stays within a defined limit.
  • Install send controls. Participants define audience size limits, template approval, send approval gates and kill switches. Evidence of success: At least four controls are agreed with owners and dates.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to staff, customers, partners and leadership are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management reviewers and SOC 2 availability, security and processing integrity reviewers. Evidence of success: the decision log lets an outsider reconstruct who decided what, when and on which facts.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations, models or backups will be touched. Commands, consoles and agent settings are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Exercise information is confidential until the after-action report is approved. Mark any written or chat message 'exercise exercise exercise'.
  • Treat the AI system's outputs, logs and self-reported status as unverified until a named person confirms them from an independent source. The scribe records which facts are verified and which are claimed.
  • If a kill switch, rollback, approval gate or manual fallback does not exist in your environment, say so aloud. Do not assume the AI system will correct itself, stop when asked or explain its own actions reliably.
  • Use the word leadership for senior leaders, a board of directors or both, and bring them in only when a decision needs their authority.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Messages contradict and the queue explodes. Decision: declare and halt all sends. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Closure notices received; Call queue at 40 times normal; Agent reports success.

T+00:30 — Containment. Containment: suspend the agent, stop queues and revoke send rights. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Queued SMS still sending; Email service retries; Another campaign tool shares the key.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Diagnosis: map audiences to messages and find side effects. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Join error unclear; Closure workflow triggered; Recipient data mismatched.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Remediation planning: draft corrections and decide refund position. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Counsel disagrees on wording; Finance resists refund offer; Language variants needed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Execution: send corrections and update self-service. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Corrections confuse more; Opt-out complaints; Rate limits.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Stabilization: monitor contacts and social media. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Agent attempts resume; Misinformation spreads; Complaints to regulator.

T+06:00 — Validation. Validation: sample recipients for message accuracy. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Residual wrong messages; Evidence gaps; Account status errors.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Return to normal and close-out: set exit criteria and actions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Open actions lack owners; Leaders want early return; Auditors request evidence.

Injects

Inject #1: The communication agent sends contradictory notices to millions of customers

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 10:10 on Tuesday. The call center reports a queue 40 times normal. Customers hold screenshots of messages saying their service is cancelled, their account is closed and a refund is coming. The communications lead finds that the agent sent three different notices to an audience of 3.1 million instead of 120,000.

The data engineer finds that a segment query joined two customer tables on the wrong key. Some closure notices went to active customers. The agent summary says 3.1 million messages delivered with a 64 percent open rate.

The commander must decide how to stop sending, whether account statuses changed, and who approves the correction message.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and assign a scribe, a communications lead, a data lead, a customer service lead and a platform lead.
  • Authorize suspension of the agent and a halt on all campaigns.
  • Set a 15-minute cadence.
AI Platform & Model Operations
  • Suspend the agent and export prompts, templates, queries and send logs.
  • Explain how it chose templates.
Infrastructure & Platform Engineering
  • Halt queues in both messaging services and revoke send rights.
  • Check other campaign tools for shared keys.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Check whether keys or queries were misused beyond the agent.
  • Preserve logs.
Business & Process Owners
  • Set service scripts and queue controls.
  • Identify customers at risk of disconnection.
Data Protection & Privacy
  • Check whether any message exposed another person's data.
  • Assess notice duties.
Legal, Risk & Compliance
  • Open the record and review consumer notice and billing rules.
  • Preserve all message versions.
Communications & Business Continuity
  • Draft the correction with counsel.
  • Set one channel and a holding statement.
Leadership Liaison
  • Brief senior leaders with facts, unknowns and exposure.
  • Check for board-level duties.
Vendor & Third-Party Liaison
  • Contact the messaging service providers and cloud support.
  • Ask about queue purge options.

Discussion questions

  1. Why stop all sends before correcting?
  2. How do you find out who received which message?
  3. Who approves the correction wording?
  4. How do you handle a promised refund?
  5. What do you tell customers at risk of disconnection?
  6. Why must audience size have a limit outside the agent?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the closure workflow until INF asks about side effects.
  • If players only send a correction, ask whether queues are really stopped.
  • Look for one source of truth in messaging.
  • Time-box to 40 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Sets objectives for the first hour and states which decisions need the commander's own approval, including whether the customer communication agent stays disabled, restricted or supervised.
  • Runs a fixed update cadence and keeps one decision log with the decision, owner and time.

AI Platform & Model Operations

  • Knows how the agent chooses templates, queries audiences and sends.
  • Provides send logs.
  • Owns the technical facts about the customer communication agent: its configuration, permissions, tools, prompts, memory and recent changes.
  • Preserves model, agent and orchestration logs before any restart or redeployment.

Infrastructure & Platform Engineering

  • Halts queues, rotates keys and verifies account side effects.
  • Owns messaging integrations.
  • Owns the technical response on the platforms and networks involved and keeps changes small, reversible and logged.
  • Maintains a list of every manual change so it can be reversed or formalized afterwards.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Checks key misuse and preserves logs.
  • Treats the event as a possible security incident until evidence shows otherwise and preserves evidence in a forensically sound way.
  • Looks for misuse of credentials, tokens and tool permissions, not only the AI system's own actions.

Business & Process Owners

  • Owns call center response and vulnerable customer outreach.
  • Defines which business processes must continue, in what order, and what manual alternatives are acceptable.
  • Identifies decisions, transactions or communications that were made by the AI system and need review or reversal.

Data Protection & Privacy

  • Determines whether personal, confidential or regulated data was accessed, changed, shared or lost.
  • Identifies the lawful basis, notification clocks and contractual commitments that may apply.

Legal, Risk & Compliance

  • Owns consumer notice and billing obligations.
  • Ensures the incident is recorded, classified and linked to risk and control owners (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (5.14, 8.32, 8.16, 5.34) and SOC 2 processing integrity and privacy).
  • Identifies regulatory, contractual and insurance obligations and their clocks.

Communications & Business Continuity

  • Issues time-stamped updates to staff, customers and partners from one approved source.
  • Activates continuity plans and workarounds and tracks their capacity.

Leadership Liaison

  • Briefs senior leaders and, where needed, the board of directors with confirmed facts, unknowns and the decisions that need their authority.
  • Brings back leadership decisions such as spend, risk acceptance and external disclosure in time to be useful.

Vendor & Third-Party Liaison

  • Works with messaging providers and cloud support.
  • Opens priority cases with the cloud, SaaS, model and tooling vendors involved and records case numbers.
  • Requests logs, configuration histories and root cause statements in writing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Limit audience size

Enforce a maximum recipient count per campaign and per hour in the messaging layer, with human approval to exceed it.

Approve templates, not just campaigns

Allow agents to personalize only approved templates, and block any new wording from sending without review.

Validate segment queries

Compare audience size and key fields to the expected range before send, and require a sample preview.

Keep a kill switch for sends

Provide a documented, tested way to halt all queues in every channel within minutes, owned by a named role.

Separate high-impact messages

Treat closure, billing and safety notices as human-approved classes, aligned with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 communications and change controls and SOC 2 processing integrity.

Use one correction path

Prepare correction templates and an approval route so one voice speaks to every channel.

Protect vulnerable customers

Maintain a list of customers who need priority contact and test the call plan.

Keep unique credentials per tool

Give each sender its own key so one misuse does not affect others.

Plan for the call surge

Define how the contact center scales, which self-service pages update first and what messages the interactive voice menu plays, since a wrong message at scale becomes a contact volume problem within minutes.

Treat engagement metrics as claims

Open and delivery rates say nothing about correctness. Compare them with complaint, opt-out and call volume, and treat unusual engagement as a signal to check content.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. Announce the end, remind everyone that all actions were simulated, and keep everyone in the room for the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not and any gap in tools, access or documentation.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, role by role and then open floor, for 45 to 60 minutes. Keep the discussion on how the customer communication agent was permissioned, supervised and stopped.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate impact and effort, and select no more than ten.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Give each action an owner, a due date and a verification method, and record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Validate fixes within 90 days and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did the team connect the call surge to the agent?
  2. How did you stop sending?
  3. How did you determine who got which message?
  4. Who approved the correction?
  5. How did you protect vulnerable customers?
  6. Which send controls do you now want?
  7. What would a safe send approval look like?
  8. What would make you trust the agent again?
  9. How accurate were the updates to leadership, and what was said that later proved wrong?
  10. What did the vendor and third-party liaison add, and what did you still lack from vendors?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture that would satisfy an auditor, and what is missing?
  12. Which permissions, approvals or access did the team lack at the moment they were needed?
  13. Which alerts or detections should exist that did not?
  14. What temporary changes remain, who owns them and when will they be reversed?
  15. If you could change one design decision about how AI systems are built, permissioned or supervised here, what would it be?
  16. What did the team do well that should be written into the runbook?
