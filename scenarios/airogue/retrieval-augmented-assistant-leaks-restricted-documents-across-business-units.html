Retrieval-Augmented Assistant Leaks Restricted Documents Across Business Units

AI Gone Rogue

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a retrieval-augmented assistant spanning Google Cloud and on-premises document stores. An indexing change flattens access controls, so employees in one business unit can retrieve restricted documents from another through ordinary questions.

Participants are the working-level incident team: AI platform and search engineers, document platform owners, security analysts, business unit data owners, privacy and legal staff, communications and continuity coordinators, and a liaison to senior leaders or the board of directors where authority is needed. The exercise tests permission-aware retrieval and the difficulty of proving who saw what.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session. The scenario is industry-agnostic: substitute your own processes, platforms and teams wherever the text names a generic one.

Technology and Environment Context

Technology in scope: GCP & On-Premises. The organization built an internal assistant that answers questions from documents in Cloud Storage, a collaboration platform and on-premises file shares. A pipeline indexes content into a vector store on Google Cloud. Permissions are meant to be enforced at query time using group membership synced from the on-premises directory. The assistant returns summaries with citations.

Scenario

Rayburn Industrial Group asked its platform team to 'improve answer coverage'. The team switched the indexer to a service account with read access to all sources and removed the per-document access list field to cut storage. A query-time filter that used the access list now has nothing to filter on.

For three weeks, any user could ask about any topic and receive summaries from restricted sources such as merger planning, compensation reviews and legal matter files. A manager asking about 'plans for the eastern region' gets text from a board paper.

A human resources partner reports on Monday that a staff member quoted a compensation review in a meeting. Testing confirms the assistant returns restricted summaries to any user. Query logs show 1,900 users asked questions that retrieved documents above their clearance.

What Participants Know at the Start

  • The access list field was removed from the index.
  • The indexer service account reads all sources.
  • Query logs record questions but not which chunks were returned.
  • The assistant remains available.

What Is Not Yet Known

  • Which documents were retrieved and by whom.
  • Whether answers were copied or forwarded.
  • Whether on-premises file permissions were altered.
  • Whether any external users had access.

Exercise Objectives

  • Stop broad retrieval. The team disables the assistant or limits it to public sources while access control is restored. Evidence of success: Restricted sources cannot be retrieved, verified by tests within 30 minutes.
  • Determine exposure. Engineers and security analysts reconstruct which restricted chunks were returned to which users. Evidence of success: A list of users and restricted documents with confidence levels exists.
  • Restore permission-aware retrieval. Engineers rebuild the index with access metadata and test with users in different groups. Evidence of success: Access tests pass for representative personas.
  • Handle sensitive disclosures. Data owners, HR and legal decide on follow-up with users who saw restricted material and what to tell them. Evidence of success: A decision record with actions exists.
  • Communicate carefully. Communications and the leadership liaison explain the issue without repeating restricted content. Evidence of success: Messages are approved and avoid disclosing the content.
  • Redesign retrieval governance. Participants set access metadata requirements, tests and monitoring. Evidence of success: At least four controls are agreed with owners and dates.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to staff, customers, partners and leadership are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management reviewers and SOC 2 availability, security and processing integrity reviewers. Evidence of success: the decision log lets an outsider reconstruct who decided what, when and on which facts.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations, models or backups will be touched. Commands, consoles and agent settings are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Exercise information is confidential until the after-action report is approved. Mark any written or chat message 'exercise exercise exercise'.
  • Treat the AI system's outputs, logs and self-reported status as unverified until a named person confirms them from an independent source. The scribe records which facts are verified and which are claimed.
  • If a kill switch, rollback, approval gate or manual fallback does not exist in your environment, say so aloud. Do not assume the AI system will correct itself, stop when asked or explain its own actions reliably.
  • Use the word leadership for senior leaders, a board of directors or both, and bring them in only when a decision needs their authority.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Disclosure through the assistant is noticed. Decision: declare and restrict the assistant. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Quoted restricted content; Users retrieving above clearance; Index schema change.

T+00:30 — Containment. Containment: disable restricted sources and block the indexer's broad role. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Cached answers; Other assistants share the index; Users with exports.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Diagnosis: reconstruct retrieval and map who saw what. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Chunk-level logs missing; Citations incomplete; Forwarded content.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Remediation planning: choose rebuild approach and follow-up with data owners. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Full reindex takes days; Owners disagree; Legal holds.

T+03:00 — Execution. Execution: rebuild the index, test access and notify as decided. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Tests fail for some groups; Directory sync lag; New leaks.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Stabilization: monitor retrieval of restricted sources. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Regression after reindex; Shadow assistants; Users test boundaries.

T+06:00 — Validation. Validation: run access tests and review query logs. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Evidence gaps; More exposures; Other systems affected.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Return to normal and close-out: define assistant exit criteria and actions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Open actions lack owners; Leaders want early re-enablement; Auditors request evidence.

Injects

Inject #1: The retrieval assistant returns restricted documents to unauthorized staff

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 09:00 on Monday. An HR partner reports that a manager quoted details of a compensation review. The platform lead tests the assistant with an ordinary account and receives a summary of a restricted board paper.

The index schema no longer has an access list field. The indexer service account reads every source. Query logs show 1,900 users received answers drawn from documents above their clearance over three weeks.

The commander must decide how to stop the retrieval, how to find who saw what and how to deal with people who already saw restricted content.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and assign a scribe, an AI platform lead, a data owner lead and a security lead.
  • Authorize disabling the assistant for restricted sources.
  • Set a 20-minute cadence.
AI Platform & Model Operations
  • Disable retrieval from restricted sources and export index changes and query logs.
  • Explain why the access field was removed.
Infrastructure & Platform Engineering
  • Reduce the indexer service account and plan a rebuild with access metadata.
  • Check on-premises permissions.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Reconstruct retrieval by user and document with available logs.
  • Preserve logs.
Business & Process Owners
  • Identify the business units with the most sensitive content.
  • Support decisions on follow-up.
Data Protection & Privacy
  • Assess whether personal data in HR and legal files was disclosed.
  • Prepare notice analysis.
Legal, Risk & Compliance
  • Open the record and capture approvals for the schema change.
  • Review data classification policy.
Communications & Business Continuity
  • Prepare a staff message that does not repeat content.
  • Set one channel.
Leadership Liaison
  • Brief senior leaders and the board with counsel.
  • Check for market-sensitive information duties.
Vendor & Third-Party Liaison
  • Contact Google Cloud for vector search logging options.
  • Ask about data retention in the service.

Discussion questions

  1. Why disable retrieval before the full investigation?
  2. How do you find who saw restricted material?
  3. What do you ask users who saw it to do?
  4. How do you handle market-sensitive content?
  5. What do you tell staff without repeating content?
  6. Why must access control be enforced at query time?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the merger paper until SEC reviews retrieval logs.
  • If players only reindex, ask what happened in the three weeks.
  • Look for exposure analysis and careful messaging.
  • Time-box to 40 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Sets objectives for the first hour and states which decisions need the commander's own approval, including whether the retrieval assistant stays disabled, restricted or supervised.
  • Runs a fixed update cadence and keeps one decision log with the decision, owner and time.

AI Platform & Model Operations

  • Knows the index schema, filter logic and logging settings.
  • Provides index and query logs.
  • Owns the technical facts about the retrieval assistant: its configuration, permissions, tools, prompts, memory and recent changes.
  • Preserves model, agent and orchestration logs before any restart or redeployment.

Infrastructure & Platform Engineering

  • Rebuilds the index with access metadata and manages the indexer role.
  • Owns directory sync.
  • Owns the technical response on the platforms and networks involved and keeps changes small, reversible and logged.
  • Maintains a list of every manual change so it can be reversed or formalized afterwards.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Reconstructs retrieval and tests access with personas.
  • Treats the event as a possible security incident until evidence shows otherwise and preserves evidence in a forensically sound way.
  • Looks for misuse of credentials, tokens and tool permissions, not only the AI system's own actions.

Business & Process Owners

  • Supports data owners and prioritizes content.
  • Defines which business processes must continue, in what order, and what manual alternatives are acceptable.
  • Identifies decisions, transactions or communications that were made by the AI system and need review or reversal.

Data Protection & Privacy

  • Assesses personal data disclosure.
  • Determines whether personal, confidential or regulated data was accessed, changed, shared or lost.
  • Identifies the lawful basis, notification clocks and contractual commitments that may apply.

Legal, Risk & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded, classified and linked to risk and control owners (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (5.15, 5.12, 8.3, 8.15) and SOC 2 confidentiality).
  • Identifies regulatory, contractual and insurance obligations and their clocks.

Communications & Business Continuity

  • Issues time-stamped updates to staff, customers and partners from one approved source.
  • Activates continuity plans and workarounds and tracks their capacity.

Leadership Liaison

  • Briefs senior leaders and, where needed, the board of directors with confirmed facts, unknowns and the decisions that need their authority.
  • Brings back leadership decisions such as spend, risk acceptance and external disclosure in time to be useful.

Vendor & Third-Party Liaison

  • Works with Google Cloud on logging and retention.
  • Opens priority cases with the cloud, SaaS, model and tooling vendors involved and records case numbers.
  • Requests logs, configuration histories and root cause statements in writing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Carry permissions with the content

Every indexed chunk should store its access list or group, and queries must filter on it before retrieval, with tests that fail if the field is missing.

Use user-scoped retrieval

Prefer retrieval that runs with the user's identity, so the source system enforces access rather than a copy of it.

Test with personas

Run automated tests with users in different groups on every index change, asserting that restricted content never appears.

Log retrieval

Record which documents and chunks were returned per query in a protected store, with retention aligned to investigation needs.

Limit the indexer's reach

Scope indexer service accounts per source and review them, aligned with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 access and information classification controls and SOC 2 confidentiality criteria.

Label sensitive sources

Keep the most sensitive sources, such as board and compensation material, out of general assistants entirely.

Review schema changes

Treat index schema changes as security-relevant, with review by security before release.

Prepare the people response

Agree with HR and legal how to handle staff who saw restricted content, so follow-up is consistent and fair.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. Announce the end, remind everyone that all actions were simulated, and keep everyone in the room for the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not and any gap in tools, access or documentation.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, role by role and then open floor, for 45 to 60 minutes. Keep the discussion on how the retrieval assistant was permissioned, supervised and stopped.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate impact and effort, and select no more than ten.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Give each action an owner, a due date and a verification method, and record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Validate fixes within 90 days and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did the team link the disclosure to the assistant?
  2. How did you stop retrieval?
  3. How did you reconstruct who saw what?
  4. What did you ask users to do?
  5. What did staff hear?
  6. What retrieval controls do you now want?
  7. Which sources should never be indexed together?
  8. How would you have caught the schema change in review?
  9. How accurate were the updates to leadership, and what was said that later proved wrong?
  10. What did the vendor and third-party liaison add, and what did you still lack from vendors?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture that would satisfy an auditor, and what is missing?
  12. Which permissions, approvals or access did the team lack at the moment they were needed?
  13. Which alerts or detections should exist that did not?
  14. What temporary changes remain, who owns them and when will they be reversed?
  15. If you could change one design decision about how AI systems are built, permissioned or supervised here, what would it be?
  16. What did the team do well that should be written into the runbook?
