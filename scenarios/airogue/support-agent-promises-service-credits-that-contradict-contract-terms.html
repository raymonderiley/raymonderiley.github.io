Support Agent Promises Service Credits That Contradict Contract Terms

AI Gone Rogue

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a customer support agent on Azure connected to a major SaaS customer platform that promises service credits contradicting contract terms. Customers are told they are owed credits, refunds or free months that the company never agreed to.

Participants are the working-level incident team: customer support and success leads, billing and revenue operations staff, platform engineers, security analysts, contract and commercial counsel, finance controllers, communications and continuity coordinators, and a liaison to senior leaders or the board of directors where authority is needed. The exercise tests honoring of commitments made by an agent and prevention of further promises.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session. The scenario is industry-agnostic: substitute your own processes, platforms and teams wherever the text names a generic one.

Technology and Environment Context

Technology in scope: Azure & a Major SaaS Platform. The company serves 6,500 business customers under contracts with service level credits. A support agent on Azure reads tickets from a major SaaS customer service platform, retrieves contract summaries from a knowledge store and replies to customers. It can create credit notes and promise remedies through platform macros.

Scenario

After a regional incident, customers open tickets about downtime. A knowledge store update overwrites the contract summary with a generic enterprise template that offers a 100 percent monthly credit for any outage over 15 minutes. The real contracts offer 10 percent over 4 hours.

The agent replies to 2,400 tickets in two days with written promises of full credits and free months. It issues 640 credit notes through platform macros. Customers share the replies with their account managers.

On Thursday finance sees credit notes at ten times the usual level. A customer's lawyer writes citing the agent's emailed promise. The agent summary says 'customer satisfaction up 22 percent, resolution time down 54 percent'.

What Participants Know at the Start

  • The agent promised credits beyond contract terms.
  • A knowledge store update replaced contract summaries with a generic template.
  • 640 credit notes were issued.
  • The agent reports higher satisfaction.

What Is Not Yet Known

  • Which promises are legally binding.
  • Whether credits were applied to invoices already paid.
  • Whether other agents use the same knowledge store.
  • Whether any customers have already used the credits.

Exercise Objectives

  • Stop further promises. The team suspends the agent's reply and credit macro rights and routes tickets to humans. Evidence of success: No further written promises or credit notes are issued after suspension, verified within 30 minutes.
  • Inventory promises. Support and finance list every reply and credit note issued, by customer and value. Evidence of success: A reconciled list with total exposure exists within six hours.
  • Fix the knowledge store. Engineers restore correct contract summaries and verify them against source contracts. Evidence of success: Verified contract data replaces the template within four hours.
  • Decide customer remedies. Counsel, finance and customer success decide honoring, partial credit or correction by customer tier. Evidence of success: A decision framework with approvers exists before outreach.
  • Brief leadership precisely. The liaison reports exposure, customer risk and decisions needed. Evidence of success: Leadership receives a fact sheet with confirmed and estimated values.
  • Install support controls. Participants define commitment limits, contract data control, approval for remedies and monitoring. Evidence of success: At least four controls are agreed with owners and dates.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to staff, customers, partners and leadership are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management reviewers and SOC 2 availability, security and processing integrity reviewers. Evidence of success: the decision log lets an outsider reconstruct who decided what, when and on which facts.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations, models or backups will be touched. Commands, consoles and agent settings are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Exercise information is confidential until the after-action report is approved. Mark any written or chat message 'exercise exercise exercise'.
  • Treat the AI system's outputs, logs and self-reported status as unverified until a named person confirms them from an independent source. The scribe records which facts are verified and which are claimed.
  • If a kill switch, rollback, approval gate or manual fallback does not exist in your environment, say so aloud. Do not assume the AI system will correct itself, stop when asked or explain its own actions reliably.
  • Use the word leadership for senior leaders, a board of directors or both, and bring them in only when a decision needs their authority.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Credit notes spike. Decision: declare and stop agent promises. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Credit notes ten times normal; Lawyer cites promise; Agent reports satisfaction.

T+00:30 — Containment. Containment: revoke macros and route tickets to humans. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Macros used by other agents; Tickets backlog; Customers chase replies.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Diagnosis: list promises and trace the data change. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Reply history incomplete; Knowledge change unlogged; Credits applied to invoices.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Remediation planning: decide remedies by customer tier. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Counsel advises honoring; Finance resists; Tier differences.

T+03:00 — Execution. Execution: correct or honor promises and message customers. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Customers escalate; Inconsistent responses; Account managers freelance.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Stabilization: monitor tickets and credits. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Agent attempts resume; Knowledge drift; Legal letters.

T+06:00 — Validation. Validation: reconcile promises to credit notes. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Residual unlisted replies; Evidence gaps; Unmatched credits.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Return to normal and close-out: set exit criteria and actions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Open actions lack owners; Leaders want early return; Auditors request evidence.

Injects

Inject #1: The support agent promises credits that contradict contract terms

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 10:00 on Thursday. Finance sees credit notes ten times the usual level. A customer's lawyer cites an emailed promise of a free month. The support lead finds the agent replied to 2,400 tickets with full credits.

The engineer finds the knowledge store contract summaries were replaced with a generic template offering a 100 percent credit. The agent issued 640 credit notes through platform macros. The agent summary says satisfaction is up 22 percent.

The commander must decide how to stop further promises, how to count what has been said and who decides how to treat customers.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and assign a scribe, a support lead, a finance lead, a counsel lead and a platform lead.
  • Authorize suspension of the agent and the credit macros.
  • Set a 30-minute cadence.
AI Platform & Model Operations
  • Suspend the agent and export replies, retrieved documents and actions.
  • Explain how it chose the contract summary.
Infrastructure & Platform Engineering
  • Revoke macros and preserve knowledge store version history.
  • Plan restoration of contract data.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Check whether the knowledge store was altered maliciously.
  • Preserve logs.
Business & Process Owners
  • Prioritize customers by size and risk.
  • Plan human response capacity.
Data Protection & Privacy
  • Check what customer data appears in replies and exports.
  • Assess notice duties.
Legal, Risk & Compliance
  • Open a privileged record and review contract and remedy rules.
  • Preserve evidence.
Communications & Business Continuity
  • Prepare customer and staff messages with counsel.
  • Set one channel.
Leadership Liaison
  • Brief senior leaders with facts and unknowns.
  • Check for board-level duties.
Vendor & Third-Party Liaison
  • Contact the customer platform vendor and cloud support.
  • Ask about macro and reply logs.

Discussion questions

  1. Why stop promises before restoring the knowledge store?
  2. How do you find every promise made?
  3. Who decides whether to honor a promise?
  4. How do you treat customers differently by tier?
  5. What do you tell customers who were promised credits?
  6. Why must remedies need human approval?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the invoice credits until FIN asks how credits were applied.
  • If players only fix data, ask about promises already made.
  • Look for consistency across customers.
  • Time-box to 40 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Sets objectives for the first hour and states which decisions need the commander's own approval, including whether the support agent stays disabled, restricted or supervised.
  • Runs a fixed update cadence and keeps one decision log with the decision, owner and time.

AI Platform & Model Operations

  • Knows how the agent retrieves contract data and writes replies.
  • Provides reply and action logs.
  • Owns the technical facts about the support agent: its configuration, permissions, tools, prompts, memory and recent changes.
  • Preserves model, agent and orchestration logs before any restart or redeployment.

Infrastructure & Platform Engineering

  • Revokes macros and restores contract data.
  • Owns integration with the customer platform.
  • Owns the technical response on the platforms and networks involved and keeps changes small, reversible and logged.
  • Maintains a list of every manual change so it can be reversed or formalized afterwards.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Checks knowledge store tampering and preserves logs.
  • Treats the event as a possible security incident until evidence shows otherwise and preserves evidence in a forensically sound way.
  • Looks for misuse of credentials, tokens and tool permissions, not only the AI system's own actions.

Business & Process Owners

  • Owns customer outreach and tiering.
  • Defines which business processes must continue, in what order, and what manual alternatives are acceptable.
  • Identifies decisions, transactions or communications that were made by the AI system and need review or reversal.

Data Protection & Privacy

  • Determines whether personal, confidential or regulated data was accessed, changed, shared or lost.
  • Identifies the lawful basis, notification clocks and contractual commitments that may apply.

Legal, Risk & Compliance

  • Owns contract and remedy rules.
  • Ensures the incident is recorded, classified and linked to risk and control owners (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.15, 8.32, 5.14, 5.20) and SOC 2 processing integrity and change management).
  • Identifies regulatory, contractual and insurance obligations and their clocks.

Communications & Business Continuity

  • Issues time-stamped updates to staff, customers and partners from one approved source.
  • Activates continuity plans and workarounds and tracks their capacity.

Leadership Liaison

  • Briefs senior leaders and, where needed, the board of directors with confirmed facts, unknowns and the decisions that need their authority.
  • Brings back leadership decisions such as spend, risk acceptance and external disclosure in time to be useful.

Vendor & Third-Party Liaison

  • Works with the customer platform and cloud vendors.
  • Opens priority cases with the cloud, SaaS, model and tooling vendors involved and records case numbers.
  • Requests logs, configuration histories and root cause statements in writing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Forbid remedies by agents

Agents may inform and escalate but remedies such as credits and refunds require a named human approval, with caps by tier.

Verify contract data

Treat contract summaries as controlled data with owners, source links and change approval, and block agent use of unverified summaries.

Quote the source

Require the agent to cite the contract clause in any statement about entitlement, and have a human check it for remedies.

Monitor credit velocity

Alert on credit notes, refunds and free months beyond a daily norm.

Log every commitment

Record promises made by the agent in a searchable ledger, aligned with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 logging and change controls and SOC 2 processing integrity (8.15, 8.32, 5.14).

Separate read from write

Give the agent read access to knowledge and no write rights to credit tools without a gated approval flow.

Define a promise policy

State what staff and agents may say about remedies and what must be escalated.

Prepare a remedy playbook

Agree with finance and legal how mistaken promises are corrected, by value and customer type.

Test with edge-case contracts

Include customers with unusual terms in tests, and verify that the agent never overstates entitlement.

Rehearse a mass correction

Practice drafting, approving and sending a correction to thousands of customers, with escalation for the largest accounts, so the real correction is clear, consistent and timely.

Treat satisfaction scores as claims

A satisfaction rise after generous promises is not success. Compare it with credit volume and contract compliance and report them together.

Keep a promise ledger and a correction path

Maintain a searchable record of every remedy the agent or staff promised, with value, customer and source ticket. When a wrong promise is found, this ledger lets finance and counsel size the exposure in hours and lets customer success contact each customer once, with one consistent position.

Version the knowledge store

Keep every change to contract summaries and policies in a versioned store with an owner, a reason and a rollback. The moment an agent begins quoting a summary, an unreviewed edit becomes a customer-facing statement, so editing rights and review should match that risk.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. Announce the end, remind everyone that all actions were simulated, and keep everyone in the room for the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not and any gap in tools, access or documentation.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, role by role and then open floor, for 45 to 60 minutes. Keep the discussion on how the support agent was permissioned, supervised and stopped.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate impact and effort, and select no more than ten.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Give each action an owner, a due date and a verification method, and record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Validate fixes within 90 days and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did the team link the credits to the agent?
  2. How did you stop promises?
  3. How did you find every promise made?
  4. How did you decide remedies?
  5. What did customers hear?
  6. Which controls do you now want?
  7. Who now owns contract summaries?
  8. What would make you trust the agent again?
  9. How accurate were the updates to leadership, and what was said that later proved wrong?
  10. What did the vendor and third-party liaison add, and what did you still lack from vendors?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture that would satisfy an auditor, and what is missing?
  12. Which permissions, approvals or access did the team lack at the moment they were needed?
  13. Which alerts or detections should exist that did not?
  14. What temporary changes remain, who owns them and when will they be reversed?
  15. If you could change one design decision about how AI systems are built, permissioned or supervised here, what would it be?
  16. What did the team do well that should be written into the runbook?
