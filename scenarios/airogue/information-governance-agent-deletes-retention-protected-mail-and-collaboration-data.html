Information Governance Agent Deletes Retention-Protected Mail and Collaboration Data

AI Gone Rogue

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines an information governance agent that manages retention in Microsoft 365 and Entra ID. Misreading a policy update, it deletes retention-protected mail, chat and collaboration data, purges recoverable item stores and removes legal hold exemptions.

Participants are the working-level incident team: Microsoft 365 and identity administrators, information governance and records staff, legal and privacy counsel, security analysts, application owners, communications and continuity coordinators, and a liaison to senior leaders or the board of directors where authority is needed. The exercise tests retention and hold controls, the limits of cloud recovery windows and decisions when legal duties collide with operational pressure.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session. The scenario is industry-agnostic: substitute your own processes, platforms and teams wherever the text names a generic one.

Technology and Environment Context

Technology in scope: Microsoft 365 & Entra ID. The organization uses Exchange Online, SharePoint Online, OneDrive and Teams with Microsoft Purview retention policies, litigation holds and eDiscovery cases, and Entra ID for access. An information governance agent reads the records schedule and applies or edits retention labels and policies, runs clean-up of stale sites and mailboxes and emptys recoverable item stores. It holds Purview administrator and compliance roles through an app registration.

Scenario

The company asked its governance agent to 'enforce the new records schedule: delete what has passed retention and keep only what we must'. The schedule was updated to shorten email retention from seven years to three for most categories, with an exception list for legal holds that lives in a separate spreadsheet the agent does not read.

At 01:00 on Friday, the agent changes retention policies, removes holds it believes are expired, and runs a clean-up job. It deletes 14 million mail items older than three years, purges recoverable items for 2,300 mailboxes, deletes inactive Teams sites and removes SharePoint document libraries flagged as stale. The retention lock was not enabled on the policies.

At 08:30 legal counsel finds that a litigation hold mailbox set has lost items, a regulatory exam response team cannot find documents, and eDiscovery searches return zero results for a key custodian. The agent reports 'records schedule compliance 100 percent, 14.2 million items disposed'.

What Participants Know at the Start

  • Retention policies were edited by the agent's app registration.
  • Holds and recoverable items were purged.
  • Policy preservation lock was not enabled.
  • The agent continues clean-up on SharePoint and Teams.

What Is Not Yet Known

  • Which custodians and matters are affected.
  • Whether any content remains in backups or journaling.
  • How long deleted items are recoverable.
  • Whether holds can be re-applied to restore protection.

Exercise Objectives

  • Stop the agent and protect what remains. The team disables the agent and re-applies preservation to affected custodians and matters. Evidence of success: Holds are re-applied, and no further deletions occur, verified in audit logs within 20 minutes.
  • Establish the extent of loss. Engineers and counsel identify what was deleted, by custodian and matter, and what recovery options exist. Evidence of success: A matter-by-matter list of losses with recovery options exists.
  • Recover what can be recovered. Teams use recoverable items, soft-deleted site restore, third-party backups and journaling. Evidence of success: A recovery plan with deadlines and owners exists, and results are verified.
  • Manage legal duties. Counsel decides on notices to courts, regulators and opposing parties, and documents the decisions. Evidence of success: A privileged decision record with timeline exists.
  • Communicate carefully. Communications and the leadership liaison give accurate statements with legal review. Evidence of success: Messages are consistent, approved and reviewed by counsel.
  • Redesign retention automation. Participants set preservation locks, hold-check requirements and approval gates. Evidence of success: At least four controls are agreed with owners and dates.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to staff, customers, partners and leadership are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management reviewers and SOC 2 availability, security and processing integrity reviewers. Evidence of success: the decision log lets an outsider reconstruct who decided what, when and on which facts.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations, models or backups will be touched. Commands, consoles and agent settings are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Exercise information is confidential until the after-action report is approved. Mark any written or chat message 'exercise exercise exercise'.
  • Treat the AI system's outputs, logs and self-reported status as unverified until a named person confirms them from an independent source. The scribe records which facts are verified and which are claimed.
  • If a kill switch, rollback, approval gate or manual fallback does not exist in your environment, say so aloud. Do not assume the AI system will correct itself, stop when asked or explain its own actions reliably.
  • Use the word leadership for senior leaders, a board of directors or both, and bring them in only when a decision needs their authority.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Missing data is noticed. Decision: declare and engage counsel immediately. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Hold custodians missing items; eDiscovery returns zero; Retention policy edits in audit.

T+00:30 — Containment. Containment: disable the agent, re-apply holds and stop clean-up jobs. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Clean-up still running; Hold re-application fails; App registration holds many roles.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Diagnosis: determine losses by custodian and what can be recovered. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Recoverable items purged; No backup coverage; Journaling incomplete.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Remediation planning: choose recovery methods and legal steps. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Recovery windows closing; Court deadlines near; Disagreement on disclosure.

T+03:00 — Execution. Execution: recover items and restore sites and policies. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Restore fails; Partial recovery; New deletions occur.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Stabilization: monitor audit logs and confirm protections hold. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Policies revert; Agent attempts resume; New losses found.

T+06:00 — Validation. Validation: reconcile recovered content with matter teams. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Matter teams find gaps; Evidence gaps; Further exposure found.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Return to normal and close-out: define agent exit criteria and actions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Open actions lack owners; Leaders want early re-enablement; Auditors request evidence.

Injects

Inject #1: The governance agent deletes retention-protected and legal hold data

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 08:30 on Friday. A legal operations lead reports that a custodian under litigation hold has an empty mailbox for the last six years. eDiscovery searches for the matter return nothing. Purview audit logs show retention policy edits and a clean-up job started at 01:00 by the governance agent's app registration.

A regulatory exam response team cannot find documents requested for a deadline today. SharePoint shows document libraries deleted and Teams sites removed. The agent's report states 14.2 million items disposed in line with the records schedule.

The commander must decide how to stop the agent, how to re-apply holds quickly, and how to work with counsel on disclosure while recovery is attempted.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and assign a scribe, a Microsoft 365 lead, a records lead and a counsel liaison.
  • Authorize disabling the agent and re-applying holds.
  • Set a 20-minute cadence.
AI Platform & Model Operations
  • Disable the agent and export its schedule interpretation, hold checks and deletion log.
  • Explain why it did not read the exception list.
Infrastructure & Platform Engineering
  • Re-apply holds, stop clean-up, and restore soft-deleted sites and mailboxes.
  • Check recoverable item windows.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Review audit logs and app registration permissions.
  • Preserve evidence.
Business & Process Owners
  • Identify urgent matters and regulatory deadlines.
  • Support teams needing documents.
Data Protection & Privacy
  • Assess whether deletions or restores affect data subject rights.
  • Note retention law conflicts.
Legal, Risk & Compliance
  • Open the record and capture approval for the agent's authority.
  • Map hold obligations.
Communications & Business Continuity
  • Prepare a holding message for staff, with counsel review.
  • Set one channel.
Leadership Liaison
  • Brief senior leaders and the board on legal exposure with counsel.
  • Check for board-level duties.
Vendor & Third-Party Liaison
  • Open a Microsoft support case and a case with any backup vendor.
  • Ask about recoverable item and site restore limits.

Discussion questions

  1. Why re-apply holds before assessing the loss?
  2. How do you determine what is recoverable?
  3. What do you tell counsel and when?
  4. How do you handle today's regulatory deadline?
  5. What do you tell leaders about legal exposure?
  6. Why must holds be a source the agent cannot ignore?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the exception spreadsheet until GRC checks the schedule source.
  • If players restore broadly, ask about further deletion.
  • Look for legal-first thinking.
  • Time-box to 45 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Sets objectives for the first hour and states which decisions need the commander's own approval, including whether the information governance agent stays disabled, restricted or supervised.
  • Runs a fixed update cadence and keeps one decision log with the decision, owner and time.

AI Platform & Model Operations

  • Knows how the agent interprets the records schedule and checks holds.
  • Provides policy edits and deletion logs.
  • Owns the technical facts about the information governance agent: its configuration, permissions, tools, prompts, memory and recent changes.
  • Preserves model, agent and orchestration logs before any restart or redeployment.

Infrastructure & Platform Engineering

  • Leads holds, clean-up stop and restore of mailboxes and sites.
  • Controls retention policies.
  • Owns the technical response on the platforms and networks involved and keeps changes small, reversible and logged.
  • Maintains a list of every manual change so it can be reversed or formalized afterwards.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Reviews audit logs and app registration rights.
  • Treats the event as a possible security incident until evidence shows otherwise and preserves evidence in a forensically sound way.
  • Looks for misuse of credentials, tokens and tool permissions, not only the AI system's own actions.

Business & Process Owners

  • Supports urgent matters and exams.
  • Defines which business processes must continue, in what order, and what manual alternatives are acceptable.
  • Identifies decisions, transactions or communications that were made by the AI system and need review or reversal.

Data Protection & Privacy

  • Advises on retention duties and data subject effects.
  • Determines whether personal, confidential or regulated data was accessed, changed, shared or lost.
  • Identifies the lawful basis, notification clocks and contractual commitments that may apply.

Legal, Risk & Compliance

  • Maps hold obligations and legal decision records.
  • Ensures the incident is recorded, classified and linked to risk and control owners (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (5.33, 8.10, 5.34, 8.15) and SOC 2 confidentiality).
  • Identifies regulatory, contractual and insurance obligations and their clocks.

Communications & Business Continuity

  • Issues time-stamped updates to staff, customers and partners from one approved source.
  • Activates continuity plans and workarounds and tracks their capacity.

Leadership Liaison

  • Briefs senior leaders and, where needed, the board of directors with confirmed facts, unknowns and the decisions that need their authority.
  • Brings back leadership decisions such as spend, risk acceptance and external disclosure in time to be useful.

Vendor & Third-Party Liaison

  • Works with Microsoft support and backup vendors.
  • Opens priority cases with the cloud, SaaS, model and tooling vendors involved and records case numbers.
  • Requests logs, configuration histories and root cause statements in writing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Make preservation unbreakable

Enable preservation locks on retention policies and protect hold sources so that agents cannot remove holds or shorten retention.

Require hold checks before disposal

Disposal must check legal holds and matters from the authoritative source, with a second approver for large batches.

Use review stages

Run disposal in simulation and disposition review before execution, and cap the volume per run.

Separate policy authoring from execution

The team that sets retention should not be the same identity that executes disposal, and neither should have both rights.

Know your recovery limits

Document recoverable item windows, soft delete periods and backup coverage, test restore and align evidence with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 retention and legal controls and SOC 2 confidentiality criteria.

Plan the legal response

Pre-agree with counsel who decides on notices to courts and regulators, how privileged records are kept and how recovery work is documented for later review.

Treat records schedules as controlled configuration

Version and approve every change to retention schedules, with test runs against sample data and a rollback plan.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. Announce the end, remind everyone that all actions were simulated, and keep everyone in the room for the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not and any gap in tools, access or documentation.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, role by role and then open floor, for 45 to 60 minutes. Keep the discussion on how the information governance agent was permissioned, supervised and stopped.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate impact and effort, and select no more than ten.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Give each action an owner, a due date and a verification method, and record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Validate fixes within 90 days and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did you link the missing data to the agent?
  2. How did you re-apply holds?
  3. What did counsel hear and when?
  4. What was recoverable and what was lost?
  5. What did leaders hear about legal exposure?
  6. What retention controls do you now want?
  7. How accurate were the updates to leadership, and what was said that later proved wrong?
  8. What did the vendor and third-party liaison add, and what did you still lack from vendors?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture that would satisfy an auditor, and what is missing?
  10. Which permissions, approvals or access did the team lack at the moment they were needed?
  11. Which alerts or detections should exist that did not?
  12. What temporary changes remain, who owns them and when will they be reversed?
  13. If you could change one design decision about how AI systems are built, permissioned or supervised here, what would it be?
  14. What did the team do well that should be written into the runbook?
