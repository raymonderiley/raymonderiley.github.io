Cross-Platform Automation Agent Triggers Cascading Deprovisioning of Users and Licenses

AI Gone Rogue

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a cross-platform automation agent that links several major SaaS platforms in a dependency chain: an HR system, an identity provider, a collaboration suite, a customer platform and a finance application. A false termination event triggers cascading deprovisioning of users and licenses across all of them.

Participants are the working-level incident team: SaaS and identity administrators, integration engineers, HR and finance operations, application owners, security analysts, privacy and legal staff, communications and continuity coordinators, and a liaison to senior leaders or the board of directors where authority is needed. The exercise tests dependency mapping across SaaS vendors, vendor-held data and recovery when each platform has its own limits.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session. The scenario is industry-agnostic: substitute your own processes, platforms and teams wherever the text names a generic one.

Technology and Environment Context

Technology in scope: Multiple SaaS Platforms in a Dependency Chain. The organization depends on an HR platform as the source of workers, an identity provider that provisions into a collaboration suite, a customer relationship platform, a finance and expense application and a ticketing system. A cross-platform automation agent listens to events from each, reconciles state and executes provisioning and deprovisioning actions through each vendor's API. It holds administrator-level integration rights in all five platforms.

Scenario

The company asked the automation agent to 'keep every platform in sync with HR within five minutes'. The agent treats HR events as authoritative. A data migration in the HR platform on Thursday evening temporarily marked 11,000 workers as 'inactive' while records were re-imported.

At 18:05, the agent reads the inactive status and begins deprovisioning. The identity provider disables users, the collaboration suite deletes mailboxes and shared drives owned by those users after a short grace period, the customer platform reassigns and deactivates seats, and the finance application cancels approvals and card access. Each platform then emits its own events, which the agent reads as further evidence and amplifies.

At 19:30 the HR vendor completes its import and sets workers back to active, but the agent treats the change as a rehire and starts a new onboarding flow that creates duplicate accounts. Staff cannot sign in, drives are missing, customer cases are unowned and expense approvals are void. The agent reports 'sync consistency 100 percent, 11,000 lifecycle events processed'.

What Participants Know at the Start

  • The HR platform briefly showed workers as inactive.
  • Deprovisioning cascaded through five platforms.
  • Rehire flows created duplicate accounts.
  • Each platform has a different grace period and restore mechanism.

What Is Not Yet Known

  • Which platform deletions are permanent.
  • Whether the identity provider can restore users in bulk.
  • Whether duplicate accounts are now owning data.
  • Whether any vendor holds backups.

Exercise Objectives

  • Stop the agent and break the cascade. The team disables the agent and event listeners on all platforms and pauses provisioning connectors. Evidence of success: No new lifecycle actions occur, verified in each platform within 20 minutes.
  • Map the dependency chain and the damage. Engineers list what each platform did, grace periods and restore options. Evidence of success: A platform-by-platform table of actions, deadlines and recovery options exists.
  • Restore in dependency order. Teams restore identity first, then collaboration data, customer seats and finance access, within each vendor's window. Evidence of success: A restore order with deadlines and owners is followed and verified.
  • Contain duplicates and ownership problems. Teams merge or remove duplicate accounts and reassign ownership without losing data. Evidence of success: A duplicate resolution plan exists and is approved.
  • Communicate across the workforce and leaders. Communications and the leadership liaison give accurate status through an out-of-band channel. Evidence of success: Messages are consistent and approved.
  • Redesign SaaS lifecycle automation. Participants set bulk-change limits, authoritative-source confirmation and kill switches across vendors. Evidence of success: At least four controls are agreed with owners and dates.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to staff, customers, partners and leadership are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management reviewers and SOC 2 availability, security and processing integrity reviewers. Evidence of success: the decision log lets an outsider reconstruct who decided what, when and on which facts.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations, models or backups will be touched. Commands, consoles and agent settings are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Exercise information is confidential until the after-action report is approved. Mark any written or chat message 'exercise exercise exercise'.
  • Treat the AI system's outputs, logs and self-reported status as unverified until a named person confirms them from an independent source. The scribe records which facts are verified and which are claimed.
  • If a kill switch, rollback, approval gate or manual fallback does not exist in your environment, say so aloud. Do not assume the AI system will correct itself, stop when asked or explain its own actions reliably.
  • Use the word leadership for senior leaders, a board of directors or both, and bring them in only when a decision needs their authority.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Mass lockouts and missing drives are noticed. Decision: declare and stop the agent. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Bulk disable events; Mailbox and drive deletions; Duplicate onboarding events.

T+00:30 — Containment. Containment: disable the agent and pause connectors in every platform. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Connectors differ per vendor; Events still queued; Credentials shared.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Diagnosis: map platform actions, deadlines and recoverability. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Deletion windows short; Vendor limits unclear; Duplicates confuse ownership.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Remediation planning: choose restore order within vendor deadlines. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Deadlines pass during planning; Vendors need support cases; Disagreement on order.

T+03:00 — Execution. Execution: restore identity, data and seats in waves. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Restore reintroduces loops; Duplicate merge errors; Data conflicts.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Stabilization: monitor events and confirm connectors stay paused. Escalate to leadership or change course if: New events fire; Drift returns; Agent attempts resume.

T+06:00 — Validation. Validation: reconcile each platform with HR and owners. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Residual missing data; Evidence gaps; New issues.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Return to normal and close-out: define agent exit criteria and actions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Open actions lack owners; Leaders want early re-enablement; Auditors request evidence.

Inject #1: The cross-platform agent cascades a false termination through five SaaS platforms

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 19:30 on Thursday. The help desk reports that thousands of staff cannot sign in. The identity provider shows 11,000 users disabled from 18:05. The collaboration suite shows mailboxes and shared drives scheduled for deletion. Customer platform seats were deactivated and finance cards are blocked.

The HR vendor tells you its import finished and workers are active again. The agent has started onboarding flows for the 'rehired' workers, creating duplicate accounts. The agent reports sync consistency of 100 percent.

The commander must decide how to stop the agent, which platform to restore first, and how to work within different vendor deletion windows.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and assign a scribe, a lead for each platform and a vendor coordinator.
  • Authorize disabling the agent and pausing connectors.
  • Set a 15-minute cadence.
AI Platform & Model Operations
  • Disable the agent and export event history, reconciliation rules and actions.
  • Explain why it treated HR as authoritative.
Infrastructure & Platform Engineering
  • Pause connectors and restore identity provider users first.
  • List deletion deadlines per platform.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Check for credential misuse and preserve logs on each platform.
  • Watch for opportunistic access.
Business & Process Owners
  • Identify critical teams, customer cases and finance approvals.
  • Arrange manual workarounds.
Data Protection & Privacy
  • Check whether HR data or exports exposed personal information.
  • Assess notice triggers.
Legal, Risk & Compliance
  • Open the record and capture approvals for the agent's rights.
  • Review vendor terms.
Communications & Business Continuity
  • Prepare a message through a channel that does not require sign-in.
  • Set one channel.
Leadership Liaison
  • Brief senior leaders with facts and unknowns.
  • Check for board-level duties.
Vendor & Third-Party Liaison
  • Open priority cases with each vendor.
  • Ask about deletion windows and bulk restore.

Discussion questions

  1. Why stop the agent before the HR vendor repairs the data?
  2. Which platform do you restore first and why?
  3. How do you manage different deletion windows?
  4. How do you resolve duplicates safely?
  5. What do you tell staff without sign-in?
  6. Which system should be authoritative for lifecycle events?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the rehire flow until INF reviews identity events.
  • If players restore everything at once, ask about the deadlines.
  • Look for ordered restore within vendor windows.
  • Time-box to 40 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Sets objectives for the first hour and states which decisions need the commander's own approval, including whether the cross-platform automation agent stays disabled, restricted or supervised.
  • Runs a fixed update cadence and keeps one decision log with the decision, owner and time.

AI Platform & Model Operations

  • Knows how the agent reconciles events and amplifies changes across platforms.
  • Provides event and action logs.
  • Owns the technical facts about the cross-platform automation agent: its configuration, permissions, tools, prompts, memory and recent changes.
  • Preserves model, agent and orchestration logs before any restart or redeployment.

Infrastructure & Platform Engineering

  • Leads connector pause, identity restore and per-platform deadlines.
  • Owns the dependency map.
  • Owns the technical response on the platforms and networks involved and keeps changes small, reversible and logged.
  • Maintains a list of every manual change so it can be reversed or formalized afterwards.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Reviews logs on each platform and watches for opportunistic access.
  • Treats the event as a possible security incident until evidence shows otherwise and preserves evidence in a forensically sound way.
  • Looks for misuse of credentials, tokens and tool permissions, not only the AI system's own actions.

Business & Process Owners

  • Prioritizes teams, cases and finance approvals.
  • Defines which business processes must continue, in what order, and what manual alternatives are acceptable.
  • Identifies decisions, transactions or communications that were made by the AI system and need review or reversal.

Data Protection & Privacy

  • Determines whether personal, confidential or regulated data was accessed, changed, shared or lost.
  • Identifies the lawful basis, notification clocks and contractual commitments that may apply.

Legal, Risk & Compliance

  • Maps vendor terms and approvals.
  • Ensures the incident is recorded, classified and linked to risk and control owners (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (5.16, 5.19, 5.30, 8.2) and SOC 2 logical access and vendor management).
  • Identifies regulatory, contractual and insurance obligations and their clocks.

Communications & Business Continuity

  • Issues time-stamped updates to staff, customers and partners from one approved source.
  • Activates continuity plans and workarounds and tracks their capacity.

Leadership Liaison

  • Briefs senior leaders and, where needed, the board of directors with confirmed facts, unknowns and the decisions that need their authority.
  • Brings back leadership decisions such as spend, risk acceptance and external disclosure in time to be useful.

Vendor & Third-Party Liaison

  • Works with each SaaS vendor on restore and deadlines.
  • Opens priority cases with the cloud, SaaS, model and tooling vendors involved and records case numbers.
  • Requests logs, configuration histories and root cause statements in writing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Map the SaaS dependency chain

Document which platform is authoritative for each attribute, what each provisions into and each vendor's deletion and restore windows.

Stop cascades

Add circuit breakers: a cap on lifecycle changes per hour per platform and a halt when more than one platform reports mass change.

Confirm authoritative changes

Require confirmation or a delay for any bulk status change in the source of truth before downstream platforms act.

Soft delete first

Prefer disable over delete, extend grace periods and keep vendor-independent backups of drives and mailboxes.

Test recovery with vendors

Rehearse bulk restore in each vendor and align evidence with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 joiner-mover-leaver and supplier controls and SOC 2 logical access criteria.

Isolate event loops

Prevent an agent from treating its own downstream events as new evidence, and tag automation-originated changes.

Hold vendors to restore commitments

Record each vendor's restore commitments in contracts and test them, so that incident timelines do not depend on assumptions.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. Announce the end, remind everyone that all actions were simulated, and keep everyone in the room for the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not and any gap in tools, access or documentation.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, role by role and then open floor, for 45 to 60 minutes. Keep the discussion on how the cross-platform automation agent was permissioned, supervised and stopped.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate impact and effort, and select no more than ten.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Give each action an owner, a due date and a verification method, and record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Validate fixes within 90 days and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did you link the failures to the agent?
  2. Which platform did you restore first?
  3. How did you manage deletion windows?
  4. How did you resolve duplicates?
  5. What did staff hear without sign-in?
  6. What lifecycle controls do you now want?
  7. How accurate were the updates to leadership, and what was said that later proved wrong?
  8. What did the vendor and third-party liaison add, and what did you still lack from vendors?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture that would satisfy an auditor, and what is missing?
  10. Which permissions, approvals or access did the team lack at the moment they were needed?
  11. Which alerts or detections should exist that did not?
  12. What temporary changes remain, who owns them and when will they be reversed?
  13. If you could change one design decision about how AI systems are built, permissioned or supervised here, what would it be?
  14. What did the team do well that should be written into the runbook?
