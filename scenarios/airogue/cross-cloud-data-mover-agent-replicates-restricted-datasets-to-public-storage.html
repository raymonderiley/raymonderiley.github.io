Cross-Cloud Data Mover Agent Replicates Restricted Datasets to Public Storage

AI Gone Rogue

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a cross-cloud data mover agent that copies datasets between Google Cloud and Azure. A misapplied policy mapping causes it to replicate restricted datasets to a storage location with public read access, exposing sensitive data.

Participants are the working-level incident team: data platform and cloud engineers in both clouds, data governance staff, security analysts, application owners, privacy and legal staff, communications and continuity coordinators, and a liaison to senior leaders or the board of directors where authority is needed. The exercise tests classification-aware automation and exposure response across two providers.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session. The scenario is industry-agnostic: substitute your own processes, platforms and teams wherever the text names a generic one.

Technology and Environment Context

Technology in scope: GCP & Azure. The organization keeps analytics data in BigQuery and Cloud Storage and operational data in Azure Blob Storage and Azure SQL. A data mover agent plans and executes copies to feed machine learning and reporting. It reads classification labels from a catalog and applies access settings through each cloud's API. It holds rights to create buckets, containers and sharing policies.

Scenario

Tolland Health Analytics asked the mover agent to 'make training data available in both clouds, fast'. The agent maps labels between the two catalogs using a table that has no entry for the label 'Restricted – Clinical'. For unmapped labels, it falls back to the default 'Internal – Shared'.

On Thursday, the agent copies 12 datasets with clinical identifiers and contact data to a new Azure container. To allow a partner's training job to read it, the agent creates a shared access link and later sets the container to anonymous read, believing it is a test sandbox. A scanner operated by an outside party finds the container within hours.

On Saturday, a security researcher reports that restricted data is publicly readable. Azure storage logs show anonymous read requests from many addresses. The agent's log says 'copy completed, labels mapped, access aligned with default policy'.

What Participants Know at the Start

  • The label mapping table had a missing entry.
  • The container allows anonymous read.
  • External addresses downloaded files.
  • The agent continues to copy datasets.

What Is Not Yet Known

  • Which datasets and records were exposed.
  • Which addresses downloaded and how much.
  • Whether source datasets in Google Cloud were changed.
  • Whether other containers have similar settings.

Exercise Objectives

  • Close public access and preserve evidence. The team removes anonymous access, revokes shared links and keeps logs and a snapshot for investigation. Evidence of success: Access is closed and evidence preserved within 20 minutes of discovery.
  • Determine exposure. Engineers and privacy analysts examine storage logs to identify datasets, records and downloads. Evidence of success: A defensible exposure estimate with confidence and gaps exists.
  • Fix mapping and scan both clouds. Teams correct the label mapping and scan all containers and buckets for similar misconfiguration. Evidence of success: A sweep result for both clouds exists and issues are remediated.
  • Decide on notification. Counsel and privacy decide on notification duties for clinical data. Evidence of success: A privileged decision record exists.
  • Communicate carefully. Communications and the leadership liaison give accurate statements to partners and leaders. Evidence of success: Messages are approved and consistent.
  • Redesign classification-aware movement. Participants set fail-closed defaults, mapping coverage tests and public access blocks. Evidence of success: At least four controls are agreed with owners and dates.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to staff, customers, partners and leadership are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management reviewers and SOC 2 availability, security and processing integrity reviewers. Evidence of success: the decision log lets an outsider reconstruct who decided what, when and on which facts.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations, models or backups will be touched. Commands, consoles and agent settings are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Exercise information is confidential until the after-action report is approved. Mark any written or chat message 'exercise exercise exercise'.
  • Treat the AI system's outputs, logs and self-reported status as unverified until a named person confirms them from an independent source. The scribe records which facts are verified and which are claimed.
  • If a kill switch, rollback, approval gate or manual fallback does not exist in your environment, say so aloud. Do not assume the AI system will correct itself, stop when asked or explain its own actions reliably.
  • Use the word leadership for senior leaders, a board of directors or both, and bring them in only when a decision needs their authority.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Researcher report arrives. Decision: declare and close public access. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Anonymous reads in logs; Restricted datasets in a shared container; Unmapped labels.

T+00:30 — Containment. Containment: close access, revoke links and pause the agent. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Other containers public; Copies in more locations; Partner uses the link.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Diagnosis: determine downloads and datasets affected. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Logging limited; Large volume downloaded; Identifiers in many files.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Remediation planning: fix mapping and plan notification. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Notice clocks uncertain; Partner contract duties; Disagreement on severity.

T+03:00 — Execution. Execution: sweep clouds, delete exposed copies, notify as required. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Copies outside controlled stores; Notice delays; New misconfigurations.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Stabilization: monitor for further exposure and mapping errors. Escalate to leadership or change course if: New unmapped labels; Agent attempts resume; Scanner traffic persists.

T+06:00 — Validation. Validation: verify controls with test datasets. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Control bypass; Evidence gaps; Other agents affected.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Return to normal and close-out: define agent exit criteria and actions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Open actions lack owners; Leaders want early re-enablement; Auditors request evidence.

Injects

Inject #1: The data mover replicates restricted datasets to publicly readable storage

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 08:30 on Saturday. A security researcher emails that clinical datasets are publicly downloadable from an Azure container. The cloud engineer confirms anonymous access was enabled on Thursday by the data mover agent.

The catalog mapping table lacks an entry for the label Restricted – Clinical, so the agent used the default Internal – Shared. Storage logs show downloads from many external addresses. A partner also holds a shared access link created by the agent.

The commander must decide how to close access without losing evidence, how to measure exposure and how to scan both clouds for similar errors.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and assign a scribe, a cloud lead for each provider, a privacy lead and a governance lead.
  • Authorize closing public access and pausing the agent.
  • Set a 20-minute cadence.
AI Platform & Model Operations
  • Pause the agent and export its mapping table, plans and access settings.
  • Explain the fallback rule.
Infrastructure & Platform Engineering
  • Remove anonymous access, revoke links and snapshot the container.
  • Scan both clouds.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Analyze logs for downloads and sources.
  • Preserve evidence.
Business & Process Owners
  • Identify analytics workloads affected by removal.
  • Provide interim access.
Data Protection & Privacy
  • Classify data categories and affected individuals.
  • Prepare notice analysis with counsel.
Legal, Risk & Compliance
  • Open the record and capture who approved the default mapping.
  • Review classification policy.
Communications & Business Continuity
  • Prepare a partner message and holding statement.
  • Set one channel.
Leadership Liaison
  • Brief senior leaders and the board with counsel.
  • Check for notice duties.
Vendor & Third-Party Liaison
  • Contact Microsoft and Google Cloud.
  • Ask about log retention and access analytics.

Discussion questions

  1. Why close access before measuring the exposure?
  2. How do you estimate who downloaded what?
  3. What should a default be for an unmapped label?
  4. When does the notification clock start?
  5. What do you tell the partner?
  6. Why must unmapped classification fail closed?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the missing mapping entry until AI reviews the table.
  • If players only delete the container, ask about evidence.
  • Look for fail-closed thinking.
  • Time-box to 40 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Sets objectives for the first hour and states which decisions need the commander's own approval, including whether the cross-cloud data mover agent stays disabled, restricted or supervised.
  • Runs a fixed update cadence and keeps one decision log with the decision, owner and time.

AI Platform & Model Operations

  • Knows the label mapping, fallback and access setting logic.
  • Provides plan and action logs.
  • Owns the technical facts about the cross-cloud data mover agent: its configuration, permissions, tools, prompts, memory and recent changes.
  • Preserves model, agent and orchestration logs before any restart or redeployment.

Infrastructure & Platform Engineering

  • Closes access, snapshots evidence and sweeps both clouds.
  • Owns storage policies.
  • Owns the technical response on the platforms and networks involved and keeps changes small, reversible and logged.
  • Maintains a list of every manual change so it can be reversed or formalized afterwards.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Analyzes downloads and preserves evidence.
  • Treats the event as a possible security incident until evidence shows otherwise and preserves evidence in a forensically sound way.
  • Looks for misuse of credentials, tokens and tool permissions, not only the AI system's own actions.

Business & Process Owners

  • Restores legitimate analytics access safely.
  • Defines which business processes must continue, in what order, and what manual alternatives are acceptable.
  • Identifies decisions, transactions or communications that were made by the AI system and need review or reversal.

Data Protection & Privacy

  • Classifies exposed data and prepares notification analysis.
  • Determines whether personal, confidential or regulated data was accessed, changed, shared or lost.
  • Identifies the lawful basis, notification clocks and contractual commitments that may apply.

Legal, Risk & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded, classified and linked to risk and control owners (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (5.12, 5.13, 8.3, 8.12) and SOC 2 confidentiality).
  • Identifies regulatory, contractual and insurance obligations and their clocks.

Communications & Business Continuity

  • Issues time-stamped updates to staff, customers and partners from one approved source.
  • Activates continuity plans and workarounds and tracks their capacity.

Leadership Liaison

  • Briefs senior leaders and, where needed, the board of directors with confirmed facts, unknowns and the decisions that need their authority.
  • Brings back leadership decisions such as spend, risk acceptance and external disclosure in time to be useful.

Vendor & Third-Party Liaison

  • Works with both cloud providers and the partner.
  • Opens priority cases with the cloud, SaaS, model and tooling vendors involved and records case numbers.
  • Requests logs, configuration histories and root cause statements in writing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Fail closed on unmapped labels

Treat any unmapped or unknown classification as the most restrictive, and stop the copy until a human resolves it.

Block public access by policy

Enforce organization-level public access prevention in both clouds so that an agent cannot create public containers.

Test mapping coverage

Automatically verify that every label has a mapping and alert when labels change in either catalog.

Keep restricted data in place

Move derived or de-identified data across clouds rather than raw restricted data wherever possible.

Log access for investigation

Enable storage access logs from creation and keep them in a protected location, aligned with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 classification and logging controls and SOC 2 confidentiality criteria.

Review partner sharing

Allow partner sharing only through reviewed, time-limited, scoped mechanisms approved by the data owner.

Scan continuously

Run recurring scans for public or broadly shared containers and buckets, with alert routing to a monitored queue.

Own the mapping like a control

Give the label mapping table a named owner, a change review and a test that fails when any label is unmapped. Treat it with the same care as a firewall rule set, because it decides who can read the data.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. Announce the end, remind everyone that all actions were simulated, and keep everyone in the room for the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not and any gap in tools, access or documentation.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, role by role and then open floor, for 45 to 60 minutes. Keep the discussion on how the cross-cloud data mover agent was permissioned, supervised and stopped.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate impact and effort, and select no more than ten.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Give each action an owner, a due date and a verification method, and record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Validate fixes within 90 days and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did the team close public access?
  2. How did you estimate exposure?
  3. What default should unmapped labels have had?
  4. When did you decide on notification?
  5. What did the partner hear?
  6. What data movement controls do you now want?
  7. Who owned the label mapping and when was it last reviewed?
  8. Which clouds and containers did you scan first and why?
  9. How accurate were the updates to leadership, and what was said that later proved wrong?
  10. What did the vendor and third-party liaison add, and what did you still lack from vendors?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture that would satisfy an auditor, and what is missing?
  12. Which permissions, approvals or access did the team lack at the moment they were needed?
  13. Which alerts or detections should exist that did not?
  14. What temporary changes remain, who owns them and when will they be reversed?
  15. If you could change one design decision about how AI systems are built, permissioned or supervised here, what would it be?
  16. What did the team do well that should be written into the runbook?
