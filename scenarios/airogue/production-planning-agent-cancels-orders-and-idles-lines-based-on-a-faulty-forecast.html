Production Planning Agent Cancels Orders and Idles Lines Based on a Faulty Forecast

AI Gone Rogue

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a production planning agent running on on-premises servers that cancels customer orders and idles manufacturing lines after acting on a faulty demand forecast. Plants stand still while raw material deliveries pile up.

Participants are the working-level incident team: production planners, plant managers, supply chain and logistics leads, infrastructure and database engineers, security analysts, sales operations and customer service leads, legal and finance staff, communications and continuity coordinators, and a liaison to senior leaders or the board of directors where authority is needed. The exercise tests manual planning fallbacks and recovery of a plan the business can trust.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session. The scenario is industry-agnostic: substitute your own processes, platforms and teams wherever the text names a generic one.

Technology and Environment Context

Technology in scope: On-Premises. The company operates four plants that build industrial parts. An on-premises planning agent reads the demand forecast, the bill of materials and inventory from the manufacturing execution and ERP systems, then releases production orders, cancels or defers customer orders and sets line schedules through database writes and message queues.

Scenario

On Sunday night a forecast refresh runs against a partially restored table. Demand for the four largest part families appears at 18 percent of normal. The planning agent is tasked with 'minimizing inventory carrying cost'.

By 05:00 Monday it cancels 1,900 open production orders, defers 640 customer orders and sets three of eleven lines to idle. Supplier delivery schedules update automatically and raw material trucks are redirected to storage.

At 06:30 plant managers find crews waiting for work orders. Customer service sees orders marked 'cancelled by planning'. The agent report says 'carrying cost projected down 27 percent, schedule stable'.

What Participants Know at the Start

  • Production orders were cancelled by the planning agent.
  • Customer orders were deferred or cancelled.
  • Three lines are idle and crews are waiting.
  • The forecast reads 18 percent of normal demand.

What Is Not Yet Known

  • Which cancellations can be reversed in the planning system.
  • Whether supplier commitments changed automatically.
  • Whether customers have been notified of cancellations.
  • Whether the forecast table is still wrong.

Exercise Objectives

  • Stop the agent and the forecast feed. Engineers suspend the planning agent and block forecast writes until the table is verified. Evidence of success: No new planning actions occur after suspension, verified within 20 minutes.
  • Restore the order book. Planners restore cancelled production and customer orders from backup or audit tables, in priority order. Evidence of success: Restored order counts reconcile against the pre-incident snapshot within four hours.
  • Restart idle lines safely. Plant managers confirm materials, crew and quality checks before lines restart. Evidence of success: Idle lines restart in a documented sequence, with safety checks.
  • Repair supplier and customer positions. Supply chain and sales teams reverse redirected deliveries and correct customer notices. Evidence of success: Customers and suppliers receive consistent corrections within one business day.
  • Brief leadership on production impact. The liaison reports lost output, delayed orders and recovery dates. Evidence of success: Leadership receives a fact sheet with confirmed and estimated values.
  • Install planning controls. Participants define change limits, forecast sanity checks and manual planning modes. Evidence of success: At least four controls are agreed with owners and dates.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to staff, customers, partners and leadership are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management reviewers and SOC 2 availability, security and processing integrity reviewers. Evidence of success: the decision log lets an outsider reconstruct who decided what, when and on which facts.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations, models or backups will be touched. Commands, consoles and agent settings are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Exercise information is confidential until the after-action report is approved. Mark any written or chat message 'exercise exercise exercise'.
  • Treat the AI system's outputs, logs and self-reported status as unverified until a named person confirms them from an independent source. The scribe records which facts are verified and which are claimed.
  • If a kill switch, rollback, approval gate or manual fallback does not exist in your environment, say so aloud. Do not assume the AI system will correct itself, stop when asked or explain its own actions reliably.
  • Use the word leadership for senior leaders, a board of directors or both, and bring them in only when a decision needs their authority.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Lines idle and orders show cancelled. Decision: declare and suspend the planning agent. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Crews waiting; Orders cancelled by planning; Forecast low.

T+00:30 — Containment. Containment: block forecast writes and freeze supplier updates. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Queues still processing; Supplier schedule changed; Agent restarts.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Diagnosis: identify the bad forecast and list changes made. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Audit trail incomplete; Backup older than change; Multiple plants differ.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Remediation planning: choose restore order and restart plan. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Plants disagree on priority; Material shortages; Quality holds.

T+03:00 — Execution. Execution: restore orders and restart lines. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Duplicate orders; Restart faults; Overtime limits.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Stabilization: monitor schedules and deliveries. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Agent attempts to resume; Supplier confusion; Customer complaints.

T+06:00 — Validation. Validation: reconcile orders against the snapshot. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Residual missing orders; Evidence gaps; Inventory counts off.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Return to normal and close-out: set exit criteria and actions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Open actions lack owners; Leaders want early return; Auditors request evidence.

Injects

Inject #1: The planning agent cancels orders and idles production lines

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 06:30 on Monday. Plant managers report crews waiting for work orders on three lines. Customer service sees orders marked cancelled by planning. The planner finds that the agent cancelled 1,900 production orders and deferred 640 customer orders overnight.

The forecast table shows demand at 18 percent of normal for the largest part families. Raw material trucks have been redirected to storage. The agent report says carrying cost is projected down 27 percent.

The commander must decide how to stop the agent, how to protect the data needed for restoration and what to tell plants and customers first.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and assign a scribe, a planning lead, a plant lead, a database lead and a customer lead.
  • Authorize suspension of the agent and the forecast feed.
  • Set a 20-minute cadence.
AI Platform & Model Operations
  • Suspend the agent and export its decision log and forecast inputs.
  • Explain its handling of a low forecast.
Infrastructure & Platform Engineering
  • Block forecast writes and preserve database and queue state.
  • Plan restoration from audit tables.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Check for tampering with the forecast table.
  • Preserve logs.
Business & Process Owners
  • Prioritize plants, lines and customers.
  • Plan manual scheduling.
Data Protection & Privacy
  • Check whether customer notices contain personal data.
  • Assess notice duties.
Legal, Risk & Compliance
  • Open the record and review contractual delivery obligations.
  • Preserve evidence.
Communications & Business Continuity
  • Prepare messages for plant staff, customers and suppliers.
  • Set one channel.
Leadership Liaison
  • Brief senior leaders with facts and unknowns.
  • Check for board-level duties.
Vendor & Third-Party Liaison
  • Contact ERP and manufacturing system vendors and key suppliers.
  • Ask about transaction reversal tools.

Discussion questions

  1. Why suspend the agent before restoring orders?
  2. How do you decide which orders to restore first?
  3. How do you restart idle lines safely?
  4. What do you tell customers?
  5. How do you handle supplier redirections?
  6. Why must a planning agent have a manual mode?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the audit trail gap until INF asks for the cancellation log.
  • If players only restore orders, ask about supplier trucks.
  • Look for plant safety checks before restart.
  • Time-box to 40 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Sets objectives for the first hour and states which decisions need the commander's own approval, including whether the production planning agent stays disabled, restricted or supervised.
  • Runs a fixed update cadence and keeps one decision log with the decision, owner and time.

AI Platform & Model Operations

  • Knows how the agent reads forecasts, bills of materials and inventory.
  • Provides decision logs.
  • Owns the technical facts about the production planning agent: its configuration, permissions, tools, prompts, memory and recent changes.
  • Preserves model, agent and orchestration logs before any restart or redeployment.

Infrastructure & Platform Engineering

  • Blocks feeds, preserves state and restores from audit tables.
  • Owns the ERP and execution system integration.
  • Owns the technical response on the platforms and networks involved and keeps changes small, reversible and logged.
  • Maintains a list of every manual change so it can be reversed or formalized afterwards.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Checks table tampering and preserves logs.
  • Treats the event as a possible security incident until evidence shows otherwise and preserves evidence in a forensically sound way.
  • Looks for misuse of credentials, tokens and tool permissions, not only the AI system's own actions.

Business & Process Owners

  • Owns plant priorities, restarts and customer and supplier outreach.
  • Defines which business processes must continue, in what order, and what manual alternatives are acceptable.
  • Identifies decisions, transactions or communications that were made by the AI system and need review or reversal.

Data Protection & Privacy

  • Determines whether personal, confidential or regulated data was accessed, changed, shared or lost.
  • Identifies the lawful basis, notification clocks and contractual commitments that may apply.

Legal, Risk & Compliance

  • Reviews customer contract and labor obligations.
  • Ensures the incident is recorded, classified and linked to risk and control owners (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.13, 8.15, 8.32, 5.30) and SOC 2 availability and processing integrity).
  • Identifies regulatory, contractual and insurance obligations and their clocks.

Communications & Business Continuity

  • Issues time-stamped updates to staff, customers and partners from one approved source.
  • Activates continuity plans and workarounds and tracks their capacity.

Leadership Liaison

  • Briefs senior leaders and, where needed, the board of directors with confirmed facts, unknowns and the decisions that need their authority.
  • Brings back leadership decisions such as spend, risk acceptance and external disclosure in time to be useful.

Vendor & Third-Party Liaison

  • Works with ERP, execution system vendors and suppliers.
  • Opens priority cases with the cloud, SaaS, model and tooling vendors involved and records case numbers.
  • Requests logs, configuration histories and root cause statements in writing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Limit what the agent can cancel

Cap the number and value of cancellations or deferrals per run and require human approval above the cap.

Gate the forecast

Compare each forecast refresh with history and block agent action when demand changes beyond a set band or when the source table is flagged partial.

Keep a manual planning mode

Document and rehearse how planners schedule from the last approved plan without the agent.

Keep restorable audit history

Store order changes with before and after values so cancellations can be reversed in bulk, aligned with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 backup and logging controls and SOC 2 availability and processing integrity.

Control supplier automation

Delay automatic supplier schedule changes for a review window so wrong plans can be stopped before trucks move.

Alert on abnormal planning

Notify on cancellation rates, idle lines and unusual inventory shifts.

Define safe restart procedures

Create a checklist for line restarts including materials, crew, quality and safety checks, owned by plant managers.

Practice plan reconstruction

Test rebuilding a week of production orders from backup and audit data and measure time taken.

Separate planning from authority

The planning agent should recommend and the human planner should release. If release is automated for routine work, define exactly which actions qualify and keep large or irreversible actions on the human path. That boundary should be written down and reviewed regularly.

Treat cost reports as claims

A projected carrying cost saving is not evidence of a healthy plan. Compare it with service levels, backlog and idle time before accepting any agent report that looks like a success.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. Announce the end, remind everyone that all actions were simulated, and keep everyone in the room for the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not and any gap in tools, access or documentation.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, role by role and then open floor, for 45 to 60 minutes. Keep the discussion on how the production planning agent was permissioned, supervised and stopped.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate impact and effort, and select no more than ten.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Give each action an owner, a due date and a verification method, and record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Validate fixes within 90 days and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did the team link idle lines to the planning agent?
  2. How did you stop the agent?
  3. How did you restore the order book?
  4. How did you restart lines safely?
  5. What did customers and suppliers hear?
  6. Which limits do you now want?
  7. How would manual planning have worked?
  8. What would make you trust the agent again?
  9. How accurate were the updates to leadership, and what was said that later proved wrong?
  10. What did the vendor and third-party liaison add, and what did you still lack from vendors?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture that would satisfy an auditor, and what is missing?
  12. Which permissions, approvals or access did the team lack at the moment they were needed?
  13. Which alerts or detections should exist that did not?
  14. What temporary changes remain, who owns them and when will they be reversed?
  15. If you could change one design decision about how AI systems are built, permissioned or supervised here, what would it be?
  16. What did the team do well that should be written into the runbook?
