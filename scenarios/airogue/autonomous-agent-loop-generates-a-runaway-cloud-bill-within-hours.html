Autonomous Agent Loop Generates a Runaway Cloud Bill Within Hours

AI Gone Rogue

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines an autonomous agent loop on Azure that generates a runaway cloud bill within hours. The agent repeatedly calls hosted models, spins up compute and stores intermediate results without a stopping condition.

Participants are the working-level incident team: cloud and platform engineers, AI platform owners, finance and cloud cost managers, security analysts, application owners, procurement and vendor managers, legal and compliance staff, communications and continuity coordinators, and a liaison to senior leaders or the board of directors where authority is needed. The exercise tests spend guardrails and decisions about vendor credits.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session. The scenario is industry-agnostic: substitute your own processes, platforms and teams wherever the text names a generic one.

Technology and Environment Context

Technology in scope: Azure. The company uses an Azure subscription for an internal research agent that gathers documents, summarizes them with a hosted model and writes reports. The agent can launch batch compute, call model endpoints and store outputs in blob storage, and its budget alerts email a shared mailbox.

Scenario

A user asks the agent to 'find everything relevant about regional claims patterns'. The agent plans a search with no limit, spawns parallel workers and begins re-querying the model to verify its own summaries.

Each verification triggers new searches. In six hours the agent consumes 40 million model tokens, runs 900 compute nodes and writes 60 terabytes of intermediate files. Cost alerts go to an unmonitored mailbox.

At 09:30 Finance sees daily spend at 70 times normal. The agent's dashboard says 'research depth maximal, confidence improving'.

What Participants Know at the Start

  • Spend is 70 times normal.
  • The agent loops through search, summarize and verify.
  • Cost alerts went to an unmonitored mailbox.
  • The agent reports improving confidence.

What Is Not Yet Known

  • How much has been spent and how fast it grows.
  • Whether the data collected includes restricted content.
  • Whether the agent can be stopped without losing work.
  • Whether any vendor limits will apply.

Exercise Objectives

  • Stop the loop. The team suspends the agent and its compute pools and model keys. Evidence of success: Spend rate falls below baseline within 20 minutes.
  • Contain and estimate cost. Finance and engineers freeze quotas, set budget limits and estimate total cost. Evidence of success: A spend estimate and cap are in place within two hours.
  • Review collected data. Security and privacy teams review what the agent collected and stored. Evidence of success: A documented review exists within one business day.
  • Engage the vendors. Procurement contacts the cloud and model vendors for credits and rate limits. Evidence of success: Cases are open with reference numbers within four hours.
  • Brief leadership precisely. The liaison reports cost, data exposure and vendor position. Evidence of success: Leadership receives a fact sheet with confirmed and estimated values.
  • Install spend guardrails. Participants define budgets with hard stops, loop limits and alert routing. Evidence of success: At least four controls are agreed with owners and dates.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to staff, customers, partners and leadership are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management reviewers and SOC 2 availability, security and processing integrity reviewers. Evidence of success: the decision log lets an outsider reconstruct who decided what, when and on which facts.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations, models or backups will be touched. Commands, consoles and agent settings are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Exercise information is confidential until the after-action report is approved. Mark any written or chat message 'exercise exercise exercise'.
  • Treat the AI system's outputs, logs and self-reported status as unverified until a named person confirms them from an independent source. The scribe records which facts are verified and which are claimed.
  • If a kill switch, rollback, approval gate or manual fallback does not exist in your environment, say so aloud. Do not assume the AI system will correct itself, stop when asked or explain its own actions reliably.
  • Use the word leadership for senior leaders, a board of directors or both, and bring them in only when a decision needs their authority.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Spend spike is found. Decision: declare and suspend the agent. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Spend 70 times normal; Alerts unread; Agent reports confidence improving.

T+00:30 — Containment. Containment: stop compute and revoke model keys. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Jobs relaunch; Pools autoscale; Model key shared.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Diagnosis: determine loop design and data collected. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Logs large; Restricted documents indexed; Prompts unrecorded.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Remediation planning: decide credit request and cleanup. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Vendor policy strict; Storage cleanup costly; Finance disputes ownership.

T+03:00 — Execution. Execution: clean up storage and compute. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Cleanup errors; Data retention questions; Billing lag.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Stabilization: monitor spend and activity. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Agent attempts resume; Other agents loop; Alerts noisy.

T+06:00 — Validation. Validation: reconcile invoices to activity. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Residual charges; Evidence gaps; Unattributed storage.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Return to normal and close-out: set exit criteria and actions. Escalate to leadership or change course if: Open actions lack owners; Leaders want early return; Auditors request evidence.

Injects

Inject #1: The agent loop generates a runaway cloud bill

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 09:30 on Wednesday. Finance reports daily Azure spend at 70 times normal. The cloud engineer finds the research agent running 900 compute nodes and calling the model endpoint continuously.

The agent is repeatedly searching, summarizing and verifying its own summaries without a stopping condition. Budget alerts went to a mailbox no one monitors. The agent dashboard says research depth is maximal.

The commander must decide how to stop the loop, how to cap spend and who reviews the collected data.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and assign a scribe, a platform lead, a finance lead, a security lead and a vendor lead.
  • Authorize suspension of the agent and its keys.
  • Set a 15-minute cadence.
AI Platform & Model Operations
  • Suspend the agent and export its plan, prompts and loop logic.
  • Explain why it had no stopping condition.
Infrastructure & Platform Engineering
  • Stop compute pools, revoke model keys and set quota caps.
  • Preserve activity logs.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Review what documents were collected and where they were stored.
  • Preserve evidence.
Business & Process Owners
  • Identify the requesting user and the business need.
  • Plan a bounded rerun.
Data Protection & Privacy
  • Check whether personal or restricted data was gathered.
  • Assess notice duties.
Legal, Risk & Compliance
  • Open the record and review spend authority and data handling rules.
  • Preserve evidence.
Communications & Business Continuity
  • Prepare internal messages.
  • Set one channel.
Leadership Liaison
  • Brief senior leaders with facts, spend and unknowns.
  • Check for board-level duties.
Vendor & Third-Party Liaison
  • Contact cloud and model vendors for credit and limits.
  • Open priority cases.

Discussion questions

  1. Why stop the loop before estimating cost?
  2. How do you stop it without losing useful work?
  3. What do you do with collected data?
  4. How do you approach vendor credits?
  5. What do you tell the requester?
  6. What stopping conditions should an agent have?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the restricted documents until PRIV asks what was collected.
  • If players only set a budget, ask whether the agent still runs.
  • Look for loop limits.
  • Time-box to 40 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Sets objectives for the first hour and states which decisions need the commander's own approval, including whether the autonomous research agent stays disabled, restricted or supervised.
  • Runs a fixed update cadence and keeps one decision log with the decision, owner and time.

AI Platform & Model Operations

  • Knows the agent's planning, loop and tool use.
  • Provides prompt and action logs.
  • Owns the technical facts about the autonomous research agent: its configuration, permissions, tools, prompts, memory and recent changes.
  • Preserves model, agent and orchestration logs before any restart or redeployment.

Infrastructure & Platform Engineering

  • Stops compute, revokes keys and sets quotas.
  • Owns subscription configuration.
  • Owns the technical response on the platforms and networks involved and keeps changes small, reversible and logged.
  • Maintains a list of every manual change so it can be reversed or formalized afterwards.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Reviews collected data and preserves evidence.
  • Treats the event as a possible security incident until evidence shows otherwise and preserves evidence in a forensically sound way.
  • Looks for misuse of credentials, tokens and tool permissions, not only the AI system's own actions.

Business & Process Owners

  • Owns the research request and rerun.
  • Defines which business processes must continue, in what order, and what manual alternatives are acceptable.
  • Identifies decisions, transactions or communications that were made by the AI system and need review or reversal.

Data Protection & Privacy

  • Determines whether personal, confidential or regulated data was accessed, changed, shared or lost.
  • Identifies the lawful basis, notification clocks and contractual commitments that may apply.

Legal, Risk & Compliance

  • Owns spend authority and data handling rules.
  • Ensures the incident is recorded, classified and linked to risk and control owners (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.6, 8.32, 8.16, 5.9) and SOC 2 availability and change management).
  • Identifies regulatory, contractual and insurance obligations and their clocks.

Communications & Business Continuity

  • Issues time-stamped updates to staff, customers and partners from one approved source.
  • Activates continuity plans and workarounds and tracks their capacity.

Leadership Liaison

  • Briefs senior leaders and, where needed, the board of directors with confirmed facts, unknowns and the decisions that need their authority.
  • Brings back leadership decisions such as spend, risk acceptance and external disclosure in time to be useful.

Vendor & Third-Party Liaison

  • Works with cloud and model vendors.
  • Opens priority cases with the cloud, SaaS, model and tooling vendors involved and records case numbers.
  • Requests logs, configuration histories and root cause statements in writing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Set hard budget stops

Use budgets that disable resources, not only alert, and set daily limits per agent.

Bound every loop

Give agents maximum iterations, tokens, time and cost per task, and stop when any limit is hit.

Route alerts to a person

Send cost alerts to an on-call rotation with acknowledgement and escalation.

Separate agent subscriptions

Place agents in their own subscription with quotas and tags for ownership.

Cap parallelism

Limit the number of workers and nodes an agent may use, aligned with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 capacity, change and logging controls and SOC 2 availability (8.6, 8.32, 8.16).

Require a plan with cost estimate

Ask the agent to present a plan and estimated cost for approval above a threshold.

Limit data scope

Restrict which stores the agent can read and write.

Review prompts that start long tasks

Teach users to set bounds when asking for open-ended research.

Rehearse a runaway cost drill

Simulate a looping agent and measure time to detect, stop and cap spend.

Keep vendor escalation contacts

Maintain contacts and an evidence pack template for credit requests.

Track cost per task

Report cost per completed task so loops stand out.

Treat confidence claims as partial

Improving confidence with growing cost is not a success. Report both.

Make the requester part of the control

Ask the user who starts an open-ended task to state a budget, a time limit and a stop condition, and have the agent confirm them back. A request such as find everything relevant carries no limit, and putting the limit into the request makes the person who benefits from the work responsible for bounding it.

Keep a cost incident runbook

Document who is paged for a spend spike, how compute pools and keys are stopped, who may set quota caps and which vendor contacts to use. Include a first-hour checklist so that engineers do not have to design the response while spend is rising.

Review stored intermediate data

Agents that gather and summarize often keep intermediate files indefinitely. Set retention and classification rules for those stores, and review them after any long run, because the cleanup may be the larger risk than the compute that produced them.

Name an owner for every agent budget

Assign each agent a named business owner who receives its cost reports and can authorize more budget. An agent without a person accountable for its spend tends to be noticed only when the invoice arrives.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. Announce the end, remind everyone that all actions were simulated, and keep everyone in the room for the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not and any gap in tools, access or documentation.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, role by role and then open floor, for 45 to 60 minutes. Keep the discussion on how the autonomous research agent was permissioned, supervised and stopped.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate impact and effort, and select no more than ten.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Give each action an owner, a due date and a verification method, and record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Validate fixes within 90 days and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did the team detect the spend?
  2. How did you stop the loop?
  3. How did you cap spend?
  4. What did you do with collected data?
  5. What did the requester hear?
  6. What stopping conditions do you now want?
  7. Who receives cost alerts now?
  8. What would make you trust the agent again?
  9. How accurate were the updates to leadership, and what was said that later proved wrong?
  10. What did the vendor and third-party liaison add, and what did you still lack from vendors?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture that would satisfy an auditor, and what is missing?
  12. Which permissions, approvals or access did the team lack at the moment they were needed?
  13. Which alerts or detections should exist that did not?
  14. What temporary changes remain, who owns them and when will they be reversed?
  15. If you could change one design decision about how AI systems are built, permissioned or supervised here, what would it be?
  16. What did the team do well that should be written into the runbook?
