Azure Managed Disks Ultra Disk Latency Spike and IO Freeze

Azure Cloud Resiliency

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines an Azure Ultra Disk latency spike and IO freeze under a high-frequency trading and order matching database, where a platform issue and a mis-sized throughput setting cause IO to stall, transactions queue, database engines enter recovery risk and failover options are limited by zonal placement.

Participants are working-level: cloud storage and infrastructure engineers, database and application engineers, application owners, the service desk, cloud operations and monitoring staff, the Microsoft support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of disk IOPS and throughput provisioning, VM-level caps, latency symptoms, snapshots, zonal constraints and database crash recovery.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Granite Markets runs a matching engine database on a VM with Ultra Disks provisioned for 80,000 IOPS and 1,200 MB/s. The VM size limits disk throughput to 1,000 MB/s. A synchronous replica sits in another zone using a premium SSD tier for cost reasons. Snapshots run every 6 hours.

At 09:35 on Wednesday, a market open surge pushes IO to the VM cap. Disk latency jumps from 0.4 ms to 60 ms, then to multi-second freezes. At the same time, Azure Service Health posts an advisory for storage performance degradation in the zone. Database commits stall, the engine reports log write waits, order acknowledgments exceed risk limits and the matching engine halts new orders.

What the team does not yet know: the VM-level throughput cap is the first bottleneck, increasing disk provisioned performance would not help, a resize of the VM needs a restart, the synchronous replica cannot keep the commit rate with its lower IOPS tier, and a forced failover could lose a few seconds of committed transactions if the replica lags.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize IO saturation versus platform degradation. Participants read disk, VM and database metrics to separate a self-inflicted cap from a platform fault.
  • Stabilize transactions. The team reduces IO demand, drains queues and avoids actions that risk database corruption.
  • Decide on resizing or failover. Engineers weigh VM resize, replica promotion and waiting, with explicit data loss and downtime estimates.
  • Preserve data integrity. Participants plan consistency checks and reconciliation of in-flight orders.
  • Communicate with participants. The team informs trading venues, clients and risk teams with accurate status.
  • Improve storage resilience. Participants review IO sizing, VM caps, replica tiering, zone strategy and latency alerting.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • State the data loss and downtime estimate for every failover or resize option.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. IO stalls and order halts lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Load is reduced and orders are safely paused.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Bottleneck and platform status are diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A resize, failover or wait decision is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. The chosen option is executed.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Latency and throughput stabilize.

T+06:00 — Validation. Data is validated and reconciled.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Inject #1: Disk latency freezes the matching engine

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 09:50. Disk latency on the matching engine database is at 60 ms with multi-second freezes. The database reports log write waits and the matching engine has stopped accepting orders.

The infrastructure engineer finds VM throughput at its 1,000 MB/s cap while the disk is provisioned for 1,200 MB/s. Service Health lists a storage performance advisory in the zone.

The synchronous replica is in another zone on a lower IOPS tier and lags by 8 seconds. Snapshots are 6 hours old. A resize needs a restart.

Facilitator provides: disk and VM metrics, database wait stats, replica lag chart and the Service Health text.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Authorize pausing order acceptance.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Cloud Storage & Infrastructure Engineering (Managed Disks)
  • Confirm the cap and platform status.
  • Plan resize options.
  • Plan snapshot use.
Database & Application Engineering
  • Assess database state and replica lag.
  • Plan promotion with loss estimate.
  • Prepare integrity checks.
Application Owners & Service Desk
  • Decide on pause and participant messaging.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Prepare manual procedures.
Cloud Operations & Monitoring
  • Build the dashboard.
  • Monitor platform status.
  • Preserve logs.
Microsoft Support & TAM Liaison
  • Open a case.
  • Ask about limits.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform leadership and venues.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Prepare regulator contact.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about conduct obligations.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. How do you tell a VM cap from a platform fault?
  2. Is it safe to resize while freezes continue?
  3. What are the data loss and downtime numbers for promoting the replica?
  4. What do you do about in-flight orders?
  5. What should participants and venues be told?
  6. Why did the replica use a lower tier?
  7. How do you protect database integrity during freezes?
  8. What must be validated before reopening?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the cap versus provisioned mismatch until T1 reads VM metrics.
  • If players reboot the VM, ask about crash recovery.
  • Look for explicit loss numbers.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes data integrity before speed and sets the decision rule for failover.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes resize, failover and market notifications.
  • Decides on resumption.

Cloud Storage & Infrastructure Engineering (Managed Disks)

  • Reads disk latency, IOPS, throughput and VM cap metrics.
  • Plans VM resize or disk reconfiguration.
  • Checks Service Health and zone status.
  • Plans snapshot use.

Database & Application Engineering

  • Assesses database state, log writes and replica lag.
  • Plans promotion or recovery with loss estimates.
  • Prepares integrity checks.
  • Reconciles orders.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners decide when to pause matching and what to tell participants.
  • The service desk logs member calls.
  • Owners prepare manual procedures.

  • Monitors platform status.
  • Preserves logs.

Microsoft Support & TAM Liaison

  • Asks about throughput limits and zone health.
  • Requests guidance on resize with minimal downtime.
  • Records guidance.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for market operations and informs leadership and venues.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates regulators contact preparation.
  • Tracks impact.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.28, 8.6 capacity management; SOC 2 availability and processing integrity).
  • Assesses market conduct obligations.
  • Captures evidence of decisions.
  • Logs findings on capacity design.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Size the whole IO path, not just the disk: align VM throughput and IOPS caps with disk provisioning, test at peak with headroom and alert on latency before saturation.

Keep replicas at parity for the role they must play: same IO tier, tested promotion and clear loss numbers.

Document zone strategy and what happens when a platform advisory affects both zones.

Practice database recovery under IO stalls: runbooks for pause, promote and validate.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.6, 8.14) and SOC 2 expect capacity planning evidence and tested recovery procedures.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on end-to-end IO sizing and replica parity.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

  1. How quickly did you identify the VM cap?
  2. How did you separate platform from self-inflicted causes?
  3. How did you decide on resize versus promotion?
  4. How were loss numbers estimated?
  5. How did you handle in-flight orders?
  6. How did you protect database integrity?
  7. How were venues and members handled?
  8. How did you react to the expanding advisory?
  9. How accurate were updates to the CEO and trading head?
  10. What did Microsoft support add?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  12. How is IO sizing validated?
  13. What access or permissions were missing?
  14. What alerts are missing?
  15. What would you change in storage design?
