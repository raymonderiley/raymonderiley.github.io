Telecommunications Carrier BGP Hijacking Isolating Primary European DC

Cross-Border & Supply Chain Resilience

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines telecommunications carrier bgp hijacking isolating primary european dc.

Participants are working-level: security and recovery engineers, infrastructure operations engineers, application owners, the service desk, security operations and forensics analysts, the forensics and vendor support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of continuity, workarounds, vendor and supplier coordination, contract and regulatory duties, and honest communication for this specific event.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Lindqvist Financial Technology runs its primary European data center behind two carriers. Its address blocks have no route origin validation records, and traffic is routed over the internet to partners.

At 06:00 on Wednesday, a carrier's customer announces Lindqvist's address blocks by mistake or by attack. Traffic from many networks is drawn away and dropped. Partners and customers cannot reach the data center, though it is healthy.

What the team does not yet know: whether the hijack is accidental or malicious, whether traffic is being inspected, how fast carriers will filter it, and what direct links exist.

Exercise Objectives

  • Confirm the hijack. Participants verify routing with external looking glasses.
  • Contact carriers. The team escalates to both carriers and the announcing network.
  • Announce more specific routes. Engineers advertise more specific prefixes where possible.
  • Protect data. Participants consider whether intercepted traffic exposes data.
  • Maintain partner links. The team uses private links and alternate paths.
  • Improve routing security. Participants review route origin validation and monitoring.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Confirm the hijack from outside the network and escalate to carriers while advertising more specific routes to pull traffic back.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. The disruption leads to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Impact is contained and vendor contact begins.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Scope is diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A recovery plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Recovery proceeds in order.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Operations stabilize.

T+06:00 — Validation. Systems are validated.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 06:30. Traffic to the data center is dropped across many networks.

The data center is healthy.

Partners cannot connect.

Facilitator provides: logs, status reports, inventory and the recovery plan.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Authorize workarounds and vendor escalation.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Platform Engineering
  • Verifies routing from external views and advertises more specific prefixes.
  • Prepares private link failover.
Recovery & Platform Engineering
  • Confirms the data center is healthy and checks for odd inbound connections.
  • Plans failback.
Application Owners & Service Desk
  • Application owners and partner managers define priority partner links.
  • Service desk: log reports.
  • Define manual procedures.
Service Operations & Monitoring
  • Monitors routing and traffic loss.
  • Records the timeline.
  • Record timelines for contract claims.
Vendor & Supplier Liaison
  • Escalates with both carriers and the announcing network.
  • Requests prefix filtering.
  • Open cases.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform leadership.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Plan customer and staff notices.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about notification duties.
  • Start the decision and contract log.

Discussion questions

  1. How do you confirm the hijack?
  2. Who do you call?
  3. Does more specific routing help?
  4. Could traffic be inspected?
  5. What do partners hear?
  6. What do customers hear?
  7. What private links exist?
  8. Why was validation missing?

Facilitator notes

  • Reveal the second fact only after the right team asks for it.
  • If players wait on the vendor, ask about workarounds and customer impact.
  • Look for workaround and vendor-escalation discipline.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes reachability when internet routing is wrong and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Platform Engineering

  • Verifies routing from external views and advertises more specific prefixes.
  • Prepares private link failover.

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 8.20 network security, 8.21 security of network services, 8.16 monitoring, 5.30 ICT readiness; SOC 2 availability and security).

  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Publish route origin validation records for all address blocks.

Monitor routing announcements from external vantage points and alert quickly.

Use private connectivity for critical partners.

Agree carrier escalation paths and filtering commitments.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.20, 8.21, 8.16, 5.30) and SOC 2 expect network and monitoring evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on confirm the hijack from outside the network and escalate to carriers while advertising more specific routes to pull traffic back..
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. What did you do first and why?
  2. What vendor commitments did you obtain??
  3. What did you find about scope?
  4. How did you sequence recovery?
  5. What did you tell leaders, customers and regulators?
  6. What design do you want?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the Vendor & Supplier Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
