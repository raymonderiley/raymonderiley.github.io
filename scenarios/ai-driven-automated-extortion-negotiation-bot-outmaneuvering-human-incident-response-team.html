AI-Driven Automated Extortion Negotiation Bot Outmaneuvering Human Incident Response Team

AI Failure & Autonomous Agent Disruption

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines an AI negotiation bot used by attackers that responds instantly, adapts demands to the victim's disclosed information and applies mounting pressure, while the response team's slow, siloed communication leaks information.

Participants are working-level: incident response engineers, infrastructure operations engineers, application owners, the service desk, security operations analysts, the AI platform and cloud support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of negotiation governance, information discipline, decision timing and independent recovery.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Brightmoor Hospitality is two days into a ransomware incident. A single channel exists for the attacker portal, accessed by several staff who also chat about recovery progress and insurance limits on a shared channel. A negotiator has been engaged but is not yet on the call.

The attackers' portal runs a bot that replies in seconds, quotes details from stolen files, and shifts its demand upward when staff mention restoration progress. A junior analyst replies from the portal revealing that backups are intact and that the cyber insurance limit is 5 million. The bot responds that the price is now 5 million and sets a countdown of six hours, threatening to publish data in stages.

What the team does not yet know: how much data was stolen, whether backups are truly clean, whether anyone else has replied, and what legal and insurer requirements apply to negotiation.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize adversarial automation. Participants notice instant replies and adaptation to disclosed facts.
  • Control the channel. The team restricts who may communicate and what is said.
  • Reset the timeline. Engineers and leaders decide independent of the countdown.
  • Assess leverage. Participants verify what data was stolen and whether backups are usable.
  • Meet legal duties. The team coordinates counsel, insurer and authorities.
  • Improve negotiation readiness. Participants review roles, scripts and information discipline.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Restrict portal access to the engaged negotiator and speak only from counsel-approved facts, because every disclosed detail raises the price.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. A disclosure and a new demand lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Channel controlled and facts verified.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Leverage and exposure are diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A decision and communication plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Decisions and recovery proceed in order.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Pressure stabilizes.

T+06:00 — Validation. Facts are validated.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes negotiation and decisions that are not driven by an adversary's tempo and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Incident Response Engineering

  • Restricts portal access and credentials.
  • Verifies backup integrity independently.
  • Plans recovery without decryptors.
  • Plans information rules.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Infrastructure Operations Engineering

  • Reviews logs of who accessed the portal.
  • Restores critical systems.
  • Validates systems.
  • Tracks changes.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners and finance define the cost of downtime and options.
  • The service desk logs questions.
  • Owners confirm manual procedures.
  • Prepare validation checks.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Security Operations & Monitoring

  • Reviews stolen data samples.
  • Analyzes the bot's language and timing.
  • Preserves evidence.
  • Adds guidance for negotiation hygiene.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

AI Platform Vendor & Cloud Support Liaison

  • Opens cases with the insurer and the negotiator.
  • Asks for guidance on legal steps.
  • Requests incident response help.
  • Escalates through account teams.
  • Records Google Cloud guidance and case numbers.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for hospitality group operations and informs leadership.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates workarounds and external contacts.
  • Tracks business impact.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.28 incident management, 5.5 contact with authorities, 5.31 legal requirements, 6.3 awareness; SOC 2 security).
  • Assesses incident decision governance.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Define who may communicate with attackers, pre-engage a negotiator and use counsel-approved scripts.

Keep recovery progress and insurance details out of any attacker-facing channel.

Verify backup truth independently and decide on the organization's timeline, not the attacker's.

Coordinate insurer, counsel and authorities before any payment decision.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (5.24, 5.26, 5.5, 5.31) and SOC 2 expect incident decision and communication evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on channel-control-first and information discipline.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. What did the bot learn?
  2. What did you restrict first?
  3. How did you resist the countdown?
  4. How did you verify leverage?
  5. What did you decide on payment?
  6. What do leaders need to hear?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the AI Platform Vendor & Cloud Support Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
