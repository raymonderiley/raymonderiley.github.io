AWS Athena S3 Scan Cost Explosion and Query Execution Lock

AWS Cloud Resiliency

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a runaway Athena workload that scans hundreds of terabytes in S3 because of unpartitioned tables, a broken partition projection and a looping scheduled query. Costs accumulate rapidly, concurrent query limits are hit so legitimate analytics and regulatory reports queue or fail, and S3 request throttling affects other workloads.

Participants are working-level: data lake and analytics engineers, cloud FinOps and platform engineers, data consumers, the service desk, cloud operations and monitoring staff, the AWS support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of Athena workgroups, data scanned limits, partitions, query concurrency quotas and cost controls.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Pinecrest Analytics runs a data lake of 3.2 PB in S3 queried by Athena for finance reporting, risk analytics and ad hoc exploration. All teams share the default workgroup. Tables for clickstream data were migrated last week and lost their partitioning definitions. A scheduled query meant to run daily was misconfigured to run every minute.

At 03:00 on Wednesday, the scheduled query begins scanning the full unpartitioned clickstream table, 400 TB per run. By 06:00, the account has scanned 2.5 PB, concurrency limits of active DML queries are exhausted, and queries from finance start failing with 'TooManyRequestsException'. S3 request rates on the clickstream prefix also trigger 503 SlowDown responses affecting an ETL job. Projected spend for the day exceeds 50 times the daily budget. A regulatory report due at 10:00 depends on Athena.

What the team does not yet know: no workgroup data scanned limits exist, budget alerts email an unmonitored list, the looping query was created by a service account whose owner left, and cancelling queries may leave partial CTAS tables and orphaned result files.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize a cost and concurrency event. Participants read Athena query history, workgroup metrics, Cost Explorer and CloudWatch to see scanned bytes and failing queries.
  • Stop the runaway safely. The team cancels and disables the looping query, handles partial outputs and prevents re-triggering.
  • Protect critical reports. Engineers create or use a dedicated workgroup and capacity reservation to run the regulatory report.
  • Control cost immediately. Participants set data scanned limits per query and per workgroup and engage FinOps and AWS about credits.
  • Repair the data layout. The team restores partitions, uses partition projection and converts to columnar formats.
  • Improve data lake governance. Participants review workgroups, budgets, quotas and ownership of scheduled queries.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • State bytes scanned or dollars when discussing impact; avoid vague statements such as 'it's expensive'.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Failing queries and cost alerts lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. The looping query is stopped and limits are set.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Root causes of scans and failures are identified.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A plan to run the regulatory report and fix layout is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Workgroup limits, partitions and reports are executed.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Concurrency and costs stabilize.

T+06:00 — Validation. Report accuracy and spend are verified.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Injects

Inject #1: 2.5 PB scanned and finance reports failing

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 06:10. Finance analysts report Athena queries fail with 'TooManyRequestsException' and queue indefinitely. The regulatory liquidity report due at 10:00 cannot run.

The analytics engineer sees query history: a scheduled query from a service account ran 340 times since 03:00, each scanning about 400 TB. Cost Explorer shows the day's Athena spend at 52 times budget. S3 returns 503 SlowDown on the clickstream prefix and an ETL job is failing.

There are no workgroup scan limits. Budget alerts went to an unmonitored mailbox. The service account owner left the company. The AWS Health Dashboard shows no events.

Facilitator provides: the query history extract, cost report, table definitions and the schedule configuration.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Authorize cancelling the looping query and disabling the account.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Data Lake & Analytics Engineering (Athena)
  • Cancel and disable the looping query.
  • Identify the unpartitioned table cause.
  • Plan cleanup.
Cloud FinOps & Platform Engineering
  • Set workgroup limits and budgets.
  • Quantify cost.
  • Engage AWS on credits.
Data Consumers & Service Desk
  • List critical queries and deadlines.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Agree on a protected workgroup.
Cloud Operations & Monitoring
  • Build the dashboard.
  • Confirm alerts.
  • List missing alarms.
AWS Support & TAM Liaison
  • Open a case.
  • Ask about credits.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform compliance reporting.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Consider a regulator extension.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about ownership of scheduled queries.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. How do you find which queries are scanning so much?
  2. How do you stop the loop without causing further problems?
  3. How do you protect the regulatory report?
  4. What cost controls can you apply right now?
  5. Why is the table scanning everything?
  6. What about partial CTAS tables and result files?
  7. Can AWS provide credits and how do you ask?
  8. What do compliance and finance need to hear?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the broken partitions until DLE inspects the table.
  • If players only increase quotas, ask what that does to cost.
  • Look for a dedicated workgroup as the protection.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

  • Assesses regulatory reporting obligations.
  • Captures evidence of cost controls.
  • Logs findings on ownership and limits.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Put guardrails on queries: separate workgroups per team, data scanned limits per query and per workgroup, and result encryption and location controls.

Treat data layout as a reliability issue: partitioning or partition projection, columnar formats, compaction and automated checks after migrations.

Control cost proactively: budgets and anomaly detection routed to monitored owners, plus approval for scheduled queries and ownership of service accounts.

Protect critical reporting with dedicated capacity or workgroups and tested runbooks.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.6, 8.32) and SOC 2 expect capacity and cost controls, ownership records and change management.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on cost guardrails and ownership of scheduled queries.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.
