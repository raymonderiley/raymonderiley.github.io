AWS Key Management Service (KMS) Throttling Enterprise Cryptography

AWS Cloud Resiliency

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines throttling of AWS KMS requests across an enterprise. A burst of encrypt and decrypt calls, driven by a new data pipeline and misconfigured S3 and application encryption, exceeds KMS request quotas on shared keys, causing failures in services that depend on the keys, from S3 reads to RDS, EBS attach and secrets retrieval.

Participants are working-level: cloud security and cryptography engineers, platform and SRE engineers, application owners, the service desk, cloud operations and monitoring staff, the AWS support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of KMS quotas, data key caching, S3 Bucket Keys, grants and key policy design.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Ashford Capital centralizes encryption on a small set of customer managed KMS keys shared across 40 accounts: one key for S3 data, one for EBS, one for RDS and one for application secrets. A new data lake ingestion pipeline began running at 20 TB per day, writing millions of small objects to S3 encrypted with SSE-KMS without S3 Bucket Keys. Application code calls KMS Decrypt directly for every request instead of caching data keys.

At 10:15 on Tuesday, the pipeline's volume doubles for quarter-end processing. Requests per second to the S3 data key exceed the shared regional quota. ThrottlingException errors appear across accounts: S3 GET and PUT fail with 'KMS.ThrottlingException', applications decrypting secrets fail, and EBS volumes fail to attach during Auto Scaling events because they need KMS grants and decrypts.

What the team does not yet know: one key is shared across 40 accounts so every account competes for the same quota, retries amplify load, the application secrets key is also throttled because several services call Decrypt on every request, and the quota increase process takes time and is per region and per request type.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize KMS throttling. Participants read CloudTrail errors, CloudWatch KMS metrics and service-specific errors to see that many symptoms share one cause.
  • Reduce KMS request volume. The team enables S3 Bucket Keys, introduces data key caching and pauses the heavy pipeline.
  • Protect critical workloads. Engineers prioritize payment, trading and customer-facing paths over batch work.
  • Request and use quota safely. Participants request quota increases, understand limits and plan key redistribution.
  • Avoid unsafe shortcuts. The team does not disable encryption or share key material to bypass throttling.
  • Improve cryptographic architecture. Participants review key hierarchy, per-account keys, caching and monitoring.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Encryption must not be weakened or disabled as a workaround; any proposal to do so must be recorded and rejected or formally escalated.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Cross-account encryption errors lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. The heavy pipeline is paused and critical paths protected.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. CloudTrail confirms the KMS throttling pattern.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Bucket Keys, caching and quota plans are agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Changes are applied and quota requests submitted.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Error rates fall and backlogs drain.

T+06:00 — Validation. Services and data integrity are verified.

Injects

Inject #1: KMS throttling breaks S3, secrets and EBS across accounts

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 10:50. Multiple teams report failures: S3 GETs return 'KMS.ThrottlingException', applications cannot decrypt secrets, and new EC2 instances fail to attach encrypted volumes. Payment authorization and trade settlement services show elevated errors.

The cryptography engineer queries CloudTrail and finds 70 percent of Decrypt and GenerateDataKey calls on the S3 data key come from the new data lake pipeline account. The application secrets key shows high volume from three services calling Decrypt on every request.

The key is shared by 40 accounts. A quota increase is possible but takes hours. Someone suggests turning off encryption for the data lake bucket temporarily. The AWS Health Dashboard shows no events.

Facilitator provides: CloudTrail summary by caller, the quota table, the key policy overview and the pipeline configuration.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Decide to pause the data lake pipeline.
  • Reject any plan to disable encryption and record it.
Cloud Security & Cryptography Engineering
  • Identify top callers and explain how.
  • Propose Bucket Keys and data key caching.
  • Plan quota requests.
Cloud Platform & SRE Engineering
  • Pause or throttle the pipeline.
  • Implement backoff and caching quickly.
  • Protect EBS attach flows.
Application Owners & Service Desk
  • List KMS-dependent services.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Plan degraded modes.
Cloud Operations & Monitoring
  • Build the dashboard.
  • Check CloudTrail.
  • List missing alarms.
AWS Support & TAM Liaison
  • Open a quota case.
  • Ask about Bucket Keys.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform finance.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Ask for deadlines.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Record the rejected shortcut.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. How do you see that unrelated failures share a KMS cause?
  2. Who is consuming the quota and how do you prove it?
  3. Why is turning off encryption unacceptable, and what do you do instead?
  4. What do S3 Bucket Keys and data key caching change?
  5. How do you prioritize payments over batch?
  6. How long does a quota increase take and what do you do meanwhile?
  7. Why is one key shared across 40 accounts a risk?
  8. What do finance and leadership need to hear?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not volunteer that the key is shared until the cryptography engineer reviews the key policy.
  • If players propose disabling encryption, ask who could approve and what the record would say.
  • Look for the pause as the fastest lever.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes customer-facing and trading systems and sets the batch pause policy.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Rejects proposals to weaken encryption and records them.
  • Decides on temporary service degradation.

Cloud Security & Cryptography Engineering

  • Analyzes CloudTrail and KMS metrics to find top callers by key and principal.
  • Plans enabling S3 Bucket Keys, data key caching and key redistribution.
  • Reviews key policies and grants, and quota requests.
  • Considers multi-Region keys and per-account keys.

Cloud Platform & SRE Engineering

  • Pauses or throttles the data lake pipeline and sets backoff.
  • Implements encryption SDK caching where code changes are quick.
  • Protects Auto Scaling and EBS attach flows.
  • Monitors service health.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners identify which services depend on KMS Decrypt per request.
  • The service desk logs reports across teams.
  • Owners prepare degraded modes such as cached secrets.
  • Prepare validation tests.

Cloud Operations & Monitoring

  • Builds a dashboard of KMS requests, throttles by key and service error rates.
  • Confirms CloudTrail ingestion.
  • Checks AWS Health and quotas.
  • Adds alarms on throttling.

AWS Support & TAM Liaison

  • Opens a case for urgent KMS quota increases with evidence.
  • Asks about Bucket Keys behavior and best practice.
  • Requests guidance on key redistribution.
  • Records guidance.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for quarter-end processing and informs finance.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates manual processing.
  • Tracks the outage.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.28, 8.24 use of cryptography; SOC 2 availability and security).
  • Assesses regulatory reporting deadlines.
  • Captures evidence of rejected insecure shortcuts.
  • Logs findings on key architecture.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Design the key hierarchy for scale: per-account or per-workload keys, multi-Region keys where needed, and S3 Bucket Keys to cut request volume by an order of magnitude.

Use envelope encryption with data key caching in applications so KMS is called per data key, not per request, and implement backoff with jitter.

Monitor and alarm on KMS request rates, throttles and quota utilization, and request increases before known peaks.

Never trade away encryption for availability: have pre-approved safe alternatives and a rule that disabling or sharing keys requires formal risk acceptance.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.24) and SOC 2 expect documented key management, usage monitoring and tested capacity.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on resisting insecure shortcuts and on reducing KMS call volume.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

  7. What did the quota process involve?

  10. What did AWS support add?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
