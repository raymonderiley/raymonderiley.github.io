AWS Step Functions State Machine Deadlock in Financial Settlement

AWS Cloud Resiliency

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a Step Functions workflow that orchestrates end-of-day financial settlement and stalls because executions wait on each other: parallel branches hold locks on shared ledger records and callback tasks never receive their task tokens. Executions accumulate in a running state, timeouts do not fire as expected, and settlement files are not sent before the cutoff.

Participants are working-level: workflow and integration engineers, payments and settlement engineers, application owners, the service desk, cloud operations and monitoring staff, the AWS support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of Standard versus Express workflows, task tokens, timeouts, retries, idempotency and safe manual intervention.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Hollis Payments settles card transactions with partner banks each evening through a Standard Step Functions workflow. For each batch, the workflow runs parallel branches: one posts ledger entries via Lambda to a relational database with row-level locks, another sends a settlement file to a bank through an SFTP gateway and waits for an acknowledgment using a callback task token. Batches must be submitted before the bank's 21:00 cutoff.

At 19:30, a code change to the ledger Lambda introduces a lock ordering change. Two parallel branches for overlapping merchants each acquire a lock the other needs. The database detects deadlocks and aborts one transaction, but the Lambda's retry logic is configured with long backoff and the state's TimeoutSeconds is missing on the callback task. The workflow's file branch is waiting for an acknowledgment that never comes because the bank file was never sent. By 20:15, 140 executions are 'RUNNING' with no progress.

What the team does not yet know: some executions already posted partial ledger entries, a few branches did send files that the bank has acknowledged late, the dead letter queue for failed Lambda invocations is not configured, and manual re-execution without idempotency checks could post duplicate entries.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize a stalled workflow. Participants use execution history, CloudWatch metrics and X-Ray traces to see stuck states and their causes.
  • Contain without corrupting the ledger. The team pauses new executions, identifies partial postings and avoids duplicate settlement.
  • Resolve the deadlock. Engineers fix lock ordering or serialize conflicting branches and decide on stopping or redriving executions.
  • Meet or manage the cutoff. Participants coordinate with the bank on extended cutoff or manual submission and track the financial exposure.
  • Reconcile precisely. The team uses control totals and ledger checks to confirm that every transaction is settled exactly once.
  • Improve workflow resilience. Participants review timeouts, heartbeats, idempotency, locking and alarms.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • No manual database or workflow action may be proposed without stating how duplicates are prevented.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Stalled executions and missed milestones lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. New executions are paused and bank contact is initiated.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Deadlock and partial postings are identified.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A fix and reconcile plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Fix is deployed and executions are stopped or redriven.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Settlement files are sent and acknowledged.

T+06:00 — Validation. Reconciliation is verified.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operations resume and the incident is closed for debrief.

Injects

Inject #1: 140 settlement executions stuck and the cutoff approaches

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 20:20. Treasury reports that settlement files have not reached the partner bank for most batches. The Step Functions console shows 140 executions in 'Running' for over 45 minutes. The cutoff is 21:00.

The workflow engineer finds the file branch waiting on a callback with no timeout. The ledger posting branch shows Lambda errors for deadlock victim aborts and long retries. The database logs show lock waits on overlapping merchant rows.

The payments engineer finds some batches have partial ledger postings. No dead letter queue was configured. The AWS Health Dashboard shows no events. The bank's operations desk says an extension requires a request by 20:45.

Facilitator provides: execution history samples, the state machine definition, the database lock log and the batch status summary.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Decide to pause new executions.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Workflow & Integration Engineering (Step Functions)
  • Identify stuck states and the missing timeout.
  • Plan safe stop and redrive.
  • Prepare a corrected version.
Payments & Settlement Engineering
  • Identify partial postings and sent files.
  • Explain the lock ordering fix.
  • Plan reconciliation.
Application Owners & Service Desk
  • Confirm priorities and merchant impact.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Prepare messaging.
Cloud Operations & Monitoring
  • Build the dashboard.
  • Check alarms and DLQs.
  • List missing alarms.
AWS Support & TAM Liaison
  • Open a case.
  • Ask about redrive.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform treasury.
  • Contact the bank.
  • Ask for an extension.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about idempotency controls.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. How do you tell stuck executions from slow ones?
  2. Why is there no timeout on the callback and what does that cause?
  3. How do you stop executions without losing track of what posted?
  4. How do you prevent duplicate settlement on redrive?
  5. What do you ask the bank and when?
  6. What is the lock ordering problem?
  7. Can some batches be submitted manually and how?
  8. What do treasury and merchants need to hear?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal partial postings until PAY queries the ledger.
  • If players redrive everything, ask what idempotency exists.
  • Look for the extension request as time-critical.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.

  • Decides on contacting senior bank contacts for an extension.

  • Assesses regulatory and bank obligations.
  • Captures evidence of manual actions.
  • Logs findings on testing and idempotency.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Make every workflow step idempotent and every wait bounded. Set TimeoutSeconds and heartbeats on tasks, use DLQs and catch/retry with exponential backoff and jitter.

Design locking deliberately: consistent lock ordering, short transactions and serialization of conflicting work, with tests that reproduce deadlocks.

Store correlation data outside execution state for callbacks and keep a reconciliation process independent of the workflow.

Build the cutoff into design: alarms that fire well before the deadline and pre-agreed options with partners.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.32) and SOC 2 expect processing integrity controls, tested changes and documented manual interventions.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on idempotency and controlled manual intervention.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.
