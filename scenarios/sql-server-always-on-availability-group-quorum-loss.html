SQL Server Always On Availability Group Quorum Loss

On-Premises Infrastructure

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines the response to a loss of Windows Server Failover Cluster (WSFC) quorum underneath a SQL Server Always On Availability Group. When quorum is lost the cluster service stops, the availability group goes offline and listeners disappear, even though databases on individual replicas may be intact. The team must decide whether to force quorum, accept possible data loss, and restore service without causing split-brain.

The exercise is for working-level participants: SQL Server DBAs, Windows and cluster engineers, network and DNS staff, application and batch owners, storage and backup operators, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. It tests understanding of quorum models, the difference between synchronous and asynchronous replicas and the risks of forced failover.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Copperline Insurance runs its policy and claims platform on a four-node WSFC with an Always On Availability Group of five databases (about 3 TB). SQL1 and SQL2 are in the primary data center as synchronous replicas with automatic failover; SQL3 is a synchronous replica in the secondary data center; SQL4 is an asynchronous readable secondary at the disaster recovery site. The cluster uses node majority with a file share witness hosted on a file server in the primary data center.

At 03:05 a scheduled storage firmware update on the file server hosting the witness takes it offline and the cluster raises a warning that nobody sees. At 03:20 a switch fault isolates SQL3 and SQL4 from the primary data center. Votes: SQL1, SQL2 are on one side with no witness, SQL3 and SQL4 on the other. Neither side has a majority of five votes (two nodes plus no witness gives only two of five votes on either side). The cluster service stops on all nodes at 03:21 and the listener name goes offline. The claims application and the broker portal fail.

What the team does not yet know: dynamic quorum was disabled by a previous consultant, SQL2 had been lagging in synchronization for 40 minutes due to a log send queue, the asynchronous replica SQL4 is 11 minutes behind, and the application connection strings use the listener name with MultiSubnetFailover disabled, which will complicate failover even after quorum returns.

Exercise Objectives

  • Diagnose the quorum loss. Participants use cluster logs, Event IDs (1135, 1177, 1069, 1573) and the Get-ClusterQuorum and Get-ClusterNodeState outputs to determine that the cluster is down by design, not corrupted.
  • Decide whether and how to force quorum. The team compares restoring the witness, restoring network connectivity, and forcing quorum on a chosen node, understands the risk of split-brain and the effect on replica roles, and agrees on who decides.
  • Protect data integrity and quantify data loss. Participants assess synchronization state of each replica, the log send queue and redo queue, and estimate the potential data loss from each failover option.
  • Restore the listener and applications. The team restores the listener, tests application connectivity and resolves client-side issues such as connection string settings.
  • Control re-synchronization. Participants plan how to resume data movement, re-seed lagging replicas and prevent log growth and disk exhaustion.
  • Improve quorum design. The team examines witness placement, dynamic quorum and the monitoring of quorum health.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Be explicit about which replica you would trust as the source of truth, and why.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Application errors, listener failure and cluster alerts prompt declaration.

T+00:30 — Quorum diagnosis. Cluster state, vote counts and replica synchronization are established.

T+01:15 — Options and risk. Witness recovery, network repair and forced quorum are compared.

T+02:00 — Data loss assessment. Potential data loss for each option is quantified and accepted or rejected.

T+03:00 — Recovery path decision. A path is chosen and approved.

T+04:30 — Execution. Quorum restored, listener online and replicas resynchronized.

T+06:00 — Validation. Application tests, data reconciliation and monitoring are verified.

T+07:30 — Return to service and close-out. Services released and the incident closed for debrief.

Injects

Inject #1: Cluster service stops and the listener disappears

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 03:24. The claims application reports 'cannot connect to database'. The on-call DBA finds that the Availability Group listener does not respond. On SQL1, Failover Cluster Manager cannot connect to the cluster; the cluster service has stopped. Event ID 1177 appears: the cluster service is shutting down because quorum was lost.

SQL Server on SQL1 and SQL2 is running, but the databases show as 'Not Synchronizing / Resolving' in SSMS. SQL3 and SQL4 are unreachable from the primary data center. The network team says a core switch fault began at 03:20. The storage team notes that the file server hosting the witness restarted for a firmware update at 03:05 and has not come back.

Dashboards show that the broker portal is down and 340 claims are queued at the call center. A batch job for premium collection is scheduled at 04:00 and would run against the claims databases.

Facilitator provides: the cluster event log extract, the vote map, SSMS replica states screenshot and the application connection string examples.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the DBA lead, cluster lead and scribe.
  • Decide whether to hold the 04:00 batch.
  • Set the next update at T+00:30.
SQL Server DBA & Availability Group Engineering
  • Explain what the database state means and why databases are Resolving.
  • Check synchronization and last commit times of SQL1 and SQL2, and estimate the state of SQL3 and SQL4.
  • List the options and the data loss risk.
Windows Server & Failover Cluster Engineering
  • Interpret the vote counts and why neither side has quorum.
  • Check whether dynamic quorum is enabled.
  • Describe how forced quorum works and what risks it brings.
Network & DNS Operations
  • Diagnose and prioritize the switch fault.
  • Check whether the witness share is reachable by any node.
  • Verify listener IP and DNS.
Application Owners & Batch Operations
  • List which applications use the listener and how they connect.
  • Hold the batch job.
  • Check MultiSubnetFailover settings.
Storage & Backup Operations
  • Check the witness file server and the firmware update status.
  • Confirm log backups are current.
  • Check log disk space on SQL1.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Activate manual claims intake.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Ask for documented RTO and RPO.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask whether the firmware change was approved and communicated.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. Why is the cluster down, and why did dynamic quorum not save it?
  2. What is the difference between restoring the witness and forcing quorum, and what are the risks of each?
  3. What do you know about the synchronization state of each replica, and which has the latest data?
  4. SQL2 was lagging by 40 minutes. How does that affect your decision?
  5. Who decides to accept data loss, and how do you document it?
  6. If you force quorum on SQL1 and the network returns, what happens to SQL3 and SQL4?
  7. How will applications connect after failover given the connection string settings?
  8. What should the call center tell claimants?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the lag on SQL2 until the DBA checks replica states.
  • If the team forces quorum immediately, ask what they checked first and who approved data loss.
  • Watch for confusion between node majority, witness and dynamic quorum.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, owns the decision on forcing quorum and data loss tolerance, informed by the DBA and cluster leads.
  • Runs a 30-minute cadence, assigns the scribe and records decisions with the data loss accepted.
  • Confirms who has authority to accept data loss for policy and claims data.
  • Authorizes emergency changes including witness reconfiguration.

SQL Server DBA & Availability Group Engineering

  • Examines replica states using sys.dm_hadr_database_replica_states: synchronization state, log send queue, redo queue and last commit time.
  • Determines which replica has the latest committed transactions and what is lost if each is chosen.
  • Plans forced failover with allow data loss, and the later resume of data movement and suspended databases.
  • Prepares to reseed or restore databases on replicas that diverge.

Windows Server & Failover Cluster Engineering

  • Diagnoses the cluster state, vote assignments and the witness failure, and reviews cluster logs.
  • Decides how to start the cluster with forced quorum (/fq) on one node and what happens to the other nodes when they return.
  • Plans witness replacement and enabling dynamic quorum and dynamic witness.
  • Checks prevention of quorum arbitration problems.

Network & DNS Operations

  • Diagnoses the switch fault isolating SQL3 and SQL4 and plans restoration.
  • Verifies DNS records, listener IP addresses across subnets and static routes.
  • Checks firewall rules for cluster ports and the file share witness.
  • Prepares for DNS TTL and client caching issues.

Application Owners & Batch Operations

  • Application owners list services dependent on the listener and test connection strings and MultiSubnetFailover settings.
  • Batch operators hold jobs and plan restart.
  • Claims and broker portal owners prepare messaging and manual workarounds.
  • Prepare validation tests.

Storage & Backup Operations

  • Checks the file server storage firmware update and the witness share state.
  • Confirms backups and log backups are current for all databases and available for restore.
  • Monitors log disk space for the primary as log truncation is blocked by unsent logs.
  • Prepares restore capacity.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity plans for claims intake and broker support.
  • Issues time-stamped updates to leadership and brokers.
  • Coordinates manual claims intake.
  • Tracks downtime against RTO and RPO.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.28; SOC 2 availability and processing integrity).
  • Evaluates regulatory implications of any lost claims data.
  • Captures evidence for decisions to accept data loss.
  • Logs findings on quorum design, change control for firmware updates and monitoring.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Quorum is a design decision. Use dynamic quorum with dynamic witness, place the witness in a third failure domain (not on a primary-site file server) or use a cloud witness, and test every site-failure combination.

Monitor quorum health, not just node health: alert when the witness is offline, when vote count drops and when a node is removed from voting. A warning that nobody sees at 03:05 is a design gap.

Know the data loss of each failover option at any time. Track log send queue, redo queue and last commit time per replica, and alert on thresholds so that decision-makers have numbers.

Prepare a forced-quorum runbook with named approvers and explicit data loss acceptance, plus procedures for resuming, reseeding and validating replicas afterward.

Review client configuration: use MultiSubnetFailover, sensible timeouts and listener DNS settings. Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 and SOC 2 expect evidence of failover testing and change control for infrastructure maintenance.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on how the team reasoned about quorum and who accepted data loss.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How long did it take to conclude that quorum loss, not corruption, was the cause?
  2. How is quorum configured today and who reviewed the witness placement?
  3. Why did dynamic quorum not protect the cluster?
  4. What were the log send queue and last commit time for each replica and where were those numbers found?
  5. Who has authority to accept data loss and how was that decision recorded?
  6. What are the risks of forced quorum and how were they mitigated?
  7. How did the team prevent two clusters from forming?
  8. How did applications connect after recovery, and what client changes were needed?
  9. How did the team handle the batch job and log disk growth?
  10. How were lost claims identified and recovered?
  11. What communications went to claims, brokers and leadership and were they accurate?
  12. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  13. What monitoring should have caught the witness outage?
  14. What access or credentials were missing?
  15. What would you change in cluster design?
