Fire Suppression Agent Accidental Discharge in Mainframe Vault

On-Premises Infrastructure

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines the accidental discharge of a clean-agent fire suppression system in the room housing the mainframe and its tape vault. There is no fire, but the discharge creates a loud pressure wave, a rapid temperature drop, a room that cannot be entered for a period of time and possible physical damage to disks, tape drives and other equipment sensitive to acoustic shock and pressure.

The exercise is for working-level participants: mainframe operators and systems staff, facilities and fire-safety engineers, tape and storage operators, physical security, application owners, the service desk, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. It focuses on rapid assessment of equipment health, safe re-entry, the status of the system after sudden shutdown and the unusual loss of access to tape media stored in the vault.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Pinnacle Health Plans operates a mainframe, its disk subsystems and a 3,000-cartridge tape library in a dedicated vault-like computer room protected by a clean-agent (HFC-227ea) suppression system with dual-zone smoke detection and a manual release station. The room supports claims adjudication, eligibility and a nightly reporting cycle, and houses the on-site tape vault that holds weekly full backups and archive logs.

At 14:08 on Monday, a contractor testing a smoke detector in the adjacent corridor uses a heat gun that triggers a cross-zone alarm in the vault room. The control panel, whose abort button had been taped over after nuisance alarms last month, begins its 30-second countdown without a visible abort option. The system discharges at 14:09. The noise reaches roughly 120 dB at the nozzles. The mainframe disk subsystem logs multiple disk errors during the discharge and a number of hard drives show degraded performance. The room's emergency power off (EPO) is pressed by an operator, shutting down all equipment. Occupants evacuate and the building fire department arrives.

What the team does not yet know: the fire department will not allow re-entry until the agent concentration is verified, the discharge exceeded the designed hold time after the door was propped open, some tape drives were mid-write when power was removed, the abort button modification was not recorded in the change log and the manufacturer says the nozzles must be inspected and the agent recharged before the room can be protected again.

Exercise Objectives

  • Respond safely to an accidental discharge. The team prioritizes life safety, evacuation, accounting for personnel, fire department coordination and safe re-entry criteria.
  • Assess equipment and data state after EPO and acoustic shock. Participants plan the inspection order for disk subsystems, tape drives, mainframe processors and network gear, and determine which devices are likely to have suffered damage or data loss.
  • Protect and recover media. The team determines the condition and accessibility of tapes in the vault, the risk of magnetic or physical damage and the alternatives while the vault is inaccessible.
  • Decide on recovery path. Participants decide between restoring in place after inspection and failing over to the DR site, with decision criteria and timing.
  • Operate without fire protection. The team decides what compensating controls (fire watch, portable extinguishers, reduced operations) apply until the system is recharged and certified.
  • Examine the control failure. Participants discuss the taped abort button, change control and testing procedures for fire systems.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Life safety decisions follow your site emergency procedures. Do not simulate entering a discharged room without the fire authority's clearance.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Discharge, EPO and evacuation lead to incident declaration.

T+00:30 — Safety and access. Personnel are accounted for and the fire department gives re-entry criteria.

T+01:15 — Initial damage assessment. Remote and visual indicators are reviewed; inspection plan agreed.

T+02:00 — Recovery path options. In-place recovery or DR failover is evaluated.

T+03:00 — Decision point. A recovery path is chosen with data loss and time estimates.

T+04:30 — Execution. Re-entry, inspection, restart or DR activation proceeds.

T+06:00 — Validation. Data integrity, application tests and tape media checks.

T+07:30 — Return to service and close-out. Services restored, fire protection restored or compensated, incident closed.

Injects

Inject #1: Agent discharges with no fire and the room is powered off

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 14:12. The fire panel shows 'AGENT RELEASED' in the mainframe room. Operators say a deafening blast occurred, the room filled with white fog and they evacuated, pressing the EPO on the way out. All equipment in the room is off. The building fire department is on site and has not yet allowed re-entry.

A contractor says he was testing a smoke detector in the corridor with a heat gun. The panel log shows a cross-zone alarm at 14:08, a 30-second pre-discharge countdown and release at 14:09. A facilities technician notes that the abort button had been taped over after nuisance alarms last month.

Monitoring portals show the disk subsystem sent 'multiple disk drive failure' alerts as the discharge occurred, and one tape drive reported a write error. The claims system, member eligibility and the provider portal are down. The nightly cycle starts at 22:00.

Facilitator provides: the fire panel event log, the monitoring alerts, the evacuation roster and the DR site status.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and confirm all personnel are accounted for.
  • Decide when to alert the DR site team and what must be true before invoking.
  • Set the next update at T+00:30.
Mainframe & Systems Operations
  • State what happened to the mainframe when EPO was pressed: which workloads and data sets were open.
  • List the checks required before power-on.
  • Estimate DB2 recovery time.
Facilities, Fire Safety & Environmental Engineering
  • Explain the discharge, the hold time and what the fire department requires before re-entry.
  • Describe what equipment damage could result from the acoustic shock.
  • Explain what compensating controls are required while the room is unprotected.
Storage, Tape & Media Operations
  • Check the vault tape inventory and assess exposure.
  • Identify the tapes mid-write and the media at risk.
  • Arrange offsite tape retrieval.
Security & Physical Access
  • Confirm headcount and set the exclusion zone.
  • Preserve CCTV and panel logs.
  • Control re-entry.
Application Owners & Service Desk
  • List affected services and rank restoration order.
  • Service desk: issue holding message.
  • Claims owner: manual intake.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Activate continuity plan for claims and eligibility.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Ask for documented RTO.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask why the abort button was taped and who approved.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. What is the first priority and who decides when it is safe to re-enter?
  2. What damage can an agent discharge and acoustic shock cause to disks and tape, and how do you check without power?
  3. What happened to workloads when EPO was pressed and what recovery does that imply?
  4. What are the decision criteria for restoring in place or invoking DR?
  5. What are the vault tapes' risks and which backups matter most?
  6. Can production run unprotected without fire suppression, and what controls would you demand?
  7. How did the taped abort button get approved and who owns that control?
  8. What should claims and providers be told?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the taped abort button until the facilities lead explains the panel timeline.
  • If teams ignore life safety, bring them back: the fire department controls re-entry.
  • Look at whether DR invocation criteria are time-based or condition-based.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, ensures life safety is first priority and sets the recovery path decision criteria.
  • Runs a 30-minute cadence, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Coordinates with the fire department commander and building management.
  • Authorizes DR invocation if re-entry or recovery is not feasible in the target time.

Mainframe & Systems Operations

  • Determines the state of the mainframe at EPO: which jobs and DB2 or VSAM data sets were open, and whether an orderly shutdown occurred.
  • Plans a careful power-on sequence, including hardware checks, disk subsystem diagnostics and IPL.
  • Prepares DB2 recovery steps and checks for corrupted catalogs.
  • Prepares to invoke DR using the replicated disk and decides on data loss.

Facilities, Fire Safety & Environmental Engineering

  • Works with the fire department to verify agent concentration, ventilate and certify the room safe.
  • Inspects the suppression system, nozzles and the control panel, and arranges for agent recharge and certification.
  • Investigates the control panel abort button and the heat gun incident and documents the findings.
  • Implements compensating controls such as fire watch, and decides whether production can run unprotected.

Storage, Tape & Media Operations

  • Assesses the condition and access of tapes in the vault; checks for exposure to the agent, pressure and temperature shock.
  • Identifies tape drives that were mid-write and the media that may be incomplete.
  • Arranges retrieval of offsite tapes as an alternative.
  • Plans a media verification program for critical cartridges.

Security & Physical Access

  • Manages evacuation accountability, access control and re-entry authorization.
  • Secures the room and vault after the evacuation, with CCTV review of the incident.
  • Ensures only authorized personnel and contractors enter.
  • Investigates whether contractor actions followed permit-to-work rules.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners list services affected and set priorities for restoration.
  • The service desk issues guidance to claims and member services.
  • Owners prepare manual workarounds, such as delayed adjudication and paper intake.
  • Prepare validation tests.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates the continuity plan and coordinates communications to leadership, providers and members.
  • Manages regulatory deadlines for claim processing.
  • Coordinates manual processes.
  • Tracks downtime against RTO.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.28; 7.5 protecting against physical threats; SOC 2 availability).
  • Evaluates regulatory notification and reporting obligations.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and the change control gap for the abort button.
  • Logs findings on contractor permit control, fire system testing and compensating measures.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Fire suppression is both a protection and a risk to availability. Review discharge scenarios: abort stations, cross-zone logic, pre-discharge delay, acoustic mitigation (silent nozzles) and the effect of pressure and noise on disk drives.

Control modifications to life-safety and protection systems. A taped abort button is a change that bypassed control; require work permits, change records and testing for any alteration, and require supervision of contractors working near detectors.

Document EPO behavior: which equipment is on EPO, what happens to open data sets and tapes, and the recovery steps. Provide for orderly shutdown alternatives and training.

Keep media safe: diversify tape storage, replicate to an offsite or cloud tier and avoid housing the only copy of backups in the same fire zone as the primary systems.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (7.5, 7.8) and SOC 2 expect evidence of physical protection testing, contractor controls and recovery testing with DR invocation criteria.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on how life safety and recovery decisions were separated and the quality of the DR invocation criteria.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How was life safety managed and who had authority over re-entry?
  2. What are the documented effects of EPO on your mainframe workloads and how was it recovered?
  3. How did the team assess possible damage from the acoustic shock and moisture?
  4. What is your DR invocation criteria and did the team apply it?
  5. How was the vault tape situation handled and what are the alternatives?
  6. What compensating controls did the team put in place and who approved?
  7. How did the taped abort button escape change control?
  8. How are contractors permitted to work near detectors and who supervises them?
  9. How many minutes of data did the DR site lack and who accepted it?
  10. How accurate were communications to claims, members and providers?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture for insurers and auditors?
  12. What would you change about the room's suppression design?
  13. What access or credentials were missing during recovery?
  14. How would you handle a repeat discharge before the recharge?
  15. What does the vault's tape strategy need to change?
