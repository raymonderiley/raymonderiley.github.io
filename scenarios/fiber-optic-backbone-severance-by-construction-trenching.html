Fiber Optic Backbone Severance by Construction Trenching

On-Premises Infrastructure

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines the loss of the primary fiber optic backbone between the main data center and the rest of the organization when construction trenching cuts the cable duct. The team must discover how much of the redundancy it believed it had actually exists, how to restore connectivity with carriers, and how to operate with degraded capacity.

Participants are working-level: network engineers and carrier managers, data center operations, server, storage and replication engineers, application owners, the service desk, security operations, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The scenario uses a physical-world event to test logical designs.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Redwood Regional Bank operates a main data center in the city center and 38 branches, a call center and an operations center connected by an MPLS network and leased wavelengths over a dark fiber ring leased from a carrier. Two diverse paths exist, a north and a south route, to the disaster recovery site 120 km away, plus an internet edge with two ISPs terminating at the main data center.

At 09:35 on Thursday a contractor, trenching for a new water main under a county road, cuts a 288-count fiber duct. The carrier's alarm shows loss of signal on the north route wavelengths, and the dark fiber ring is cut at one point. The south route also fails at 09:41 because the carrier's engineering records show that it was re-routed through the same conduit during a recent upgrade. Ten of the branches lose connectivity, the replication link to the disaster recovery site is down and the internet edge loses one of two ISPs, whose backhaul was in the same duct.

What the team does not yet know: the carrier estimates 16 to 36 hours to restore a duct of this size, a microwave backup was decommissioned last year, the branch routers fail over to LTE with only 5 Mbps shared and no QoS, the DR replication is configured to stop and alert after 4 hours but the alert goes to a mailbox no one monitors, and the carrier's maintenance notice about the re-route was sent to a former employee.

Exercise Objectives

  • Establish the extent and cause of connectivity loss. Participants use carrier alarms, routing tables, optical power measurements and branch reachability to build an accurate picture of what is down and what remains.
  • Engage the carrier effectively. The team opens priority tickets, asks for restoration estimates, requests alternative routing or wavelength reallocation and escalates through senior carrier contacts.
  • Operate with degraded connectivity. Participants prioritize traffic and branches, use LTE and secondary ISPs, apply QoS and shed non-critical traffic.
  • Protect data protection and DR posture. The team assesses the replication gap, the RPO exposure and the risk of running without DR connectivity for 36 hours.
  • Keep customer and branch services running. Application owners and branch operations implement fallback procedures for tellers, ATMs and the call center.
  • Identify why redundancy was not real. The team examines carrier route diversity assurance, documentation, notification handling and test practice.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Use your actual carrier names, circuit IDs and procedures if known. Do not assume the carrier can repair faster than their stated times.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Carrier alarms, branch outages and replication alerts lead to incident declaration.

T+00:30 — Scope and carrier engagement. The extent is established and carrier tickets are escalated.

T+01:15 — Degraded operations. LTE failover, traffic shaping and branch prioritization are applied.

T+02:00 — DR and data protection posture. Replication gap and RPO exposure are assessed.

T+03:00 — Alternative connectivity decision. Temporary links, rerouting and cloud-based options are decided.

T+04:30 — Execution. Temporary measures are implemented.

T+06:00 — Validation. Connectivity, replication and application performance are verified.

T+07:30 — Return to service and close-out. Services restored in priority order and incident closed for debrief.

Injects

Inject #1: Construction crew cuts the fiber duct and both routes fail

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 09:44. The network operations center shows loss of light on the north route wavelengths at 09:35 and on the south route at 09:41. Ten branches show offline, the call center's trunk to the main data center is degraded and the DR replication session is down. One of two internet providers has lost its circuit.

The carrier's helpdesk confirms 'a major fiber cut affecting multiple customers' in the county road area and gives an initial estimate of 16 to 36 hours. The carrier's records show that the south route was re-routed through the same duct during a recent upgrade; the notice was sent but to a contact who left the organization.

The ten affected branches have failed over to LTE. Users report very slow teller systems. The DR replication alert generated at 09:36 went to a shared mailbox. The microwave backup site was decommissioned last year.

Facilitator provides: the network topology showing both routes, the carrier ticket, the list of ten branches and the LTE capacity per branch.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident, set priorities (tellers and ATMs, call center, DR replication) and name leads.
  • Decide when to escalate to senior carrier contacts.
  • Set the next update at T+00:30.
Network Engineering & Carrier Management
  • Explain what survives and what does not.
  • Escalate with the carrier and request alternatives.
  • Prioritize LTE traffic and shape it.
Data Center & Colocation Operations
  • Confirm the issue is outside the data center.
  • Prepare for temporary install options.
  • Support carrier technicians.
Server, Storage & Replication Operations
  • Check the replication state and growth of queues.
  • Decide whether to pause some replication.
  • Review DR invocation criteria.
Application Owners & Service Desk
  • Rank applications and prepare offline procedures for tellers.
  • Service desk: issue branch guidance.
  • Call center: adjust routing.
Security Operations
  • Check that LTE paths use VPN and logging.
  • Monitor for opportunistic attacks.
  • Review emergency firewall changes.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Activate the continuity plan for branches.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Ask for documented RTO.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask how route diversity was verified.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. How diverse were your two routes in reality, and who verified it?
  2. The carrier says 16 to 36 hours. How do you plan for the worst case rather than the estimate?
  3. How should 5 Mbps of LTE per branch be used and what traffic is dropped?
  4. What is the exposure from the replication gap and who has to know?
  5. What was the process for carrier maintenance notices and why did this one not reach you?
  6. What is the alternative if the carrier cannot restore within a day?
  7. How do tellers and ATMs work during the outage?
  8. What do you tell customers and regulators?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the shared duct or the decommissioned microwave until network staff ask about alternatives or route diversity.
  • If the team plans around the carrier estimate, ask what happens if it doubles.
  • Look for use of QoS and traffic classification.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, sets branch and service priorities and decides when to invoke degraded-mode procedures.
  • Runs a 30-minute cadence, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Escalates to senior carrier contacts through the CIO when carrier response lags.
  • Authorizes emergency purchase of temporary connectivity.

Network Engineering & Carrier Management

  • Analyzes routing and optical alarms to determine which circuits are down and which survive.
  • Opens severity-one tickets with the carrier and requests restoration estimates, alternative wavelengths and priority.
  • Enables and tunes LTE failover, QoS and traffic shaping, and shifts critical traffic to the surviving ISP.
  • Reviews route diversity evidence and tracks that carrier maintenance notices reach the correct contacts.

Data Center & Colocation Operations

  • Checks power, cooling and cross-connects in the main data center and colocation and confirms that the fault is outside the walls.
  • Supports temporary installs such as a microwave or alternative carrier cross-connect.
  • Verifies physical connectivity and meet-me room status.
  • Provides access to carriers' technicians.

Server, Storage & Replication Operations

  • Checks replication status to the DR site, its lag and the log or queue growth on source arrays.
  • Identifies services reliant on the DR link and those that could fail if replication queues fill.
  • Plans to throttle or pause replication selectively to avoid storage exhaustion.
  • Prepares DR invocation criteria in case the main site is lost during the outage.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners rank services by branch and customer impact.
  • The service desk and branch operations issue guidance for offline procedures for tellers and ATMs.
  • The call center owner adjusts routing and scripts.
  • Prepare validation tests.

Security Operations

  • Verifies that the degraded paths, LTE and temporary links are secured with VPN and monitoring.
  • Watches for opportunistic attacks during the outage and checks that security monitoring is not blind.
  • Reviews firewall rules for emergency routing changes.
  • Ensures logging continues.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates the continuity plan for branch operations and customer service.
  • Issues time-stamped updates to leadership, branches and customers.
  • Coordinates with regulators if outage extends.
  • Tracks downtime against RTO.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.28; 5.19 supplier relationships; SOC 2 availability).
  • Evaluates regulatory notification for a material service disruption.
  • Captures evidence of carrier communications and decisions.
  • Logs findings on route diversity assurance, notification handling and unmonitored alerts.

Resilience Architect Guidance

True diversity is physical. Require carriers to document and attest to route diversity, ask for conduit-level maps, and re-verify after every carrier network change. Include contractual commitments to notify you of re-routes.

Keep contact data for carrier notices current and monitored by a team mailbox, not an individual. A maintenance notice that reaches nobody is a control failure.

Design degraded operation: classify traffic, set QoS, size LTE or backup links for critical services and rehearse the switch to offline procedures at branches.

Treat the DR replication link as critical: monitor it with alerts that reach a staffed channel, pre-define what happens when replication stops and set explicit RPO exposure thresholds and decision makers.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (5.19, 5.20 supplier relationships) and SOC 2 look for supplier management, tested failover and evidence that redundancy claims are verified.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on how the team found out the redundancy was not real and how they operated in the degraded state.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How did the team learn that the south route shared the same duct, and how long did it take?
  2. What does your carrier contract say about route diversity and re-route notification?
  3. Who receives carrier maintenance notices and are those contacts current?
  4. How were branch priorities set and which traffic was dropped on LTE?
  5. What offline procedures did tellers and ATMs follow and how long can they run?
  6. What was the replication gap at each point, and who accepted the RPO exposure?
  7. Why did the replication alert go unnoticed and what will change?
  8. What temporary connectivity options exist and who can approve them?
  9. How was the carrier escalated and did senior contacts help?
  10. What security risks arose from emergency routing changes?
  11. How accurate were the communications to branches, customers and regulators?
  12. What evidence did the scribe capture that auditors would need?
  13. Which decisions were slowed by missing authority or budget?
  14. What access or credentials were missing?
  15. What would make your next redundancy test more realistic?
