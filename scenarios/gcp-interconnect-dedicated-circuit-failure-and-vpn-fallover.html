GCP Interconnect Dedicated Circuit Failure and VPN Fallover

Google Cloud Resiliency

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines the failure of a Dedicated Interconnect circuit and the fallback to Cloud VPN, which has a fraction of the capacity, causing congestion, asymmetric routing and failures in replication and batch transfers.

Participants are working-level: cloud network engineers, platform and SRE engineers, application owners, the service desk, cloud operations and monitoring staff, the Google Cloud support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of BGP, MED and priorities, redundancy, VPN throughput, traffic prioritization and carrier coordination.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Everwood Manufacturing connects its datacenter to Google Cloud with two 10 Gbps Dedicated Interconnect circuits in one metro and a Cloud VPN tunnel pair of about 3 Gbps as backup. The factory MES, ERP and a database replication stream use the link.

At 10:00 on Tuesday, a fiber cut takes both circuits out because they share a conduit in the carrier's path. BGP shifts all traffic to the VPN. Bandwidth falls from 20 Gbps to 3 Gbps. Replication lag climbs, MES calls time out and a nightly batch transfer that was delayed starts at full speed, saturating the tunnels. The carrier estimates repair in 12 hours.

What the team does not yet know: the VPN has route priority issues causing asymmetric return paths on one firewall, traffic cannot be shaped at the datacenter edge easily, a third circuit order would take weeks, and the plant cannot tolerate MES latency above 150 ms.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize shared-fate failure. Participants confirm both circuits are down and understand the shared path.
  • Prioritize traffic. The team protects MES and transactional traffic and defers replication and batch.
  • Fix asymmetric routing. Engineers correct route priorities and firewall state issues.
  • Coordinate with carriers. Participants engage carriers and Google with precise evidence.
  • Protect operations. The team prepares plant fallback modes.
  • Improve connectivity design. Participants review diversity, capacity of backups and shaping.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Traffic prioritization decisions must name what is protected and what is deferred.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Circuit loss and congestion lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Traffic is prioritized and carriers engaged.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Routing and capacity are diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A prioritization and repair plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Changes are applied and verified.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Traffic stabilizes on the backup.

T+06:00 — Validation. Services and replication are validated.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Inject #1: Both circuits are down and tunnels are saturated

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 10:20. Both Interconnect circuits are down. VPN tunnels run at 98 percent. MES latency is 600 ms and the plant reports stoppages.

The network engineer finds a fiber cut in a shared conduit. A delayed batch transfer started at full speed. One firewall drops return traffic due to asymmetric paths.

The carrier estimates 12 hours for repair. The plant limit is 150 ms for MES.

Facilitator provides: BGP state, utilization graphs, carrier notice and the traffic inventory.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Decide on pausing batch and replication.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Cloud Network Engineering (Interconnect)
  • Fix routing.
  • Plan shaping.
  • Engage carrier.
Platform & SRE Engineering
  • Pause batch.
  • Throttle replication.
  • Plan plant fallback.
Application Owners & Service Desk
  • Rank traffic.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Coordinate plants.
Cloud Operations & Monitoring
  • Build the dashboard.
  • Watch carrier.
  • Preserve logs.
Google Cloud Support & TAM Liaison
  • Open a case.
  • Ask about restoration.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform leadership.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Plan plant messaging.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about safety obligations.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. How do you confirm shared-fate failure?
  2. What traffic do you protect and what do you defer?
  3. How do you stop the batch safely?
  4. How do you fix asymmetric return paths?
  5. What can the plant do in degraded mode?
  6. What should carriers and Google be asked?
  7. What do plant managers hear?
  8. Why did two circuits share a conduit?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the asymmetry until T1 checks firewall logs.
  • If players leave the batch running, ask about MES.
  • Look for explicit protection.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes business-critical traffic between the datacenter and Google Cloud and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Cloud Network Engineering (Interconnect)

  • Confirms circuit state and BGP routes.
  • Corrects route priorities and asymmetry.
  • Plans traffic shaping and rate limits.
  • Engages carrier and Google.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Platform & SRE Engineering

  • Pauses batch transfers and throttles replication.
  • Checks MES and ERP latency.
  • Plans plant fallback modes.
  • Validates services.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners rank traffic by plant impact.
  • The service desk logs reports.
  • Owners coordinate with plant managers.
  • Prepare validation checks.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Cloud Operations & Monitoring

  • Watches carrier updates.
  • Preserves logs.
  • Adds utilization alerts.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Google Cloud Support & TAM Liaison

  • Requests guidance on route policy.
  • Escalates through the TAM.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for plant and ERP operations and informs leadership.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates workarounds and external contacts.
  • Tracks business impact.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.30, 8.14 redundancy; SOC 2 availability).
  • Assesses plant safety and production continuity.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Demand true path diversity from carriers, in writing, and test it, not just two circuits.

Size the backup for what you must run, and design traffic shaping so critical flows win.

Pre-plan degraded modes for plants and DR objectives.

Monitor both paths and alert on loss of redundancy.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.14, 5.30) and SOC 2 expect redundancy evidence and tested fallback.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on path diversity and traffic prioritization.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How did you confirm both circuits were down?
  2. What did you protect and what did you defer?
  3. How did you fix asymmetry?
  4. How did you engage carriers?
  5. How did you decide on plant fallback?
  6. How did you plan the cutover back?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the Google Cloud Support & TAM Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
