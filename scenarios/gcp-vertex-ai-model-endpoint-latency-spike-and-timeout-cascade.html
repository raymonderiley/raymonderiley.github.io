GCP Vertex AI Model Endpoint Latency Spike and Timeout Cascade

Google Cloud Resiliency

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a Vertex AI online prediction endpoint whose latency spikes after a model version change and autoscaling limits, causing client timeouts, retries and a cascade into upstream services that call the model synchronously.

Participants are working-level: ML platform engineers, application engineers, product owners, the service desk, cloud operations and monitoring staff, the Google Cloud support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of endpoint autoscaling, traffic splitting between model versions, timeouts, retries, circuit breakers, fallbacks and model rollback.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Alpine Commerce calls a Vertex AI endpoint for product ranking and for fraud scoring in checkout. The endpoint serves two model versions with a 90/10 traffic split. Autoscaling is set between 2 and 10 replicas. Client services wait up to 5 seconds and retry twice.

At 11:00 on Saturday, a new model version is promoted to 100 percent after a short test. The new model is 4 times heavier and p95 latency rises from 80 ms to 2.5 seconds. Autoscaling reaches its 10-replica cap, requests queue and timeouts begin. Retries triple the load. Ranking times out and checkout waits on fraud scoring, so the checkout error rate climbs to 30 percent during a weekend sale.

What the team does not yet know: the previous model version is still deployed but at zero traffic, a rollback is a one-step traffic change, some fraud decisions made during timeouts defaulted to approve, and the new model quality is unknown because evaluation used a small sample.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize a model-induced outage. Participants correlate latency, replica counts and version changes.
  • Roll back or split traffic quickly. The team restores traffic to the previous version using split controls.
  • Stop the cascade. Engineers reduce retries, add timeouts and activate fallbacks.
  • Assess decisions made during the incident. Participants identify fraud decisions made by default and assess exposure.
  • Protect checkout. The team prioritizes fraud scoring over ranking.
  • Improve ML release practice. Participants review canary traffic, latency gates, fallbacks and evaluation.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Any model fallback or default decision must be explicit and recorded.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Checkout errors and endpoint latency lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Traffic is rolled back and retries are reduced.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Latency and decision defaults are diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A rollout and review plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. The stable model is restored and fallbacks tuned.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Latency and error rates stabilize.

T+06:00 — Validation. Decisions made during the incident are reviewed.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Promote models through canary traffic with latency, error and quality gates, and keep the previous version warm for instant rollback.

Design clients with timeouts, bounded retries and circuit breakers, and explicit, risk-based fallbacks for decisions like fraud.

Separate critical from optional model calls so optional ones degrade first.

Alert on latency and default decision rates.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.32, 8.6) and SOC 2 expect change testing and processing integrity evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on canary gates and explicit fallback decisions.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How did you link the latency to the model?
  2. How did you roll back?
  3. How did retries affect you?
  4. How did you assess defaulted decisions?
  5. How did you choose degradations?
  6. How did you decide on the new model's future?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the Google Cloud Support & TAM Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
