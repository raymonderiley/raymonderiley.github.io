Physical Site Perimeter Security Power Interruption and Bypass

On-Premises Infrastructure

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines the loss of power to the perimeter security systems of a critical facility (fence line sensors, gates, CCTV, lighting and intrusion detection) and the opportunity this gives an intruder to bypass controls. The incident begins as an electrical failure on a perimeter power circuit and is complicated by signs that someone may be exploiting the blind spot.

Participants are working-level: physical security operators and the guard force, facilities and electrical engineers, security systems engineers, network and infrastructure operators, site operations managers, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise explores compensating controls, escalation to law enforcement and the protection of the data center and operations inside the perimeter.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Ironwood Logistics operates a 40-acre fulfillment campus with a data center building, a parts warehouse and a fuel depot, surrounded by a 3 km fence with vehicle gates at two entrances. Perimeter security consists of fence-mounted vibration sensors, thermal and visible cameras on poles, pole lighting, two vehicle gates with barrier arms and a card reader plus a perimeter intrusion detection (PID) system feeding the security control room. A separate perimeter power feed (480 V) is distributed to pole-mounted enclosures, with a battery backup in each enclosure designed for 4 hours, last inspected 14 months ago.

At 21:52 on Friday, a vehicle crashes into a utility pole outside the east boundary, taking down the utility feed to the perimeter distribution. The circuit breaker at the main panel trips and the perimeter system switches to battery. Eleven of the 38 enclosures have degraded batteries and drop out within minutes; at 22:10, most of the east and north perimeter sensors, lighting and cameras go offline and the east vehicle gate's barrier fails in the open position. The PID system reports communication failures across 52 percent of the zones.

What the team does not yet know: the utility estimates 10 to 14 hours to repair, a guard on patrol reports a gap in the fence on the northeast corner that may have been cut, the contract guard force has only 3 officers on shift, the backup diesel generator supplies the main buildings but not the perimeter circuit, and the data center's critical loads are intact.

Exercise Objectives

  • Establish the security posture and protect critical assets. Participants determine which zones are blind, which assets are most at risk (data center, fuel depot, inventory) and set priorities for guard deployment and hardening.
  • Apply compensating controls. The team deploys guards, vehicles, temporary lighting, portable cameras and barriers, closes the east gate manually and restricts access to critical buildings.
  • Respond to possible intrusion. Security decides when to treat the fence gap as an intrusion, calls law enforcement, sweeps the grounds safely and preserves evidence.
  • Restore perimeter power. Facilities determine options: generator tie-in to the perimeter circuit, temporary power at key poles, battery replacement and utility coordination.
  • Protect people and operations. Operations managers decide whether to continue shifts, restrict movement and protect staff, and handle shipments and visitors.
  • Strengthen design and maintenance. The team examines battery maintenance, generator coverage, gate fail modes, and the manual procedures for perimeter loss.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Personal safety comes first. No simulated action involves confronting an intruder; the exercise follows your site policy for escalation to law enforcement.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Perimeter alarms, the vehicle crash and power loss lead to incident declaration.

T+00:30 — Security posture. Blind zones are mapped and guards deployed; the east gate is secured.

T+01:15 — Possible intrusion. The fence gap is investigated and law enforcement is engaged.

T+02:00 — Compensating controls. Temporary lighting, cameras and barriers are placed.

T+03:00 — Power restoration plan. Generator tie-in and temporary power are approved.

T+04:30 — Execution. Power is restored to priority zones in stages.

T+06:00 — Validation. Sensors, cameras and gates are tested and a perimeter walk is done.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal security posture is restored and incident closed for debrief.

Inject #1: Perimeter power fails and the east gate opens

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 22:15 on Friday. The security control room sees 52 percent of the perimeter intrusion zones show 'communication failure', cameras on the east and north sides have gone dark and pole lighting is out along 1.2 km of the fence. The east vehicle gate barrier is in the open position and will not close.

A guard on patrol reports that a car struck a utility pole outside the east boundary at 21:52. The facilities duty manager sees the perimeter breaker tripped at the main panel and learns that battery backups in 11 of the 38 pole enclosures are depleted. The utility estimates 10 to 14 hours for repair.

A second guard at the northeast corner reports what appears to be a gap in the fence line, possibly cut, and wheel marks inside. Only three guards are on shift. The data center building, parts warehouse and fuel depot are inside the perimeter. Friday night operations are active, with 140 staff on site.

Facilitator provides: the PID zone map with blind areas, the camera coverage description, the battery inspection record and the site plan.

  • Decide when to call law enforcement.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Physical Security Operations & Guard Force
  • Deploy guards and secure the east gate manually.
  • Investigate the fence gap safely and preserve evidence.
  • Request additional officers and begin a manual log.
Facilities, Electrical & Life Safety
  • Assess the damaged pole and electrical safety.
  • Plan generator tie-in and temporary lighting.
  • Check data center power and cooling.
Security Systems Engineering (Access, CCTV, Intrusion)
  • Map blind zones and verify gate fail-open settings.
  • Plan temporary cameras and sensors.
  • Check for tamper alarms.
Network & Infrastructure Operations
  • Verify security network integrity and recordings.
  • Support wireless communications.
  • Check for unauthorized access.
Operations & Site Management
  • Decide on night shift activity and staff safety.
  • Secure inventory and fuel depot.
  • Control visitors and shipments.

  • Activate the continuity plan.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Ask for documented RTO.
GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ask when batteries were last tested.

Discussion questions

  1. What are the highest-value assets and how do three guards protect them?
  2. How do you close and control the east gate without power?
  3. Is the fence gap an intrusion, and what is your trigger to call law enforcement?
  4. How do you protect staff safety if an intruder is on site?
  5. What can the generator power, and what does it take to tie in the perimeter circuit?
  6. What temporary measures can you deploy in two hours?
  7. Should night operations continue and who decides?
  8. What do customers or corporate security need to know?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the wheel marks until the guard investigates the fence gap.
  • If players send guards to confront, redirect toward law enforcement and safety.
  • Note whether the team keeps a manual log and preserves evidence.
  • Time-box to 30 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes life safety and protection of the data center and fuel depot, and sets the posture level.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Decides on calling law enforcement and on notifying corporate security and customers.
  • Authorizes emergency guard augmentation and temporary equipment spend.

Physical Security Operations & Guard Force

  • Deploys the three guards to the highest-risk points and requests additional officers from the contract provider.
  • Closes and mans the east vehicle gate manually and controls all entry and exit.
  • Investigates the northeast fence gap with proper safety procedures, preserves evidence and coordinates with law enforcement.
  • Maintains a manual log of patrols, observations and visitors.

Facilities, Electrical & Life Safety

  • Diagnoses the perimeter power loss, battery state and breaker status, and assesses the safety of the damaged pole and live wires.
  • Plans generator tie-in to the perimeter circuit or temporary generators at key poles.
  • Sets up temporary lighting towers and coordinates with the utility.
  • Verifies that the data center's power and cooling are stable.

Security Systems Engineering (Access, CCTV, Intrusion)

  • Determines which PID zones, cameras and gates are down and what remains, and confirms the gate's fail-open setting.
  • Prepares portable cameras, wireless sensors and temporary barriers.
  • Plans sequencing for sensor and camera restart and checks for tamper alarms.
  • Reviews logs for the exact time of loss of each zone.

Network & Infrastructure Operations

  • Verifies network connectivity for security systems and wireless links at poles and prevents unauthorized access to the security network.
  • Ensures that the control room remote views and recordings are preserved.
  • Supports portable devices and communications for guards.
  • Checks that the data center perimeter network is secure.

Operations & Site Management

  • Site managers decide whether to continue night shift activities, restrict access and move staff.
  • Coordinate shipments and visitors and secure inventory and the fuel depot.
  • Maintain communication with staff using radios and mass notification.
  • Prepare to reduce operations if the posture cannot be assured.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates the continuity plan and informs corporate leadership and key customers as required.
  • Issues time-stamped updates to leadership.
  • Coordinates with insurance, law enforcement liaison and the utility.
  • Tracks incident impact against operational requirements.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.28, 7.1 physical security perimeters, 7.4 physical security monitoring; SOC 2 security).
  • Evaluates contractual security obligations to customers and any reporting duties.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and law enforcement contact.
  • Logs findings on battery maintenance, generator coverage and gate fail mode.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Perimeter systems need their own resilience: generator coverage, battery capacity tested and replaced on schedule, and monitoring of battery health with alerts.

Define gate fail modes deliberately. High-security gates should fail closed or hold in place with a manual override; failing open at night is a design decision that needs risk acceptance.

Prepare manual procedures: guard force sizing for degraded perimeter, rapid-deploy lighting and cameras, a call-out list and clear rules for engaging law enforcement.

Protect inner layers: layered security so that perimeter loss does not mean data center compromise, with independent power and alarms for critical buildings.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (7.1, 7.4, 7.8) and SOC 2 expect physical security testing, maintenance records and tested incident procedures.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on the posture decisions in the first hour and the compensating controls.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did the team understand which zones were blind?
  2. What assets did you prioritize and why?
  3. How was the east gate controlled and what is its documented fail mode?
  4. How did the team assess the fence gap and when did they call law enforcement?
  5. What did the guards need that they did not have?
  6. How long would it take to deploy temporary lighting and cameras?
  7. What does the generator cover and what changes would help?
  8. When were pole batteries last tested and what did the records show?
  9. How did operations decide about continuing the night shift?
  10. What layers protected the data center when the perimeter failed?
  11. What was communicated to corporate and customers?
  12. What evidence did the scribe capture that auditors and insurers would need?
  13. How did staff safety shape decisions?
  14. What access or credentials were missing?
  15. What will you change in perimeter power design?
