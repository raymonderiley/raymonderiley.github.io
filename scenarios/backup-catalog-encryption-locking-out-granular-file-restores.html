Backup Catalog Encryption Locking Out Granular File Restores

Ransomware & Cyber-Recovery Forensics

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines ransomware encrypting the backup catalog and index database so that backup data exists but cannot be browsed or restored at file level without lengthy catalog rebuilds.

Participants are working-level: backup and recovery engineers, infrastructure operations engineers, application owners, the service desk, security operations and forensics analysts, the forensics and vendor support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of catalog protection, rebuild time, full-image fallback and restore prioritization.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Castlebrook Law Group backs up file servers, mailboxes and databases with a product that keeps a catalog database on the backup server. The catalog is backed up nightly to the same server. File-level restores depend on the catalog. A full image restore is possible but slow.

At 04:00 on Monday, ransomware encrypts file servers and the backup server, including the catalog database and its local backup. Backup data blocks remain intact. Without a catalog, attorneys cannot restore individual matter files. A catalog rebuild from storage is estimated at five days for the largest repositories.

What the team does not yet know: whether the catalog can be rebuilt faster in parts, whether offsite copies of the catalog exist, which matters have court deadlines this week, and whether a full image restore is safe.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize catalog loss. Participants see that the data exists but is inaccessible.
  • Protect backup data. The team isolates repositories to protect the blocks.
  • Choose a restore approach. Engineers weigh catalog rebuild against full image restore.
  • Prioritize matters. Participants rank by court deadlines and client commitments.
  • Restore and validate. The team restores in order with integrity checks.
  • Improve catalog resilience. Participants review catalog copies and recovery options.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Isolate backup data blocks first and prioritize by court deadline, then choose between partial catalog rebuild and full image restore.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Encrypted file servers and catalog lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Repositories isolated and options evaluated.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Rebuild and risk are diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A restoration plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Restoration proceeds by priority.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Operations stabilize.

T+06:00 — Validation. Data is validated.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes backups that can be found and restored selectively and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Backup & Recovery Engineering

  • Isolates repositories and locks credentials.
  • Evaluates partial catalog rebuild versus image restore.
  • Plans catalog protection.
  • Plans copies.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Infrastructure Operations Engineering

  • Starts catalog rebuild for priority repositories.
  • Prepares image restores.
  • Validates restored files.
  • Tracks changes.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners and attorneys define matters with deadlines.
  • The service desk logs requests.
  • Owners define manual document retrieval.
  • Prepare validation checks.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Investigates the intrusion path.
  • Hunts for persistence.
  • Preserves evidence.
  • Adds alerts for catalog database changes.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Forensics & Vendor Support Liaison

  • Opens cases with the backup vendor and forensics firm.
  • Asks for fast catalog rebuild options.
  • Requests support engineers.
  • Escalates through account teams.
  • Records Google Cloud guidance and case numbers.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for law firm operations and informs leadership.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates workarounds and external contacts.
  • Tracks business impact.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 8.13 backup, 5.30 ICT readiness, 8.2 privileged access, 5.28 evidence collection; SOC 2 availability and confidentiality).
  • Assesses recoverability and client confidentiality.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Replicate the backup catalog to an independent, protected location frequently.

Rehearse catalog rebuild and image restore and measure times.

Maintain restore priorities tied to deadlines and obligations.

Protect backup servers with separate credentials and network isolation.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.13, 5.30, 8.2, 5.28) and SOC 2 expect backup and recovery testing evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on isolate-and-prioritize.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. Why was the data unusable?
  2. What did you isolate first?
  3. Which approach did you choose?
  4. How did you prioritize?
  5. What did clients hear?
  6. What design do you want?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the Forensics & Vendor Support Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
