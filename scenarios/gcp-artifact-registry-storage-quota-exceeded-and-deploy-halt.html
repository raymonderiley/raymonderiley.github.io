GCP Artifact Registry Storage Quota Exceeded and Deploy Halt

Google Cloud Resiliency

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines an Artifact Registry repository that reaches its storage quota and blocks image pushes while an urgent security fix is waiting to ship, and cleanup policies risk deleting images that production still needs.

Participants are working-level: platform engineers, application engineers, release managers, the service desk, cloud operations and monitoring staff, the Google Cloud support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of repository quotas, cleanup policies, tags versus digests, immutability, rollback needs and cost.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Plover Software builds hundreds of container images per day into a shared Artifact Registry repository. Cleanup policies were configured to delete untagged images older than 30 days and are in dry-run mode. Production deployments reference images by tag.

At 09:00 on Monday, a CI migration begins pushing a new image for every commit on every branch, including large debug layers. Storage reaches the project quota at 11:30 and pushes fail. A critical vulnerability fix for the payments service must ship by 14:00 and its pipeline fails at the push step. Someone enables cleanup policies in active mode without review at 11:45.

What the team does not yet know: the cleanup policy deletes images by age without regard to which digests production runs, tags in production point to images that are older than 30 days and now untagged, rollback to a previous version depends on those images, and increasing quota requires approval that takes hours.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize a registry quota block. Participants read quota metrics and pipeline errors to link failures to storage limits.
  • Protect production images. The team prevents deletion of images in use and verifies digests before cleanup.
  • Ship the security fix. Engineers create a path for the urgent fix such as a separate repository or cleaned space.
  • Control cleanup safely. Participants disable or correct policies with a dry run and review.
  • Reduce growth. The team stops the flood of branch images.
  • Improve registry governance. Participants review quotas, cleanup rules, retention and rollback guarantees.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • No image may be deleted unless it is verified not to be referenced by a running or rollback deployment.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Failed pushes and a waiting fix lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Cleanup is halted and the urgent path is created.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Quota and image usage are diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A safe cleanup and quota plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. The fix ships and cleanup runs under control.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Storage and pipelines stabilize.

T+06:00 — Validation. Rollback ability is verified.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Inject #1: Pushes fail and an active cleanup starts deleting

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 12:00. Image pushes fail with quota errors. The payments security fix must ship by 14:00. Cleanup policies were switched to active at 11:45 and deletions are in progress.

The platform engineer finds production references images by tag that are now untagged and older than 30 days.

A quota increase needs approval. Deleted images may not be recoverable.

Facilitator provides: quota graph, cleanup policy, deletion log and production image list.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Authorize halting cleanup.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Platform Engineering (Artifact Registry)
  • Disable cleanup.
  • Create separate repo.
  • Request quota.
Application Engineering
  • Stop branch pushes.
  • List digests in use.
  • Ship fix by digest.
Release Management & Service Desk
  • Prioritize fix.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Confirm rollback needs.
Cloud Operations & Monitoring
  • Watch deletions.
  • Watch deployments.
  • Preserve logs.
Google Cloud Support & TAM Liaison
  • Open a case.
  • Ask about recovery.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform leadership.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Plan customer notice.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about vulnerability timelines.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. How do you stop further damage first?
  2. What was already deleted and can it be recovered?
  3. How do you ship the fix without the main repository?
  4. Why is tag-based deployment risky?
  5. How do you decide what is safe to delete?
  6. How do you stop branch image growth?
  7. What do customers hear about the vulnerability?
  8. Why was cleanup enabled without review?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the tag-to-digest issue until T2 lists production images.
  • If players run broader deletes, stop and discuss.
  • Look for halting cleanup first.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes delivery of the urgent security fix and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Platform Engineering (Artifact Registry)

  • Reads quota metrics and repository contents.
  • Disables active cleanup and plans a verified policy.
  • Creates a separate repository for urgent releases.
  • Requests quota increases.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Application Engineering

  • Stops branch image pushes and removes debug layers.
  • Lists digests used in production and rollback sets.
  • Ships the security fix by digest.
  • Validates pipelines.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Release Management & Service Desk

  • Release managers decide the priority of the fix and approve the urgent path.
  • The service desk logs developer reports.
  • Owners confirm rollback needs.
  • Prepare validation checks.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Cloud Operations & Monitoring

  • Monitors repository growth and deletion events.

  • Coordinates workarounds and external contacts.

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.28, 8.8 vulnerability management, 8.32 change management; SOC 2 security and change management).
  • Assesses vulnerability remediation timelines.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Deploy by digest and keep a protected set of release and rollback images that cleanup cannot delete.

Run cleanup in dry run, review results and enable with approval.

Separate release repositories from developer build repositories and set quotas and alerts.

Limit CI image production by branch rules.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.8, 8.32) and SOC 2 expect vulnerability remediation timelines and change evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on cleanup safety and rollback guarantees.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How did you stop the damage?
  2. What was deleted and recovered?
  3. How did you ship the fix?
  4. How did you set safe cleanup rules?
  5. How did you stop the branch flood?
  6. How did you verify rollback?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the Google Cloud Support & TAM Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
