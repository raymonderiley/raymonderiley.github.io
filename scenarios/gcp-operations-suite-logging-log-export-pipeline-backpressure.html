GCP Operations Suite Logging Log Export Pipeline Backpressure

Google Cloud Resiliency

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a Cloud Logging export pipeline that falls behind after a log volume spike and a failing sink destination, so security detections lag and audit logs risk being dropped before they reach the SIEM and long-term storage.

Participants are working-level: observability engineers, platform and SRE engineers, application owners, the service desk, security operations and monitoring staff, the Google Cloud support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of sinks, exclusions, log buckets, Pub/Sub export, retention, and costs.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Thistle Finance exports organization-wide audit and application logs through sinks to a Pub/Sub topic feeding a SIEM and to a Cloud Storage bucket for seven-year retention. Log buckets retain 30 days. A verbose debug flag was enabled in a trading application during testing.

At 10:00 on Thursday, debug logging produces 10 times normal volume. The SIEM ingestion function throttles and Pub/Sub backlog grows. At 11:00 a permissions change on the storage bucket causes the archive sink to fail with errors. Detections that depend on SIEM events lag by 90 minutes. Log-based alerts stop firing for some projects due to ingestion limits.

What the team does not yet know: sink failures are retried only for a limited time and some audit logs may be lost from the archive, the SIEM license limits daily ingestion and has paused lower-priority sources, the debug flag also exposes sensitive fields in logs, and a high-severity alert was missed during the lag.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize pipeline backpressure. Participants read sink metrics, Pub/Sub backlog and SIEM lag to see the whole path.
  • Restore detection first. The team prioritizes security and audit sources over application noise.
  • Stop the flood. Engineers fix the debug flag and add exclusions carefully.
  • Protect audit completeness. Participants repair sink permissions and assess lost logs.
  • Handle sensitive log content. The team assesses exposure from verbose logs.
  • Improve observability governance. Participants review sink design, quotas, ownership and alerts.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Audit and security logs take priority over application logs at every decision.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Lagging detections and sink errors lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Security sources are prioritized and the flood is cut.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Sink failures and log loss are diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A repair and review plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Sinks are repaired and backlog is drained.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Lag returns to normal.

T+06:00 — Validation. Completeness and exposure are validated.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Inject #1: Detections lag and the archive sink fails

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 11:30. The SIEM shows 90 minutes of lag. The archive sink reports permission errors. Debug logging from a trading app is flooding the pipeline.

The observability engineer finds Pub/Sub backlog growing and SIEM license limits pausing lower-priority sources.

Some audit logs may expire from the 30-day log bucket if not exported. Debug logs may contain sensitive fields.

Facilitator provides: sink metrics, backlog graph, SIEM status and log samples.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Prioritize security sources.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Observability Engineering (Cloud Logging)
  • Fix sink permissions.
  • Add exclusions.
  • Plan drain.
Platform & SRE Engineering
  • Disable debug.
  • Identify sensitive fields.
  • Validate.
Application Owners & Service Desk
  • Confirm critical logs.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Review log content.
Security Operations & Monitoring
  • Hunt manually.
  • Review missed alerts.
  • Preserve evidence.
Google Cloud Support & TAM Liaison
  • Open a case.
  • Ask about retries.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform leadership.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Plan stakeholder contact.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about audit log obligations.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. How do you tell where in the pipeline logs are stuck?
  2. What do you prioritize and what do you drop?
  3. How do you protect audit logs that may expire?
  4. How do you hunt during a detection gap?
  5. What risks come from verbose debug logs?
  6. What did the lag cost in detections?
  7. What do auditors need to hear?
  8. Why was debug enabled in a trading app?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the missed alert until OPS reviews detections.
  • If players exclude everything, ask about audit needs.
  • Look for source prioritization.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

  • Assesses audit log completeness and security detection continuity.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Tier logs by value: audit and security logs get dedicated sinks, capacity and alerts, separate from application noise.

Alert on sink errors and backlog age, and test export recovery.

Control debug flags and redact sensitive fields at the source.

Review permission changes on sink destinations.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.15, 8.16) and SOC 2 expect log completeness and monitoring evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on audit log completeness and detection gap handling.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How did you find where logs were stuck?
  2. How did you prioritize sources?
  3. How did you protect audit logs?
  4. How did you hunt during the gap?
  5. How did you handle sensitive logs?
  6. How did you respond to the auditor?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the Google Cloud Support & TAM Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
