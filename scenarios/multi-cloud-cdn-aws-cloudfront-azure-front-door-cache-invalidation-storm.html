Multi-Cloud CDN (AWS CloudFront & Azure Front Door) Cache Invalidation Storm

Hybrid Cloud (AWS & Azure)

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines an automated cache invalidation job that fires against both CloudFront and Front Door after a content release, with a loop bug that repeats it, so caches are purged continuously, origins are hit by a thundering herd and sites slow or fail.

Participants are working-level: edge and CDN engineers, cloud platform engineers, application owners, the service desk, cloud operations and monitoring staff, the AWS and Microsoft support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of invalidation limits, origin shielding, cache hit ratios, rate limits and stale-while-revalidate.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Harlow News publishes articles and media through CloudFront for North America and Front Door for Europe, with shared origins in both clouds. A publishing pipeline calls wildcard invalidations after each release. A recent change added a retry on failure without a backoff or a cap.

At 08:00 on Monday, a release triggers an invalidation that partially fails on Front Door. The retry loop repeats wildcard invalidations on both CDNs every minute. Cache hit ratio drops from 95 percent to 12 percent. Origins receive 15 times normal traffic, response times climb, and some requests return errors. Invalidation quotas are exhausted so legitimate purges for breaking news are rejected.

What the team does not yet know: stopping the loop does not rebuild caches, origins need protection while caches warm, breaking news needs targeted purges, and an editor mistakenly published a wrong headline that needs urgent removal.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize an invalidation storm. Participants correlate hit ratio, origin load and invalidation logs.
  • Stop the loop. The team halts the pipeline and clears retry queues.
  • Protect origins. Engineers add shielding, rate limits and capacity while caches warm.
  • Handle urgent purges. Participants remove the wrong headline with targeted invalidation.
  • Warm caches safely. The team restores hit ratios in a controlled way.
  • Improve CDN operations. Participants review invalidation design, quotas and release process.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Stop the invalidation pipeline before touching CDN configuration, and record the number of invalidations issued.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Origin overload and low hit ratios lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. The loop is stopped and origins protected.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Quota and cache state are diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A warm-up and purge plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Caches are restored in order.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Performance stabilizes.

T+06:00 — Validation. Hit ratios and content are validated.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Inject #1: Caches are purged in a loop and origins buckle

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 08:30. Pages are slow or failing. Cache hit ratio fell from 95 to 12 percent and origins see 15 times normal traffic.

The CDN engineer finds a publishing pipeline retrying wildcard invalidations every minute on both CDNs.

Invalidation quotas are exhausted. An editor published a wrong headline that must be removed.

Facilitator provides: hit ratio graphs, invalidation logs, origin metrics and the pipeline change.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Authorize stopping the publishing pipeline.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Edge & CDN Engineering
  • Stop loop.
  • Review logs.
  • Plan targeted purge.
Cloud Platform Engineering (AWS & Azure)
  • Add shielding.
  • Scale origin.
  • Validate.
Application Owners & Service Desk
  • Rank pages.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Define freeze.
Cloud Operations & Monitoring
  • Watch ratios.
  • Build warm-up view.
  • Preserve logs.
AWS, Microsoft & Carrier Support Liaison
  • Open cases.
  • Ask about quotas.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform leadership.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Plan reader notice.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about editorial correction duties.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. How do you prove invalidations are the cause?
  2. What do you stop first?
  3. How do you protect origins while caches are cold?
  4. How do you remove the wrong headline without quota?
  5. How do you warm caches without another stampede?
  6. What do readers see?
  7. What do editors do meanwhile?
  8. Why did the retry have no backoff or cap?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the quota exhaustion until T1 checks the invalidation APIs.
  • If players invalidate everything again, ask about quota.
  • Look for stop-first discipline.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes fast, correct content delivery at the edge and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Edge & CDN Engineering

  • Disables the publishing pipeline and retry loop.
  • Reviews invalidation logs on both CDNs.
  • Applies targeted purge for the wrong headline.
  • Plans controlled warm-up.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

  • Assesses content delivery availability and accuracy.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Use versioned asset names or targeted purges instead of wildcard invalidations.

Cap and back off retries in release pipelines.

Use origin shielding and serve stale content while revalidating.

Alert on invalidation rate and hit ratio.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.32, 8.16, 8.14) and SOC 2 expect change and availability evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on stop-first response and origin protection.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How did you identify the cause?
  2. What did you stop first?
  3. How did you protect origins?
  4. How did you remove the headline?
  5. How did you warm caches?
  6. What design changes do you want?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the AWS, Microsoft & Carrier Support Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
