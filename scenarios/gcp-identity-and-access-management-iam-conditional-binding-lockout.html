GCP Identity and Access Management (IAM) Conditional Binding Lockout

Google Cloud Resiliency

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a Google Cloud IAM change that adds conditional bindings and a deny policy with a flawed time and resource condition, removing administrative access for the platform team and breaking service accounts while break-glass access depends on the broken path.

Participants are working-level: cloud identity and access engineers, platform and SRE engineers, application owners, the service desk, security operations and monitoring staff, the Google Cloud support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of conditional role bindings, deny policies, organization policy, break-glass accounts and recovery of control.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Fairmont Health runs production in 40 projects under a folder structure with IAM managed through Terraform. A security hardening initiative replaces broad Owner roles with conditional bindings limited to business hours and tagged resources, and adds an organization-level deny policy for destructive actions outside change windows.

At 06:30 on Tuesday, the change is applied. A condition uses the wrong time zone and a resource tag that does not exist on most projects, so conditional administrator bindings never match. The deny policy applies to the platform admins' group. Engineers cannot modify firewall rules, restart services or change IAM. Several service accounts lose permissions and batch jobs fail. The break-glass group depends on a conditional binding as well.

What the team does not yet know: organization administrators in the super admin role are outside the conditions but only three people hold it and one is traveling, a deny policy can be removed only by someone with the right permission, Terraform pipelines also run as affected service accounts, and a clinical scheduling system needs a configuration fix this morning.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize an IAM-caused lockout. Participants use policy troubleshooter and audit logs to prove conditions and denies are the cause.
  • Regain control securely. The team uses documented break-glass paths and super admin roles with oversight.
  • Fix the policy. Engineers correct conditions and deny scope in small, verified steps through reviewed changes.
  • Protect patient systems. Participants prioritize clinical operations and restore service account permissions.
  • Avoid creating new risk. The team avoids granting broad roles as a shortcut and logs every emergency action.
  • Improve IAM governance. Participants review break-glass design, condition testing, deny policy scope and pipeline dependencies.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Every emergency IAM action must be approved, logged, narrow and reversed after use.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Lost admin access and failing jobs lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Break-glass access is used and priorities set.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Conditions and denies are diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A corrective plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Policies and permissions are corrected.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Access and jobs stabilize.

T+06:00 — Validation. Security posture is verified.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Injects

Inject #1: Admins locked out after the hardening change

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 07:00. The platform team cannot change firewall rules, restart services or modify IAM. Batch jobs fail. A clinical scheduling fix is waiting.

The IAM engineer finds conditional bindings with a wrong time zone and a missing tag condition, plus an organization deny policy covering the admin group.

The break-glass group also depends on a conditional binding. Three super admins exist and one is traveling. Terraform pipelines run as affected service accounts.

Facilitator provides: IAM policy snapshot, condition expressions, deny policy, super admin list and audit logs.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Authorize use of super admin access with two approvals.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Cloud Identity & Access Engineering
  • Prove the cause.
  • Use super admin to fix.
  • Plan corrected policy.
Platform & SRE Engineering
  • Restore service accounts.
  • Prioritize clinical fix.
  • Validate.
Application Owners & Service Desk
  • Rank systems.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Prepare manual steps.
Security Operations & Monitoring
  • Monitor break-glass.
  • Review logs.
  • Preserve logs.
Google Cloud Support & TAM Liaison
  • Open a case.
  • Ask about recovery.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform leadership.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Plan clinical communication.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about emergency access rules.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. How do you prove conditions and denies are the cause?
  2. Who may use super admin access and how is it approved?
  3. Why did break-glass also fail?
  4. How do you fix policy without granting broad roles?
  5. What clinical work cannot wait?
  6. How do you test conditions before applying them?
  7. What does security watch during emergency access?
  8. Why did Terraform depend on affected accounts?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the break-glass dependency until T1 reviews the group.
  • If players grant Owner to everyone, stop and discuss.
  • Look for narrow, logged actions.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes restoring administrative access and securing the organization and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Cloud Identity & Access Engineering

  • Uses policy troubleshooter to prove which condition or deny applies.
  • Uses a super admin to correct or remove the faulty policy.
  • Plans a corrected version with tests.
  • Plans Terraform recovery.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Platform & SRE Engineering

  • Restores service account permissions and checks pipelines.
  • Prioritizes the clinical scheduling fix.
  • Validates systems after each change.
  • Tracks temporary grants.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners rank systems and identify clinical impact.
  • The service desk logs access requests.
  • Owners prepare manual procedures.
  • Prepare validation checks.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Security Operations & Monitoring

  • Monitors break-glass use and detects misuse.
  • Reviews audit logs.
  • Preserves logs.
  • Adds alerts for IAM and deny changes.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Google Cloud Support & TAM Liaison

  • Opens a Sev A case on IAM conditions and deny policies.
  • Asks about recovery options and evaluation order.
  • Requests guidance if super admins are unavailable.
  • Escalates through the TAM.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for clinical operations and informs leadership.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates workarounds and external contacts.
  • Tracks business impact.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.28, 8.2 privileged access, 8.32 change management; SOC 2 security).
  • Assesses clinical system availability and access control.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Test IAM changes with policy simulation and staged rollout; never apply conditions or denies organization-wide first.

Design break-glass so it does not depend on the policies it must rescue, and test it regularly.

Scope deny policies narrowly and exclude emergency principals deliberately.

Separate pipeline identities and alert on IAM changes.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.2, 8.5, 8.32) and SOC 2 expect privileged access controls and emergency access evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on break-glass design and policy testing.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How did you prove the cause?
  2. How did you gain access safely?
  3. Why did break-glass fail?
  4. How did you handle the clinical fix?
  5. How did you handle the deny policy?
  6. How did you manage leadership frustration?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the Google Cloud Support & TAM Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
