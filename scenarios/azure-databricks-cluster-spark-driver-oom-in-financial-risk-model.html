Azure Databricks Cluster Spark Driver OOM in Financial Risk Model

Azure Cloud Resiliency

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines an Azure Databricks nightly financial risk model that fails when the Spark driver runs out of memory after data volume growth and a join change, delaying value-at-risk and limit reports needed before markets open, while retries consume the cluster budget.

Participants are working-level: data platform engineers, quantitative and risk modeling staff, business owners, the service desk, cloud operations and monitoring staff, the Microsoft support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of driver and executor memory, broadcast joins, skew, job retries, run reproducibility and reporting deadlines.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Cobalt Asset Management runs a nightly risk pipeline on Azure Databricks that ingests positions and market data from ADLS, computes value-at-risk, stress tests and limit utilization, and publishes results to the risk dashboard by 06:30 for the 07:00 trading desk briefing. The job runs on a job cluster with a driver of 28 GB.

At 02:10 on Monday, the job fails with 'Driver ran out of memory' after a code change replaced a sort-merge join with a broadcast join and a collect() call to build a debug summary. Weekend position volume grew 35 percent after a fund onboarded. Automatic retries restarted the job three times, each running about 70 minutes before failing at the same stage. By 05:50, no output exists.

What the team does not yet know: a rerun with a bigger driver may succeed but produce slightly different results if random seeds are not fixed, one input partition is heavily skewed due to a single large position set, the previous night's results are available but stale, and regulatory limit reporting requires documented methodology for any model change.

Exercise Objectives

  • Diagnose the failure. Participants read driver logs, Spark UI, stage metrics and the code change to find the memory cause.
  • Meet the deadline safely. The team chooses between rerunning with more resources, using a lighter model variant or publishing prior results with disclosure.
  • Preserve model integrity. Participants control seeds, versions and methodology so results are reproducible and defensible.
  • Control cost and retries. Engineers stop wasteful retries and set limits.
  • Communicate with risk consumers. The team provides honest status and caveats to the trading desk and risk managers.
  • Improve data engineering resilience. Participants review code review for collects and broadcasts, skew handling, test data scale and run guardrails.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Any model or methodology change must be named, approved and documented before results are used.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Missing risk output and repeated failures lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Retries are stopped and the deadline plan is chosen.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. The memory cause is diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A rerun or fallback plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. The job is rerun or the fallback is published with disclosure.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Results are validated.

T+06:00 — Validation. Consumers are briefed and reconciled.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Injects

Inject #1: Risk job fails three times before market open

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 05:50. The overnight risk job has failed three times. The last failure reports 'Driver ran out of memory' at a stage that builds the exposure summary. No new risk output exists. The trading briefing is at 07:00.

The data engineer sees the code change from last Friday used a broadcast join on a table that has grown, plus a collect() to the driver for a debug summary. Position volume is up 35 percent. Retries cost about 900 USD each.

Yesterday's results are available but stale. A rerun with a larger driver may succeed in 75 minutes if seeds and versions match. Limit reporting requires documented methodology for changes.

Facilitator provides: driver logs, Spark UI stage summary, the pull request diff and cost figures.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Authorize stopping retries.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Data Platform Engineering (Databricks)
  • Stop retries and size a clean rerun.
  • Check skew and AQE settings.
  • Control cost.
Quantitative & Risk Modeling Team
  • Remove the collect and revert the join.
  • Fix seeds and versions.
  • Plan validation against prior results.
Business Owners & Service Desk
  • Define the 07:00 needs.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Decide on stale results caveats.
Cloud Operations & Monitoring
  • Monitor cluster cost and runs.
  • Check quotas.
  • Preserve logs.
Microsoft Support & TAM Liaison
  • Open a case.
  • Ask about sizing.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform the trading desk head.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Plan manual checks.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about methodology approval.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. How do you confirm the memory cause?
  2. What options meet the 07:00 deadline and what are their risks?
  3. Is publishing stale results acceptable and with what disclosure?
  4. How do you ensure the rerun is reproducible?
  5. Who approves a methodology change made under pressure?
  6. Why were retries allowed to repeat identically?
  7. What does the trading desk need to hear?
  8. How did the code change pass review?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the skew issue until T2 inspects partitions.
  • If players rerun immediately with no changes, ask what will differ.
  • Look for stopping retries first.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

  • Decides on publishing stale results.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Make critical batch pipelines predictable: scale tests with production-like volume, memory-safe patterns, no driver collects on large data and review rules for join strategy changes.

Build recovery paths into the schedule: checkpoints, a fallback with clearly labeled prior results, and run guardrails that stop identical retries and alert on runtime drift.

Protect model integrity: fixed seeds, versioned code and data, documented methodology and approval for any change under pressure.

Manage capacity and cost: quotas planned for burst reruns and budgets per run.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.32) and SOC 2 expect change control, testing and processing integrity evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on reproducibility and disclosure of stale or changed results.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How long did it take to find the memory cause?
  2. What did you do about retries?
  3. How did you meet or miss the deadline?
  4. How did you ensure reproducibility?
  5. How did you decide on methodology changes?
  6. How was stale data handled?
  7. How did you resolve capacity?
  8. How was skew handled?
  9. How accurate were updates to the desk and the CRO?
  10. What did Microsoft support add?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  12. How are code changes tested at scale?
  13. What access or permissions were missing?
  14. What alerts are missing?
  15. What would you change in the pipeline?
