GCP BigQuery Slot Allocation Exhaustion in Leadership Reporting

Google Cloud Resiliency

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a BigQuery reservation whose slots are exhausted by an unbounded ad hoc query and a changed transformation, causing the month-end board and regulatory reports to queue and miss their deadline.

Participants are working-level: data platform engineers, analytics engineers, reporting owners, the service desk, cloud operations and monitoring staff, the Google Cloud support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of slot reservations, assignments, query priority, cost controls and report dependency management.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Redstone Capital runs month-end consolidation and reporting in BigQuery with a 1,000-slot reservation shared by finance, risk and analytics projects. Board packs and a regulatory submission are due at 09:00 on the third working day. Queries are scheduled from an orchestrator.

At 22:00 on the night before the deadline, an analyst runs a cross join without a filter against a 40 TB table in the shared reservation, and a changed transformation job reruns a full history rebuild. All 1,000 slots are consumed, scheduled reporting queries queue for hours and some time out. At 03:00 the monitoring team notices the board pack job still has not started.

What the team does not yet know: killing the analyst query frees slots but the rebuild job needs 5 hours of slots even alone, an on-demand fallback would cost much more, partial results from earlier runs may be reusable, and the regulatory submission has a hard cutoff.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize slot exhaustion. Participants read job metadata, reservation usage and queue times to find which jobs consume slots.
  • Free and prioritize capacity. The team cancels or throttles low-value jobs and assigns priority to reporting.
  • Meet deadlines honestly. Engineers evaluate options such as burst capacity, partial results and delayed submission.
  • Control cost. Participants set approvals for spend on on-demand capacity.
  • Protect report integrity. The team ensures reports use consistent, validated data.
  • Improve governance. Participants review reservations, quotas, query guardrails and job dependency design.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • No report goes out unless its inputs are complete and validated, even under deadline pressure.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Missing reports and queued jobs lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Slots are freed and priorities set.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Job costs and options are diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A delivery plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Reports are generated and validated.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Reports are delivered.

T+06:00 — Validation. Reconciliation and review are done.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Inject #1: Board pack jobs are stuck in the queue

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 03:10. The board pack and regulatory jobs have not started. The reservation shows 100 percent slot use since 22:00.

The data engineer finds an unfiltered cross join and a full-history rebuild consuming all slots.

The rebuild needs about 5 hours of slots on its own. On-demand capacity would be costly. The regulatory submission has a 09:00 cutoff.

Facilitator provides: job list, slot usage graph, cost estimate and the report dependency map.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Approve cancellation and spend limit.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Data Platform Engineering (BigQuery)
  • Cancel offending jobs.
  • Plan reservation change.
  • Control spend.
Analytics Engineering
  • Prioritize reporting jobs.
  • Reuse partial results.
  • Fix transformation.
Reporting Owners & Service Desk
  • Decide what must be ready.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Contact regulator if needed.
Cloud Operations & Monitoring
  • Build the dashboard.
  • Watch cost.
  • Preserve logs.
Google Cloud Support & TAM Liaison
  • Open a case.
  • Ask about flex slots.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform leadership.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Plan board communication.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about submission rules.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. How do you find which jobs consume the slots?
  2. What do you cancel first and who approves?
  3. How do you meet the 09:00 cutoff or decide to miss it?
  4. What can be reused from earlier runs?
  5. What spend is acceptable?
  6. How do you prevent incomplete data in reports?
  7. What do the board and regulator hear?
  8. Why can one query starve the reservation?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the rebuild duration until T1 estimates it.
  • If players run reports on partial data without notice, ask about integrity.
  • Look for explicit deadline trade-offs.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes board and regulatory reporting deadlines and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Data Platform Engineering (BigQuery)

  • Reads job metadata and reservation usage.
  • Cancels or throttles the offending jobs.
  • Plans reservation changes and burst capacity.
  • Controls spend.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Analytics Engineering

  • Prioritizes the board pack and regulatory jobs.
  • Reuses validated partial results where safe.
  • Fixes the transformation change.
  • Validates outputs.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Reporting Owners & Service Desk

  • Reporting owners decide what must be ready and what can slip.
  • The service desk logs requests.
  • Owners coordinate with regulators and board secretary.
  • Prepare validation checks.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Cloud Operations & Monitoring

  • Builds a dashboard of slot use, queue time and job progress.
  • Watches costs.
  • Preserves job logs.
  • Adds queue-time alerts.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

  • Asks about temporary capacity.
  • Requests guidance on query prioritization.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for board and regulatory reporting and informs leadership.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates workarounds and external contacts.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.28, 8.6 capacity management; SOC 2 availability and processing integrity).
  • Assesses timeliness and accuracy of the regulatory submission.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Separate critical reporting from ad hoc analysis with dedicated reservations or priorities and per-project quotas.

Add guardrails such as maximum bytes billed and query review for large scans.

Design reporting pipelines with dependency timing, early-start checkpoints and validated fallbacks.

Alert on queue time and slot saturation.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.6, 8.16) and SOC 2 expect capacity management and processing integrity evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on report integrity under deadline and reservation governance.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How did you find the slot consumers?
  2. What did you cancel and who approved?
  3. How did you decide on spend?
  4. How did you handle reuse of results?
  5. How did you decide on the regulatory deadline?
  6. How did you validate outputs?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the Google Cloud Support & TAM Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
