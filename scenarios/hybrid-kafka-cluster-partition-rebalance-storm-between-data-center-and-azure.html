Hybrid Kafka Cluster Partition Rebalance Storm Between Data Center and Azure

Hybrid Cloud (Azure Focus)

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a Kafka deployment stretched across an on-premises data center and Azure where a network latency spike triggers repeated consumer group rebalances and partition leader elections, causing consumers to stall, lag to grow and duplicate processing.

Participants are working-level: streaming platform engineers, network and infrastructure engineers, application and data owners, the service desk, cloud operations and monitoring staff, the Microsoft support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of consumer group rebalancing, session timeouts, leader election, replication factor and rack awareness, and idempotency.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Granite Payments runs Kafka brokers in an on-premises data center and in Azure, with replication across both. Payment events, fraud signals and ledger updates flow through topics. Consumer session timeouts are short and brokers are rack-aware only partly.

At 13:30 on Friday, a WAN latency spike causes broker heartbeats to time out between sites. Partition leaders move repeatedly, and consumer groups rebalance continuously. Consumers pause, lag on fraud and ledger topics grows to 40 minutes and some consumers reprocess messages after rebalances. Downstream ledger entries appear duplicated in a reconciliation view.

What the team does not yet know: some consumers commit offsets before processing, rebalances are being retriggered by the consumers themselves, an unclean leader election setting may have been triggered on one topic, and fraud scoring is delayed for live payments.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize a rebalance storm. Participants read group and leader metrics to see the repeating pattern.
  • Stabilize the cluster. The team calms elections and rebalancing without data loss.
  • Protect payment flows. Engineers prioritize fraud and ledger topics.
  • Handle duplicates and loss. Participants assess idempotency and offset commits.
  • Maintain data integrity. The team checks for lost or duplicated events.
  • Improve streaming resilience. Participants review timeouts, cross-site design and idempotent consumers.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Do not reset consumer offsets or force leader elections without owner approval and a recorded rationale.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Lag and rebalancing lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. The cluster is stabilized and priority set.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Network and consumer behavior are diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A stabilization and integrity plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Changes are applied in order.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Lag clears.

T+06:00 — Validation. Data integrity is validated.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Inject #1: Consumers stall in repeated rebalances

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 14:00. Fraud and ledger consumers lag 40 minutes. Consumer groups rebalance continuously after a WAN latency spike.

The streaming engineer finds partition leaders moving between sites and heartbeats timing out. Some consumers reprocess messages.

A reconciliation view shows duplicate ledger entries. Fraud scoring for live payments is delayed.

Facilitator provides: group state, leader election log, lag graphs and link metrics.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Authorize adjusting timeouts and pausing scaling.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Streaming Platform Engineering (Kafka)
  • Read state.
  • Adjust timeouts.
  • Avoid unclean election.
Network & Infrastructure Engineering
  • Check WAN.
  • Prioritize traffic.
  • Engage carrier.
Application & Data Owners and Service Desk
  • Rank topics.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Define duplicate handling.
Cloud Operations & Monitoring
  • Watch lag.
  • Find duplicates.
  • Preserve logs.
Microsoft, Carrier & Vendor Support Liaison
  • Open a case.
  • Ask about latency.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform leadership.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Plan merchant notice.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about payment duties.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. How do you know it is a rebalance storm and not a broker failure?
  2. Which topics matter most and why?
  3. How do you stabilize without losing messages?
  4. What happens to duplicate entries?
  5. Who approves offset changes?
  6. How do you handle delayed fraud scoring?
  7. What do merchants hear?
  8. Why are consumers not idempotent?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal early offset commits until APP reviews consumer code.
  • If players reset offsets, ask about data loss.
  • Look for integrity before speed.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes message integrity and consumer progress for critical event streams and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Streaming Platform Engineering (Kafka)

  • Reads group state, leader elections and lag.
  • Adjusts session timeouts and pauses scaling.
  • Avoids unclean leader election.
  • Plans controlled recovery.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Network & Infrastructure Engineering

  • Investigates WAN latency and packet loss.

  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

  • Assesses payment integrity and settlement timing.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Avoid stretching a single cluster across an unstable WAN or design carefully with rack awareness and tolerant timeouts.

Make consumers idempotent and commit offsets after processing.

Disable unclean leader election for critical topics.

Alert on rebalance rate and lag.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.14, 8.16, 8.32) and SOC 2 expect availability and processing integrity evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on data integrity and avoiding offset shortcuts.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
