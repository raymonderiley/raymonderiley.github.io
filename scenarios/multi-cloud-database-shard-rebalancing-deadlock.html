Multi-Cloud Database Shard Rebalancing Deadlock

Multi-Cloud (AWS & Azure, No On-Prem)

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a shard rebalancing operation in a distributed database spanning AWS and Azure nodes that deadlocks when two moves wait on each other, leaving some shards locked, queries blocked and replication stalled.

Participants are working-level: database engineers, cloud platform engineers, application owners, the service desk, cloud operations and monitoring staff, the AWS and Microsoft support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of shard moves, locks, rebalancer scheduling, cross-cloud latency, cancel and resume behavior and data verification.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Crestline Marketplace runs an order and inventory database sharded across 48 nodes, 30 in AWS and 18 in Azure, to meet latency goals for two regions. A scheduled rebalance moves shards to even out storage after a growth spurt. Cross-cloud latency is around 35 ms.

At 13:00 on Thursday, the rebalancer starts concurrent moves. Two moves lock shards that each other need. Cross-cloud transfer slows and the moves exceed timeouts without releasing locks. Eleven shards are locked for writes, queries touching them stall, and checkout for 20 percent of customers fails. Replication lag grows on the locked shards.

What the team does not yet know: cancelling moves may leave partial copies, some shards are mid-transfer and not safe to resume, the rebalancer has no automatic deadlock resolution, and a flash sale starts at 16:00.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize shard deadlock. Participants identify which moves and locks form the cycle.
  • Restore writes safely. The team cancels or completes moves to release locks.
  • Protect data integrity. Engineers verify shard content before and after.
  • Manage business impact. Participants handle checkout failures and the upcoming sale.
  • Prevent recurrence during recovery. The team pauses further rebalancing.
  • Improve rebalancing governance. Participants review scheduling, limits and monitoring.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Pause the rebalancer before cancelling any move, and record each shard, its lock state and the action taken.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Locked shards and failed checkouts lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. The rebalancer is paused and priority set.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. The lock cycle is diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A resolution and verification plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Moves are resolved in order.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Writes are restored.

T+06:00 — Validation. Data is validated.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes available shards and consistent data during rebalancing and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Database Engineering (Distributed SQL)

  • Pauses the rebalancer and maps the lock cycle.
  • Cancels or completes moves in a safe order.
  • Verifies shard content after each action.
  • Plans deadlock detection.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Cloud Platform Engineering (AWS & Azure)

  • Checks cross-cloud network and storage health.
  • Supports data transfer retries.
  • Validates replication.
  • Tracks changes.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners rank affected customers and flows.
  • The service desk logs checkout failures.
  • Owners decide on the flash sale.
  • Prepare validation checks.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Cloud Operations & Monitoring

  • Watches lock waits and replication lag.
  • Builds a shard status view.
  • Preserves logs.
  • Adds lock duration alerts.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

AWS & Microsoft Support Liaison

  • Opens cases with AWS and Microsoft on cross-cloud throughput.
  • Asks about network limits.
  • Requests guidance on cross-cloud topology.
  • Escalates through both TAMs and the database vendor.
  • Records Google Cloud guidance and case numbers.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for marketplace operations and informs leadership.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates workarounds and external contacts.
  • Tracks business impact.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.28, 8.6 capacity management, 8.32 change management; SOC 2 availability and processing integrity).
  • Assesses order integrity and marketplace availability.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Limit concurrent shard moves, order them to avoid cycles and detect deadlocks automatically.

Schedule rebalancing outside peak periods and before known events.

Set cross-cloud transfer limits and timeouts that release locks.

Verify shard integrity after every move.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.32, 8.6, 8.16) and SOC 2 expect change and availability evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on pausing first and verifying shards.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How did you find the cycle?
  2. What did you pause first?
  3. How did you resolve moves?
  4. How did you verify shards?
  5. How did you handle the sale?
  6. What governance do you want?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the AWS & Microsoft Support Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
