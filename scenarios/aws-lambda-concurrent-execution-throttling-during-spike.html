AWS Lambda Concurrent Execution Throttling During Spike

AWS Cloud Resiliency

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a traffic spike that exhausts the regional Lambda concurrency quota. A single high-volume function consumes unreserved concurrency, other functions are throttled with 429 errors, asynchronous events back up, and downstream systems time out or retry, making the spike worse.

Participants are working-level: serverless and application engineers, platform and SRE engineers, application owners, the service desk, cloud operations and monitoring staff, the AWS support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of reserved and provisioned concurrency, burst limits, retries and dead-letter handling.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Quillmark Ticketing sells event tickets through a serverless platform in one AWS region. API Gateway invokes Lambda functions for search, cart, payment and confirmation. An SQS queue feeds a confirmation-email function and an EventBridge rule triggers inventory updates. The account concurrency quota is 1,000, and only the payment function has reserved concurrency (100).

At 12:00 on Friday, tickets for a major tour go on sale. Traffic jumps from 150 to 4,000 requests per second. The search function, written with a slow downstream call, runs for 8 seconds per invocation and consumes most of the account's unreserved concurrency. By 12:04, ConcurrentExecutions equals the account limit, Throttles climb for cart and confirmation functions, API Gateway returns 502 and 429 errors, and clients retry aggressively. SQS-triggered functions are throttled and messages return to the queue.

What the team does not yet know: the search function's downstream database is slow because of connection exhaustion, the cart function has no reserved concurrency so it competes with search, the SQS event source mapping has maximum concurrency unset, and the account quota increase request filed last quarter was never completed.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize concurrency throttling. Participants read ConcurrentExecutions, Throttles, Errors, Duration and IteratorAge metrics and tell throttling from function errors.
  • Apply reserved concurrency and throttles deliberately. The team decides which functions get reserved concurrency, which are capped and the effect of each choice on the others.
  • Break retry amplification. Participants limit client and event source retries, use backoff, and protect downstream systems.
  • Handle backlogs safely. Engineers manage SQS queues, dead-letter queues and EventBridge retries without losing or duplicating orders.
  • Obtain more capacity. The team requests a quota increase through support, understanding timelines and burst scaling limits.
  • Improve resilience design. Participants examine timeouts, concurrency planning, load testing and decoupling.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Cite the specific function, metric or setting in every recommendation; avoid generic statements such as 'scale it up'.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Throttling errors and failed purchases lead to incident declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Retry storms are limited and critical functions are protected.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. The slow search function and its downstream bottleneck are found.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Reserved concurrency and quota actions are planned.

T+03:00 — Execution. Concurrency settings, rate limits and queue handling are applied.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Error rates fall and backlogs drain.

T+06:00 — Validation. Order integrity and customer flows are verified.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Injects

Inject #1: On-sale spike exhausts account concurrency

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 12:08. Customers report that checkout fails and queues spin. API Gateway shows 38 percent 5xx and 26 percent 429 responses. CloudWatch shows account ConcurrentExecutions flat at 1,000 and Throttles rising on the cart and confirmation functions. The payment function, with reserved concurrency, still runs but is starved of cart requests.

The serverless engineer sees search averaging 8 seconds of duration against a normal 300 ms, at 3,200 concurrent executions requested. The SQS confirmation queue has 90,000 visible messages and mobile clients are retrying every second.

The AWS Health Dashboard shows no events. A quota increase request from last quarter is in 'draft' status. The promoter's operations lead asks whether the sale can continue.

Facilitator provides: the metric graphs, function configuration summary, queue depth and the API error breakdown.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Decide to protect cart and payment over search.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Serverless & Application Engineering
  • Identify the consuming function and why its duration is high.
  • Set reserved concurrency for cart and confirmation and cap search.
  • Explain the effect on search users.
Cloud Platform & SRE Engineering
  • Apply API Gateway throttling and check retry behavior.
  • Limit SQS maximum concurrency.
  • Check database connections.
Application Owners & Service Desk
  • List critical flows and degraded options.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Prepare a waiting room.
Cloud Operations & Monitoring
  • Build the dashboard.
  • Check quotas and Health.
  • List missing alarms.
AWS Support & TAM Liaison
  • Open an urgent quota case.
  • Ask about burst scaling.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Contact the promoter and venues.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Ask for the documented RTO.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask when quotas were last reviewed.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. How do you distinguish throttling from function errors in the metrics?
  2. Why does one function starve the others and what is the fix?
  3. What are the consequences of capping search?
  4. How do retries make the spike worse and how do you stop them?
  5. How do you drain 90,000 queued messages without duplicates?
  6. What does a quota increase involve and how long does it take?
  7. Who decides to use a waiting room?
  8. What should customers and the promoter be told?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the database bottleneck until the SRE checks downstream metrics.
  • If players only ask for more quota, ask what happens while waiting.
  • Look for reserved concurrency as protection and as a limit.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Inject #4: Downstream database connection exhaustion

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, puts completed purchases first and sets the load shedding policy.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes restrictions on search to protect payment and cart.
  • Approves customer-facing waiting room or throttling.

Serverless & Application Engineering

  • Reads Lambda metrics and identifies which function consumes concurrency and why its duration is high.
  • Sets reserved concurrency on cart and confirmation and caps the search function.
  • Reviews timeouts, retries and async configuration, and DLQ settings.
  • Plans fixes for the slow downstream call.

Cloud Platform & SRE Engineering

  • Configures API Gateway throttling and usage plans and checks for client retry storms.
  • Manages SQS event source mapping maximum concurrency and visibility timeouts.
  • Checks downstream database connection use and applies RDS Proxy or limits.
  • Coordinates the quota increase with support.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners confirm the critical purchase path and acceptable degradation for search.
  • The service desk tracks failed purchase reports and duplicate charges.
  • Owners prepare a waiting room or read-only catalog.
  • Prepare validation tests.

Cloud Operations & Monitoring

  • Builds a dashboard of concurrency, throttles, duration, queue depth and API error rates by function.
  • Confirms alarms and sets up anomaly alerts.
  • Checks AWS Health and service quotas.
  • Creates missing alarms afterward.

AWS Support & TAM Liaison

  • Opens a high-severity case for an urgent concurrency quota increase with evidence.
  • Asks about burst limits and scaling rates per region.
  • Requests guidance on provisioned concurrency.
  • Records the guidance.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for ticket sales and coordinates with the promoter and venues.
  • Issues time-stamped updates to leadership.
  • Coordinates customer messaging and social channels.
  • Tracks the outage against targets.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.28, 8.6 capacity management; SOC 2 availability).
  • Assesses consumer protection and refund exposure.
  • Captures evidence of capacity planning gaps.
  • Logs findings on quotas and load testing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Plan concurrency as a shared budget. Set reserved concurrency for critical functions and caps for noisy ones, request quota increases ahead of known events and use provisioned concurrency where cold starts matter.

Protect downstream systems. Use RDS Proxy, timeouts shorter than client timeouts, and bulkheads so one slow dependency cannot consume the account's capacity.

Control retries. Use exponential backoff with jitter on clients, set maximum concurrency on SQS event sources, configure DLQs and make handlers idempotent.

Load test at and above expected peak, including downstream limits, and alarm on throttles, concurrency percentage and queue age.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.6) and SOC 2 expect capacity planning evidence, quota management and tested peak readiness.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on concurrency allocation and retry control.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.
