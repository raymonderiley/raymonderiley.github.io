SWIFT / Payment Gateway Fraud via Compromised AI-Driven Integration Gateway

AI Failure & Autonomous Agent Disruption

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a compromised AI-driven integration gateway that translates and routes payment messages to an interbank messaging network, which the attacker uses to alter beneficiary details and issue fraudulent payment instructions.

Participants are working-level: payments integration engineers, payments technology engineers, application owners, the service desk, fraud and security operations analysts, the AI platform and cloud support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of message integrity, recall procedures, gateway isolation, reconciliation and regulatory reporting.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Ashbourne Trust uses an integration gateway with an AI mapping component that translates internal payment formats into interbank messages. The gateway has signing authority through an HSM-backed service. Reconciliation with the messaging network runs at end of day. A subset of high-value payments requires manual release.

At 15:00 on Friday, reconciliation alerts show 11 outgoing payments whose beneficiary account numbers differ from the source records. Total value is 9.4 million dollars. A mapping rule was changed by a compromised integration account the previous night. Additional altered messages continue to flow.

What the team does not yet know: how many messages are altered in total, whether the HSM signing path is also compromised, which funds can be recalled and how quickly, and whether manual release was bypassed.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize message tampering. Participants link mismatches to the gateway mapping change.
  • Stop outgoing messages. The team halts the gateway and suspends signing for it.
  • Recall payments. Engineers and operations contact correspondent banks.
  • Scope the compromise. Participants determine the account, change history and other paths.
  • Report and notify. The team assesses duties to the network operator and regulators.
  • Improve integration security. Participants review mapping change control and independent validation.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Halt outgoing messages and suspend the gateway's signing before recall work, and begin recall for every identified payment in value order.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Mismatched payments lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Gateway halted and recall started.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Scope is diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A reporting and recovery plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Recall and containment proceed.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Payments stabilize.

T+06:00 — Validation. Integrity is validated.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes payment messages that cannot be forged through the integration layer and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Payments Integration Engineering

  • Halts the gateway and suspends signing.
  • Reverts the mapping rule.
  • Plans independent message validation.
  • Plans change control.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Payments Technology Engineering

  • Reviews the integration account and logs.
  • Isolates the gateway.
  • Validates the HSM path.
  • Tracks changes.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners and payments operations compile affected payments and begin recall.
  • The service desk logs correspondent and customer contacts.
  • Owners define manual payment fallback.
  • Prepare validation checks.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Fraud & Security Operations

  • Searches for other altered messages.
  • Investigates how the account was compromised.
  • Preserves evidence.
  • Adds alerts for beneficiary field changes.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

AI Platform Vendor & Cloud Support Liaison

  • Opens cases with the gateway vendor and messaging network operator.
  • Asks about message audit support.
  • Requests guidance on signing controls.
  • Escalates through account teams.
  • Records Google Cloud guidance and case numbers.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for payments operations and informs leadership.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates workarounds and external contacts.
  • Tracks business impact.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 8.32 change management, 8.15 logging, 5.15 access control, 5.3 segregation of duties; SOC 2 security and processing integrity).
  • Assesses payment integrity and fraud response.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Validate every outgoing message against its source record before signing and make mapping changes require two-person approval.

Reconcile in near real time, not only at end of day.

Remove bypass paths around manual release thresholds.

Isolate and vault integration accounts and monitor them.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.32, 8.15, 5.15, 5.3) and SOC 2 expect change control, segregation and processing integrity evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on halt-first and recall speed.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How did you confirm tampering?
  2. What did you halt first?
  3. How did you recall payments?
  4. How did you assess signing?
  5. What did you report?
  6. What design do you want?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the AI Platform Vendor & Cloud Support Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
