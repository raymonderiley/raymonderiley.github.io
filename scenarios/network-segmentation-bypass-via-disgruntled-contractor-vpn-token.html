Network Segmentation Bypass via Disgruntled Contractor VPN Token

Ransomware & Cyber-Recovery Forensics

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a disgruntled former contractor using an unrevoked VPN token and knowledge of segmentation gaps to reach production and backup networks and stage a destructive attack.

Participants are working-level: network security engineers, infrastructure operations engineers, application owners, the service desk, security operations and forensics analysts, the forensics and vendor support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of offboarding, token revocation, segmentation validation, insider response and evidence handling.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Sandford Energy Services used a contractor for network design. The contract ended six weeks ago after a dispute. His VPN token and a jump host account were not removed because the contractor offboarding list was not shared with the network team. Segmentation rules contain a legacy exception used during a migration.

At 22:00 on Thursday, the token authenticates and the user reaches the jump host, then uses the legacy exception to reach the production database network and backup management network. He runs reconnaissance and changes firewall rules. By 23:30 a script has been staged on backup servers to delete repositories at 02:00.

What the team does not yet know: what rule changes were made, whether other tokens remain active, whether the script is the only payload, and what legal steps apply to a former contractor.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize insider token misuse. Participants link activity to a former contractor's identity.
  • Cut access. The team revokes tokens and removes the jump host account.
  • Stop the staged attack. Engineers remove scripts and protect backups.
  • Reverse changes. Participants review and restore firewall rules.
  • Engage legal. The team coordinates counsel and law enforcement as needed.
  • Improve offboarding and segmentation. Participants review contractor lifecycle and exceptions.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Revoke the token and jump host account and remove the staged script before reviewing anything else, and preserve all logs and artifacts for legal use.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Misused access and staged scripts lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Access revoked and scripts removed.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Scope and changes are diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A restoration and legal plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Fixes proceed in order.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Operations stabilize.

T+06:00 — Validation. Controls are validated.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes contractor access that ends when the contract ends and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Network Security Engineering

  • Revokes tokens and disables accounts.
  • Reviews firewall rule changes.
  • Plans exception removal.
  • Plans contractor offboarding.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Infrastructure Operations Engineering

  • Removes the staged script and checks backup servers.
  • Protects repositories.
  • Validates segmentation.
  • Tracks changes.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners and procurement list all contractors and their access.
  • The service desk logs questions.
  • Owners define manual procedures.
  • Prepare validation checks.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Reconstructs actions from logs.
  • Hunts for other tokens and implants.
  • Preserves evidence.
  • Adds alerts for access by terminated identities.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Forensics & Vendor Support Liaison

  • Opens cases with the VPN and firewall vendors and forensics firm.
  • Asks for token logs.
  • Requests guidance on segmentation tests.
  • Escalates through account teams.
  • Records Google Cloud guidance and case numbers.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for energy services IT operations and informs leadership.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates workarounds and external contacts.
  • Tracks business impact.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 6.5 responsibilities after termination, 5.18 access rights, 8.22 segregation of networks, 5.19 supplier relationships; SOC 2 security).
  • Assesses access revocation and segmentation.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Link contractor contracts to identity lifecycle so access ends automatically on the end date.

Use short-lived tokens and alert on activity by terminated identities.

Review and expire segmentation exceptions on a schedule.

Test segmentation from remote access paths regularly.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (6.5, 5.18, 8.22, 5.19) and SOC 2 expect access removal and segmentation evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on revoke-and-remove-first and legal coordination.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. What should have ended with the contract?
  2. What did you revoke first?
  3. What did the script do?
  4. How did you restore rules?
  5. When did you involve law enforcement?
  6. What design do you want?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the Forensics & Vendor Support Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
