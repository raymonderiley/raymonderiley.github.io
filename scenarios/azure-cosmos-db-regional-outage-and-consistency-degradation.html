Azure Cosmos DB Regional Outage and Consistency Degradation

Azure Cloud Resiliency

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a regional outage that affects the write region of a multi-region Cosmos DB account. Failover occurs, but applications that assumed strong consistency or single-region writes see stale reads, conflicts and unexpected errors, and request unit (RU) throttling rises in surviving regions.

Participants are working-level: data platform engineers, application engineers, application owners, the service desk, cloud operations and monitoring staff, the Microsoft support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of Cosmos DB consistency levels, failover priorities, multi-region writes, conflict resolution and RU capacity.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Quillfeather Travel runs booking and loyalty services on a Cosmos DB for NoSQL account with regions East US (write), West Europe and Southeast Asia (read), using Session consistency. Automatic failover is enabled with West Europe as the first failover priority. Several services read with the default consistency and cache locally.

At 13:15 on Friday, Azure reports a regional incident in East US. Cosmos DB fails over the write region to West Europe at 13:30. Replication lag at failover meant about 8 seconds of writes were not replicated. Some services use East US endpoints hard-coded in configuration and fail. Others reconnect but see stale data and conflicts. West Europe, now handling full write traffic, runs out of provisioned RUs and returns 429s.

What the team does not yet know: the last 8 seconds of bookings exist only in the unavailable region, the loyalty service uses a unique key that now conflicts, the SDK is not configured with preferred regions, and Southeast Asia read traffic has been rebalanced to West Europe, doubling load.

Exercise Objectives

  • Interpret failover and consistency behavior. Participants explain what automatic failover guarantees, what Session consistency means after region change and what data loss window exists.
  • Restore application connectivity. The team fixes endpoint configuration and SDK regional preferences.
  • Handle throttling. Engineers raise RUs or autoscale, shed load and protect booking paths.
  • Reconcile missing and conflicting data. Participants identify lost bookings and resolve conflicts with business owners.
  • Decide on failback. The team plans when and how to return to East US without a second disruption.
  • Improve resilient design. Participants review consistency choices, SDK configuration, capacity in secondary regions and testing.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Specify the consistency level and region when you describe read or write behavior.

Inject #1: Write region fails over and bookings show stale data

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 13:50. Customers see booking confirmations that later disappear, and the loyalty service shows old point balances. The booking API returns 429 from West Europe. The Azure portal shows West Europe as write region since 13:30.

The data engineer finds replication lag at failover of about 8 seconds. The application engineer discovers two services still use the East US endpoint explicitly and fail. RU consumption in West Europe is at 160 percent of provisioned.

Azure Service Health shows the East US incident. The SDK is not configured with preferred regions. A flash sale on flights begins in an hour.

Facilitator provides: account region configuration, RU graphs, error samples and the lag estimate.

  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Decide on load shedding for noncritical flows.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Data Platform Engineering (Cosmos DB)
  • Confirm failover state and data window.
  • Raise RUs or enable autoscale.
  • Plan reconciliation.
Application Engineering
  • Fix endpoints and preferred regions.
  • Add backoff for 429s.
  • Review consistency settings.
Application Owners & Service Desk
  • Rank flows.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Prepare manual booking.
Cloud Operations & Monitoring
  • Build the dashboard.
  • Confirm alerts.
  • List missing alarms.
Microsoft Support & TAM Liaison
  • Open a case.
  • Ask about failback.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform customer service.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Ask for RTO and RPO.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about consistency decisions.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. What does automatic failover guarantee and not guarantee about recent writes?

  8. What do customers need to hear?

Resilience Architect Guidance

Choose consistency deliberately. Know what each service assumes, and test those assumptions through a region failover, including data loss windows for the chosen replication settings.

Configure clients for regional change: preferred regions, no hard-coded endpoints, retry with backoff and idempotent operations.

Size secondary regions for failover load with autoscale or reserved RUs, and design partition keys to avoid hot partitions.

Plan conflict resolution for multi-region writes with business-owned rules and a reconciliation process.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.14) and SOC 2 expect tested regional failover, data integrity checks and capacity evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on consistency assumptions and client configuration.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

  2. Why did services see stale data?

  6. How were conflicts resolved?

  10. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  11. What consistency level does each service assume?

  13. What alarms are missing?
