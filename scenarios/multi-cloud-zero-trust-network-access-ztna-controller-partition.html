Multi-Cloud Zero Trust Network Access (ZTNA) Controller Partition

Multi-Cloud (AWS & Azure, No On-Prem)

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a ZTNA architecture with controllers in AWS and Azure that partitions after a certificate and routing issue, so policy decisions diverge, some users are denied everywhere and others are allowed with stale policy.

Participants are working-level: network security engineers, cloud platform engineers, application owners, the service desk, security operations and monitoring staff, the AWS and Microsoft support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of policy distribution, fail-open versus fail-closed, session caching, break-glass access and policy staleness.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Marigold Biotech uses a ZTNA platform with policy controllers in AWS and Azure and connectors in both clouds to reach internal applications and lab systems. Policies are replicated every minute. Devices cache decisions for 8 hours. The connectors authenticate to controllers with certificates.

At 07:00 on Monday, a certificate expiry and a route table change partition the two controllers. The Azure controller stops receiving policy updates and fail-closes for new sessions. The AWS controller continues with updated policies that revoked access for a terminated contractor. Cached sessions on devices continue to allow access in places. Researchers cannot reach lab data systems, and the revoked contractor retains access through a cached session.

What the team does not yet know: how long cached sessions last for each application, lab systems running experiments need uninterrupted access, break-glass accounts exist but their path depends on the same controllers, and a security event involving the contractor may be underway.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize policy divergence. Participants compare controller policy versions and session states.
  • Revoke risky access. The team removes access for the terminated contractor even through caches.
  • Restore legitimate access. Engineers restore connectivity for researchers and operations.
  • Heal the partition. Participants repair certificates and routing.
  • Maintain break-glass. The team uses emergency access with logging.
  • Improve ZTNA resilience. Participants review failure modes, caches and independent emergency paths.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Revoke the terminated contractor's access first, including sessions, before restoring anything for others.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Access denials and stale access lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Risky access is revoked and priority set.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Partition and cache behavior are diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A repair and restoration plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Repairs proceed in order.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Access stabilizes.

T+06:00 — Validation. Policies and sessions are validated.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

The security engineer finds a certificate expiry and route change partitioned the controllers. A terminated contractor retains access through a cached session.

  • Authorize revoking the contractor.

  • Revoke contractor.
  • Repair certs.
Cloud Platform Engineering (AWS & Azure)
  • Check connectors.
  • Restore lab access.
  • Validate.
Application Owners & Service Desk
  • Rank systems.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Define needs.
Security Operations & Monitoring
  • Review activity.
  • Watch access.
  • Preserve logs.
AWS & Microsoft Support Liaison
  • Open cases.
  • Ask about HA.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform leadership.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Plan lab notice.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about access duties.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. Which risk do you address first, the contractor or the lab outage?
  2. How do you revoke a cached session?
  3. How do you restore lab access safely?
  4. Do you let the Azure controller fail open?
  5. How do you use break-glass when it depends on the controllers?
  6. What did the contractor access?
  7. What do researchers hear?
  8. Why was session cache 8 hours?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the contractor's recent activity until OPS reviews logs.
  • If players fail open everywhere, ask about the revoked user.
  • Look for revocation first.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes secure access that fails safe without locking out the business and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Network Security Engineering (ZTNA)

  • Compares policy versions across controllers.
  • Revokes the contractor on both controllers and sessions.
  • Repairs certificates and routing.
  • Plans resync.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Cloud Platform Engineering (AWS & Azure)

  • Checks connector health and routes.
  • Restores lab access through verified paths.
  • Validates policies per app.
  • Tracks changes.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners rank lab and research systems.
  • The service desk logs access issues.
  • Owners define continuity needs.
  • Prepare validation checks.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Security Operations & Monitoring

  • Reviews the contractor's recent activity.
  • Watches for suspicious access.
  • Preserves logs.
  • Adds policy version mismatch alerts.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

AWS & Microsoft Support Liaison

  • Opens cases with AWS and Microsoft on connectivity.
  • Asks about controller HA design.
  • Requests guidance on certificate rotation.
  • Escalates through both TAMs and the ZTNA vendor.
  • Records Google Cloud guidance and case numbers.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for research operations and informs leadership.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates workarounds and external contacts.
  • Tracks business impact.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.28, 5.18 access rights, 8.2 privileged access, 8.20 network security; SOC 2 security).
  • Assesses access control integrity and research continuity.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Design revocation to take effect quickly through short caches and active session termination.

Track controller certificate expiry and alert on policy version mismatch.

Keep break-glass access independent of the ZTNA control plane.

Decide fail behavior per application based on risk.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (5.18, 8.2, 8.20) and SOC 2 expect access revocation and network control evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on revoking risky access before restoring access to others.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

  2. How did you revoke the contractor?
  3. How did you restore lab access?
  4. How did you handle fail-open requests?
  5. How did you use break-glass?
  6. What design do you want?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the AWS & Microsoft Support Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
