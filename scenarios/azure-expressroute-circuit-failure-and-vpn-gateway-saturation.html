Azure ExpressRoute Circuit Failure and VPN Gateway Saturation

Azure Cloud Resiliency

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines the loss of an Azure ExpressRoute circuit with traffic failing over to a Site-to-Site VPN gateway that cannot carry production volume. The VPN gateway saturates, latency and loss rise, database replication lags and applications built around the private circuit degrade.

Participants are working-level: cloud network engineers, platform and SRE engineers, application owners, the service desk, cloud operations and monitoring staff, the Microsoft and carrier liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of BGP failover, gateway SKU throughput, route preference and traffic prioritization.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Ashgrove Manufacturing connects its two plants and data center to Azure with a single 5 Gbps ExpressRoute circuit through one provider, backed by a VPN gateway (VpnGw2, about 1.25 Gbps aggregate in practice). Production systems in Azure include ERP, MES integration and a SQL replica fed by on-premises replication at about 2.5 Gbps.

At 04:10 on Thursday, a provider fiber cut takes the ExpressRoute circuit down. BGP over ExpressRoute drops and traffic fails over to the VPN gateway. Within minutes the VPN gateway is saturated, packet loss reaches 12 percent, MES integration times out and replication lag grows to 50 minutes. The plant floor systems begin to buffer locally.

What the team does not yet know: the second circuit planned last year was never ordered, the VPN gateway has a single instance configuration without active-active, no QoS prioritizes MES traffic, and the provider estimates 18 to 36 hours to repair.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize circuit loss and failover behavior. Participants read ExpressRoute and VPN metrics, BGP state and gateway throughput.
  • Prioritize traffic. The team ranks flows and pauses or throttles bulk replication and file transfers.
  • Increase safe capacity. Engineers consider gateway SKU upgrade, active-active, additional tunnels and ECMP.
  • Manage replication and plant operations. Participants decide on RPO acceptance, buffering and manual procedures.
  • Engage carrier and Microsoft effectively. The team coordinates repair estimates and options.
  • Improve connectivity design. Participants review dual circuits, diverse paths, gateway sizing and failover testing.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Quantify bandwidth in Gbps for each flow you keep or pause.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Link loss and slow applications lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Bulk flows are paused and critical flows protected.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Carrier ETA and VPN limits are confirmed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A capacity and priority plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Traffic changes and capacity increases are applied.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Latency and replication stabilize.

T+06:00 — Validation. Plant and data consistency are verified.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Circuit restoration and failback are planned.

Injects

Inject #1: ExpressRoute lost and VPN saturated

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 04:25. Monitoring shows the ExpressRoute BGP session down and the VPN gateway at 100 percent. Packet loss is 12 percent. MES integration times out and replication lag is 50 minutes.

The network engineer confirms the circuit loss through the provider portal: a fiber cut with an estimated 18 to 36 hours. The second circuit was never ordered. The VPN gateway is a single non-zone-redundant instance.

Top talkers: database replication at 2.5 Gbps demand, nightly file sync at 1.5 Gbps and MES API calls. The gateway carries about 1.25 Gbps. Azure Service Health shows no events.

Facilitator provides: BGP and gateway metrics, top talker breakdown, provider ticket and circuit inventory.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Decide which flows pause first.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Cloud Network Engineering
  • Confirm failover state and gateway limits.
  • Propose SKU upgrade and active-active.
  • Plan additional tunnels.
Cloud Platform & SRE Engineering
  • Throttle replication and file sync.
  • Identify top talkers.
  • Adjust timeouts.
Application Owners & Service Desk
  • Rank flows.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Prepare manual procedures.
Cloud Operations & Monitoring
  • Build the dashboard.
  • Confirm alerts.
  • List missing alarms.
Microsoft Support & Carrier Liaison
  • Contact provider.
  • Open a case.
  • Ask about SKU upgrade.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform plant managers.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Ask for RTO.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about the unordered circuit.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. How do you prioritize flows when the VPN carries about 1.25 Gbps?
  2. What do you do with the 2.5 Gbps replication demand?
  3. What is the RPO risk of pausing replication?
  4. How can you add capacity quickly and what are the risks of a SKU change?
  5. Why does a single gateway instance matter?
  6. What do plants need to be told?
  7. What does the provider ETA mean for the plan?
  8. What did the missing second circuit cost?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the single instance until T1 reviews the gateway.
  • If players keep everything, ask them to add up the bandwidth.
  • Look for numeric reasoning.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

  • Logs findings on single points of failure.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Remove single points of failure: two ExpressRoute circuits with diverse providers and peering locations, and tested failover under load.

Size the backup path for what must run during failure: zone-redundant active-active VPN gateway at an adequate SKU, ECMP and QoS ready.

Prioritize and shape traffic with automated bulk pause on failover and replication designed to tolerate reduced bandwidth with explicit RPO.

Track circuit orders in a risk register so an unordered backup is visible to leadership.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (5.30, 8.14) and SOC 2 expect documented redundancy, tested failover and risk acceptance.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on traffic prioritization and the backup path gap.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did you confirm the circuit loss and cause?
  2. How did you rank flows by bandwidth and criticality?
  3. What did you decide about replication and RPO?
  4. How did you weigh SKU upgrade downtime?
  5. What did the single gateway instance mean?
  6. How did you protect plant data?
  7. What did carrier ETA changes do to the plan?
  8. How was failback planned?
  9. How accurate were updates to plants and customers?
  10. What did Microsoft support add?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  12. Why was the second circuit not ordered?
  13. What access or permissions were missing?
  14. How often is failover tested?
  15. What would you change in connectivity design?
