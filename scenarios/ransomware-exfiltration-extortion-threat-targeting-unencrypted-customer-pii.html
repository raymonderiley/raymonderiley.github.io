Ransomware Exfiltration Extortion Threat Targeting Unencrypted Customer PII

Ransomware & Cyber-Recovery Forensics

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a ransomware group threatening to publish stolen unencrypted customer personal data unless paid, with evidence that the data was staged and exfiltrated weeks earlier.

Participants are working-level: data protection engineers, infrastructure operations engineers, application owners, the service desk, security operations and forensics analysts, the forensics and vendor support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of data discovery, exfiltration forensics, notification timelines, monitoring and customer protection.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Meadowlark Health Plans holds member records in databases and file shares, some unencrypted and some copied into analytic stores. Encryption at rest covers production databases but not exports. Egress monitoring flags only large single transfers.

At 10:00 on Wednesday, a group posts a sample of 5,000 member records with names, addresses, birth dates and plan identifiers and demands payment within five days or it will publish 2.4 million records. Forensics finds archive files staged on a file server and exfiltrated in 200 megabyte chunks over three weeks.

What the team does not yet know: which data sets are in the archives, whether the sample is genuine and complete, whether encryption also occurred, and what notification clocks have started.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize extortion by data. Participants confirm exfiltration and its scope.
  • Stop further loss. The team blocks egress and secures staging servers.
  • Identify the data. Engineers determine exactly which records and fields left.
  • Meet notification duties. Participants coordinate counsel, regulators and members.
  • Protect members. The team arranges monitoring and support.
  • Improve data protection. Participants review discovery, encryption and egress monitoring.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Block egress and preserve staging evidence first, then determine exactly what data left before making any public statement.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. A leak sample and demand lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Egress blocked and evidence preserved.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Data scope is diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A notification and member plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Notifications and support proceed.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Pressure stabilizes.

T+06:00 — Validation. Facts are validated.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes knowing exactly what data left and acting fast on it and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Data Protection Engineering

  • Blocks egress destinations and secures staging servers.
  • Plans data discovery on the archives.
  • Plans encryption for exports.
  • Plans egress monitoring.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Infrastructure Operations Engineering

  • Preserves server images and archives.
  • Reviews transfers.
  • Validates controls.
  • Tracks changes.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners and privacy staff define data sets and member impact.
  • The service desk prepares scripts and logs contacts.
  • Owners define call center capacity.
  • Prepare validation checks.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Reconstructs transfers and archive content.
  • Hunts for other staging.
  • Preserves evidence.
  • Adds low-and-slow egress detection.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Forensics & Vendor Support Liaison

  • Opens cases with the forensics firm and monitoring vendor.
  • Asks for archive analysis.
  • Requests credit monitoring and call center support.
  • Escalates through account teams.
  • Records Google Cloud guidance and case numbers.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for health plan operations and informs leadership.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates workarounds and external contacts.
  • Tracks business impact.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.34 privacy and PII, 8.11 data masking, 8.12 data leakage prevention, 8.24 cryptography; SOC 2 confidentiality and privacy).
  • Assesses member data protection and notification.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Maintain a data inventory including exports and analytics copies and encrypt all of it.

Detect low-and-slow exfiltration with cumulative egress analytics.

Minimize and expire staged data copies.

Pre-arrange notification, monitoring and call center support.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (5.34, 8.11, 8.12, 8.24) and SOC 2 expect confidentiality and privacy evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on block-and-preserve-first and exact scope.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How did you confirm exfiltration?
  2. What did you block first?
  3. How did you determine scope?
  4. What did you tell regulators?
  5. How did you protect members?
  6. What design do you want?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the Forensics & Vendor Support Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
