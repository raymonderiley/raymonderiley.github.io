On-Premises Backup Copy Job Failure to Azure Blob Immutable Tier

Hybrid Cloud (Azure Focus)

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a backup copy job that stops sending on-premises backups to an Azure Blob immutable tier after a credential expiry and a bandwidth limit change, so the offsite immutable copy falls weeks behind while the on-premises repository is hit by ransomware precursors.

Participants are working-level: backup and recovery engineers, storage and infrastructure engineers, application owners, the service desk, cloud operations and monitoring staff, the Microsoft support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of copy jobs, immutability, retention locks, SAS and identity expiry, restore tests and recovery points.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Larkfield Pharma keeps backups on an on-premises repository and copies them nightly to an Azure Blob container with a locked time-based immutability policy. The copy job uses a SAS token that was set to expire in 30 days. Weekly health reports show job status but not recovery point age offsite.

At 08:00 on Thursday, security finds suspicious admin activity on the backup server, including attempts to access repository credentials. A check shows the last successful offsite copy was 24 days ago. The SAS token expired and a firewall bandwidth cap added last month throttled the job before that. Immutable copies exist but are old. On-premises backups are now at risk.

What the team does not yet know: the on-premises repository may be compromised, the job's failure alerts went to a retired mailbox, restoring from 24-day-old immutable copies loses substantial data, and new immutable copies need a large transfer over a limited link.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize the offsite gap. Participants verify the age of offsite recovery points directly.
  • Protect the repository. The team isolates the backup server and preserves evidence.
  • Create fresh offsite protection. Engineers prioritize which systems to copy first within bandwidth limits.
  • Plan recovery with old points. Participants consider restoring from 24-day-old points and rebuilding data.
  • Fix the pipeline. The team repairs credentials, bandwidth rules and alerts.
  • Improve backup assurance. Participants review outcome metrics, credential lifecycle and restore tests.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Do not claim any system is protected offsite without a verified recent immutable recovery point.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Backup server alerts and an old offsite copy lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. The repository is isolated and copy priorities set.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Exposure and gaps are diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A protection and recovery plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. New copies are made in priority order.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Offsite coverage is rebuilt.

T+06:00 — Validation. Backups are verified by test restores.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Inject #1: Offsite copies are 24 days old as the backup server shows signs of attack

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 08:30. Security flags suspicious admin activity on the backup server. The offsite immutable copy is 24 days old.

The backup engineer finds an expired SAS token and a bandwidth cap added last month. Failure alerts went to a retired mailbox.

The on-premises repository may be compromised. A full new copy needs days over the link.

Facilitator provides: job history, recovery point inventory, firewall change record and security alerts.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Authorize isolating the backup server.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Backup & Recovery Engineering
  • Inventory offsite points.
  • Renew credentials.
  • Plan priority copy.
Storage & Infrastructure Engineering
  • Isolate server.
  • Validate repository.
  • Plan test restores.
Application Owners & Service Desk
  • Rank systems.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Define acceptable loss.
Cloud Operations & Monitoring
  • Investigate activity.
  • Watch precursors.
  • Preserve logs.
Microsoft, Carrier & Vendor Support Liaison
  • Open a case.
  • Ask about offline import.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform leadership.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Plan regulator readiness.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about data protection duties.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. How do you verify the offsite recovery point age?
  2. What do you protect first?
  3. How do you copy within the bandwidth limit?
  4. What do you do if the repository is compromised?
  5. What does restoring from 24-day-old copies mean?
  6. Why did failure alerts not reach anyone?
  7. What do leaders hear?
  8. Is an offline transfer an option?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the bandwidth cap until T1 reviews network changes.
  • If players delete old immutable copies, stop and discuss.
  • Look for verification first.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes protecting recoverability of critical systems and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Backup & Recovery Engineering

  • Inventories offsite recovery points by system.
  • Renews credentials and removes the bandwidth cap for the job.
  • Plans priority copy order.
  • Plans immutability checks.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Storage & Infrastructure Engineering

  • Isolates the backup server and preserves evidence.
  • Validates repository integrity.
  • Plans test restores.
  • Rebuilds trust in backup credentials.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners rank systems by recovery priority.
  • The service desk logs impacts.
  • Owners define acceptable data loss.
  • Prepare validation checks.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Cloud Operations & Monitoring

  • Investigates the suspicious admin activity.
  • Watches for ransomware precursors.

  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Microsoft, Carrier & Vendor Support Liaison

  • Requests guidance on SAS and identity best practice.
  • Escalates through the TAM.
  • Records Google Cloud guidance and case numbers.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for manufacturing and quality systems and informs leadership.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates workarounds and external contacts.
  • Tracks business impact.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.28, 8.13 information backup, 8.2 privileged access; SOC 2 availability and security).
  • Assesses backup coverage and data protection.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Measure offsite recovery point age per system and alert on it to a monitored on-call.

Use managed identities or tracked credentials with expiry alerts and reserve bandwidth for backup traffic.

Test restores from the immutable tier regularly.

Isolate and harden backup infrastructure.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.13, 8.2) and SOC 2 expect backup coverage, access control and restore test evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on outcome-based backup metrics and credential lifecycle.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How did you verify offsite recovery points?
  2. What did you protect first?
  3. How did you prioritize copies?
  4. How did you respond to the suspected compromise?
  5. What did the test restore show?
  6. How did you prove coverage restored?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the Microsoft, Carrier & Vendor Support Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
