GCP Backup and DR Service Policy Enforcement Failure

Google Cloud Resiliency

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a Backup and DR Service policy enforcement failure in which backup plans silently stop applying to newly created and modified resources, leaving critical systems unprotected for weeks until a data corruption event proves there is nothing to restore from.

Participants are working-level: backup and recovery engineers, application and database engineers, application owners, the service desk, cloud operations and monitoring staff, the Google Cloud support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of backup plans, vaults, resource selection by labels, policy drift, restore testing and recovery point reporting.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Ridgeway Bank protects Compute Engine VMs and databases with Backup and DR Service backup plans applied by resource labels. A migration project last month moved services into new projects and renamed labels. A dashboard reports 'plans configured' but not actual recovery points per system. Restore tests run annually.

At 11:00 on Monday, a failed batch job corrupts the loan servicing database. The team goes to restore and finds that the most recent recovery point is 19 days old. The plan applies by label and the new label scheme no longer matches. Seven other systems have no recent recovery points. The old recovery points exist but loan transactions from 19 days are missing.

What the team does not yet know: transaction logs and source system exports may allow rebuilding some data, other critical systems may also be unprotected, an alert for missed backups was never configured, and the regulator expects documented recovery capability.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize the backup gap. Participants verify recovery points for each critical system instead of trusting the dashboard.
  • Recover the most data possible. The team combines the old recovery point with logs and upstream sources.
  • Close the gap immediately. Engineers apply correct plans to unprotected systems and take fresh backups.
  • Be honest about loss. Participants quantify data loss and communicate carefully.
  • Assess regulatory exposure. The team prepares for supervisory conversations.
  • Improve backup assurance. Participants review recovery point alerting, label governance and regular restore tests.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Do not claim a system is protected unless a recent, restorable recovery point has been verified.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Corruption and a stale recovery point lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Fresh backups are taken and the corrupted database is isolated.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Recovery options and gaps are diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A reconstruction and communication plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Data is rebuilt and unprotected systems are protected.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Systems stabilize.

T+06:00 — Validation. Data and backup coverage are validated.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Inject #1: Corrupted database and a nineteen-day-old backup

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 11:45. The loan servicing database is corrupted by a failed batch job. The latest recovery point is 19 days old.

The backup engineer finds the plan applies by label and the new label scheme no longer matches. Seven other systems also lack recent points.

Transaction logs and upstream exports may rebuild some data. No missed-backup alert existed.

Facilitator provides: recovery point inventory, plan selection rules, label change record and the system criticality list.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Prioritize verification of all critical systems.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Backup & Recovery Engineering (Backup and DR Service)
  • Inventory recovery points.
  • Apply plans and back up now.
  • Plan isolated restore.
Application & Database Engineering
  • Isolate and preserve the database.
  • Reconstruct from logs.
  • Validate.
Application Owners & Service Desk
  • Rank systems.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Coordinate branches.
Cloud Operations & Monitoring
  • Build the dashboard.
  • Watch jobs.
  • Preserve logs.
Google Cloud Support & TAM Liaison
  • Open a case.
  • Ask about plan logs.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform leadership.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Plan regulator contact.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about regulatory duties.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. How do you verify recovery points rather than trust the dashboard?
  2. What do you do first about the other unprotected systems?
  3. How do you rebuild 19 days of transactions?
  4. How do you preserve the corrupted database?
  5. What data loss can you accept and who accepts it?
  6. How should the bank talk to the regulator?
  7. What do customers hear?
  8. Why did the dashboard hide the gap?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the other seven systems until T1 inventories recovery points.
  • If players restore over the corrupted database, ask about evidence.
  • Look for verification first.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes verified recoverability of critical systems and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Backup & Recovery Engineering (Backup and DR Service)

  • Inventories recovery points per critical system.
  • Applies correct plans and takes immediate backups.
  • Plans restore of the old recovery point to an isolated environment.
  • Plans label governance.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Application & Database Engineering

  • Isolates the corrupted database and preserves it.
  • Reconstructs missing transactions from logs and sources.
  • Validates data and reconciles.
  • Tests restore.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners rank systems by criticality and accept or reject loss.
  • The service desk logs customer impacts.
  • Owners coordinate reconciliation with branches.
  • Prepare validation checks.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Cloud Operations & Monitoring

  • Builds a dashboard of recovery point age by system.
  • Watches backup job status.
  • Preserves logs.
  • Adds missed backup alerts.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Google Cloud Support & TAM Liaison

  • Opens a Sev A case on plan application and restore.
  • Asks about policy enforcement logs.
  • Requests guidance on log-based recovery.
  • Escalates through the TAM.
  • Records Google Cloud guidance and case numbers.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for loan servicing and payments and informs leadership.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates workarounds and external contacts.
  • Tracks business impact.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.30, 8.13 information backup; SOC 2 availability and processing integrity).
  • Assesses data loss and recovery capability.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Measure outcomes, not configuration: alert on recovery point age per system and on resources with no plan.

Govern selection labels and detect drift during migrations.

Test restores regularly, with a rotating schedule that covers every critical system.

Keep logs and upstream exports as secondary recovery paths.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.13, 5.30) and SOC 2 expect backup coverage, restore test and recovery evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on outcome-based backup assurance and restore testing.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How did you verify recovery points?
  2. What did you do for unprotected systems?
  3. How did you rebuild lost transactions?
  4. How did you preserve evidence?
  5. How did you decide on the regulator?
  6. How did you prove coverage restored?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the Google Cloud Support & TAM Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
