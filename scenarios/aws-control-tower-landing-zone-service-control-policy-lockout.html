AWS Control Tower Landing Zone Service Control Policy Lockout

AWS Cloud Resiliency

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a Control Tower landing zone update that applies a new set of preventive guardrails (service control policies) which block actions required by the Control Tower account factory, drift remediation and the pipelines that deploy workloads. Accounts cannot be provisioned or repaired, and the landing zone enters a drifted or failed state.

Participants are working-level: cloud governance and landing zone engineers, cloud identity and security engineers, application and pipeline owners, cloud operations and monitoring staff, the AWS support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of Control Tower, SCP inheritance, managed controls and recovery paths.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Calder Pharma uses AWS Control Tower to govern 52 accounts across regulated and non-regulated organizational units. The platform team enables a batch of new controls recommended by a compliance review, including region deny, deny public S3 and mandatory encryption, and adds a custom SCP to the 'Workloads' OU to deny all actions in regions other than two approved regions.

At 15:00 on Thursday, the landing zone update runs. The custom region-deny SCP does not exempt global services and Control Tower's own roles such as AWSControlTowerAdmin and StackSet execution roles. Within minutes, account factory fails, drift detection reports errors, StackSet updates fail with AccessDenied, and CloudFormation pipelines in 18 accounts fail. Control Tower shows 'Landing zone: drifted' and the dashboard shows enrollment failing.

What the team does not yet know: the same SCP blocks IAM and Route 53 global actions that are routed through us-east-1, a new regulated account needed for a clinical trial system by tomorrow cannot be created, repairing the landing zone requires the very roles that are blocked, and the compliance change was approved without a test OU.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize SCP-driven failure in Control Tower. Participants interpret AccessDenied messages, CloudTrail events and the Control Tower dashboard to see that an SCP is blocking managed roles.
  • Restore governance operations. The team detaches or corrects the SCP, then repairs the landing zone and checks drift.
  • Avoid a worse state. Engineers sequence fixes to avoid leaving accounts without any guardrails or in a broken enrollment state.
  • Protect deadlines. Participants decide how to provision the clinical trial account and which pipelines to prioritize.
  • Preserve compliance posture. The team assesses whether controls were bypassed during the window and records exceptions.
  • Improve governance practice. Participants review testing in a sandbox OU, exemptions for service roles and staged rollouts.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Be explicit about which OU or account an SCP is attached to when you discuss changes.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Failed pipelines and landing zone drift lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. SCP changes are frozen and the blocking policy is identified.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. The affected roles and OUs are confirmed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A fix plan and repair sequence is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. SCP is corrected and landing zone repaired.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Drift and enrollment return to healthy.

T+06:00 — Validation. Pipelines and account provisioning are verified.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operations resume and the incident is closed for debrief.

Injects

Inject #1: Landing zone drift and failing account factory

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 15:40. Engineers report CloudFormation and StackSet failures with AccessDenied citing 'explicit deny in a service control policy'. The Control Tower dashboard shows the landing zone as drifted and Account Factory fails to enroll a new account for the clinical trial system required tomorrow.

The governance engineer inspects CloudTrail and sees AWSControlTowerAdmin and StackSet execution role calls denied in regions outside the two approved regions, and certain global actions routed via us-east-1. A custom region-deny SCP was attached to the Workloads OU at 14:58.

The change was approved by two reviewers who did not test it. The AWS Health Dashboard shows no events. Compliance asks whether the new controls are in effect.

Facilitator provides: the SCP JSON, the CloudTrail AccessDenied extract, the Control Tower status and the failed StackSet list.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Freeze SCP and landing zone changes.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Cloud Governance & Landing Zone Engineering
  • Identify the blocking SCP and OU.
  • Plan the correction and landing zone repair.
  • Prepare exemptions for Control Tower roles.
Cloud Identity & Security Engineering
  • Analyze the policy and global service actions.
  • Test the fix in a sandbox OU.
  • Plan staged reattachment.
Application & Pipeline Owners
  • List failed deployments.
  • Confirm the clinical account deadline.
  • Hold releases.
Cloud Operations & Monitoring
  • Build the dashboard.
  • Confirm logs.
  • List missing alarms.
AWS Support & TAM Liaison
  • Open a case.
  • Ask about repair paths.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform trial leaders.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Ask for deadlines.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask how the change was tested.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. How do you recognize an SCP block in Control Tower from the error and logs?
  2. Which roles and actions did the SCP block that it should not have?
  3. What is the safest way to fix it: detach, edit or add exemptions?
  4. What order restores governance without leaving accounts unprotected?
  5. How do you meet the clinical account deadline?
  6. Were controls bypassed during the window?
  7. Why did the change skip a test OU?
  8. What do trial leaders and compliance need to hear?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the global service impact until IAM analyzes the SCP.
  • If players detach the SCP everywhere, ask what protections vanish.
  • Look for a sandbox OU test.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes restoring governance operations and the clinical trial account and sets a change freeze.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes SCP detachment or edits.
  • Decides on temporary manual account creation.

Cloud Governance & Landing Zone Engineering

  • Reads the Control Tower dashboard, drift reports and CloudTrail for AccessDenied events citing SCPs.
  • Identifies which SCP and OU are responsible.
  • Plans correction and landing zone repair or re-register OUs.
  • Prepares exemptions for Control Tower roles.

Cloud Identity & Security Engineering

  • Analyzes policy documents and conditions and the effect on service-linked and global actions.
  • Tests the fix in a sandbox OU.
  • Plans staged reattachment to OUs.
  • Reviews whether controls were bypassed.

Application & Pipeline Owners

  • Pipeline owners list failed deployments and hold releases.
  • Application owners confirm which workloads cannot change.
  • Owners identify the clinical trial account deadline and impact.
  • Prepare validation tests.

Cloud Operations & Monitoring

  • Builds a dashboard of failed StackSet operations, drift status and AccessDenied counts.
  • Confirms CloudTrail and Config.
  • Checks AWS Health.
  • Adds alarms.

AWS Support & TAM Liaison

  • Opens a case on landing zone repair and enrollment failures.
  • Asks about supported recovery paths.
  • Requests review of the SCP design.
  • Records guidance.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for clinical operations and informs trial leaders.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates alternative provisioning.
  • Tracks deadlines.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.28, 8.2 privileged access, 8.32 change management; SOC 2 security and availability).
  • Assesses regulatory implications for the clinical system.
  • Captures evidence of approvals.
  • Logs findings on testing and exemptions.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Test guardrails before production. Use a sandbox OU with representative accounts and a policy simulator, and roll out OU by OU with staged waves and automated checks.

Exempt the governance plane by design: Control Tower roles, StackSet execution roles and break-glass roles must be excluded from deny guardrails through reviewed conditions.

Handle global services properly in region deny policies using NotAction lists and condition keys, and test with real pipelines.

Keep a documented recovery path for landing zone failures, including how to repair, who can act and how to provision critical accounts manually.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.2, 8.32) and SOC 2 expect tested change management, evidence of approvals and controls during exceptions.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on guardrail testing and exemptions for governance roles.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did you link failures to the SCP?
  2. Which roles and actions did the SCP block?
  3. How did you decide between detach and edit?
  4. What order did you use for repair?
  5. How did you handle the clinical account?
  6. Were controls bypassed during the window?
  7. How did you handle global services?
  8. How was drift cleared?
  9. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  10. What did AWS support add?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  12. Why was there no test OU?
  13. What access or permissions were missing?
  14. Who owns exemptions for governance roles?
  15. What would you change in guardrail rollout?
