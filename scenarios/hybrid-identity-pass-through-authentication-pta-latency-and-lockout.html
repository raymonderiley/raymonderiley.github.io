Hybrid Identity Pass-Through Authentication (PTA) Latency and Lockout

Hybrid Cloud (Azure Focus)

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines pass-through authentication agents that become slow after a domain controller issue and a network change, so cloud sign-ins time out, users retry, accounts lock out in Active Directory and a password spray hides in the noise.

Participants are working-level: identity engineers, directory services engineers, application owners, the service desk, security operations and monitoring staff, the Microsoft support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of PTA agent behavior, domain controller selection, smart lockout, password hash sync fallback and break-glass sign-in.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Thornfield Logistics uses pass-through authentication with three agents on-premises for 12,000 users. Password hash sync is not enabled. Smart lockout is set to default and AD lockout threshold is 5 attempts. Two domain controllers in the main site handle most authentication.

At 07:30 on Monday, a domain controller starts responding slowly after a patch and a network change adds latency to the site hosting the agents. PTA agents time out, cloud sign-ins to Microsoft 365 and line-of-business apps fail, and users retry repeatedly. AD lockouts spike to 900 accounts in 40 minutes. The service desk is overwhelmed. Security notices many failed attempts from external addresses.

What the team does not yet know: enabling password hash sync as a fallback needs a full sync and changes risk, unlocking all accounts at once would reopen the attack path, some failures are a real password spray, and break-glass cloud accounts may be locked by the same conditional access policy.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize PTA latency. Participants read agent logs and domain controller health to find the delay.
  • Restore sign-in. The team relieves the slow domain controller and agents and considers fallback options.
  • Manage lockouts. Engineers unlock affected users in a controlled way and avoid reopening abuse.
  • Detect a spray. Participants separate legitimate retries from attacks.
  • Support the service desk. The team prepares scripts and priorities for staff.
  • Improve identity resilience. Participants review agent count and placement, fallback and lockout design.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Unlock accounts in batches by priority and verify that the source is legitimate before unlocking.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Failed sign-ins and lockouts lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Agents and domain controllers are relieved and the priority set.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Latency and attack signals are diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A fallback and unlock plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Fixes and unlocks are applied in order.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Sign-in stabilizes.

T+06:00 — Validation. Security posture is validated.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Inject #1: Sign-in times out and accounts lock

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 08:10. Cloud sign-ins fail and 900 accounts are locked in AD. The service desk queue exceeds 400 calls.

The identity engineer finds PTA agent timeouts and a slow domain controller after a patch and network change.

Security sees many failed attempts from external addresses. No hash sync fallback exists.

Facilitator provides: agent logs, lockout counts, sign-in analytics and the change record.

Incident Commander

  • Authorize fallback assessment.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.

  • Plan fallback.
  • Add agents.
Directory Services Engineering
  • Investigate DC.
  • Roll back change.
  • Prepare unlock.
Application Owners & Service Desk
  • Rank users.
  • Service desk: follow scripts.
  • Plan alternatives.
Security Operations & Monitoring
  • Separate spray.
  • Block sources.
  • Preserve logs.
Microsoft, Carrier & Vendor Support Liaison
  • Open a case.
  • Ask about timeouts.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform leadership.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Plan staff notice.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about access control duties.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. How do you separate legitimate retries from a spray?
  2. What do you fix first: the DC, the agents or the network?
  3. Should you enable hash sync and what are the risks?
  4. How do you unlock accounts safely?
  5. Who must sign in first?
  6. How does the service desk cope?
  7. What do staff hear?
  8. Why were there only three agents in one site?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the spray until OPS analyzes sign-in logs.
  • If players mass unlock, ask about the attack.
  • Look for prioritization.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes sign-in for critical staff while preventing account lockout and abuse and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Identity Engineering (Pass-Through Authentication)

  • Reviews PTA agent health and sign-in logs.
  • Plans fallback such as hash sync with risk review.
  • Adds agents in a healthy site.
  • Plans smart lockout tuning.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Directory Services Engineering

  • Investigates the slow domain controller and network change.
  • Rolls back the change or shifts authentication.
  • Prepares unlock scripts.
  • Validates authentication.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners rank who must sign in first.
  • The service desk follows scripts and logs contacts.
  • Owners prepare alternatives.
  • Prepare validation checks.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Security Operations & Monitoring

  • Separates spray traffic from retries using IP and pattern analysis.
  • Blocks abusive sources.
  • Preserves logs.
  • Adds alerts for lockout spikes.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Microsoft, Carrier & Vendor Support Liaison

  • Opens a case on PTA agent behavior and fallback.
  • Asks about timeouts and recommended agent counts.
  • Requests guidance on smart lockout.
  • Escalates through the TAM.
  • Records Google Cloud guidance and case numbers.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for workforce operations and informs leadership.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates workarounds and external contacts.
  • Tracks business impact.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.28, 8.5 secure authentication, 8.32 change management; SOC 2 security and availability).
  • Assesses workforce access and account security.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Spread agents across sites and monitor their latency, and prepare a tested fallback such as hash sync under agreed conditions.

Tune smart lockout and AD lockout so a spray or retry storm cannot lock the workforce.

Test break-glass accounts and keep them out of risky policies.

Equip the service desk with scripts and priorities.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.5, 8.2, 8.32) and SOC 2 expect authentication, privileged access and change evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on fallback authentication and lockout design.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How did you find the latency?
  2. What did you fix first?
  3. How did you decide on hash sync?
  4. How did you unlock accounts?
  5. How did you detect the spray?
  6. How did you restore admin access?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the Microsoft, Carrier & Vendor Support Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
