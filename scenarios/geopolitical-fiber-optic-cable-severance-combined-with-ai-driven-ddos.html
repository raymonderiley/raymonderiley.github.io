Geopolitical Fiber-Optic Cable Severance Combined with AI-Driven DDoS

AI Failure & Autonomous Agent Disruption

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a severed regional fiber route during geopolitical tension combined with an AI-driven DDoS attack that adapts to mitigation and targets the remaining links.

Participants are working-level: network engineers, infrastructure operations engineers, application owners, the service desk, security operations analysts, the AI platform and cloud support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of path diversity, traffic prioritization, DDoS mitigation, carrier coordination and degraded operations.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Tidewater Clearing Services connects its data center to financial networks through two carriers. Both carriers share a submarine and terrestrial route segment in the region. A DDoS scrubbing service is available through a provider with a capacity limit. Failover between carriers is automated but untested at full load.

At 06:00 on Tuesday, a fiber cut takes down Carrier A and reduces Carrier B's capacity by 60 percent through shared route damage. At 06:20 a DDoS attack begins with traffic that changes protocols and sources every few minutes as mitigation rules are applied. Settlement messaging to financial networks begins to time out. By 07:00, 35 percent of settlement traffic fails.

What the team does not yet know: the repair time for the cut, whether the attack is related to the cut, how much scrubbing capacity remains, and which partners can accept alternative connectivity.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize combined failure. Participants separate capacity loss from attack traffic.
  • Protect critical traffic. The team prioritizes settlement flows.
  • Mitigate DDoS. Engineers adapt scrubbing and rate limits.
  • Use alternate paths. Participants activate backup connectivity such as satellite, cloud or partner links.
  • Coordinate with partners. The team informs counterparties and agrees manual fallbacks.
  • Improve network resilience. Participants review route diversity and DDoS design.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Prioritize and protect settlement traffic before trying to defeat the attack in full, and record all mitigation changes with times.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Link loss and attack traffic lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Critical traffic is prioritized and mitigation applied.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Route and attack patterns are diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A connectivity and partner plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Alternate paths and mitigations proceed.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Traffic stabilizes.

T+06:00 — Validation. Flows are validated.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes connectivity and protection when capacity is scarce and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Network Engineering

  • Prioritizes settlement traffic classes.
  • Adjusts scrubbing and rate limits.
  • Plans alternate connectivity.
  • Plans route diversity.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Infrastructure Operations Engineering

  • Activates backup circuits and cloud paths.
  • Validates failover.
  • Handles capacity.
  • Tracks changes.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners and operations define critical flows and message rates.
  • The service desk logs partner contacts.
  • Owners define manual fallbacks.
  • Prepare validation checks.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Security Operations & Monitoring

  • Analyzes attack patterns and adaptation.
  • Distinguishes legitimate spikes.
  • Preserves logs.
  • Adds alerts for adaptive traffic.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

AI Platform Vendor & Cloud Support Liaison

  • Opens cases with both carriers and the scrubbing provider.
  • Asks for repair estimates and route diversity.
  • Requests surge capacity.
  • Escalates through account teams.
  • Records Google Cloud guidance and case numbers.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for clearing operations and informs leadership.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates workarounds and external contacts.
  • Tracks business impact.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 8.20 network security, 8.14 redundancy, 5.30 ICT readiness, 8.6 capacity management; SOC 2 availability).
  • Assesses network resilience and settlement continuity.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Verify physical route diversity with carriers in writing and test failover at realistic load.

Define traffic priority classes for critical flows and reserve capacity.

Contract surge DDoS capacity and rehearse adaptive attack handling.

Maintain tested alternate connectivity and partner manual fallbacks.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.20, 8.14, 5.30, 8.6) and SOC 2 expect network and availability evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on traffic prioritization and verified diversity.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How did you separate the problems?
  2. What did you protect first?
  3. How did you handle adaptive traffic?
  4. What alternates did you use?
  5. What did you tell partners?
  6. What design do you want?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the AI Platform Vendor & Cloud Support Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
