Azure ExpressRoute BGP Route Flapping Over Primary Carrier

Hybrid Cloud (Azure Focus)

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines BGP sessions on the primary ExpressRoute circuit that repeatedly drop and re-establish after a carrier maintenance event, so routes flap, traffic shifts between the circuit and a backup VPN, sessions reset and applications see intermittent failures instead of a clean outage.

Participants are working-level: network engineers, cloud platform engineers, application owners, the service desk, network operations and monitoring staff, the Microsoft and carrier support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of BGP timers, route dampening, BFD, failover design, asymmetric routing and carrier escalation.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Brindle Retail runs production in Azure connected to two data centers through a primary ExpressRoute circuit and a backup site-to-site VPN. BGP uses default timers and BFD is not enabled. Route preference favors ExpressRoute with local preference.

At 11:20 on Tuesday, the carrier performs unannounced maintenance on the primary circuit. BGP sessions drop every 4 to 6 minutes and re-establish. Each flap shifts routes to the VPN, then back, causing session resets, asymmetric routing through a stateful firewall and intermittent application errors. Point-of-sale authorization and inventory sync time out repeatedly.

What the team does not yet know: the carrier says its status is green, the VPN has only a fraction of the bandwidth, dampening on one router is making recovery slower, and the firewall drops flows that return on a different path.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize flapping versus outage. Participants read BGP logs and route tables to see the repeating pattern.
  • Stabilize routing. The team chooses between holding traffic on the VPN and tolerating flaps.
  • Prevent asymmetry damage. Engineers address stateful firewall drops from path changes.
  • Escalate to the carrier. Participants build evidence and press for a fix.
  • Protect critical transactions. The team prioritizes point-of-sale and inventory traffic.
  • Improve connectivity design. Participants review BFD, dampening, dual circuits and failover testing.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Make routing changes one at a time and record each, so cause and effect stay clear.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Flapping and application errors lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Routing is stabilized and carrier engaged.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Asymmetry and capacity are diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A stability and priority plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Fixes are applied in order.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Connectivity stabilizes.

T+06:00 — Validation. Behavior is validated across a full interval.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Inject #1: BGP flaps every few minutes after carrier maintenance

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 11:50. Point-of-sale authorization and inventory sync fail intermittently. BGP on the primary circuit drops and re-establishes every 4 to 6 minutes.

The network engineer finds routes shifting between ExpressRoute and VPN. The stateful firewall drops flows that return on a different path.

The carrier reports status green. The VPN carries only part of the load.

Facilitator provides: BGP logs, route change history, firewall drops and application error rates.

  • Authorize holding traffic on the VPN.

  • Decide on VPN preference.
  • Plan failback.
Cloud Platform Engineering
  • Check routes.
  • Review firewall.
  • Validate paths.
Application Owners & Service Desk
  • Rank flows.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Prepare offline modes.
Network Operations & Monitoring
  • Build the timeline.
  • Collect carrier evidence.
  • Preserve logs.
Microsoft, Carrier & Vendor Support Liaison
  • Open cases.
  • Ask for records.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform leadership.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Plan store notice.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about payment processing duties.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. How do you tell flapping from a clean failure?
  2. Should you force traffic to the VPN, and what are the limits?
  3. How do you handle asymmetric routing and stateful firewalls?
  4. What evidence convinces the carrier?
  5. Which flows get the limited VPN bandwidth?
  6. What do stores do meanwhile?
  7. What do leaders hear?
  8. Why was BFD not in place?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the firewall asymmetry until T2 reviews drops.
  • If players disable dampening everywhere, ask about side effects.
  • Look for single, recorded changes.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes stable connectivity for latency-sensitive production traffic and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Network Engineering (ExpressRoute & BGP)

  • Reads BGP logs and flap timings.
  • Decides to prefer the VPN while the circuit is unstable.
  • Adjusts timers and dampening carefully.
  • Plans failback criteria.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Cloud Platform Engineering

  • Reviews route tables and effective routes in Azure.
  • Checks firewall and gateway behavior during path changes.
  • Validates application paths.
  • Tracks changes.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners rank flows by importance.
  • The service desk logs store and ordering contacts.
  • Owners prepare retry and offline modes.
  • Prepare validation checks.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Network Operations & Monitoring

  • Builds a flap timeline with application error rates.
  • Collects evidence for the carrier.
  • Preserves logs.
  • Adds flap-rate alerts.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Microsoft, Carrier & Vendor Support Liaison

  • Opens cases with Microsoft and the carrier.
  • Asks for maintenance records and optical levels.
  • Requests guidance on BFD and timers.
  • Escalates through the TAM and carrier account team.
  • Records Google Cloud guidance and case numbers.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for retail operations and informs leadership.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates workarounds and external contacts.
  • Tracks business impact.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.28, 8.14 redundancy, 8.20 network security; SOC 2 availability).
  • Assesses payment processing continuity.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Use BFD and sensible timers so failure is detected and settles cleanly, and avoid configurations that cause prolonged flapping.

Use diverse circuits and carriers and size the backup path for critical traffic.

Design for symmetric routing or stateful tolerance.

Test failover and failback under load.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.14, 8.20, 8.21) and SOC 2 expect network resilience and change evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on single recorded changes and flap evidence.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How did you identify flapping?
  2. What did you choose for routing?
  3. How did you handle asymmetry?
  4. How did you press the carrier?
  5. How did you protect payments?
  6. What were your failback criteria?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the Microsoft, Carrier & Vendor Support Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
