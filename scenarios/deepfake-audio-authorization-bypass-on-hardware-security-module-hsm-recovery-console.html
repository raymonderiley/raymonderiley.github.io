Deepfake Audio Authorization Bypass on Hardware Security Module (HSM) Recovery Console

AI Failure & Autonomous Agent Disruption

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a deepfake audio attack in which a cloned voice of a key custodian authorizes a remote HSM recovery console session, attempting to export or restore master key material.

Participants are working-level: cryptographic key management engineers, infrastructure operations engineers, application owners, the service desk, security operations analysts, the AI platform and cloud support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of split knowledge, custodian verification, ceremony integrity and key compromise response.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Redwater Payments protects payment keys in HSMs with a recovery console that requires three of five custodians to authorize. The ceremony supports remote verification by video and voice when a custodian travels. Custodian identities are verified by a security officer through a call.

At 21:00 on Sunday, an operator receives a request to recover an HSM partition after reported failure. Two custodians authenticate with smart cards. A third custodian joins by voice and, after a challenge question, confirms. The recovery console opens a key export operation. The security officer notices the third custodian's call came from a number not on record. The real custodian is asleep on a different continent.

What the team does not yet know: whether key material was exported, whether the other two custodians were deceived or involved, whether the failure report was real, and which keys must be considered compromised.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize bypassed ceremony. Participants spot a custodian verified only by voice.
  • Stop the console session. The team terminates the session and locks the partition.
  • Assess key compromise. Engineers determine which keys may be exported.
  • Rotate keys. Participants plan staged rotation of exposed keys.
  • Investigate. The team reviews custodian conduct and the failure report.
  • Improve ceremony control. Participants review custodian verification and remote rules.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Terminate the recovery session and lock the partition before reviewing anything, and treat any key accessible during the session as potentially compromised.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. A questionable custodian and an export operation lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Session terminated and partition locked.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Exposure is diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A rotation plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Rotation proceeds in order.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Operations stabilize.

T+06:00 — Validation. Controls are validated.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Require every custodian to authenticate with a hardware credential and never accept voice or video alone.

Limit or eliminate remote recovery ceremonies and alert on export operations.

Keep key rotation practiced and staged so rotation is possible quickly.

Verify failure reports through independent monitoring.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.24, 5.17, 5.3, 8.16) and SOC 2 expect cryptographic key management and segregation evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on terminate-first and custodian authentication.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. Why was voice enough?
  2. What did you stop first?
  3. Which keys are exposed?
  4. How did you plan rotation?
  5. How did you treat custodians?
  6. What design do you want?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the AI Platform Vendor & Cloud Support Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
