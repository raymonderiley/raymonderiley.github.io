AWS Direct Connect and Azure ExpressRoute Simultaneous Carrier Outage

Hybrid Cloud (AWS & Azure)

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a single carrier that supplies both the AWS Direct Connect and Azure ExpressRoute circuits suffering a fiber cut, so both private links fail together and traffic falls to VPN backups with a fraction of the capacity.

Participants are working-level: network engineers, cloud platform engineers, application owners, the service desk, network operations and monitoring staff, the AWS, Microsoft and carrier support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of circuit diversity, backup VPN capacity, traffic prioritization and carrier escalation.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Juniper Ridge Bank connects its data center to AWS through Direct Connect and to Azure through ExpressRoute. Both circuits were procured from the same carrier and share a metro fiber route. Backup is IPsec VPN over the internet with 1 Gbps per cloud. Normal traffic is 8 Gbps.

At 04:50 on Thursday, construction severs a fiber bundle. Both private circuits drop. BGP fails over to the VPNs, which saturate. Batch jobs, replication and interactive banking APIs all compete. Replication to the cloud DR sites stalls and the morning batch window is at risk. The carrier gives an estimated repair of 18 hours.

What the team does not yet know: the repair time may extend, the VPN gateways have encryption throughput limits below link speed, some applications are chatty across clouds, and a payment file transmission is due at 09:00.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize correlated failure. Participants identify that diversity was assumed but not real.
  • Prioritize limited bandwidth. The team shapes traffic so critical flows get capacity.
  • Protect replication and recovery points. Engineers assess exposure from stalled replication.
  • Escalate with the carrier. Participants press for restoration and alternatives.
  • Maintain operations. The team defers noncritical jobs and uses degraded modes.
  • Improve connectivity design. Participants review carrier diversity, path diversity and backup sizing.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Decide traffic priority by written criteria and record who approved shedding each flow.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Both private links fail and VPNs saturate leading to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Traffic is prioritized and the carrier engaged.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Capacity and exposure are diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A prioritization and recovery plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Changes are applied in order.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Services stabilize on backup paths.

T+06:00 — Validation. Behavior is validated until restoration.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Restoration occurs and the incident is closed for debrief.

Inject #1: Both private links fail on one fiber cut

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 05:20. Direct Connect and ExpressRoute are both down. Traffic fell back to VPNs that are saturated.

The network engineer finds both circuits share a carrier and a metro fiber route. VPN gateways cap below link speed.

Replication to cloud DR sites stalls. The carrier estimates 18 hours to repair.

Facilitator provides: BGP state, VPN graphs, carrier notice and replication lag.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Authorize shedding noncritical traffic.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Network Engineering (Private Connectivity)
  • Confirm failover.
  • Apply QoS.
  • Defer batch.
Cloud Platform Engineering (AWS & Azure)
  • Check gateways.
  • Add tunnels.
  • Check routes.
Application Owners & Service Desk
  • Rank flows.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Prepare degraded modes.
Network Operations & Monitoring
  • Watch replication.
  • Build allocation view.
  • Preserve logs.
AWS, Microsoft & Carrier Support Liaison
  • Open cases.
  • Ask about alternates.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform leadership.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Plan client notice.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about operational resilience duties.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. Why did both circuits fail together?
  2. Which flows get the limited bandwidth?
  3. What happens to replication and recovery points?
  4. How do you handle the 09:00 payment file?
  5. What alternatives exist besides waiting 18 hours?
  6. What do you shed and who approves?
  7. What do clients hear?
  8. What would real diversity look like?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the shared fiber route until T1 reviews carrier records.
  • If players shed everything non-API, ask who approved.
  • Look for written priorities.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes continuity of private connectivity and data flows and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Network Engineering (Private Connectivity)

  • Confirms BGP failover and VPN utilization.
  • Applies QoS and shapes traffic.
  • Defers batch and bulk replication.
  • Plans return to private links.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Cloud Platform Engineering (AWS & Azure)

  • Reviews VPN gateway throughput limits.
  • Adds temporary VPN tunnels if possible.
  • Checks cloud-side routes.
  • Tracks changes.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners rank flows and jobs.
  • The service desk logs customer impact.
  • Owners prepare degraded modes.
  • Prepare validation checks.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Network Operations & Monitoring

  • Watches replication lag and RPO exposure.
  • Builds a bandwidth allocation view.
  • Preserves logs.
  • Adds correlated failure alerts.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

AWS, Microsoft & Carrier Support Liaison

  • Opens cases with the carrier, AWS and Microsoft.
  • Asks about repair progress and alternate routes.
  • Requests temporary capacity options.
  • Escalates through carrier account teams and TAMs.
  • Records Google Cloud guidance and case numbers.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for banking operations and informs leadership.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates workarounds and external contacts.
  • Tracks business impact.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.30, 8.14 redundancy, 8.20 network security; SOC 2 availability).
  • Assesses operational resilience of critical banking services.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Verify carrier and physical path diversity in writing, not by contract language alone.

Size backup VPN capacity for critical flows and test the encryption limits.

Define traffic priority and degraded modes before an event.

Monitor replication lag against recovery objectives during failures.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.14, 5.30, 8.20) and SOC 2 expect resilience and capacity evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on written traffic priorities and true path diversity.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How did you identify the correlated failure?
  2. How did you prioritize traffic?
  3. How did you protect replication?
  4. How did you handle the payment file?
  5. How did you escalate to the carrier?
  6. What would you change in design?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the AWS, Microsoft & Carrier Support Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
