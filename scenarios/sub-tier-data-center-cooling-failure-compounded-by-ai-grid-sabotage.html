Sub-Tier Data Center Cooling Failure Compounded by AI Grid Sabotage

AI Failure & Autonomous Agent Disruption

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a cooling failure at a sub-tier colocation data center combined with manipulated building and grid control signals from a compromised AI energy optimization service, which hides the true thermal state and delays shutdown decisions.

Participants are working-level: facilities and data center engineers, infrastructure operations engineers, application owners, the service desk, operations and monitoring staff, the AI platform and cloud support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of independent sensors, controlled shutdown order, colocation provider coordination and trust in automation.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Pinecrest Health Network hosts secondary clinical systems in a colocation facility operated by a provider that uses an AI energy optimization service to manage chillers and power draw. The provider's dashboard is the main source of thermal data. Pinecrest has its own rack sensors on only some racks.

At 13:00 on a hot Tuesday, a chiller fails. The provider's dashboard shows normal temperatures because the optimization service reports smoothed values. Pinecrest rack sensors show inlet temperatures climbing by 1.5 degrees per minute. By 13:40 several servers throttle. The provider says its systems show no fault and its grid load shedding is running as designed. A power draw reduction cuts available capacity.

What the team does not yet know: whether the provider's service is compromised or malfunctioning, how long before hardware shutdown thresholds are reached, which systems to shut down first, and whether the provider can restore cooling in time.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize conflicting thermal data. Participants compare independent sensors with provider data.
  • Protect hardware and data. The team begins a controlled shutdown in priority order.
  • Maintain clinical services. Engineers fail over critical workloads.
  • Coordinate with the provider. Participants escalate and demand facts and access.
  • Restore safely. The team brings systems back after thermal recovery.
  • Improve facility resilience. Participants review independent monitoring and contract terms.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Trust independent sensors over the provider dashboard until proven otherwise, and shut down in documented priority order before thresholds are reached.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Throttling and conflicting data lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Shutdown order is started and clinical workloads moved.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Provider status is challenged and diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A restoration plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Systems are restored in order.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Services stabilize.

T+06:00 — Validation. Hardware and data are validated.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes thermal and power resilience when automation is untrusted and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Facilities & Data Center Engineering

  • Reads independent rack sensors and trend rates.
  • Starts controlled shutdown of low priority systems.
  • Plans failover.
  • Plans independent monitoring.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Infrastructure Operations Engineering

  • Fails over clinical workloads to the primary site and cloud.
  • Validates replication.
  • Handles hardware health.
  • Tracks changes.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners and clinical leaders confirm what can be shut down.
  • The service desk logs clinical contacts.
  • Owners define downtime procedures.
  • Prepare validation checks.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Operations & Monitoring

  • Compares sensor sources and logs readings.
  • Monitors thresholds.
  • Preserves logs.
  • Adds alerts for sensor disagreement.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

AI Platform Vendor & Cloud Support Liaison

  • Opens cases with the colocation provider and its management.
  • Demands facility access and an on-site contact.
  • Requests cooling status evidence.
  • Escalates through account teams.
  • Records Google Cloud guidance and case numbers.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for hospital IT operations and informs leadership.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates workarounds and external contacts.
  • Tracks business impact.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 7.5 environmental threats, 7.11 supporting utilities, 8.14 redundancy, 5.20 supplier agreements; SOC 2 availability and environmental controls).
  • Assesses facility safety and supplier oversight.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Install independent temperature and power sensors on all critical racks and alert on disagreement with provider data.

Keep a tested shutdown and failover order.

Negotiate contract terms for facility transparency and notice.

Treat optimization automation as untrusted for safety decisions.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (7.5, 7.11, 8.14, 5.20) and SOC 2 expect environmental and supplier evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on independent data and controlled shutdown.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. Which data did you trust?
  2. What did you shut down first?
  3. How did you protect clinical services?
  4. What did you demand from the provider?
  5. How did you restart?
  6. What design do you want?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the AI Platform Vendor & Cloud Support Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
