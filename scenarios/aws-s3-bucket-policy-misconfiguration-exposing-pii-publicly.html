AWS S3 Bucket Policy Misconfiguration Exposing PII Publicly

AWS Cloud Resiliency

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a data exposure caused by an S3 bucket policy change that grants public read access to a bucket holding customer personal data. The exercise follows detection, containment, determining whether the data was accessed, and the privacy, legal and customer obligations that follow.

Participants are working-level: cloud security engineers, platform and storage engineers, data and application owners, security operations and forensics staff, privacy and legal contacts, the AWS support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests knowledge of Block Public Access, bucket policies, ACLs, access logging and evidence handling.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Marlowe Health Benefits stores member enrollment documents and exported claims files in S3 buckets across several accounts. Organization-wide S3 Block Public Access is enabled at the account level for most accounts, but a legacy data-sharing account was exempted two years ago for a partner integration. Server access logging and CloudTrail data events are enabled only on a few buckets.

At 16:45 on Thursday, an engineer updates the bucket policy on 'marlowe-claims-exports' to let a partner's account read a prefix. The policy uses a Principal of '*' with a condition the engineer believed limited access to the partner, but the condition key was misspelled and ignored. At 17:10, a security researcher emails the company's security mailbox with a link that lists the bucket contents. IAM Access Analyzer had raised a public access finding at 16:52 that went to an unmonitored queue.

What the team does not yet know: the bucket contains 1.8 million records with names, dates of birth and member IDs, versioning is enabled so older objects are also exposed, CloudTrail data events are off so object-level reads are not recorded for this bucket, and the researcher has posted a screenshot to a private forum.

Exercise Objectives

  • Confirm and contain public exposure. Participants verify public access with policy analysis and Access Analyzer, then close it using Block Public Access and a corrected policy while preserving evidence.
  • Preserve evidence before changing everything. The team decides what to snapshot first: policy versions, configuration history, logs and Access Analyzer findings.
  • Determine access to the data. Security reviews S3 server access logs, CloudFront or load balancer logs, and any CloudTrail data events, and acknowledges limits where logging was absent.
  • Assess data sensitivity and obligations. Privacy and legal determine data types, affected individuals and whether breach notification duties apply.
  • Handle the researcher and communications. Teams manage the contact with the reporter, internal communications and any customer or regulator messaging.
  • Prevent recurrence. Participants examine guardrails, review of policy changes, alert routing and logging standards.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Do not speculate about whether data was accessed without citing an available log source. State what is unknown.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. The researcher report and Access Analyzer finding lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Public access is closed and evidence is preserved.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Logs are reviewed to determine possible access.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. Scope, data types and notification analysis are planned.

T+03:00 — Execution. Notifications, researcher contact and remediation are executed.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Monitoring for misuse and follow-up with partners continues.

T+06:00 — Validation. Controls and logging are verified.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. The incident is closed for debrief with regulatory decisions recorded.

Injects

Inject #1: Researcher reports a publicly listable claims bucket

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 17:20. The security mailbox contains a message from an external researcher with a URL that lists objects in 'marlowe-claims-exports'. The security engineer opens the link and sees filenames suggesting member claims data. The bucket policy was changed at 16:45. Access Analyzer shows a public finding created at 16:52, unreviewed.

The platform engineer reports versioning is enabled and the bucket holds about 1.8 million member records across 340 objects, plus older versions. The data sensitivity tag says 'confidential' but the bucket is in the exempted legacy account where Block Public Access is off.

CloudTrail management events show the policy change, but data events are not enabled for this bucket, and server access logging is off. The researcher has asked for acknowledgement within 24 hours and mentioned posting evidence to a forum.

Facilitator provides: the policy JSON with the misspelled condition key, the Access Analyzer finding, the object inventory summary and the researcher email.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare a potential data breach and name leads for technical, privacy and communications.
  • Authorize immediate closure of public access after a quick evidence snapshot.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Cloud Security Engineering
  • Confirm public exposure and enable Block Public Access.
  • Explain the misspelled condition and how it should be validated.
  • List other buckets at risk.
Cloud Platform & Storage Engineering
  • Snapshot the policy, versions and inventory first.
  • Describe the objects and versions exposed.
  • Plan logging for the future.
Data Owners & Application Owners
  • Classify the data and affected populations.
  • Identify the partner integration impacted by the closure.
  • Prepare member support scripts.
Security Operations & Forensics
  • Identify available logs and their limits.
  • Look for unknown IP access.
  • Preserve evidence with hashes.
Privacy, Legal & AWS Support Liaison
  • Start the notification analysis and legal hold.
  • Draft the researcher acknowledgement.
  • Ask the AWS liaison about log availability.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Prepare internal and call center guidance.
  • Notify the partner contact.
  • Draft the first update.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Start the regulatory clock log.
  • Ask how the policy change was approved.

Discussion questions

  1. How do you confirm the bucket is public and what closes it fastest?
  2. What do you capture before closing access, and does it delay containment?
  3. What can you conclude about access without data events or server access logs?
  4. How do you treat older object versions?
  5. What data elements are involved and do notification duties apply?
  6. How do you respond to the researcher and the forum threat?
  7. Why did the Access Analyzer finding sit unreviewed?
  8. What does the partner integration need now?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not volunteer the absence of logs until the SOC lead asks which logs exist.
  • If players want to delete the bucket, ask about evidence and legal hold.
  • Check that the clock for notification is tracked.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Inject #2: Closing access breaks the partner

Time frame T+00:20 · Variant A

Situation

Block Public Access is enabled and the partner's integration begins failing, delaying their daily claims reconciliation. The partner's operations lead calls, upset and demanding access restoration.

The partner's account ID is known, but the original cross-account arrangement was never documented.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Decide on timing of restoration.
Cloud Security Engineering
  • Review and approve the new policy with a second person.
Cloud Platform & Storage Engineering
  • Design a least-privilege cross-account policy using the partner account principal and an external ID or organization condition.
Data Owners & Application Owners
  • Coordinate with the partner.

Discussion questions

  1. How do you restore partner access safely?
  2. What does a correct cross-account policy look like?
  3. Who approves it?
  4. Should access wait until the investigation advances?

Facilitator notes

  • Tests safe restoration.
  • Look for second-person review.
  • Avoid reopening the hole.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident as a potential data breach and sets containment first, evidence second, notification analysis in parallel.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes immediate closure of public access.
  • Decides when to engage outside counsel and forensics.

Cloud Security Engineering

  • Confirms public access via bucket policy review, ACLs and Access Analyzer, and enables Block Public Access.
  • Identifies other buckets with the same pattern and exempt accounts.
  • Reviews how the policy change passed review.
  • Plans guardrails with service control policies and Config rules.

Cloud Platform & Storage Engineering

  • Preserves the policy history, object versions and inventory before changes.
  • Lists objects, prefixes and versions exposed and their dates.
  • Rotates presigned links or access tokens if relevant.
  • Enables logging and data events for the future.

Data Owners & Application Owners

  • Data owners classify the data and identify the affected populations.
  • Application owners identify systems consuming the bucket and the partner integration.
  • Plan a safe replacement for the partner access.
  • Prepare member support scripts.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Reviews server access logs, CloudFront logs and available CloudTrail for requests from unknown IPs.
  • Searches for the researcher's IP and any others.
  • Looks for related scanning or exfiltration indicators.
  • Preserves forensic evidence with hashes and chain of custody.

Privacy, Legal & AWS Support Liaison

  • Privacy and legal determine data elements, jurisdictions and notification triggers.
  • The AWS liaison opens a case for guidance on log availability.
  • Counsel reviews researcher communications and any vulnerability disclosure policy.
  • Prepare regulator and individual notification drafts.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates communications procedures and coordinates the call center for member inquiries.
  • Issues time-stamped updates to leadership.
  • Coordinates partner communications.
  • Tracks actions against internal timelines.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.28, 5.34 privacy, 8.3 information access restriction; SOC 2 confidentiality).
  • Tracks regulatory clocks and obligations.
  • Captures evidence of decisions.
  • Logs findings on policy review, alert routing and logging gaps.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Make public access hard by default. Enable S3 Block Public Access at the organization level with narrow, reviewed exemptions, and use service control policies to deny policy changes that grant public principals.

Validate policies before they ship. Use policy linting and IAM Access Analyzer checks in the pipeline, and require two reviewers for any bucket policy with a wildcard principal.

Route detective findings to a monitored queue with an on-call owner and a response target, and automate remediation for public buckets holding classified data.

Log for forensics before you need it: data events on sensitive buckets, server access logs, versioning and object lock where appropriate.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (5.34, 8.3) and SOC 2 expect data classification linked to controls, tested alerting and evidence of breach analysis.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on evidence preservation and the limits of logging.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How long between the policy change and detection, and why?
  2. What did you capture before closing access?
  3. What logs existed and what could they not tell you?
  4. How did the team treat object versions?
  5. How did you triage other buckets?
  6. How did you restore partner access and who approved it?
  7. How did you respond to the researcher?
  8. How were notification obligations assessed and tracked?
  9. What happened with the Access Analyzer queue?
  10. How accurate were holding statements?
  11. What did AWS support add?
  12. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  13. How are Block Public Access exemptions governed?
  14. What access or permissions were missing?
  15. What would you change in policy review?
