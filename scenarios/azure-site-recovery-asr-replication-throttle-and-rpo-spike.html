Azure Site Recovery (ASR) Replication Throttle and RPO Spike

Azure Cloud Resiliency

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines an Azure Site Recovery deployment whose replication falls behind after a network throttle and a churn spike, pushing recovery point objectives far beyond commitments just as a primary site risk emerges, so the team must decide whether failover is still safe and what data would be lost.

Participants are working-level: cloud infrastructure engineers, application and database engineers, application owners, the service desk, cloud operations and monitoring staff, the Microsoft support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of replication health, churn limits, recovery points, crash versus app-consistent points, test failover and runbook discipline.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Sable Energy replicates 120 on-premises VMware VMs to Azure with Azure Site Recovery. The agreed RPO is 15 minutes for trading and scheduling systems and 4 hours for the rest. Replication traffic crosses a 1 Gbps ExpressRoute shared with other workloads. App-consistent snapshots run hourly.

At 07:00 on Tuesday, a data warehouse rebuild generates 3 TB of churn on several protected VMs, and a network change applies a QoS policy that caps replication traffic at 100 Mbps. By 09:30, the ASR dashboard shows RPO of 2 hours for the trading VMs, 14 VMs in 'critical' replication health and the latest app-consistent recovery point 5 hours old. At 09:45, the primary datacenter facilities team warns of a possible power instability and cooling alarm, with a 40 percent chance of a forced shutdown within hours.

What the team does not yet know: crash-consistent recovery points are newer than app-consistent ones, but the trading database may need log recovery, a test failover has not been run in 6 months, DNS and network mappings in Azure were changed since the last test, and failing over a partially replicated VM can produce an unbootable disk.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize RPO breach. Participants read ASR health, RPO charts, churn and bandwidth data to understand real exposure.
  • Restore replication. The team removes the throttle, prioritizes critical VMs and manages churn from the warehouse.
  • Decide on failover under uncertainty. Participants weigh data loss, consistency and site risk to decide between waiting, partial failover and full failover.
  • Select recovery points. Engineers choose between crash-consistent and app-consistent points and plan application recovery.
  • Protect integrity. The team plans database recovery and validates data before returning to service.
  • Improve DR resilience. Participants review bandwidth planning, churn control, regular failover tests and RPO alerting.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • State the data loss window in minutes or hours for every failover option.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. RPO alerts and facility warnings lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Replication is prioritized and the throttle is removed.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Exposure and recovery points are diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A failover decision framework is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Failover or replication catch-up is executed.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Systems stabilize in the chosen site.

T+06:00 — Validation. Data is validated and reconciled.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Injects

Inject #1: RPO at two hours as the datacenter warns of power risk

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 10:00. ASR shows RPO at 2 hours for trading VMs against a 15-minute objective. Fourteen VMs are in critical replication health. The latest app-consistent point is 5 hours old.

The infrastructure engineer finds a QoS policy capping replication at 100 Mbps and 3 TB of churn from a warehouse rebuild. Facilities warn of possible power instability with a possible forced shutdown within hours.

The last test failover was six months ago and Azure network mappings have changed. Crash-consistent points are newer, but the trading database may need log recovery.

Facilitator provides: ASR dashboard export, bandwidth graphs, QoS change record, facility notice and the recovery plan.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Set the decision rule for failover.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Cloud Infrastructure Engineering (Site Recovery)
  • Remove the throttle and prioritize critical VMs.
  • Check network mappings.
  • Plan test failover.
Application & Database Engineering
  • Assess database recovery options.
  • Coordinate with the warehouse team on churn.
  • Plan validation.
Application Owners & Service Desk
  • Rank systems and state tolerable loss.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Prepare workarounds.
Cloud Operations & Monitoring
  • Build the dashboard.
  • Monitor replication.
  • Preserve logs.
Microsoft Support & TAM Liaison
  • Open a case.
  • Ask about recovery points.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform leadership.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Plan alternate facilities.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about loss reporting.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. How do you quantify your current data loss exposure?
  2. How do you restore replication and in what order?
  3. What is the decision rule for failover versus waiting?
  4. Which recovery point type do you choose and why?
  5. What risks come from an untested failover?
  6. How would you validate the trading database after recovery?
  7. Who accepts data loss and how is it recorded?
  8. Why did a QoS change reach replication traffic without review?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the stale network mappings until T1 reviews the recovery plan.
  • If players fail over immediately, ask about data loss and consistency.
  • Look for restoring replication in parallel with preparing failover.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes critical trading systems and sets the failover decision rule.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes failover and acceptance of data loss.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications.

Cloud Infrastructure Engineering (Site Recovery)

  • Reads ASR health, RPO and churn and removes the QoS cap.
  • Prioritizes replication and plans bandwidth.
  • Plans recovery plans and network mapping checks.
  • Prepares test failover where feasible.

Application & Database Engineering

  • Reviews database logs and recovery point options.
  • Plans application and database recovery steps.
  • Validates data after failover.
  • Coordinates with the warehouse team on churn.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners rank systems and state tolerable data loss.
  • The service desk logs user impacts.

  • Monitors replication after changes.
  • Preserves logs.

  • Coordinates alternate facilities.

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.30, 8.14 redundancy; SOC 2 availability).
  • Assesses data loss reporting obligations.
  • Captures evidence of decisions.
  • Logs findings on DR testing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Treat RPO as a monitored service level: alert at a fraction of the objective, plan bandwidth for peak churn and protect replication traffic from QoS changes.

Control churn from known heavy jobs and schedule them with awareness of replication capacity.

Test regularly and keep mappings current: test failover at least quarterly for critical systems, with documented recovery plans and validated network and DNS settings.

Define decision criteria in advance for failover under facility risk, including who may accept data loss.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.14, 5.30) and SOC 2 expect DR test evidence, RPO monitoring and decision records.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on RPO monitoring and failover decision criteria.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

  2. How did you restore replication?
  3. What was your failover decision rule?
  4. How did you choose a recovery point?
  5. How did you handle partial replication?
  6. What did the test show?
  7. How did you handle the facility risk?
  8. How was missing data reconciled?
  9. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  10. What did Microsoft support add?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  12. How often are failover tests run?
  13. What access or permissions were missing?
  14. What alerts are missing?
  15. What would you change in DR design?
