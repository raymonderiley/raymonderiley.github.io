Azure Service Bus Dead-Letter Queue Saturation in Order Pipeline

Azure Cloud Resiliency

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines an order processing pipeline built on Azure Service Bus in which a poison message pattern and a schema change cause consumers to fail repeatedly. Messages exhaust their delivery count and move to the dead-letter queue (DLQ), the DLQ approaches its size quota, and orders silently stop flowing to fulfillment while the main queue looks healthy.

Participants are working-level: messaging and integration engineers, application engineers, application owners, the service desk, cloud operations and monitoring staff, the Microsoft support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of delivery count, lock duration, DLQ behavior, sessions, duplicate detection and safe replay.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Alderwood Outfitters runs an order pipeline: web checkout publishes to a Service Bus topic 'orders', subscriptions feed payment capture, inventory reservation and fulfillment. Each subscription has a max delivery count of 5 and a lock duration of 30 seconds. Dead-letter queues are not monitored. Duplicate detection is enabled with a 10-minute window on the topic.

At 09:00 on Tuesday, the checkout team releases a change adding a new optional field and renaming another. The fulfillment consumer deserializes strictly and throws on the renamed field. Each message is retried five times and dead-lettered. The queue depth looks normal because messages are quickly moved, while the DLQ grows by 4,000 per minute. By 10:30, the DLQ holds 280,000 messages and is approaching the 80 GB quota, at which point new dead-lettering would fail and messages could be dropped or blocked.

What the team does not yet know: payments were captured for all orders even though fulfillment failed, so customers are charged but no shipment is created, the inventory subscription is also affected for a subset of messages, a bulk resubmit tool exists but would duplicate payment captures, and some messages carry session IDs that require ordered processing.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize silent failure. Participants read DLQ counts, delivery metrics and application logs to find failures hidden behind a healthy-looking main queue.
  • Stop further damage. The team rolls back or patches the producer or consumer and pauses affected subscriptions to prevent quota exhaustion.
  • Protect the DLQ. Engineers prevent quota overflow by exporting, moving or increasing capacity safely.
  • Replay safely. Participants plan idempotent, ordered replay of dead-lettered messages without duplicating payment captures.
  • Reconcile customers and orders. The team identifies charged customers without shipments and plans communication.
  • Improve messaging resilience. Participants review contract testing, DLQ alarms, retry policy and idempotency.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Never replay messages without stating how duplicates and ordering are handled.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Fulfillment gaps and DLQ growth lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. The faulty change is rolled back and the DLQ is protected.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Failure causes and affected subscriptions are identified.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A replay and reconciliation plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Messages are repaired and replayed in controlled batches.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Backlog drains and fulfillment catches up.

T+06:00 — Validation. Customers and orders are reconciled.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Injects

Inject #1: Orders paid but not fulfilled, DLQ growing fast

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 10:45. Customer service reports 'order paid but no shipping confirmation' contacts rising. The fulfillment dashboard shows near-zero new shipments since 09:05. Service Bus metrics show the main queue depth is normal.

The messaging engineer finds the fulfillment subscription's DLQ at 285,000 messages growing 4,000 per minute, approaching the 80 GB quota. Dead-letter reason shows 'MaxDeliveryCountExceeded'. The application engineer links this to the checkout release at 09:00 that renamed a field.

Payments were captured for all orders. A bulk resubmit tool exists but would trigger payment capture again. Azure Service Health shows no incident. Some messages use sessions.

Facilitator provides: DLQ metrics, message samples with the schema change, release notes and the resubmit tool description.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Decide whether to pause checkout payment capture.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Messaging & Integration Engineering (Service Bus)
  • Protect the DLQ from quota exhaustion.
  • Plan export or move of messages.
  • Plan controlled replay.
Application Engineering
  • Roll back or patch the schema change.
  • Build a repair and replay tool.
  • Check idempotency.
Application Owners & Service Desk
  • Identify charged customers without shipments.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Plan manual fulfillment.
Cloud Operations & Monitoring
  • Build the dashboard.
  • Confirm alerts.
  • List missing alarms.
Microsoft Support & TAM Liaison
  • Open a case.
  • Ask about quota.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform customer service.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Plan manual shipping.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask how the release was tested.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. Why does the main queue look healthy while orders fail?
  2. What happens when the DLQ reaches quota?
  3. How do you stop the failure: rollback, patch or pause?
  4. How do you replay without duplicating payment captures?
  5. How do you handle session ordering during replay?
  6. Do you pause checkout payment while fulfillment is broken?
  7. What do charged customers need to hear?
  8. Why did DLQ alarms not exist?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the resubmit duplication risk until T2 inspects the tool.
  • If players resubmit in bulk, ask about payments.
  • Look for protecting the DLQ first.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes stopping charges without fulfillment and sets the replay approval rule.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes pausing checkout payments if needed.
  • Decides on customer messaging.

Messaging & Integration Engineering (Service Bus)

  • Checks DLQ depth by subscription, quota usage and delivery counts.
  • Plans export or move of DLQ messages and quota increases.
  • Plans controlled replay with sessions and duplicate detection.
  • Sets alerts and TTLs.

Application Engineering

  • Identifies the schema change and prepares a rollback or tolerant reader fix.
  • Builds a repair tool to map renamed fields and mark replays.
  • Implements idempotency in payment and fulfillment.
  • Validates in a test subscription.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners rank affected flows and identify charged customers without shipments.
  • The service desk logs 'where is my order' contacts.
  • Owners prepare manual fulfillment for priority orders.
  • Prepare validation tests.

Cloud Operations & Monitoring

  • Builds a dashboard of main queue, DLQ depth, dead-letter reasons and processing rates.
  • Confirms alerts and Azure Monitor metrics.
  • Checks Azure Service Health.
  • Adds DLQ alarms.

Microsoft Support & TAM Liaison

  • Opens a Sev A case on DLQ quota and behavior.
  • Asks about quota increase options and export.
  • Requests guidance on replay.
  • Records guidance.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for order fulfillment and informs customer service.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates manual shipping.
  • Tracks impact.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.28, 8.32 change management; SOC 2 processing integrity and availability).
  • Assesses consumer protection obligations.
  • Captures evidence of release controls.
  • Logs findings on contract testing.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Alarm on dead-letter depth and growth as a first-class signal, not only on active queue length, and route alerts to an on-call owner.

Make consumers tolerant and producers disciplined: schema versioning, contract tests in the pipeline and tolerant readers.

Design for replay: idempotency keys on every side-effecting step, session-aware ordering and a controlled replay tool with rate limits.

Decouple payment from fulfillment failure handling so that a downstream outage does not leave customers charged without a path to resolution.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.32) and SOC 2 expect release testing evidence, monitoring of processing and reconciliation controls.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on DLQ monitoring and safe replay.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How long did it take to notice the DLQ and why?
  2. What caused dead-lettering?
  3. How did you protect the DLQ quota?
  4. How did you decide rollback versus patch?
  5. How did you avoid duplicate payments on replay?
  6. How did you handle session ordering?
  7. How did you handle charged customers without shipments?
  8. How was replay throttled?
  9. How accurate were updates to leadership and customers?
  10. What did Microsoft support add?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  12. How are releases contract tested?
  13. What access or permissions were missing?
  14. What alarms are missing?
  15. What would you change in the pipeline?
