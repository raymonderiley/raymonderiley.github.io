Voice-Cloned IT Director Directing Helpdesk Staff to Reset Domain Admin Passwords

AI Failure & Autonomous Agent Disruption

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a cloned voice of the IT director calling the help desk and ordering immediate resets of domain admin passwords and MFA, claiming an emergency during a live incident.

Participants are working-level: identity and access engineers, service desk and directory engineers, application owners, the service desk, security operations analysts, the AI platform and cloud support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of privileged reset controls, caller verification, tiering and rapid containment.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Whitmore Utilities has a help desk that can reset standard accounts and MFA with a manager's approval. Domain admin resets require a second approver but the help desk lead can approve verbally when the IT director calls during incidents. Caller ID shows internal numbers.

At 19:00 on Friday, a caller with the IT director's voice phones the help desk during a minor outage, saying there is a security emergency and the domain admin passwords and MFA devices must be reset immediately to fresh values he will text. A second help desk lead approves after hearing the same voice. Two domain admin accounts are reset. By 19:40 the new credentials are used from an external address.

What the team does not yet know: how many admin accounts were affected, what the actor did with them, whether the real IT director was targeted separately, and whether other help desk staff received calls.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize impersonated authority. Participants see missing verification and urgency.
  • Contain admin accounts. The team resets credentials and revokes sessions through a trusted path.
  • Scope activity. Engineers review what the compromised accounts did.
  • Protect the help desk. Participants add verification for any privileged request.
  • Investigate. The team traces calls and the texted numbers.
  • Improve privileged reset control. Participants review dual control and callback rules.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Reset and revoke the compromised admin accounts through a trusted path before anything else, and stop all privileged resets by phone.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. A suspicious reset and external use lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Admin accounts contained and resets halted.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Scope is diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A control and communication plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Containment and review proceed.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Operations stabilize.

T+06:00 — Validation. Controls are validated.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes privileged resets that follow verification, not seniority and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Identity & Access Engineering

  • Disables and resets affected admin accounts and revokes sessions.
  • Suspends help desk privileged resets.
  • Reviews admin activity.
  • Plans callback verification.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Service Desk & Directory Engineering

  • Reviews domain controller changes and new persistence.
  • Blocks the external address.
  • Validates directory.
  • Tracks changes.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners and service leads identify systems the admins can reach.
  • The service desk logs calls and routes privileged requests to security.
  • Owners define manual verification.
  • Prepare validation checks.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Security Operations & Monitoring

  • Investigates the calls and texted details.
  • Looks for other targeted staff.
  • Preserves evidence.
  • Adds alerts for privileged resets outside tickets.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

AI Platform Vendor & Cloud Support Liaison

  • Opens cases with the telephony provider and directory vendor.
  • Asks for call logs.
  • Requests guidance on privileged workflows.
  • Escalates through account teams.
  • Records Google Cloud guidance and case numbers.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for utility IT operations and informs leadership.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates workarounds and external contacts.
  • Tracks business impact.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 8.2 privileged access, 5.16 identity management, 5.17 authentication information, 6.3 awareness; SOC 2 security).
  • Assesses privileged access integrity.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Never let the help desk reset domain admin credentials by phone and route all privileged requests through a ticket with callback and security approval.

Alert on privileged resets and new admin sessions from unusual locations.

Use tiered administration and separate admin identities.

Brief the help desk on voice impersonation.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.2, 5.16, 5.17, 6.3) and SOC 2 expect privileged access and authentication evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on contain-and-halt-first and callback verification.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. What should have stopped the reset?
  2. What did you contain first?
  3. What did the accounts do?
  4. How do you verify now?
  5. How did you treat staff?
  6. What design do you want?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the AI Platform Vendor & Cloud Support Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
