GCP Cloud Spanner Regional Quorum Loss and Read/Write Halt

Google Cloud Resiliency

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a Cloud Spanner multi-region instance that loses quorum after a regional impairment combined with a leader placement misconfiguration, causing read/write halts on the payments database while read-only stale reads still work.

Participants are working-level: database platform engineers, application engineers, application owners, the service desk, cloud operations and monitoring staff, the Google Cloud support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of quorum, leader regions, witness replicas, stale reads, timestamps and recovery trade-offs.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Oakhaven Payments stores balances and transfers in a Cloud Spanner instance with a custom multi-region configuration: read-write replicas in two regions and a witness in a third. Leader placement is pinned to one region by a default leader setting to reduce latency. Applications use strong reads and read-write transactions.

At 14:10 on Tuesday, the pinned leader region suffers a network partition and a second region experiences elevated errors. Spanner cannot form a write quorum. Read-write transactions time out, strong reads fail and error rates reach 95 percent. Stale reads with a 15-second bound still succeed. Google Cloud status reports a regional networking incident.

What the team does not yet know: the surviving region's replicas may be sufficient once the partition resolves, changing the leader preference is possible but may not help without quorum, stale reads could show balances that are seconds behind, and a fallback ledger used in the past was decommissioned.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize quorum loss. Participants read instance metrics, error codes and the status page to separate quorum loss from application faults.
  • Protect payment integrity. The team prevents double spends and inconsistent balances while writes are unavailable.
  • Use safe degraded modes. Engineers decide where stale reads are acceptable and where they are not.
  • Choose recovery actions carefully. Participants evaluate leader moves, configuration changes and waiting, with their risks.
  • Communicate precisely. The team provides accurate status to customers and partners.
  • Improve design. Participants review replica placement, witness choices, leader settings and fallback paths.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • No payment may be accepted unless it is durably recorded exactly once.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Write failures and a regional impairment lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Writes are paused and degraded modes are chosen.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Quorum state and causes are diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A recovery and communication plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Recovery actions are executed.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Writes and reads stabilize.

T+06:00 — Validation. Balances and transactions are reconciled.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Injects

Inject #1: Payments halt as quorum is lost

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 14:25. Payment transfers fail with deadline errors and strong reads fail. Bounded stale reads still work. The status page lists a regional networking incident.

The database engineer finds the leader region unreachable and a second region degraded. A write quorum is not possible. The default leader is pinned to the impaired region.

Changing leader preference needs quorum for some operations. The old fallback ledger was decommissioned.

Facilitator provides: instance metrics, topology diagram, error samples and the leader configuration.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Decide to pause payment acceptance.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Database Platform Engineering (Cloud Spanner)
  • Assess quorum.
  • Plan leader options.
  • Plan escalation.
Application Engineering
  • Switch to stale reads where approved.
  • Queue requests with idempotency keys.
  • Plan recovery.
Application Owners & Service Desk
  • Decide flows.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Define limits.
Cloud Operations & Monitoring
  • Build the dashboard.
  • Watch the status page.
  • Preserve logs.
Google Cloud Support & TAM Liaison
  • Open a case.
  • Ask about recovery.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform leadership.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Plan partner contact.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about payment obligations.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. How do you recognize quorum loss versus a slow database?
  2. What can still work safely and what cannot?
  3. Where are stale balances acceptable?
  4. How do you queue payments without double spending?
  5. Would changing the leader help right now?
  6. What do you tell customers and partners?
  7. Who decides to resume writes?
  8. Why was the leader pinned to one region?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the decommissioned ledger until APP asks about fallbacks.
  • If players force configuration changes, ask what they will achieve without quorum.
  • Look for integrity before speed.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes transaction integrity and the most critical payment flows and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Database Platform Engineering (Cloud Spanner)

  • Reads instance state, replica health and leader placement.
  • Assesses whether quorum can be restored by waiting or configuration.
  • Plans leader changes only if they help.
  • Plans escalation with Google.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Application Engineering

  • Switches approved read paths to bounded stale reads.
  • Queues payment requests durably with idempotency keys.
  • Prevents double processing on recovery.
  • Validates after recovery.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners decide which flows can use stale data.
  • The service desk logs customer contacts.
  • Owners define customer limits.
  • Prepare reconciliation checks.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Cloud Operations & Monitoring

  • Builds a dashboard of latency, errors and replica status.
  • Watches the status page.
  • Preserves logs.
  • Adds quorum alerts.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Google Cloud Support & TAM Liaison

  • Opens a Sev A case on quorum loss.
  • Asks about expected recovery and safe actions.
  • Requests guidance on leader settings.
  • Escalates through the TAM.
  • Records Google Cloud guidance and case numbers.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for payments and informs leadership.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates workarounds and external contacts.
  • Tracks business impact.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.30, 8.14 redundancy; SOC 2 availability and processing integrity).
  • Assesses payment availability and balance integrity.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Choose replica and witness placement for the failures you expect, and test what happens when the leader region is lost.

Avoid pinning leaders in ways that turn one regional problem into a full outage.

Build idempotent, durable queues so payments can wait without being lost or duplicated.

Define where stale reads are allowed and make that policy explicit.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.14, 5.30) and SOC 2 expect resilience testing and integrity evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on integrity during degraded mode and replica placement.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How did you recognize quorum loss?
  2. What did you keep working and what did you stop?
  3. How did you avoid double payments?
  4. How did you decide on stale reads?
  5. How did you assess the leader change?
  6. How were in-flight transfers reconciled?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the Google Cloud Support & TAM Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
