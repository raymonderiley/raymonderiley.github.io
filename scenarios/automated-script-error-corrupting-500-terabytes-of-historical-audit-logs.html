Automated Script Error Corrupting 500 Terabytes of Historical Audit Logs

Ransomware & Cyber-Recovery Forensics

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines an automated maintenance script with a path error that rewrites and corrupts 500 terabytes of historical audit logs, with no immutable copy, just before an audit and a forensic investigation need them.

Participants are working-level: log management engineers, infrastructure operations engineers, application owners, the service desk, security operations and forensics analysts, the forensics and vendor support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of log integrity, snapshot recovery, chain of custody, regulatory retention and communication with auditors.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Greystone Payments retains seven years of audit logs on a clustered file platform. A monthly compaction script rewrites log files into a compressed format. A recent change introduced a wrong path variable. Snapshots are kept for 14 days and replicated to the same platform vendor's second cluster.

At 01:00 on Saturday, the script runs with the wrong path and rewrites files with truncated headers, corrupting 500 terabytes. Alerts about job success were green because the script exits cleanly. At 09:00 an analyst opens old logs for a forensic case and finds unreadable files. The regulatory audit begins Monday.

What the team does not yet know: whether snapshots predate the run, whether replication also copied corruption, which time ranges are unrecoverable, and how forensic evidence integrity will be affected.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize silent log corruption. Participants find the corruption through the failed read.
  • Stop the job and preserve. The team halts scripts and snapshots the current state.
  • Recover. Engineers recover from snapshots and replicas.
  • Assess evidence impact. Participants judge effects on forensic cases and audits.
  • Communicate with auditors. The team prepares honest, factual disclosure.
  • Improve log protection. Participants review immutability, change testing and integrity checks.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Halt all maintenance scripts and snapshot the current state before any repair, and never overwrite corrupted files in place.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Unreadable logs lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Scripts halted and state preserved.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Recoverability and impact are diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A recovery and disclosure plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Recovery proceeds in order.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Log platform stabilizes.

T+06:00 — Validation. Integrity is validated.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes audit logs that remain complete and provable and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Log Management Engineering

  • Disables maintenance scripts and takes a snapshot.
  • Reviews the script and the change.
  • Plans integrity checks.
  • Plans immutable storage.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Infrastructure Operations Engineering

  • Evaluates snapshots and replicas.
  • Restores logs to a separate area.
  • Validates with hash checks.
  • Tracks changes.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners and compliance define priority time ranges for audit and forensics.
  • The service desk logs questions.
  • Owners define alternate evidence sources.
  • Prepare validation checks.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Security Operations & Forensics

  • Maps corrupted ranges.
  • Checks forensic case dependencies.
  • Preserves evidence.
  • Adds checks for log readability.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Forensics & Vendor Support Liaison

  • Opens cases with the storage and log platform vendors.
  • Asks for recovery help.
  • Requests guidance on snapshots.
  • Escalates through account teams.
  • Records Google Cloud guidance and case numbers.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for payments operations and informs leadership.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates workarounds and external contacts.
  • Tracks business impact.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 8.15 logging, 8.13 backup, 8.32 change management, 5.33 protection of records; SOC 2 security and processing integrity).
  • Assesses audit log integrity and retention.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Test destructive maintenance with dry runs on realistic data and verify results by reading, not by exit codes.

Keep immutable copies of audit logs in a separate platform.

Retain snapshots long enough to detect slow damage.

Hash log files and monitor integrity.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.15, 8.13, 8.32, 5.33) and SOC 2 expect log integrity and change control evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on halt-and-preserve and honest disclosure.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. Why did the job report success?
  2. What did you halt first?
  3. What could you restore?
  4. How did you assess evidence impact?
  5. What did you tell auditors?
  6. What design do you want?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the Forensics & Vendor Support Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
