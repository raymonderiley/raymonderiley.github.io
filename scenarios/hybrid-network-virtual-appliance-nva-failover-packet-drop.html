Hybrid Network Virtual Appliance (NVA) Failover Packet Drop

Hybrid Cloud (Azure Focus)

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a pair of network virtual appliances in Azure that fail over after a health probe misfire and a software update, so state is lost, routes point at a dead appliance for minutes and traffic between on-premises and cloud drops or is silently bypassed.

Participants are working-level: network security engineers, cloud platform engineers, application owners, the service desk, network operations and monitoring staff, the Microsoft and vendor support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of NVA high availability, load balancer probes, user-defined routes, session state sync and bypass risk.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Calder Bank inspects hybrid traffic through an active-passive NVA pair in an Azure hub, using an internal load balancer and user-defined routes. State synchronization runs over a dedicated interface. A vendor software update was applied to the passive node last night.

At 09:10 on Monday, a health probe misfire fails over to the passive node, which runs the new software and does not accept synced state. Active sessions drop. The load balancer then marks both nodes unhealthy and routes blackhole for six minutes. Operations temporarily add a direct route that bypasses inspection to restore connectivity. Trading-support applications and branch traffic time out.

What the team does not yet know: the bypass route has stayed in place and traffic is uninspected, the update changed the state sync protocol version, some flows are being resumed from stale state, and a recent security alert noted scanning against the hub.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize failover failure. Participants read probe, route and HA state to see why traffic dropped.
  • Restore inspected connectivity. The team restores a healthy appliance path and removes the bypass.
  • Control bypass risk. Engineers assess exposure during uninspected traffic.
  • Fix HA state. Participants resolve the version mismatch and sync.
  • Protect critical flows. The team prioritizes trading-support and branch traffic.
  • Improve NVA resilience. Participants review probes, update procedure and route design.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Remove any emergency bypass route as soon as inspected connectivity is verified, and record how long it existed.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Drops and a bypass lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Connectivity is restored with inspection and priority set.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. HA and exposure are diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A repair and review plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Repairs are applied in order.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Connectivity stabilizes.

T+06:00 — Validation. Inspection and routes are validated.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Inject #1: Failover drops traffic and a bypass remains

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 09:40. Trading-support and branch applications time out. The NVA pair failed over and then both nodes were marked unhealthy for six minutes.

The network security engineer finds the passive node runs a newer software version that rejects synced state. A direct bypass route was added and is still active.

Traffic is flowing uninspected. A security alert noted scanning against the hub.

Facilitator provides: HA status, probe logs, route tables and the change record.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Authorize removing the bypass when safe.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Network Security Engineering (NVA & Firewall)
  • Read HA state.
  • Align versions.
  • Plan bypass removal.
Cloud Platform Engineering
  • Check probes and routes.
  • Restore routing.
  • Track changes.
Application Owners & Service Desk
  • Rank flows.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Prepare checks.
Network Operations & Monitoring
  • Review bypass traffic.
  • Watch scanning.
  • Preserve logs.
Microsoft, Carrier & Vendor Support Liaison
  • Open cases.
  • Ask about versions.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform leadership.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Plan client desk notice.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about control obligations.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. Why did the probe misfire cause a total drop?
  2. How do you remove the bypass without a second outage?
  3. What did uninspected traffic expose?
  4. How do you fix the version mismatch?
  5. Which flows come first?
  6. How do you test failover safely?
  7. What do trading-support staff hear?
  8. Why was a passive node updated without a tested failover?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the active bypass until T2 reviews effective routes.
  • If players remove the bypass instantly, ask about connectivity.
  • Look for recorded temporary changes.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes inspected connectivity for critical hybrid traffic and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Network Security Engineering (NVA & Firewall)

  • Reads HA state, probe results and software versions.
  • Aligns versions and re-syncs state.
  • Removes the bypass route when safe.
  • Plans controlled failover testing.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Cloud Platform Engineering

  • Reviews load balancer probes and user-defined routes.
  • Restores correct routing.
  • Checks effective routes.
  • Tracks all temporary changes.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners rank flows.
  • The service desk logs trading-support impact.
  • Owners prepare reconnection checks.
  • Prepare validation checks.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Network Operations & Monitoring

  • Reviews traffic seen during the bypass for suspicious activity.
  • Watches scanning alerts.
  • Preserves logs.
  • Adds alerts for bypass routes.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Microsoft, Carrier & Vendor Support Liaison

  • Opens cases with Microsoft and the NVA vendor.
  • Asks about version compatibility and probe settings.
  • Requests guidance on HA patterns.
  • Escalates through the TAM and vendor.
  • Records Google Cloud guidance and case numbers.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for trading support operations and informs leadership.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates workarounds and external contacts.
  • Tracks business impact.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.28, 8.20 network security, 8.22 segregation, 8.32 change management; SOC 2 security and availability).
  • Assesses network control integrity and client service continuity.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Update NVA pairs in a procedure that tests failover and state compatibility before production.

Alert on any route that bypasses inspection and require approval and expiry for emergency bypasses.

Use robust probes with sensible timers.

Plan capacity and path symmetry.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.20, 8.22, 8.32) and SOC 2 expect network control and change evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on bypass control and tested failover.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

  5. How did you prioritize flows?

  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the Microsoft, Carrier & Vendor Support Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?

  13. What would you change in the design?
