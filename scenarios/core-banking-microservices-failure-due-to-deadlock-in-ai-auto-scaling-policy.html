Core Banking Microservices Failure Due to Deadlock in AI Auto-Scaling Policy

AI Failure & Autonomous Agent Disruption

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines an AI-based auto-scaling policy for core banking microservices that enters a feedback deadlock, scaling down services that other services depend on and scaling up unrelated ones, which exhausts shared database connections and stalls payments.

Participants are working-level: platform reliability engineers, cloud platform engineers, application owners, the service desk, operations and monitoring staff, the AI platform and cloud support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of dependency-aware scaling, manual override, connection limits and safe restoration.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Crestline Bank runs core banking as microservices on a container platform. An AI scaling policy adjusts replicas based on predicted load and cost. Services share a connection pool to the ledger database. Manual scaling overrides exist but require platform team approval and are rarely used.

At 12:00 on Monday, the model predicts a lunchtime dip and scales down the account service, then scales up the reporting service after a request spike. Reduced account replicas cause retries that increase load on the ledger service, which the policy interprets as a dip and also scales down. Connections to the ledger database saturate. Payment postings stall. By 12:40 the model's actions alternate every minute, worsening the state.

What the team does not yet know: whether disabling the policy will cause a sudden scale change, which service ordering is safe for restoration, whether in-flight payments are inconsistent, and what the model was trained on.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize scaling deadlock. Participants link service instability to policy actions.
  • Freeze the policy. The team disables automated scaling and sets manual replicas.
  • Restore dependencies. Engineers bring up the ledger and core services in order.
  • Protect data consistency. Participants reconcile in-flight payments.
  • Resume traffic carefully. The team controls load to avoid another surge.
  • Improve scaling governance. Participants review dependency awareness and override paths.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Freeze the scaling policy and set known-good replica counts before analyzing the model, and record every scaling action with time.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Stalled payments and oscillating scaling lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. Policy frozen and manual replicas set.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Dependencies and consistency are diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A restoration plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Services restore in order.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Payments stabilize.

T+06:00 — Validation. Consistency is validated.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes scaling decisions that cannot starve the services they serve and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

Platform Reliability Engineering

  • Freezes the policy and sets known-good replicas.
  • Reviews policy decision logs.
  • Plans dependency-aware rules.
  • Plans override design.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Cloud Platform Engineering

  • Restores the ledger service first, then core services.
  • Manages connection pool limits.
  • Validates health.
  • Tracks changes.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners define service dependency order and safe replica counts.
  • The service desk logs payment questions.
  • Owners define throttling.
  • Prepare validation checks.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Operations & Monitoring

  • Monitors queues and connection use.
  • Reconciles in-flight payments.
  • Preserves logs.
  • Adds alerts for scaling oscillation.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

AI Platform Vendor & Cloud Support Liaison

  • Opens cases with the cloud provider and AI platform vendor.
  • Asks about scaling API limits.
  • Requests guidance on policy logs.
  • Escalates through account teams.
  • Records Google Cloud guidance and case numbers.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for core banking operations and informs leadership.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates workarounds and external contacts.
  • Tracks business impact.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 8.6 capacity management, 8.14 redundancy, 8.32 change management, 8.16 monitoring; SOC 2 availability and processing integrity).
  • Assesses service availability and payment integrity.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Make scaling policies dependency-aware with minimum replica floors for critical services.

Add oscillation guards and rate limits to scaling actions.

Provide fast, pre-approved manual overrides.

Isolate connection pools per service.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.6, 8.14, 8.32, 8.16) and SOC 2 expect capacity, change and availability evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on freeze-first and controlled restoration.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

  1. How did you recognize the cause?
  2. What did you freeze first?
  3. How did you restore dependencies?
  4. How did you reconcile payments?
  5. What guardrails do you want?
  6. What override do you want?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the AI Platform Vendor & Cloud Support Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
