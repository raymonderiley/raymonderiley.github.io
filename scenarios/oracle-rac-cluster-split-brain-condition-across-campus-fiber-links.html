Oracle RAC Cluster Split-Brain Condition Across Campus Fiber Links

On-Premises Infrastructure

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines the response to a split-brain condition in a four-node Oracle Real Application Clusters (RAC) deployment stretched across two buildings on a campus, after both redundant fiber paths between the buildings degrade at once. Cluster interconnect heartbeats are lost, the voting disk and node eviction logic engage, and the team must decide how to restore a consistent database without losing committed transactions or corrupting data.

Participants are working-level: Oracle DBAs, clusterware and storage engineers, network and fiber technicians, operating system engineers, application and batch owners, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise explores how clusterware fencing is meant to behave, where it can fail, and how people reason about data integrity under pressure.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours. The twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions with different injects. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Cascade Freight Logistics runs its order management, dispatch and billing on a four-node Oracle RAC 19c cluster (nodes RAC1 and RAC2 in Building A, RAC3 and RAC4 in Building B), using ASM on synchronously mirrored storage with an extended-distance configuration. The campus has two diverse fiber routes between buildings, about 1.8 km, carrying the private interconnect VLANs, the SAN extension and the public network. A third voting file sits on an NFS quorum device in a small third location.

At 10:22 on a Tuesday, a utility contractor cuts the primary fiber duct. The secondary route had been rerouted through the same duct during a recent re-pull and no one updated the diagram. Both paths fail within 90 seconds. Interconnect traffic between the two buildings stops. Cluster Synchronization Services (CSS) on each side loses heartbeats; nodes RAC3 and RAC4 can still reach the quorum NFS voting file but RAC1 and RAC2 can reach neither building B nor, because of a firewall rule, the quorum location.

What the team does not yet know: the quorum NFS path was routed through building B and fails for building A, the extended RAC node eviction timeout was tuned to 30 seconds without testing, one node has been evicted and rebooted twice and is looping, the ASM mirrored disks may be partially stale, and the application tier uses connection pools that keep reconnecting to evicted nodes.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize split-brain and its risks. Participants interpret CSS, CRS and ASM alert logs (cssd.log, ocssd, crsd, alert_<SID>.log), voting file status and interconnect error counters to determine which nodes are in the surviving cluster and which were evicted.
  • Protect data integrity. The team agrees that integrity takes priority over availability, identifies how to confirm that only one sub-cluster is writing to the shared disks, and decides whether to stop all instances before restoring connectivity.
  • Restore connectivity and cluster membership safely. Participants plan the sequence for re-establishing fiber, verifying interconnect health, rejoining nodes and checking ASM disk group status.
  • Make a recovery decision with incomplete information. The team weighs running on two nodes in one building, failing over to a standby or restoring from backup, and documents the decision criteria.
  • Manage the application layer. Application owners address connection pool storms, in-flight transactions, batch jobs and customer-facing timeouts.
  • Learn from the diverse-path failure. The team examines why path diversity was not real, how documentation became stale and how change control missed the route consolidation.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Cluster state evidence must come from logs and commands the participants would actually use. Do not assume the vendor or Oracle Support can resolve the incident quickly.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Interconnect and node eviction alarms, plus application timeouts, lead to incident declaration.

T+00:30 — Cluster state assessment. The team establishes which nodes are up, which were evicted, and whether both sides were writing.

T+01:15 — Integrity decision. Decision on stopping instances, fencing and the safe recovery path.

T+02:00 — Connectivity restoration. Fiber repair options and temporary links are evaluated.

T+03:00 — Recovery path decision. Run on one side, rejoin nodes, or fail over; approvals are given.

T+04:30 — Execution. Rejoin, ASM rebalance and application reconnect steps are performed.

T+06:00 — Validation. Data consistency checks, reconciliation and performance verification.

T+07:30 — Return to service and close-out. Normal operations resume, communications issued, and incident closed for debrief.

Injects

Inject #1: Both campus fiber paths fail and the cluster splits

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 10:25 on Tuesday. Monitoring alerts show the interconnect between Building A and Building B is down. The dispatch application is timing out, and the order desk reports 'cannot save' errors. The DBA on call sees in the cluster logs that RAC3 and RAC4 evicted RAC1 and RAC2 at 10:23 and that RAC1 and RAC2 evicted each other repeatedly afterward. One node is in a reboot loop.

The alert log on RAC3 shows instance recovery completed for threads 1 and 2. The alert log on RAC1 shows that it continued to write redo for 46 seconds after the interconnect failed because its eviction timer was still counting down. The ASM alert log shows disks from the Building A failure group as offline on the B side and vice versa.

The network team reports that both fiber pairs show loss of light. The contractor on site says they cut a duct that has 'all the fiber on campus.' The quorum NFS voting file in the third location is reachable from Building B but not from Building A.

Facilitator provides: log excerpts from RAC1 and RAC3, the ASM disk group status, the fiber map showing two supposed routes and the application connection pool configuration.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident. State the priority between data integrity and availability.
  • Decide whether to stop all instances now.
  • Set the next update at T+00:30.
Oracle DBA & RAC Engineering
  • Identify which instances are alive and which wrote redo after the split.
  • Explain the risk if both sides wrote to the same disks and how you would check.
  • List the checks before allowing nodes to rejoin.
Storage & Clusterware Operations
  • Report the ASM disk group state and any stale mirrors.
  • Check which voting files each side can reach.
  • Estimate resync time and performance impact.
Network & Campus Fiber Operations
  • Confirm that both paths are down and the reason.
  • Ask the contractor for estimated repair time and review temporary link options.
  • Check why building A cannot reach the quorum location.
Application Owners & Batch Operations
  • Identify which services are failing and which are partially working.
  • Hold batch jobs and enable maintenance pages.
  • Reduce connection pool reconnects.
Server & Operating System Engineering
  • Stop reboot loops on the failing node.
  • Check clusterware status and fencing behavior on each host.
  • Preserve logs.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Activate manual dispatch and order entry procedures.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Ask for the documented RTO.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask whether diversity of fiber routes was validated.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. What is your leading hypothesis for the node evictions and how would you confirm which side is authoritative?
  2. RAC1 wrote redo for 46 seconds after the split. What are the possible consequences and how would you test for them?
  3. Should you stop everything now or keep the B side running? What are the trade-offs?
  4. What do you know about the voting file configuration and why can Building A not reach the quorum?
  5. What does your runbook say about rejoining evicted nodes?
  6. How would you know whether the ASM mirrors are consistent?
  7. Why was the second route in the same duct, and how did documentation go stale?
  8. What do you tell the order desk and dispatch teams right now?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the shared duct until the network team checks the physical route.
  • If players want to restart the evicted nodes immediately, ask what they would check first and who approves.
  • Pay attention to whether the DBA identifies the 46-second window as a data integrity risk.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, sets severity, and states that data integrity takes priority over availability.
  • Decides whether to stop the database on all nodes until the cluster state is verified.
  • Runs a 30-minute cadence, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency work from the fiber contractor and any temporary link.

Oracle DBA & RAC Engineering

  • Reviews alert logs and cluster logs on each node, notes the eviction events and reason codes, and determines which instances are running.
  • Confirms whether both sides issued writes after the interconnect failure, using redo thread and SCN analysis.
  • Runs consistency checks when instances return, including instance recovery messages, and decides if RMAN restore or Data Guard fallback is needed.
  • Plans the order for restarting instances and enabling services.

Storage & Clusterware Operations

  • Checks ASM disk group state, mirror consistency and failure groups after the split, including any offline or stale disks.
  • Verifies the voting file configuration and quorum access from each building.
  • Prepares for ASM resync, noting the duration and performance impact.
  • Ensures write ordering and storage-level mirroring does not mask an inconsistency.

Network & Campus Fiber Operations

  • Confirms the fiber path status with the carrier or contractor and identifies that the second path shares the damaged duct.
  • Looks for temporary links: wireless bridge, leased circuit, rerouted wavelengths.
  • Verifies that firewall rules do not block the quorum path from building A.
  • Corrects network documentation and prepares a path diversity audit.

Application Owners & Batch Operations

  • Application owners identify which services run on which nodes, and which transactions were in flight.
  • Batch operations hold the scheduler and queue restarts.
  • Owners adjust connection pools to prevent reconnect storms and use the maintenance page.
  • Prepare reconciliation tests and the business sign-off.

Server & Operating System Engineering

  • Checks the operating system and clusterware health on evicted nodes, including reboot loops and node fencing behavior.
  • Ensures that nodes are not rebooted repeatedly in a way that damages evidence or storage.
  • Confirms time synchronization and kernel messages.
  • Prepares for rejoining nodes in a controlled order.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates the continuity plan for order management and dispatch, and coordinates manual dispatch procedures.
  • Issues time-stamped updates to leadership and customer service.
  • Communicates with key customers about delays.
  • Tracks actual downtime vs RTO.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 controls 5.24-5.28; 8.14 redundancy; SOC 2 availability).
  • Evaluates contractual obligations and customer notification.
  • Captures evidence for decisions about stopping and recovery.
  • Logs findings on path diversity and stale diagrams.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Path diversity must be verified physically. Two fibers in one duct are one path. Maintain authoritative cable maps, walk the route, and re-verify after every re-pull or carrier change.

Design extended clusters with a quorum location that both sites can reach independently, and test every failure combination: site A isolated, site B isolated, interconnect only, and quorum only. Tune eviction timeouts based on test results, not defaults.

Fencing is the main protection from split-brain. Confirm that I/O fencing (for example, via storage or IPMI) works, that reboot loops cannot rejoin, and that the 46-second write window is understood and minimized.

Prepare an integrity-first runbook: when to stop all instances, how to compare SCN and redo, when to fail over to the standby, and who decides. Keep RMAN catalogs and backups reachable from both sides.

Evidence matters. Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 and SOC 2 look for redundancy testing, change control for network and firewall changes, and recovery tests that include data validation.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on how the team protected integrity and who made the decisions about stopping or continuing instances.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How did the team determine which nodes were authoritative and how long did it take?
  2. What would you have done about the 46 seconds of redo written by RAC1, and how would you verify the impact?
  3. Which was the greater risk: continuing on the B side or stopping all instances, and who decided?
  4. How did you confirm the ASM mirrors were consistent?
  5. What does your runbook say about rejoining evicted nodes and was it followed?
  6. Why could Building A not reach the quorum, and when did that configuration change?
  7. How did the shared duct escape notice, and what verification of path diversity do you perform?
  8. Where was the RMAN catalog and backups, and were they reachable?
  9. What is the eviction timeout, who set it and was it tested?
  10. How did application owners handle connection pools and in-flight transactions?
  11. What business data was at risk and who signs off on reconciliation?
  12. What communications went to dispatch and customers and were they accurate?
  13. What evidence did the scribe capture that an auditor would need?
  14. What access or credentials were missing during recovery?
  15. What will you test before the next construction season?
