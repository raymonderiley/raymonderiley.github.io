Azure Backup Vault Soft-Delete Retention Lock Bypass Attempt

Azure Cloud Resiliency

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines an attacker with a compromised privileged identity who tries to destroy Azure Backup recovery points by disabling soft delete, attempting to remove immutability and retention locks, and deleting protected items, while the team must detect, block and preserve recoverability.

Participants are working-level: backup and recovery engineers, cloud platform and identity engineers, application owners, the service desk, security operations and monitoring staff, the Microsoft support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of soft delete, immutable vaults, multi-user authorization, resource guards, role assignments and stop-protection operations.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Ironwood Bank protects 600 VMs, 80 SQL databases and file shares with Azure Backup in four Recovery Services vaults. Soft delete is enabled with 14-day retention. Two vaults have immutability in an unlocked state, and a resource guard is configured on only one vault. Backup Contributor is assigned to 14 identities, including two service principals.

At 02:10 on Saturday, a service principal credential used by a legacy automation job is used from an unfamiliar IP. Within 30 minutes the identity issues operations to disable soft delete on three vaults, change the immutability setting, stop protection with delete data on 40 VMs and attempt to remove the resource guard association. Some operations fail due to the guard on one vault. Security alerts fire at 02:50, just as a ransomware note appears on two file servers.

What the team does not yet know: stop-protection with delete data completed on 22 VMs in the vault without a guard, soft delete protects those recovery points for 14 days if it was not disabled first, at least one vault had soft delete disabled before deletions, the attacker also obtained a second credential through the same automation account, and the immutable vault cannot be changed once locked, but it is not locked.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize a destruction attempt. Participants read vault activity logs and alerts to see the sequence of attempted and successful operations.
  • Stop the attacker and preserve recovery. The team revokes credentials, blocks backup management operations and verifies which recovery points still exist.
  • Recover deleted data. Engineers use soft delete and undelete operations to restore protected items before retention expires.
  • Lock down the vaults. Participants enable immutability, resource guards and multi-user authorization and tighten role assignments.
  • Coordinate with ransomware response. The team links the backup attack to the ransomware event and prioritizes restores.
  • Improve backup security. Participants review immutability states, role scope, service principal hygiene and monitoring.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Do not delete, stop or modify any backup item without independent verification of the requester.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Backup alerts and a ransomware note lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. The attacker identity is revoked and vault operations are blocked.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Surviving recovery points are inventoried.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A restore and hardening plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Soft-deleted items are recovered and vaults are hardened.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Restores begin for priority systems.

T+06:00 — Validation. Integrity and security posture are verified.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Injects

Inject #1: Backup deletions race against ransomware

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 03:15. Security alerts show attempts to disable soft delete and stop protection on backup items. Two file servers show ransom notes. The activity log shows operations from a legacy service principal.

The backup engineer finds soft delete disabled on one vault and stop-protection with delete data completed on 22 VMs in a vault without a resource guard. The guarded vault blocked deletions.

Two vaults have immutability unlocked. Fourteen identities hold Backup Contributor. Another credential in the same automation account may be compromised.

Facilitator provides: activity log excerpts, vault configuration table, role assignment list and ransom note evidence.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Authorize emergency lockdown.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
Backup & Recovery Engineering (Azure Backup)
  • Inventory recovery points.
  • Undelete soft-deleted items.
  • Plan vault hardening.
Cloud Platform & Identity Engineering
  • Revoke the service principals.
  • Remove excess roles.
  • Check other credentials.
Application Owners & Service Desk
  • Rank systems for restore.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Plan workarounds.
Security Operations & Monitoring
  • Hunt for persistence.
  • Check ransomware indicators.
  • Preserve logs.
Microsoft Support & TAM Liaison
  • Open a case.
  • Ask about undelete.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform leadership.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Prepare regulator contact.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about notification.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. How do you tell which recovery points still exist?
  2. What do you do first: revoke identity, lock vaults or undelete items?
  3. Which deleted items are recoverable and for how long?
  4. How do you protect the remaining backups right now?
  5. Why were soft delete and immutability not enforced everywhere?
  6. How do you link this to the ransomware response?
  7. What access paths might the attacker still have?
  8. What does leadership need to hear?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the second credential until T2 reviews the automation account.
  • If players delete the vault operations log, stop and discuss evidence.
  • Look for revocation and undelete in parallel.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes securing backups and restoring critical systems and sets approval rules for vault changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency vault lockdown.
  • Decides on notifications.

Backup & Recovery Engineering (Azure Backup)

  • Reviews vault activity and inventories recovery points.
  • Undeletes soft-deleted items and verifies integrity.
  • Enables immutability and resource guards.
  • Plans restores.

Cloud Platform & Identity Engineering

  • Revokes the compromised service principals and removes excess role assignments.
  • Blocks operations with conditional policies and checks other credentials.
  • Rotates secrets.

  • Assesses notification obligations.
  • Captures evidence of recovery point status.
  • Logs findings on controls.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Make backups survive a privileged attacker: immutable vaults locked after validation, soft delete always on with enhanced retention, and resource guards with separate identities and subscriptions.

Reduce identity risk: few backup operators, just-in-time elevation, managed identities instead of long-lived secrets and alerts on any stop-protection or retention change.

Keep an offline or logically separated copy for the most critical data.

Practice restore under attack: runbooks for undelete, isolated restore and integrity checks.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.13, 8.2) and SOC 2 expect backup protection, restore test evidence and privileged access controls.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on immutability, separation of duties and restore integrity.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

Hot wash questions

Use these with the full group of participants after the individual note-taking step.

  1. How quickly did you see the backup attack?
  2. What did you do first?
  3. How did you recover soft-deleted items?
  4. What could not be recovered?
  5. How did you scope the credentials?
  6. How did you decide on locking immutability?
  7. How did you link backups to the ransomware response?
  8. How did you validate restores?
  9. How accurate were updates to the CEO and CISO?
  10. What did Microsoft support add?
  11. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  12. How are backup operators controlled?
  13. What access or permissions were missing?
  14. What alerts are missing?
  15. What would you change in backup design?
