Hybrid Certificate Authority (CA) SCEP Enrollment Failure

Hybrid Cloud (Azure Focus)

Exercise Overview

This tabletop exercise examines a SCEP enrollment path between an on-premises certificate authority and cloud device management that fails after a service account password change and an expired NDES certificate, so devices cannot enroll or renew certificates for Wi-Fi, VPN and mutual TLS.

Participants are working-level: PKI engineers, endpoint and device management engineers, application owners, the service desk, security operations and monitoring staff, the Microsoft support liaison, business continuity coordinators and compliance staff. The exercise tests understanding of SCEP and NDES, certificate lifetimes, renewal windows, connectors and fallback authentication.

A full run takes 3 to 4 hours, and the twelve-inject library supports repeated sessions. Inject #1 alone supports a 90-minute session.

Scenario

Willowmere Health uses an on-premises enterprise CA with NDES and a cloud certificate connector for 9,000 managed devices. Device certificates last 30 days with renewals at 7 days before expiry. Wi-Fi and VPN require device certificates.

At 07:00 on Monday, a service account password rotation breaks the connector and the NDES enrollment certificate has expired. SCEP requests fail. About 1,100 devices have certificates expiring within three days. By Wednesday, they will lose Wi-Fi and VPN access. New devices cannot enroll. Clinicians' tablets are in the first expiry group.

What the team does not yet know: the CA itself is healthy, the CRL publishing point also needs attention, extending certificate lifetimes requires a template change and approval, and an earlier security review suggested unauthorized requests to the NDES server.

Exercise Objectives

  • Recognize enrollment failure early. Participants understand the countdown from expiry dates.
  • Restore SCEP. The team repairs the connector account and NDES certificate.
  • Protect clinicians. Engineers prioritize devices nearest expiry.
  • Use fallbacks safely. Participants weigh temporary measures against security.
  • Check trust components. The team validates CA, CRL and chains.
  • Improve PKI operations. Participants review lifetimes, monitoring and renewal alerts.
  • Communicate precisely and document for audit. Updates to leadership, site managers and users are accurate and time-stamped, and the evidence collected would satisfy ISO/IEC 27001:2022 incident management and SOC 2 availability reviewers.

Ground Rules

  • This is a discussion-based exercise. No production systems, accounts, configurations or backups will be touched. Commands and consoles are discussed, not executed.
  • Participants answer as they would in their real roles, using the runbooks, contact lists and tools that exist today. If a document or control does not exist, say so; that is a finding, not a failure.
  • Treat every inject as true unless the facilitator states otherwise. Do not argue the premise. Work the problem.
  • Time moves on the facilitator's clock. When an inject lands, the exercise clock is the time stated in the inject, regardless of how long discussion took.
  • Decisions must be stated aloud and recorded by the scribe with the decision maker's name and the exercise-clock time.
  • No blame. The exercise evaluates systems, process and documentation, not individuals.
  • Observers and evaluators record but do not answer questions for players unless the facilitator asks.
  • Do not weaken certificate validation to restore access without security approval and an expiry.

Exercise Timelines

Timeline and Escalation Triggers

T+00:00 — Detection and declaration. Enrollment failure and expiry countdown lead to declaration.

T+00:30 — Containment. The connector and NDES are fixed, priority set.

T+01:15 — Diagnosis. Trust and device impact are diagnosed.

T+02:00 — Remediation planning. A renewal and fallback plan is agreed.

T+03:00 — Execution. Renewals proceed in priority order.

T+04:30 — Stabilization. Enrollment stabilizes.

T+06:00 — Validation. Certificates and access are validated.

T+07:30 — Return to normal and close-out. Normal operation resumes and the incident is closed for debrief.

Inject #1: SCEP enrollment fails with certificates about to expire

Time frame T+00:00 · Variant Primary

Situation

It is 07:40. Device certificates cannot renew. About 1,100 devices expire within three days. New devices cannot enroll.

The PKI engineer finds the connector service account password changed and the NDES enrollment certificate expired.

The CA is healthy. A prior review hinted at unusual requests to NDES.

Facilitator provides: connector logs, device expiry list, CA health and NDES request history.

Role actions

Incident Commander
  • Declare the incident and name the technical lead and scribe.
  • Authorize repairing the connector and NDES.
  • Set the next update at T+00:20.
PKI Engineering
  • Repair account.
  • Replace NDES certificate.
  • Check CA and CRL.
Endpoint & Device Management Engineering
  • List by expiry.
  • Plan waves.
  • Handle failures.
Application Owners & Service Desk
  • Rank clinical devices.
  • Service desk: issue the holding message.
  • Define needs.
Security Operations & Monitoring
  • Review requests.
  • Check CRL.
  • Preserve logs.
Microsoft, Carrier & Vendor Support Liaison
  • Open a case.
  • Ask about renewals.
  • Record guidance.
Business Continuity & Communications
  • Inform leadership.
  • Prepare the first update.
  • Plan clinical notice.
GRC, Audit & Compliance
  • Open the incident record.
  • Ask about access control duties.
  • Start the evidence log.

Discussion questions

  1. How much time remains before access is lost?
  2. What do you repair first and in what order?
  3. Which devices renew first?
  4. What fallback is acceptable for clinicians?
  5. How do you review the unusual NDES requests?
  6. Should you extend lifetimes?
  7. What do clinical staff hear?
  8. Why was there no expiry alert?

Facilitator notes

  • Do not reveal the NDES request pattern until OPS reviews logs.
  • If players disable certificate checks, ask about risk.
  • Look for countdown awareness.
  • Time-box to 35 minutes.

Roles and Responsibilities Summary

Incident Commander

  • Declares the incident, prioritizes trusted device and service authentication and sets approval rules for risky changes.
  • Runs a 20-minute cadence at first, assigns the scribe and records decisions.
  • Authorizes emergency changes, exceptions and spend.
  • Decides on stakeholder communications and the end of the incident.

PKI Engineering

  • Repairs the connector service account and NDES certificate.
  • Verifies CA and CRL health.
  • Plans issuance capacity.
  • Plans template review.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Endpoint & Device Management Engineering

  • Builds the list of devices by expiry.
  • Forces renewals in waves after repair.
  • Handles devices that fail renewal.
  • Tracks status.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Application Owners & Service Desk

  • Application owners rank clinical devices.
  • The service desk logs access calls.
  • Owners define interim access needs.
  • Prepare validation checks.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Security Operations & Monitoring

  • Reviews NDES request logs for unauthorized requests.
  • Checks CRL availability.
  • Preserves logs.
  • Adds expiry alerts.
  • Records actions and results for the scribe.

Microsoft, Carrier & Vendor Support Liaison

  • Opens a case on the certificate connector and enrollment.
  • Asks about renewal behavior and fallbacks.
  • Requests guidance on safe lifetime changes.
  • Escalates through the TAM.
  • Records Google Cloud guidance and case numbers.

Business Continuity & Communications

  • Activates continuity for clinical operations and informs leadership.
  • Issues time-stamped updates.
  • Coordinates workarounds and external contacts.
  • Tracks business impact.

GRC, Audit & Compliance

  • Ensures the incident is recorded and classified (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 5.24-5.28, 8.24 use of cryptography, 8.5 secure authentication; SOC 2 security and availability).
  • Assesses device trust and clinical access.
  • Captures evidence of decisions and approvals.
  • Logs findings for the debrief.

Resilience Architect Guidance

Track expiry of every PKI component, including NDES and connector certificates, with alerts well ahead of time.

Test service account rotations against dependent connectors.

Use renewal windows long enough to survive a week-long failure.

Protect and monitor NDES endpoints.

Auditors for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (8.24 cryptography, 8.5, 8.9) and SOC 2 expect key and certificate management evidence.

Post-Exercise Debrief Guide

  1. Close the exercise formally. The facilitator announces the end of the exercise, reminds participants that all actions were simulated, and thanks the players. Nobody leaves before the hot wash.
  2. Take five minutes of silent individual notes. Each participant writes three things that worked, three that did not, and any gap in tools, access or documentation, before group discussion begins.
  3. Run the hot wash with the full group. Use the Hot wash questions below, going role by role for the first pass and then opening the floor. Keep to 45 to 60 minutes. Focus on expiry countdown and safe fallback.
  4. Hold evaluator readouts. Evaluators share timing observations, decision-log quality, communication accuracy and gaps against each objective.
  5. Prioritize findings. Group findings into people, process, technology and documentation, rate each by impact and effort, and select no more than ten for the corrective action plan.
  6. Assign corrective actions. Each action has an owner, a due date and a verification method. Record them in the GRC tracker as evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 management review and SOC 2 reviews.
  7. Publish the after-action report within 10 business days. Include the timeline, decisions, findings, root causes, corrective actions and runbook updates, and circulate for review.
  8. Schedule the follow-up. Plan a validation drill in 90 days for the highest-priority gaps and rerun the exercise with different injects within 6 to 12 months.

  2. What did you repair first?
  3. How did you renew devices?

  5. How did you review NDES requests?
  6. How did you verify enrollment?
  7. How accurate were updates to leadership?
  8. What did the Microsoft, Carrier & Vendor Support Liaison add?
  9. What evidence did the scribe capture for auditors?
  10. What access or permissions were missing?
  11. What alerts are missing?
  12. What temporary changes remain and who owns them?
  13. What would you change in the design?
  14. What did the team do well?
  15. What surprised you?
